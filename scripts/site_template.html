      Methodology

      Every filter comes from a written investment case, and every company removed is logged with a reason.

      What the score is made of

      The score adds up six things, each worth a set number of points. How reliable the figures are is shown separately as a confidence rating, so a company is not ranked higher just because its accounts are easier to read.

      How the ten were found

      From the Companies House register to a ranked list.

      1. Write the caseA testable view of the sector, turned into a list of criteria.
      2. Pull the registerAll UK companies in the sector's industry codes.
      3. Read the accountsSales, profit, cash and owners from each filing, including scanned accounts.
      4. Check and cleanOwners traced, figures checked against filings, off-topic firms removed.
      5. Score and explainSix weighted criteria, confidence, price and deal size shown separately.

      Limits of this ranking

      The ranking points to where to look and gives a first view on price. It is not a valuation and not advice.

      • Price check is a first passEach business type has its own price range (services 6–8x EBITDA, contractors 5–7x, network owners 12–18x with a 12% return target, project companies on the cash left in their contract), taken from listed peers and deal averages rather than deals in each company's niche. Debt held by owners' holding companies is not visible.
      • Deal size is an estimateEBITDA times the range for the business type, or a public deal value where one exists.
      • Repeat revenue: renewal rates are not publicThe share of repeat sales is read from the accounts for most ranked companies, but accounts rarely give renewal or churn rates, and for the rest it is judged from the type of business.
      • Net debt is checked by hand only for the leading companiesFor the rest it is read automatically from the accounts, which can miss borrowings, so it is only used to mark companies down, never up.
      • Some accounts have no profit and loss account
      • A few companies cannot be confirmed
      • Demand splits are estimatesFor the top ten, each company's sales are split across what they depend on, estimated from the accounts and public contracts; filings rarely give the split directly. Further down the list, a company with two named sources is assumed 70/30.
      • Public information onlyEverything comes from accounts filed at Companies House and public sources. Accounts can be up to two years old, and none of this is investment advice.
