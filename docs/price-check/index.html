How to read itPrice for the business applies multiples that listed companies and recent deals point to. Taking away debt and adding back cash gives the price for the shares. Cash conversion is how much of the year's EBITDA arrived as cash. The last two columns show the most a private equity buyer could pay and still make a 20% yearly return.

How the prices were set

Repeat-revenue businesses: 6.0–8.0x EBITDA. Gamma (UK business communications, 89% recurring revenue) traded near 6x before its bid and was taken private at about 7.6x; Crowe puts listed UK services at 7.5x. Our companies are far smaller, so the range sits at or below these.

Project contractors: 5.0–7.0x. SPIE, a large listed technical services group, trades near 8x; UK private deals under £250m have a median near 6x. Project-by-project revenue and small size put these at a discount to SPIE. Companies with EBITDA under £3m take 0.5x off.

Buyer's case: borrow 3.0x EBITDA (2.5x for contractors) at 8.5% interest, grow at half the historic rate (capped at 6% a year), use spare cash to repay debt, and sell after 5 years at the same multiple paid. Where a filing has a cash flow statement, the case uses that company's actual capital spending and cash conversion. Debt and cash are as at each company's latest year end. Listed peers

  • Gamma Communications, uK business communications (89% recurring revenue): about 6x EV/EBITDA before the bid. Business telecoms and managed networks.
  • SPIE, european technical and network services: about 8x EV/EBITDA. Network build and maintenance (upper reference; much larger).
  • Dycom Industries, uS telecom network contractor: about 20x EV/EBITDA. Not used: priced on the US data-centre and AI build-out boom.

Deals

  • Epiris takes Gamma Communications private (Sept 2026): about 7.6x 2025 adjusted EBITDA (EV £1.079bn on £141.7m).
  • UK private deals under £250m (H1 2025): median 6.2x EV/EBITDA.
  • UK mid-cap deals £50m-£250m (MarktoMarket): mean 10.0x; nano caps just over 6x.
  • Strategic buyers active in UK telecom services: terms not disclosed. Vinci Energies (Axians UK) bought Novo Technologies (Apr 2026); M Group bought Telent (2025); Kubus bought Ammcom (2026).

Listed infrastructure funds

  • Cordiant Digital Infrastructure, listed digital infrastructure trust (towers, data centres, fibre): shares at about a 16% discount to NAV (124p vs 146p, June 2026). Listed buyers of digital infrastructure are themselves priced below their own asset values, so they are unlikely to pay up for small service businesses; trade buyers and mid-market PE are the likelier exits.
