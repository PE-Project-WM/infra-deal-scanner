How we select companies

From 48,467 Companies House records to 82 ranked companies, in six steps. Every filter comes from a written investment case, and every company removed is logged with a reason.

Read this page asEach step keeps fewer companies. The large number is how many are left after the step; the red number is how many it removed. Bars use a square-root scale so the small steps stay visible.
  1. 48,467starting point

    Step 1. On the register

    Why: Every company in the Companies House register with one of six industry codes used by digital-infrastructure businesses.

    How: Industry codes: 61100 (fibre and wired telecoms networks), 61200 (wireless networks, towers and fixed wireless), 61300 (satellite networks), 61900 (other telecoms networks), 63110 (data centres, data processing and hosting), 42220 (building and maintaining telecoms and utility networks). Codes are self-chosen by each company, so this step casts a wide net that later steps narrow.

  2. 1,209−47,258 removed

    Step 2. Active, private, full accounts, three or more years old

    Why: Keeps companies a buyer could actually acquire and analyse.

    How: Active companies only; private or unlisted public limited companies; accounts filed as full, group or medium-sized (small and micro accounts do not show sales); incorporated at least three years ago, so there is a track record.

  3. 620−589 removed

    Step 3. Sales between £10m and £250m

    Why: The size range mid-market private equity and infrastructure investors buy.

    How: Sales come from each company's latest filed accounts. Figures for 605 companies that file scanned accounts were read by text recognition (OCR); 237 could not be read and may be missing.

  4. 211−409 removed

    Step 4. Passed ownership and quality checks

    Why: Removes companies that cannot be bought on their own or are not really in the sector.

    How: Owners are traced through the register of people with significant control and checked against the accounts and public sources. Each removal is logged with its reason:

    Outside the sector
    Software, payments, energy, equipment sellers and other businesses that registered under a telecoms code but do not run or build networks.    		224
    Foreign-owned subsidiaries
    Owned by an overseas group, so not buyable on their own.    		142
    Duplicates
    Subsidiaries of a group that is already counted, so the group is assessed once.    		39
    Part of a much larger group
    UK divisions of large groups that would not be sold separately.    		4

  5. 95−116 removed

    Step 5. Profitable

    Why: A buyout needs profit to borrow against.

    How: EBITDA must be positive in the latest accounts. Of the 211 that passed the checks, 63 are loss-making (growing network builders among them go to the growth infrastructure watchlist) and 53 have no EBITDA that could be read.

  6. 82−13 removed

    Step 6. Confirmed to trade in the sector, and ranked

    Why: An industry code is not enough: each company is checked against what it actually does.

    How: Each profitable company is checked from its accounts, website and news. Only companies confirmed as in the sector or adjacent to it are ranked; 13 are still waiting for that check. The ranked companies are then scored.

After selection

The 82 ranked companies are scored on five factors and given a confidence rating, a type of deal, a likely deal size and, for the top ten, a price check and four deal-readiness checks. See the methodology. The whole process reruns every Monday; companies move in and out as new accounts are filed.
