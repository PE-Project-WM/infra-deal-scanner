Market intelligence

How concentrated the top ten are on a few demand drivers, the filings that hint at a sale or new debt, this week's changes, and the loss-making network builders worth watching.

Ten companies, but how many separate bets?

Companies that depend on the same thing would struggle at the same time, so the ten are grouped by what their sales depend on, split where a company has more than one source.

The top ten is about 4 separate bets, not ten. Worth knowing, but no single factor dominates.

32% of the ten

Altnet funding and interest rates

  • VEA Group (85%)
  • Open Fibre Networks (70%)
  • Kcom Group (60%)
  • National Grid Telecoms (60%)
  • Wifinity (40%)

Paid by new fibre networks to lay cable. If those networks raise less money or rates stay high, they build less and these companies lose work together.

25% of the ten

Government spending

  • Alert Communications
  • The Networking People (TNP) (85%)
  • Wifinity (40%)
  • Clarus Networks (25%)

Paid from public budgets: councils, schools, the NHS, defence and publicly funded transport. Spending cuts would hit them together.

21% of the ten

Mobile operator and emergency-services spend

  • The Wireless Infrastructure Company
  • Beacon Communication Services (70%)
  • National Grid Telecoms (40%)

Depend on mobile networks and emergency services buying radio and wireless work.

10% of the ten

Construction and utility capex

  • Open Fibre Networks (30%)
  • Beacon Communication Services (30%)
  • Clarus Networks (25%)
  • VEA Group (15%)

General building and utility contractors. Depend on the construction cycle.

7% of the ten

SME IT and telecoms spending

  • Kcom Group (40%)
  • Wifinity (20%)
  • The Networking People (TNP) (15%)

Sell phone, network and hosting services to small and mid-sized businesses. A squeeze on business budgets would hit them together.

5% of the ten

Offshore oil, gas, wind and shipping activity

  • Clarus Networks (50%)

Serve ships, rigs and offshore wind farms. A fall in offshore activity would hit all of them at once.

The list is flagged when 30% or more of the ten depends on one thing, and treated as a serious concentration at 50%. Each company counts once, spread across the things its sales depend on (for example 70% maritime, 30% public sector). Splits are estimated from the accounts and are open to review.
