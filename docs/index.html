Ten UK digital-infrastructure companies worth a closer look

Every week this page reads the public accounts of UK fibre, wireless, satellite, data-centre and network-building companies, and ranks the best fits for a private investor buying a £10–250m business.

. By William Mikollovci. Updated automatically every Monday from Companies House.

    Scored out of 100 against a written investment case. A high score means a good fit with that case, not a good price: see the price check further down. Select a company for why it is on the list and what to check first.

    The type of deal says what kind of investor a company suits. Confidence says how far the figures behind the score can be trusted. Neither changes the score.

    Steady compounder
    Repeat-type revenue and margins that have held steady. Suits infrastructure funds that want reliable income.
    High-growth
    Sales growing 20%+ a year over several years (25%+ where only one year is known). Suits growth private equity.
    Fixable dip
    Falling or thin results with a named problem and a fix. Suits funds that buy and turn around.
    Under pressure
    Falling or thin results with no fix identified yet. Not a buy until one is found.
    Between types
    Fits none cleanly, for example uneven margins or project-by-project sales.

    What would they cost, and would a buyer make money?

    A first price check, kept separate from the score. The price for the business applies multiples that listed companies and recent deals point to. Taking away debt and adding back cash from each company's latest accounts gives the price for the shares. Cash conversion is how much of the year's EBITDA arrived as cash. The last two columns show the most a private equity buyer could pay for the business and still make a 20% yearly return.

    What the score is made of

    The score adds up five things, each worth a set number of points. How reliable the figures are is shown separately as a confidence rating, so a company is not ranked higher just because its accounts are easier to read.

    Limits of this screen

    The screen points to where to look and gives a first view on price. It is not a valuation and not advice.

    • Price check is a first passRanges come from listed peers and deal averages, not from deals in each company's niche. Debt and cash are read from each top-ten company's latest accounts, which can be up to two years old, and debt held by owners' holding companies is not visible.
    • Repeat revenue is partly read, partly estimatedFor the leading companies it is read from the accounts (sales split, income paid in advance, contracts described), which rarely give renewal rates. For the rest it is judged from the type of business.
    • Balance sheet, not debt capacityNet assets, cash, margin and interest cover where known. True debt capacity needs net debt, capital spending and cash conversion.
    • Some scanned accounts not read
    • Sector check pending
    • One label per companyEach company is grouped by its main source of demand. Most have several, and the split is not yet known.

    How the ten were found

    Each filter comes from the written investment case. Every company removed is logged with a reason.

    1. Write the caseA testable view of the sector, turned into a list of criteria.
    2. Pull the registerAll UK companies in the sector's industry codes.
    3. Read the accountsSales, profit, cash and owners from each filing.
    4. Check and cleanOwners traced, duplicates merged, off-topic firms removed.
    5. Score and explainFive weighted criteria, confidence shown separately, every weekly move explained.
