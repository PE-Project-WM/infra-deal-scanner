This week's findings

Market intelligence
  • Wifinity ranks first with 93 out of 100: managed wifi networks for military bases, student housing and holiday parks (Wifinity), sales of £39.8m and a 40% margin. A high score means a close fit with the investment case, not a good price.
  • The ten are really about 4 separate bets. Weighting each company by how much of its sales depend on each factor, 32% rests on altnet funding and interest rates, 25% rests on government spending, 21% rests on mobile operator and emergency-services spend.
  • A good company is not automatically a good deal. At typical market prices, none of the ten would give a private equity buyer a 20% yearly return, and 4 only at the low end of the price range. The most a buyer could pay ranges from 2.0 to 6.2 times EBITDA.
  • Deal size: 6 of the ten look like £10–250m deals. Wifinity, Open Fibre Networks, The Wireless Infrastructure Company, Kcom Group are probably or possibly above £250m, mostly network owners that infrastructure funds price well above service firms.
  • Deal readiness: none of the ten passes all four checks (size, buyable, price, figures). Clarus Networks has no outright fail.
  • No change to the top ten this week. 34 Companies House events were recorded for the ranked companies since 27 August 2026.

How the ranking is built

Every filter comes from a written investment case, and every company removed is logged with a reason.

How we select companies
  1. Find

    Every UK company registered under six digital-infrastructure industry codes, from the Companies House register: 48,467 records.

  2. Filter

    Keep active private companies with full accounts and £10–250m of sales; remove foreign-owned subsidiaries, duplicates and firms outside the sector. See each step.

  3. Rank

    Score the 82 that remain on five factors, then show price, deal size, confidence and deal readiness beside the score. Read the method.
