      Methodology

      Every filter comes from a written investment case, and every company removed is logged with a reason.

      What the score is made of

      The score adds up five things, each worth a set number of points. How reliable the figures are is shown separately as a confidence rating, so a company is not ranked higher just because its accounts are easier to read.

      How the ten were found

      From the Companies House register to a ranked list.

      1. Write the caseA testable view of the sector, turned into a list of criteria.
      2. Pull the registerAll UK companies in the sector's industry codes.
      3. Read the accountsSales, profit, cash and owners from each filing, including scanned accounts.
      4. Check and cleanOwners traced, figures checked against filings, off-topic firms removed.
      5. Score and explainFive weighted criteria, confidence, price and deal size shown separately.

      Limits of this screen

      The screen points to where to look and gives a first view on price. It is not a valuation and not advice.

      • Price check is a first passRanges come from listed peers and deal averages, not from deals in each company's niche. Debt and cash are read from each top-ten company's latest accounts, which can be up to two years old, and debt held by owners' holding companies is not visible.
      • Deal size is an estimateEBITDA times a multiple range, or a public deal value where one exists. Network owners are priced on a higher range because infrastructure funds pay more for them.
      • Repeat revenue is partly read, partly estimatedFor the leading companies it is read from the accounts (sales split, income paid in advance, contracts described), which rarely give renewal rates. For the rest it is judged from the type of business.
      • Balance sheet, not debt capacityNet assets, cash, margin and interest cover where known. True debt capacity needs net debt, capital spending and cash conversion.
      • Some scanned accounts not read
      • Sector check pending
      • Demand splits are estimatesFor the top ten, each company's sales are split across what they depend on, estimated from the accounts and public contracts; filings rarely give the split directly. Further down the list, a company with two named sources is assumed 70/30.
