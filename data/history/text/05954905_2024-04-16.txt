Strategic report
For the year ended 31 March 2023
The directors present their strategic report together with the audited financial statements for the year ended 31
March 2023.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the company during the year was the provision of hardware, cloud and subscription
licensing and the associated services involved in the design, transition and on-going management of information
technology and communication solutions.
Review of the business and future developments
The company continues to perform strongly with organic revenue growth in the year of 5% despite the disruption
caused by Covid 19 and other macroeconomic factors including supply shortages on key components leading to
extended lead times for vendors to deliver the equipment that underpins the company’s project delivery.
Year-on-year revenues were up 5% to £81,716,000 with robust growth in on-premise revenues during the year.
Overall gross margins reduced from 36% to 34% as a result of a change in product mix, whilst recurring services
increased from 55% of total revenue to 59%, with continued strong growth in managed services, cloud and
subscription licences, including those delivered using the company’s private cloud platform.
During the year, the company continued to invest in system development and security and invest in people to
ensure the company is best placed to continue to deliver organic growth going forwards. As a result, Adjusted
EBITDA declined by 37% compared to the prior year.
The company received nine awards during the year, of which six were from key vendors such as Cisco,
AppDynamics and Palo Alto Networks. The company was specifically acknowledged for it expertise in both the
Cloud and SASE. The company also received two Transform Europe awards for ‘best technology brand’ and, for
the fifth year running, the company was published in the top 50 Great Places to Work in the UK.
During the year, the company was successfully certified as a B Corporation. This means that Natilik has been
certified by B Lab, the not-for-profit organisation behind the B Corp movement, as having met rigorous social and
environmental standards which represent its commitment to goals outside of shareholder profit.
Financial risk management
The company’s principal financial instruments, comprise bank balances, trade debtors and trade creditors. The
main purpose of these instruments is to maintain sufficient liquidity for the company to finance its operations.
The policies set out by the directors are implemented by the company's finance department.
Working capital requirements are funded principally by trading, out of the bank balance. Exposure to interest
rates is reviewed regularly by the directors. Exposure to foreign exchange movements is managed on an
ongoing basis with the company having access to a number of hedging instruments allowing future purchases of
foreign exchange balances to be made at a committed rate.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Liquidity risk
This is the risk that the company will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they fall due. The company’s
approach to managing liquidity is to ensure, as far as possible, that it will always have sufficient liquidity to meet
its liabilities when due, under both normal and stressed conditions, without incurring unacceptable losses or
risking damage to the company's reputation.
Macroeconomic conditions risk
Adverse macroeconomic conditions and deterioration in the global economic environment in the markets in
which the company operates, including, but not limited to, the impact of Covid-19, Brexit, the availability of skilled
labour, the war in Ukraine and cost of living increase may lead to a reduction in the level of demand from its
customers for existing and new products and services.
Supplier reliance risk
The company has a close partner relationship with Cisco and other strategic partners as well as a number of
smaller, but important relationships with other vendors. The directors regularly review the relationships with it
partners to ensure that it meets its strategic objectives and believes its relationship with all of its important
vendors are very strong. However, should any of these relationships be terminated the directors would seek new
relationships to continue its principal activity.
Key performance indicators
Natilik’s key performance indicators are turnover, Adjusted EBITDA and sales mix. The company is focussed on
continuing to grow turnover and Adjusted EBITDA in addition to increasing recurring revenues as a proportion of
total revenue. Turnover increased by 5% versus the prior year but Adjusted EBITDA declined by 37% to
£4,460,000 following a year of significant investment in people, marketing, systems and security. Recurring
revenues represent 59% of total revenue, an increase of 4% from the prior year.

The likely consequences of any decision in the long term:
Long term value is delivered by continued focus on the 5 “founding principles” of People, Clients, Proposition,

2.3. Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared using the going concern basis of accounting. The
directors continually monitor the ability of the company to continue to operate as a going concern.
The company meets its day-to-day working capital requirements through its bank facilities and the
cash generated from profitable trading.
The company’s forecasts and projections, taking in to account the reasonable possibility of changes
in trading performance, show that the company is able to operate within the level of its current
facilities, which is supported by trading in the period since the year end when measured against both
the prior period and associated budgets which have been set.
The current economic conditions continue to create uncertainty over the level of demand for the
company’s products and services. The directors have carried out detailed stress tests in order to
consider how much performance would need to degrade before cash would be constrained, along
with the likelinood of such a scenario occurring. After undergoing this exercise, the directors are
comfortable scenario that would result in the business not having sufficient cash reserves is
extremely remote.
Share buybacks within the company are refinanced through the use of bank loans taken on and cash
made available from the company to other group companies. Such loans are only taken on following
extensive due diligence that ensures the full repayment of both interest charges and the loan amount
can be achieved during the term of the loan.
After making enquiries and undertaking appropriate stress tests, the directors have a reasonable
expectation that the company has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the
foreseeable future. The company therefore continues to adopt the going concern basis in preparing
its financial statements.
2.4 Turnover

Turnover relates to the provision of hardware, software and associated services involved in the
design, installation and on-going management of business communications solutions.
Turnover is measured based on amounts earned or earnable on contracts, excluding value added
tax.
Revenue is recognised as follows:
° On-Premise (hardware): Hardware revenue is recognised on the delivery of the goods to the

Customer;
e Transition services (installation services): Transition revenue is recognised based on

percentage of completion of the project;
° Recurring revenue (vendor support services, managed services, licensing services): Revenue

from these services is recognised rateably over the period of the contract; and
. Connectivity services: Revenue is recognised based on the volume and duration of calls made

and circuits provided.
