GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2023
Introduction
The directors present their report and the financial statements of the group for the year ended 31 December
2023.
Business review
The group's principal activity continued to be the provision of e-discovery and document support services,
primarily to the legal sector.
Acquisition of LOD
On 31 August 2023 Company acquired 100% of the issued share capital of Surf (Topco) Limited and its
Group (the “LOD Group” or “LOD”) for cash consideration of £118,027,000. The acquisition was funded by the
issuance of intercompany debt by Consilio, LLC US to the Company. The LOD group specialises in the
provision of flexible legal resourcing utilising a variety of alternative models including secondments, remote
lawyers and virtual legal teams and supporting businesses in using legal technology. The consolidated
financial statements include the results of LOD for the period 1 September 2023 to 31 December 2023.
Disposal of US businesses
On 31 December 2023 this Group disposed 100% of the share capital of several subsidiary companies to
Consilio, LLC US (a fellow subsidiary undertaking of Skopima Topco Holdings LP). 100% of the indirect
holdings in LOD US Ine, together with its direct subsidiaries LOD Solutions Inc, Lawyers on Demand Inc and
LOD Law Inc and Syke Legal Engineering Inc were sold for a total consideration of USD 25. This resulted in a
profit on disposal of £59,000 in the financial period.
Loan
During the year the Company entered into loan notes with Consilio US, LLC of £171,047,000. Interest is
charged at 8.75 per cent per annum. The loan notes are repayable in full on 31 December 2028. Interest is
payable in arrears on 31 December each year, commencing on 31 December 2024. Interest of £4,971,000
has been charged to the P&L in the period. These loan notes were used to fund the acquisition of the LOD
Group. :
Result for the year :
The group delivered sales revenue of £82,973,000 and profit before tax of £3,470,000 for the year.
Amortisation of goodwill of £8,477,000 (2022: £nil) was charged in the year.
Of the sales revenue £27,200,000 arises from the LOD Group, with the remaining business revenue OO
increasing 133% in the year. The LOD group contributed £9,359,000 gross profit in the period of ownership,
with the gross profit increasing 112% for the rest of the group. Overall gross margin for the year was 51% with
the margin decreasing from the 65% margin recorded in 2022 being driven by the LOD Group which operates
at a lower gross margin.
Our focus in 2024 is towards a high quality and enhanced client experience, integrating the LOD Group into
our business and expanding revenue through cross-selling services through all our businesses.
Outlook .
Prospects for 2024 look promising with a number of new opportunities and projects in the pipeline. Whilst this
is likely to lead to a short term fall in revenues and profits, the directors believe that these new projects
contribute towards the long term growth and success of the Group.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors remain alert to the risks prevalent in a commercial environment and continue to take steps to
minimise or mitigate these risks. The primary business risk affecting the group is the downward pressure on
pricing from our competitors across the company’s products and services and ensuring we can stay ahead of
our rivals by being competitive and providing exceptional quality and service in our delivery.
Investment is key to ensure the service provided is ahead of its competitors and attracts new clients.

Section 172(1) Statement made under sections 414C and 414CZA of the Companies Act 2006
The company is the parent entity of many subsidiaries in which it provides capital and carries out the business
of the wider Consilio Group. This statement is made as part of the company’s annual strategic report, and sets
out the issues, factors and stakeholders which the directors of the company (the Board) consider strategically
important in complying with their obligation to act in the way they consider would be most likely to promote the
success of the company in accordance with section 172(1)(a) to (f).
The Board believes that the key to the long-term success of the Group is putting our people technology and our
clients at the core of what we do. This is because:

e People, technology and clients are at the heart of the Group's business.

e The success of the Group is built on strong relationships with those people and clients.

e Investments in technology that enhance the experience for its people and clients are what drive growth

and profitability. .

e Therefore, they and the Group's reputation with them are key considerations in all the Board decisions.
People and clients are at the heart of the Group’s business
The Board recognises that the legal professionals and legal engineers we employ and contract with —.
(‘professionals’) provide valued services to our clients. In turn the quality of those professionals drives growth in
the opportunities our clients bring to us. And the availability of those opportunities is vital to sustaining our
reputation with our professionals.
This virtuous circle is fundamental to the long-term success of the Group's business and is fostered by our
head office teams. They work with both our professionals and our clients to ensure that the services we provide
are of the highest quality.
Thus, the Group’s ability to grow and prosper in the long term depends upon a high quality and motivated
workforce, appropriate technology investments, across all parts of the business, as well as loyal clients who
bring the opportunities that keep our workforce engaged and motivated.
The Group’s success is built on strong relationships
The Board believes that strong relationships at the individual level are the key to maintaining a motivated
workforce and loyal client base. This ensures the business remains fully aware of the interests and concerns of _
the Group’s workforce and clients across the diverse services, regions and cultures we work in.
Accordingly, the Board ensures that the business maintains a culture which fosters a focus on individual
relationships, enhanced by the resources and information provided by skilled support teams in our head office,
and by policies, technology, practices and procedures which are designed for that purpose. This culture also
ensures a sound and purposeful relationship exists with our suppliers and other stakeholders (including the
community and the regulators of our business).

2.5 Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Group and the turnover can be reliably measured. Turnover is measured as the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes. The following criteria must also be met before turnover is recognised:
Sale of goods
Turnover from the sale of goods is recognised when all of the following conditions are satisfied:
. the Group has transferred the significant risks and rewards of ownership to the buyer;
. the Group retains neither continuing managerial involvement to the degree usually associated
with ownership nor effective control over the goods sold; :
° the amount of turnover can be measured reliably; :
. it is probable that the Group will receive the consideration due under the transaction; and
. the costs incurred or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be measured reliably.
Rendering of services
Turnover from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are
. provided in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all of the following conditions
are satisfied:
. the amount of turnover can be measured reliably;
. it is probable that the Group will receive the consideration due under the contract;
° the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured
reliably; and
. the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.
2.6 Operating leases

lease term.

straight-line basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is representative of the time
pattern of the lessee's benefit from the use of the leased asset.
