Strategic report
The directors present their Strategic Report and the affairs of the Company for the year ended 31 December 2023.
This report covers the overall performance of Redwood Technologies Group Limited (“Group”), with key activities
of Redwood Technologies Limited and Content Guru Limited, the two largest trading entities, highlighted.
PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES
Key Group activities continue to be the provision of cloud CX (Customer Experience) and omni-channel CCaaS
(Contact Centre as a Service) solutions, which it delivers via its storm® CPaaS (Communications Platform as a
Service) environment. A fast-growing component of CX and CCaa& are Al (artificial intelligence) services (outlined
in the Strategic Review), which Group delivers through its brain® software. In connection with the delivery and
ongoing operation of storm and brain services, Group also provides consultancy, development, programme
management, testing, training, operational and support services.
The Group’s key target customer base is large enterprises and public sector organisations who need mission-critical,
feature-rich, highly-scalable, highly-available, highly-flexible CX, CCaaS and AI solutions. Customers include
central and regional government bodies, financial services institutions, retailers, travel organisations, utilities
providers, CSPs (communications service providers) and SIs (System Integrators). Services are run in tens of
countries and supported from regional offices in the Americas, Asia Pac and Europe.
STRATEGIC REVIEW
This was a critical investment year for the business. During 2023 the CX marketplace faced significant disruption
from generative AI (artificial intelligence), which presents a major threat or opportunity. With staffing the most
significant expenditure in contact centres, the potential to automate contact centre agent roles at a much lower cost
presents a compelling business case. Although there are still technical and pricing challenges to be overcome, Group
. believes AI will replace a large percentage of agents performing simple tasks. For more complex CX centres, Group
sees the main role of AJ as assisting agents, to increase their productivity and quality of work. Whether fully
replacing through automation or assisting agents, AI presents a great revenue opportunity to Group as it offers both
types of service.
< Sales bookings were strong. Group won what it believes to be the largest CCaaS deal in the world for 2023, with a 5-
year TCV (total contract value) of well over $100m. This will involve deploying services in tens of countries, which
will also strongly benefit Group’s global presence.
To meet development and delivery resource demand for contracts won, plus the expected near-term additional
demand for new AI services, Group invested significantly again in hiring, with a focus on Al-application-
development roles. Average FTE (full-time equivalent) count grew by 15% and headcount has now increased by
more than 40% over the past 2 years. Group will continue hiring in 2024 but at a much lower level as it feels
resourcing is now well catered for.
Ata macro level, Group expects 2024 and 2025 to continue to be challenging for the global economy but is confident
about its growth prospects. The combination of operational cost efficiencies plus delivering the ability for customers
to increase end-customer loyalty and end-customer spend, across a wide range of sectors, are especially valued during
challenging times. Group expects double-digit revenue growth both in 2024 and 2025.
Reviewing 2023 financial performance, revenues increased by 10% from £56.7m to £62.2m. Annual licence revenues
increased 16% to £45.4m (2022: £39.1m). Although strong, growth would have been stronger still had customers
been able to accept deliveries at a faster pace. Customers’ technical teams are typically stretched, meaning service go-
live dates have often been deferred. Group has built better automation into its processes to assist customers, so
expects go-live times to shorten considerably during 2024 and 2025.
Gross profit was £51.7m, an increase of 9% from the previous year (2022: £47.3m). Gross profit as a percentage of
revenue was 83% (2022: 83%).
Adjusted EBITDA, as defined within the key performance indicators section of this report, stood at £7.7m (2022:
£7.6m), representing 12% (2022: 13%) of revenues. Profit after tax was £3.5m (2022: £4.0m), impacted
predominantly by Group’s investment in hiring for growth.

- COVID-19 risk has eased further and Group believes that this no longer remains a principal risk for the

- Discounting from CCaaS competitors continues to be a risk. Most of Group’s major competitors have
reduced their headcount as they pivot from high growth to profitability. In conjunction, a more-experienced
customer base that understands ‘you get what you pay for’.

- Foreign exchange rate fluctuations continue to be one of the principal risks for Group and one which Group
continues to monitor and ensure that it has sufficient controls to mitigate. Group generates euro, US dollar,
Japanese yen and Bnitish pound revenues, so is not exposed to a single currency.

-  Business-as-usual technology risks, such as security and data protection, continue to command the necessary
high levels of attention from all levels of Group. Major investment continues in Group’s cloud infrastructure
to ensure the business has best-in-class protection for itself and its customers.

- In common with many other businesses, Group has experienced higher cost inflation during 2022 and 2023

: than in prior years. Whilst energy costs for data centres have stabilised, salary-increase expectations remain
significant. Group anticipates that wage inflation will continue to be high through 2024 as unemployment
rates are low and hiring of talent is a key challenge for many organisations. Group continues to invest in
automation of its processes to ensure it can scale without a directly-proportionate increase in headcount. In
addition Group has near-shored some of its hiring to maximise cost advantage and resource availability
within other labour markets.

Overall Group’s directors continue to feel confident about the coming years but are cautious about the overall global
economic outlook. Group’s rolling three-year business plan (‘the Business Plan’) forecasts strong growth for 2024-26.
The Business Plan provides strategic planning, including product development, infrastructure investment, sales and
marketing activities and financial requirements to support those activities. The Business Plan evaluates risks and
uncertainties alongside new opportunities, and factors these into its planning.

Group’s credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade debtors, with this risk mitigated by upfront payments for

Group’s policy of maintaining cash surpluses and minimising debt.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
Over the next three years Group will aim to continue its strong profitable organic growth in recurring revenues and
will expand its services infrastructure and teams across all territories.
Key product enhancements for 2024 will involve four key pillars:

1. Digital shift Group’s customers will adopt more digital technology to engage with end customers, such as
increased use of video and more social media channels in addition to traditional voice services. Group will
continue to build its support for digital channels.

2. Customer Data Platform (CDP) — Customer data continues to increase in value and is used to create better
customer experiences. Group will continue to enhance its storm® CKS® (Customer Knowledge System)
CDP solution.

3. Digital customer — Group will continue to build more connections to IoT (Internet of Things) modules such
as health devices, smart meters and tracking devices, which are termed ‘digital customers’ by Group.

4. Artificial Intelligence and Intelligent automation — This includes technologies such as generative Al (e.g.
ChatGPT) and is used to bring efficiencies through automation (e.g. customer self-serve) to customers.

Over the next five years Group anticipates continued strong revenue growth as it maintains its position as a leader in
the CCaaS (Contact Centre as a Service) market and continues to build its longer-term fifteen-year plan for the next-
generation CX (Customer Experience) and AI (artificial intelligence) marketplaces.

The Directors present their annual report on the affairs of Redwood Technologies Group Limited (“Group”), together

These financial statements include the results of Redwood Technologies Group Limited and all of its subsidiaries.
Group’s head office is the UK and it has subsidiaries in North America, Asia Pac and Europe.
RESULTS AND DIVIDENDS
The Directors are satisfied with Group’s performance in 2023, achieving strong growth in recurring revenues and
maintaining good profitability whilst also investing in the business for future growth. Group registered another strong
year of sales and its product and sales pipeline development during 2023 has created a strong foundation for growth in
2024.
Profit after tax for the financial year amounted to £3.5m (2022: £4.0m). The directors have not proposed a dividend in

Future developments
Details of future developments can be found in the Strategic Report on page 10.
Research and development
Details of research and development can be found in the Strategic Report on page 2.
FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT OBJECTIVES AND POLICIES
Group’s activities expose it to a number of financial risks including in relation to competition, technological
development, price, credit, foreign currency and liquidity risk. Group’s primary goal is to continue a policy of
sustainable and controlled growth allied to an acceptable level of profitability. Group believes these objectives can
best be met by securing long-term relationships with customers who recognise the high value added nature of Group’s
products and services.
Competitive risk
Market demand for Group’s products continues to be strong, with services such as cloud contact centre driven by the
current demand for distributed working. Group tracks competitive threats through its own research and via a number
of analyst companies. Group will continue to ensure its products and services are best-in-class and constantly monitor
its pricing position.
Technological risk
Group carries out a significant amount of research and development in the technological development and some of
this research effort may take several years to deliver a return on investment or may fail to deliver a return. The level
of risk is always kept at a financially acceptable level and is further mitigated by ensuring that research projects are
chosen according to mid to long-term business objectives. Some experimental products have no expected return on
investment and most commercial products are typically launched on a ‘fast follower’ basis to further ensure their
commercial success.
Foreign currency risk
Group’s activities expose it primarily to the financial risks of changes in foreign currency exchange rates and interest
rates. It continues to balance its cost and revenues in non-sterling currencies to minimise the effect of currency
variations. Group does not use foreign exchange forward contracts to hedge the profit element derived from non-
sterling currencies.
