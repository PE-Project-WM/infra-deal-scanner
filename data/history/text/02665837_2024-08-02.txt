Business review :
Maintel Europe Limited (‘the Company’) is a cloud and managed services company focused on communication.
It also supplies and installs voice and data equipment to managed services customers, both to our direct clients
and into our partner customers. .
2023 was a year of business transformation resulting from the successful implementation of a strategic review
in the first half of the year and new focus on Unified Communications and Collaboration, Customer Experience,
and Security and Connectivity, assisted by a more normalised trading environment, following the easing of the
global supply chain of micro-chips, and the associated unwind of the order book, which resulted in a turnaround
of the Company’s performance. ,
The results for the year and financial position of the Company are shown in the annexed financial statements.

Network Services Division
The Network Services division is made up of three strategic revenue lines: .

e Cloud — subscription and managed service revenues from cloud contracts.

¢ Data — subscription, circuit, co-location and managed service revenues from Wide Area Network (WAN),

SD-WAN, internet access and managed security service contracts.
¢ Call traffic and line rental — recurring revenues from both legacy voice and modern SIP Trunking
contracts.
Network services revenue grew by 13.0% and gross profit increased by 18.8% to £17.4m, resulting in a 1.0%
improvement in gross profit margin to 38% (2022: 37%). The growth in the higher margin cloud revenue products
has offset the decline in lower margin call traffic revenues. Our fixed line telephony revenues increased by 3.2% to
£10.6m (2022: £10.3m). Within this, our overall line rental revenues reduced by 2.1%, reflecting the overall market
decline for legacy Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) products as customers migrate to consolidated
modern SIP Trunking or Cloud Communication services. However, our revenue from Call Traffic increased by
16.7%, driven by an increase in inbound contact centre calling traffic and outbound SIP call traffic, predominantly
from our strong Financial Services customer base.
Data connectivity revenues saw a significant increase of 11.4% during the year. The acceleration of revenue since
2022 is now reflecting the increasing impact that our new Software Defined Wide Area Networking (SD-WAN) and
managed Cloud Security Services are having on the performance of this division. Much of the business closed in
these new areas had been delayed from delivery by the semiconductor supply shortage however delivery
conditions have now normalised, and the trend is set to continue as we continue to win new contracts.
Mobile .
Maintel’s mobile division generates revenue primarily from commissions received as part of its dealer agreements
with O2 which scales in line with growth in partner revenues, in addition to value added services sold alongside
mobile such as mobile fleet management and mobile device management.
Revenues decreased by 13.2% to £3.8m (2022: £4.4m) and gross profits also declined by 13.8%, reflecting the
refocus of the Maintel’s business development towards our core revenue streams. Although customer churn
remained low in the period, the lack of new business compounded by downward price pressure on contract
renewals drove the negative revenue movement. Recognising these market challenges, Maintel has been
proactively resourcing the mobile sales team to focus on customer retention as opposed to new business.

Going concern ,
The Company has a sound financial record including strong operating cash flows derived from a substantial level
of recurring revenue across a range of sectors. At 31 December 2023, the Company’s parent, Maintel Holdings
Plc, continues to benefit from a financing facility with HSBC Bank plc (“HSBC”) consisting of a revolving credit .
facility (“RCF”) of £20m with a £6m term loan on a reducing basis. These borrowings are held by the Company’s
parent and are used to support the financing of the Group. Repayments of the term loan started in October 2022,
and at 31 December 2023 £3.0m remained outstanding. The key covenants include net leverage ratio and interest
cover tests, assessed on a quarterly basis. While the main terms of the financing facility remain unchanged, in early
2023 the Company’s parent and HSBC agreed to accommodate further leeway in the covenants to allow for the
temporary deterioration in profits, whilst the Group and the Company completed its transformation programme. The
Group successfully met the temporary milestones and HSBC being satisfied that the recovery phase had been
successfully completed, reinstated the initial covenants of the loan in early 2024. In March 2024 the facility was .
extended to 30 September 2025, from the initial term ending on 24 March 2025.
As highlighted in the risk management section (see pages 4-5) the Board has put robust business continuity plans
in place to ensure continuity of trading and operations. : :
The Company’s forecasts and projection models have been built on a prudent basis, taking into account uncertainty
around the impact of the supply chain issues with regard to both project delivery and timing of pipeline conversion,
allows for actual performance to exceed management forecasts in terms of revenue expectations. The Board has
reviewed the model in detail, taking account of reasonably possible changes in trading performance, including
sensitivities in pipeline conversion and renewal risk, together with further mitigating actions it could take such as
overhead savings. As a result, the Board believes that the Company has sufficient headroom in its agreed funding
arrangements to withstand a greater negative impact on its cash flow than it currently expects. :
On this basis, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Company has adequate resources to continue
in operational existence for the foreseeable future.
