STRATEGIC REPORT

- 1 -
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 September 2025.
Review of the business
Having delivered moderate growth in both turnover and strong growth in profit in 2024, the main objective for 2025 was to accelerate turnover growth whilst maintaining higher margins through purchasing efficiencies and scale. This objective has been achieved with an increase in turnover to £38.63m (2024 £23.01m) and net profit to £3.17m (2024 £2.16m).
The company
("Radiocoms") 
has maintained a high win rate from the long-term framework agreements which have delivered a material number of large projects which Radiocoms forecasts will continue for the foreseeable future especially in 2026. In addition, Radiocoms has seen modest growth from its expansion technology offering, most notably the sales of body worn video. 
Radiocoms’ operating procedures remain robust even with a sharp increase in overheads to support the additional business. It is anticipated that the overheads will continue to grow in 2026 as Radiocoms invests in additional sales and engineering resources to generate and support increased revenues and makes further investment in its expansion technology offering.  This expansion is being carefully managed, and it is anticipated that a material return on investment will be seen in the 2026-27 financial year. 
Principal risks and uncertainties
The global component shortage continues to ease, however there remain challenges with some core products taking 9-12 weeks to deliver, and there is a risk that deliveries may be affected further by global geopolitical challenges. Radiocoms continues to manage product delivery delays and long lead times by placing large stock orders well in advance, which enables the company to supply customer demands in a shorter time than most competitors. It is anticipated that this stock policy will continue for the foreseeable future.
The UK economy is forecast to achieve negligible growth in the next 12-18 months and although there are clear indications of government spending restrictions to meet published fiscal restraints, spending in defence is forecast to grow. Government restrictions may result in departments extending the life of their equipment in use and delaying implementing projects involving capital expenditure when not considered critical.
The increased taxes on business through the increase in employers’ National Insurance contributions in 2024 took full effect in the 24/25 financial year and added materially to the cost of employment. There is a short to medium-term risk of further tax increases.
The directors have identified the existence of the above risks and are confident that they have strategies in place to manage and mitigate their effects.

The healthy cash position has enabled the business to complete a small acquisition in September 2025 (PTT Systems) and leaves the company well placed to complete further acquisitions if the opportunity arises.

Deferred tax
Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all timing differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits. Such assets and liabilities are not recognised if the timing difference arises from goodwill or from the initial recognition of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting end date and reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered.
