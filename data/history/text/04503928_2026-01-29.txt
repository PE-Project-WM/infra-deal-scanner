The directors present their strategic report of New Technology Group Holdings plc (the “company”) and its subsidiary undertakings (together, the “group”) for the year ended 31 July 2025. 
Fair review of the business
The financial results for the year are set out on page 17. The year ended 31 July 2025 marked a pivotal and transformational year for New Technology Group Holdings plc (the “Group”). Following the significant corporate reorganisation implemented in April 2024, the Group now has a simplified structure, a clearer strategic focus, and a substantially improved operational footing.
Group revenue for the year was £27.6m (2024: £31.6m). The reduction reflects the intentional exit from discontinued operations and the ongoing decline in legacy telecom services, both of which were anticipated and planned for as part of the Group’s strategic reset.
Despite this revenue reduction, the Group delivered a significant improvement in operating performance:
Operating loss reduced to £0.5m (2024: £3.1m loss)
Loss before tax reduced to £0.46m (2024: £2.9m loss)
Statutory loss after tax reduced by over £2.7m year on year
This improvement was driven by:

the elimination of loss-making and capital-intensive discontinued operations,
tighter cost control following restructuring,
a lower amortisation and impairment charge compared with the prior year, and
resilient gross margins in the Group’s core recurring revenue businesses.
Notably, continuing operations generated an operating profit of £87k, demonstrating that the Group’s core activities are now profitable on an underlying basis.
During the year, the Board executed its transformation programme for Netcentrix, successfully decommissioning its private cloud platform and exiting its data centre operations, while successfully migrating customers to more robust and efficient Azure public cloud and modern workplace solutions. In parallel, the Group implemented a stop-sell policy on new complex on-premise IT infrastructure solutions. These activities were capital intensive, operationally complex, and increasingly misaligned with the Group’s strategic direction towards scalable, cloud-based managed services. 
This decision removed operational risk, reduced capital expenditure requirements, simplified the Group’s technology stack, and allowed management to focus resources on higher-margin recurring revenue opportunities. Discontinued operations contributed an operating loss of £0.6m in 2025. Importantly, this represents the final year of impact, with these activities now fully exited.
In addition, the Group incurred £0.7m of restructuring costs, which were one-off in nature and directly related to the Board’s execution of transformative change programmes and the implementation of its new operating model. These costs are expected to deliver ongoing annualised cost savings and improved profitability going forward.
The Group remains financially robust, reporting Net Assets of £10.9m (2024: £11.4m), Net Current Assets of £8.8m (2024: £7.4m) and year-end cash of £2.0m (2024: £4.6m). The reduction in cash during the year primarily reflects the repayment of £2.0m of bank loans via the parent company. The Group continued to operate comfortably within its banking facilities and remained fully compliant with all banking covenants throughout the year. The Group retains sufficient financial headroom to support strategic M&A opportunities.

Strategy
Following the successful re-organisation last year, New Technology Group (“the Group”) now operates as a holding company with two clearly differentiated trading businesses, each with a distinct strategic mandate, operating model and technology focus. This structure is designed to improve clarity, accountability and execution, while enabling each business to respond more effectively to its respective market opportunity. The re-organisation separated the Group’s Communications and IT Services activities, allowing both Elite Group and Netcentrix to develop specialist capabilities, aligned investment priorities and clear customer propositions, supported by shared Group functions where appropriate.
Elite Group provides Cloud Communications Services including Connectivity, Unified Communications (and UCaaS), Contact Centre (and CCaaS leveraging conversational AI), Voice Services, Mobile and IoT, whilst Netcentrix focuses on IT Managed Services incorporating Cloud Applications, Cloud Infrastructure, Technology Hardware, IT Support and Cyber Security.
While operating independently, Elite Group and Netcentrix are aligned through shared Group governance, ESG commitments, brand values and a customer centric culture. The Group selectively pursues joint propositions where customers require both secure modern workplace services and resilient cloud communications solutions, supported by coordinated account planning and customer success frameworks. The re-organisation has enabled clearer KPIs, improved decision-making and stronger governance, with each business operating against defined transformation roadmaps and performance measures, overseen at Group level.
Across the Group, the strategy is underpinned by a consistent long-term vision: to become the leading sustainable technology group delivering world class customer experience (CX) and digital transformation solutions to businesses. Central to the execution of this vision is the Group’s Single Focus; an unrivalled commitment to helping customers. This principle acts as the lens through which strategic decisions are made, guiding investment priorities, go-to-market choices, platform selection, service design and people development. In practice, the Single Focus drives simplification, standardisation and a shift from reactive service delivery to proactive, outcome-based solutions that improve customer experience, resilience and trust.

Increased Competition
Competition is healthy for the Group. The Group is investing in new and existing vendor relationships to modernise and strengthen the technology stack facing customers and the market, focusing on bringing greater value and innovation by leveraging new technologies and improved Customer Experience (CX) as the future driver of value to gain competitive advantage.
Supply Chain
The Group partners with leading global technology vendors and Tier 1 Network Providers. This enables the Group to be vendor agnostic and take an independent approach when consulting with customers and providing Cloud Communications and IT Managed Services. This approach minimises the Group’s dependence on a single supplier. Accordingly, supplier price changes can be mitigated through supplier substitution as appropriate. Due diligence and identification of risk is also fundamental to the Group’s approach to supply chain management. The supply chain is proactively managed by the Group ensuring that all contractual agreements are fair, reasonable and commercially viable, mitigating risk in areas of responsibility, information security, service levels and payment terms. The Group also proactively reviews and tests current supplier contracts against ongoing changes in local and national legislation, regulatory changes, anti-​slavery and environmental impacts.
2027 PSTN Switch Off
The PSTN switch off in 2027 (formerly 2025, prior to BT announcing the delay in May 2024) is the single biggest compelling event in the communications sector since the privatisation of BT in 1984 and the deregulation of the UK telecommunications market in the 1990’s. The PSTN switch off is the national de-​commission of the public switch telephone network which will now close in December 2027 – by which time every phone line in the UK will have moved to a fully digital network across a fibre-​based service. The revenue erosion risks associated with the PSTN switch off are being managed and offset by the significant opportunity for Elitetele.com plc to capitalise on the rising demand from businesses looking to move to Cloud Communications technology like Unified Communications as a Service (UCaaS) and Contact Centre as a Service (CCaaS). The Group is well positioned to capitalise on this opportunity and Elite is making good progress in its transition to Cloud Communications.
Ofcom Regulation
The Group operates in markets regulated by Ofcom, the Office of Communications. The Group mitigates regulatory risk by monitoring and assessing the likelihood and potential impact of regulatory change. Customer contractual terms are written in line with Ofcom regulation and reviewed each time Ofcom regulations change. Ofcom regularly sends consultation papers to the network operators and the ICT channel ahead of any potential changes (usually 12-​18 months in advance), giving the Group time to consult, understand and plan accordingly. Additionally, the Group is a member of the FCS (Federation of Communication Suppliers), an organisation that provides assistance to the Group, ensuring that critical customer policies are Ofcom compliant (as an extra validation step).
Data Protection and GDPR
Like many businesses, the Group pays particular attention to Data Protection and GDPR regulation, with its policies drafted by an expert global consultant and adherence and compliance regularly audited as part of the Group’s ISO accreditations.

Employment Law
The Group now employs more than 120 people and is subject to changes in regulation associated with employment law. The Group benefits from having CIPD qualified employees within the Group alongside its partnerships with external HR expert organisations and leading employment law solicitors, all of which understand the employment regulatory changes under proposal and their potential impact. 
The Employment Rights Act 2025 introduces some of the most significant reforms to UK employment law in over a decade, with changes scheduled to come into force during 2026 and 2027. Certain elements of this legislation are expected to impact the Group. Management is aware of these forthcoming changes and has developed appropriate plans to address the resulting requirements, including updates to policies, training programmes, operational processes and employment contracts.
It is also recognised that, while the overarching reforms have been agreed, certain areas remain subject to the publication of detailed secondary legislation. As a result, not all implementation decisions can be finalised at this stage.
The most significant changes affecting the Group are those taking effect in October 2026, including the introduction of a new duty to inform employees of their right to join a trade union and the requirement to maintain robust policies demonstrating that all reasonable steps are being taken to prevent sexual harassment in the workplace. Further key changes are due from January 2027, notably the reduction of the qualifying period for unfair dismissal claims to six months and the strengthening of employees’ rights to request flexible working arrangements.
The Group has also considered the amendments to Statutory Sick Pay (SSP) effective from 1
st
 April 2026, under which SSP will become payable from the first day of absence rather than the fourth. This change is not currently expected to have a significant impact, as the Group has consistently maintained a low level of sickness absence over a number of years. This position will continue to be monitored closely.
The HR team has documented a comprehensive implementation timeline and has a practical plan in place to address each legislative change relevant to the Group.

Single Focus
For the Senior Management Team, defining and implementing a clear and simple single focus that provides groupwide alignment and clarity on what is most important and critical has been a business priority. Consistent with the Group’s sense of purpose and passion for helping customers and driven by an ambition to provide the best Customer Experience (CX) in the sector, the Group’s single focus is quite simply, an unrivalled commitment to helping customers.
Digital Transformation
Digital Transformation is the Group’s primary activity and Go to Market (GTM), supporting customers with the ongoing modernisation of business technologies to increase operational effectiveness and gain competitive advantage. The Group is investing in new and existing vendor relationships to modernise and strengthen the technology stack facing customers and the market, focusing on bringing greater value and innovation. For Elite, this means leveraging chapter 2 technologies like AI powered omnichannel applications, generative AI, as well as conversational AI and analytics as the future driver of value. For Netcentrix, focussing on proactive Managed Services and investing in Cyber Security and Managed Security Services as a differentiator and driver of value is fundamental to its plans.
IT Managed Services
Historically, the Group delivered IT services through a service delivery partner model primarily focused on reactive, break-fix support across hardware, hosted desktop, cloud applications and infrastructure services. During the year, the Group completed a strategic transition within its Netcentrix business, moving to a proactive IT Managed Services model aligned with the Group’s strategic vision and cloud-first, recurring revenue strategy.
The Board successfully delivered the Netcentrix MSP Transformation Programme in the year, resulting in the simplification and standardisation of the managed services portfolio and a shift towards proactive service management. This programme repositioned Netcentrix to focus on cloud applications, cloud infrastructure and structured IT managed services, improving scalability and consistency of service delivery.
The managed services offering was restructured into a clearly defined framework delivered across three core support pillars: Server, End User and Network Support, with optional on-site and 24/7 support services available. Server support now covers both physical and virtual environments, including Microsoft Azure infrastructure, supported by proactive patch management. End user services include Microsoft 365 administration, endpoint management and helpdesk support, while managed network services incorporate monitoring and proactive maintenance to support secure and resilient connectivity.
Cyber Security
Cyber security was established as a core component of the Group’s managed services proposition during the year through the introduction of enhanced Managed Security Services. This reflects the increasing importance of cyber resilience, particularly within cloud-based environments such as Microsoft 365. As part of this, the Group implemented its Microsoft 365 compliance and monitoring enforcement service designed to identify and address security risks arising from configuration drift and non-conformance. 
In addition, the Group completed the development and delivery of a broader set of Managed Security Services, including Managed Endpoint Detection and Response supported by a 24/7 Security Operations Centre, Managed Identity Threat Detection and Response, Security Information and Event Management (SIEM), and Security Awareness Training. These services provide a layered approach to threat detection, incident response and user awareness, supporting customer security, compliance and operational resilience.
At the same time, the Group continued to develop its Microsoft capabilities and completed the transition of private cloud hosted desktop customers to Azure Virtual Desktop and Modern Workplace solutions. This reduced dependency on legacy infrastructure and aligned service delivery more closely with public cloud platforms, generating cost efficiencies that contributed to the improvement in underlying operating profit during the year.

Cloud Communications and Contact Centre
Founded in 2000, Elitetele.com plc established a strong position in the UK B2B telecommunications market, particularly within contact centre and voice services. This heritage underpinned the Group’s credibility and supported sustained organic growth over many years. During the year, Elite advanced its strategic repositioning, strengthening its focus on Cloud Communications and Contact Centre services, with an emphasis on UCaaS, CCaaS and the increasing role of artificial intelligence in customer experience delivery.
A key strategic milestone during the year was the successful onboarding of Zoom as a core Unified Communications and Contact Centre as a Service (UCaaS and CCaaS) technology partner. Zoom is a globally recognised enterprise communications provider, serving hundreds of thousands of business customers worldwide and supporting large-scale, mission-critical contact centre deployments across regulated and complex industries. Its cloud-native architecture, strong security credentials and rapidly evolving AI capabilities position Zoom as a leading vendor in the B2B CCaaS market, particularly in the delivery of AI-enabled customer experience solutions. 
The introduction of Zoom as a strategic partner has strengthened the Group’s Cloud Communications proposition and provides a scalable platform to support AI-enhanced customer engagement, workforce optimisation and analytics. Business development activity in respect of Zoom solutions developed positively over the period, with the final quarter seeing increased sales engagement and a number of new contract wins added to the sales orderbook, reflecting ongoing demand for modern, flexible and AI-enabled communications platforms.
However, overall trading during the year took place against a backdrop of challenging UK economic conditions. Persistent inflationary pressures, increases in employer National Insurance contributions and rises in the National Minimum Wage continued to place cost pressures on UK businesses, particularly SMEs, which form a core part of the Group’s customer base. As a result, some customers reduced headcount, delayed purchasing decisions or curtailed discretionary investment. These factors moderated the momentum achieved in the prior two years, resulting in Cloud Communications recurring revenue of £3.78m for the year (2024: £4.19m), representing a 10% reduction year-on-year, while remaining 11% higher than FY23 (2023: £3.41m).
Despite these headwinds, the Board remained focused on supporting customers, maintaining service quality and managing the business prudently. During the year, the Group also completed plans to increase investment in its professional services capability aligned to the Zoom platform, recognising the growing importance of design, implementation, integration and optimisation services in delivering differentiated customer outcomes and supporting long-term value creation.
Sustainability

At the same time, the Group recognises that customers are demonstrating an increased preference for brands that demonstrate sustainable business practices; Governments are introducing more regulations and mechanisms to factor in the environmental cost of business operations like the “carbon market” and carbon credits; and banks and investors are becoming increasingly interested in ESG metrics when deciding where to allocate funds.
The growing focus on sustainability has accelerated the adoption of Industry 4.0 digital transformation technologies, including artificial intelligence, as enablers of green technology. The application of these technologies is helping organisations to deliver measurable progress against key environmental goals.
