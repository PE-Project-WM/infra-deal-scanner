Financial risk management and principal risks and uncertainties
The Company continues to face a range of risks and uncertainties from both internal and external sources, these
include technological change, health and safety and employee hiring and retention. The Board considers the
likelihood and significance of risk factors when putting in place risk management procedures.
Macroeconomic and political uncertainty
Uncertainty in macroeconomic and political areas is continually assessed to enhance business awareness in
areas such as supply chain management, product quality and the business’ ability to withstand threats by
capitalising on the company’s international coverage. The majority of the company’s contracts allow direct cost
increases to be passed on to customers thereby limiting its exposure to inflation.

Financial risk management and principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Health & safety
The Company maintains health and safety policies to ensure compliance with the relevant regulations and to
reasonably protect its employees from the risk of injury.
Competition and technological change
The group continues to invest in its core network infrastructure, corporate systems and technical training.
Product portfolio development is a major focus of the business to adapt to a changing market while additional
efforts have been put into differentiation of the customer experience through the installation of a dedicated
customer care team. Business continuity is continually exercised and enhanced with a major focus on technical
and procedural robustness to protect group concerns.
Data Protection and Cyber attack
Ouring the year the company continued to expand the capabilities of its dedicated Cyber and Information
Security team, investing significant capital into measures to ensure our business is at the forefront in its ability
to identify, protect, detect, respond, and recover from cyber events, while also being able to transfer learnings
into product enhancements for its customers. The company is externally audited each year and re-certified to
internationally recognised security standards. :
Employee hiring and retention
The company manages its exposure to staff turnover and resulting impact on areas such as service quality by
attracting and retaining high calibre employees through its competitive basic salaries, cultural engagement
initiatives and high quality training programmes.
Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk that financial loss arises from the failure of a customer or counterparty to meet its
obligations under a contract. The company’s principal financial assets are cash and trade receivables. The credit
risk associated with the cash is limited as the counterparties have high credit ratings. The principal risk arises,
therefore, from its trade receivables.
There are effective credit control procedures to guard against the risk of delinquency. The group has a diverse
customer base with minimal concentration by customer or industry sector. As a result the Directors believe that
the overall risk is low. Trade receivables are continually monitored and categorised by risk. A provision for the
impairment of trade receivables is recognised by applying a percentage, equal to the estimated risk of default,
to each risk category. Trade receivables are written-off when it is considered certain or extremely likely that no
amounts will be recovered. The maximum exposure to credit risk at the reporting date is the carrying value of
each of trade receivables and cash and cash equivalents. The group does not hold any collateral as security.
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the Company will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they fall due.
Liquidity is managed at a group level. The group generates sufficient funds from operations to meet day to day
cash requirements. The Company receives a letter of support from its ultimate controlling company, Moco
Topco Limited.

Duty to promote the success of the Company
Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 requires Directors to act in a way that they consider, in good faith, would
be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, and in doing
so have regard to:

a) The likely consequences of any decision in the long term;

b) The interests of the company’s employees;

c) The need to foster the company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;

d) The impact of the company’s operations on the community and environment;

e) The desirability of the company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct; and

f) The need to act fairly as between members of the company
The company has multiple stakeholders. The Board considers the key stakeholders to be employees, customers,
suppliers, investors, and local communities. It ensures that the requirements of section 172(1) of the Companies
Act 2006 are met and the interests of the company’s stakeholder groups are considered through a combination
of the following:
Employees
During the year, the group has experienced reduced employee attrition and has continued the process to
increase employee engagement instigated by the current leadership team. The group HR team adopts a
continual revisit of action plans to address the results of employee engagement surveys, the implementation of
new core values, improved training, and improved objective setting processes.
Talent is at the centre of M247s business strategy. Ensuring that the group has the right people in the right roles
with the right knowledge and experience to drive the business forward is key to its long term success. M247
does this in a variety of ways, recognising the many different learning styles across its teams.
Customers
The group works collaboratively with customers to develop innovations to meet consumer needs. The Board
engages with customers through the CEO and the wider Executive leadership team and receives regular
information about customers in the board and other business reports. A series of KPIs allows management to
monitor outages, provision of new services, and issue resolution on a weekly basis.
Suppliers
Supply chain integrity is a critical part of the business as the group relies on its suppliers to help meet customer
needs. Engagement with suppliers is via the group’s procurement teams as well as through other functions
including Finance, Legal and Compliance. The Board receives information through Board and other reports.
Investors
The group has a clear responsibility to engage with its shareholders and lenders. Shareholders and lenders
receive monthly updates on the performance of the organisation and are consulted in regard to all significant
investments and disposals. .
Local Communities
The group has a number of partnerships in place, for example with Manchester Youth Zone who support under-
privileged children in the Greater Manchester area, through which it can support the careers of individuals from
a wide range of backgrounds, and it has a team of people committed to giving their expertise and time through
these partnerships.
Environment
The company is committed to preventing pollution and to complying with all relevant environmental legislation,
regulations and other environmental requirements. Management regularly evaluate the environmental impact

Going concern
The Company is reliant on financial support from its ultimate parent company, Moco Topco Limited, as it has
net liabilities. Cash resources of the company and other group undertakings are managed on a group basis. The
Directors have received written confirmation from Moco Topco Limited that financial support will be provided
to the Company as a subsidiary for a period of at least 12 months from the date these financial statements are
approved.
The Directors of Moco Topco Limited have prepared a detailed forecast plan going out to 31 March 2027, the
first period of which forms the budget for the year-ended 31 March 2025. This includes a consolidated forecast
income statement, cash flow and balance sheet. Actual performance is reviewed regularly against the budget
for the financial year. In addition, covenants compliance and headroom are monitored every month. Since the
year end the business worked with its lenders and shareholders to agree revision to the underlying agreements, :
inclusive of the extension of an additional lending facility. On this basis and using these forecasts, the Directors
assessed whether the group is a going concern and is able to continue trading for the period to 31 March 2026.
Based on this assessment, The Directors have not identified any material uncertainties that cast significant doubt
on the group’s ability to continue to operate as a going concern.
Therefore, the Directors consider it appropriate to continue to prepare the financial statements of the company
on a going concern basis.

2.4 Revenue
The Company recognises revenue from the provision of ongoing services including Internet connection, server
rentals, and phone services, as well as the installation of associated products.
The Company satisfies its performance obligation of providing ongoing services and enabling associated
products, as services are rendered over the expected contract period. For more details on significant judgements
made in determining the expected contract period, please refer to Note 3.
The transaction price is based on the amount to which the entity expects to be entitled for satisfying the
performance obligations. The transaction price includes both fixed amounts and variable amounts. The
transaction price for most of the group’s contracts with customers is a fixed price. The group charges variable
considerations for some service elements whereby the price is dependent on the actual service usage during a
period of time. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price to the extent that an amount for which
: a significant reversal is not probable when the uncertainty associated with the variable consideration is resolved.
As the amount of the group’s variable consideration is highly susceptible to factors outside its control, generally
the variable consideration is recognised in the month in which the specific service is used by the customer.
Revenue from the provision of ongoing services and associated products is recognised over the expected
contract period. Progress is measured using the output method on the basis of time elapsed on a contract, which
faithfully depicts the time pattern of satisfying various services over expected contractual periods.
The Company incurs sales commission costs that are only payable upon obtaining specific contracts, which are
recognised as an asset as such costs are incremental to obtaining the specific contracts. In addition, the Company
incurs initial connection costs when setting up the contracts, which are recognised as an asset as such costs are
incurred in fulfilling the various revenue contracts. Both assets (i.e. capitalised sales commission costs and
capitalised connection costs) are amortised over the expected contract period.

2.13 Leased assets
Identifying Leases
The Company accounts for a contract, or a portion of a contract, as a lease when it conveys the right to use an
asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration. Leases are those contracts that satisfy the following
criteria:
(a) There is an identified asset;
(b) The Company obtains substantially all the economic benefits from use of the asset; and
(c) The Company has the right to direct use of the asset.
The Company considers whether the supplier has substantive substitution rights. If the supplier does have those
rights, the contract is not identified as giving rise to a lease.
In determining whether the Company obtains substantially all the economic benefits from use of the asset, the
Company considers only the economic benefits that arise use of the asset, not those incidental to legal
ownership or other potential benefits. .

Expected contract period applied in revenue recognition
In order to determine the average contract life, which includes the likelinood of renewals, past activity has been
considered using recent and longer-term historical contract data. Contract re-signs were used to determine the
average extended term for the likelihood of renewals, upgrade re-signs were deemed as a separate performance
