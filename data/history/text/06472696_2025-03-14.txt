Review of the business
4Com Network Services Limited ("the Company") is a wholly owned subsidiary of 4Com Technologies Limited
("the Group") and is responsible for managing customers line and call traffic through 4Com's own and third party
networks. Network contracts are established when new systems are sold to customers by other group
companies.
The Company's objective is to continue offering competitively priced, reliable network services whilst maintaining
high levels of customer satisfaction. Following group restructuring the Company now also has the objective of
delivering a professional service and the highest possible standard of customer care.
Financial review
Turnover for the year ended 30 June 2024 was £78,822,000 (2023: £68,340,000). A gross margin of 37% (2023:
37%) shows a consistent level from the previous year.
The profit on ordinary activities after taxation for the year was £7,830,000 (2023: profit of £9,003,000). Net assets
as at the year end were £10,072,000 (2023: net assets of £2,259,000).
Other matters ‘
The Company recognises its responsibilities with regards to environmental matters, including the impact of the
Company's business activities on the environment and where appropriate takes action to minimise any adverse
impact.
Employees are viewed as a key asset of the business and as such the Company aims to offer industry leading
benefits to employees.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risks and uncertainties and the management of these are discussed in the Directors’ Report.
Key performance indicators
The key performance indicator for the Company is deemed to be the number of active network customers. For
the year ended 30 June 2024 there were 11,578 active customers (2023: 11,060).

Going concern
At the year end, the Company's net assets exceeded its liabilities by £2,259,000 (2023: net assets exceeded
liabilities by £12,173,000) and had a net current liability position of £2,308,000 (2023: net asset position of
£9,143,000). Further, the Company has significant intercompany balances with Group entities. The Company's
ability to settle its liabilities as they fall due is dependent on the financial support of 4com Technologies Limited.
The directors have received confirmation via a letter of support from 4Com Technologies Limited which confirms
this financial support for a year to 31 March 2026.
In continuing to adopt a going concern basis for preparing the financial statements for the year ended 30 June
2024, the directors have reviewed trading and cash forecasts prepared for the Group up to 31 March 2026, being
the going concern assessment period. This included considering the availability of liquidity headroom on the
revolving credit facility, and a number of uncertainties which are set out in the Group's principal risks, as well as
the Group's exposure to credit risk, interest rate risk, liquidity risk, currency risk and foreign exchange risk as set
out in the strategic report from page 1.
The directors have also considered impacts on future trading and liquidity in the context of the current operating
and macro-economic environment, which includes movement on interest rates, rising energy prices and inflation,
all of which have been reviewed in our going concern assessment period to the 31 March 2026. We have
considered these impacts and conclude that we have taken sufficient steps to mitigate our exposure to the current
operating and macro-economic environment. The mitigation steps taken include forward purchases of inventory so
that sufficient inventory levels are held to protect against any future supply chain impacts.
We have also considered the current geopolitical environment, most notably the conflicts arising in Ukraine & the
Middle East, which has created potential supply problems, product shortages and general price rises particularly
in relation to fuel, gas and electricity. We conclude the conflicts have had a minimal effect on the group's forecast
. and liquidity position, due to the geographic location of the Group's supply chain, being predominately Asia.
The Group has performed a full going concern assessment for the year ended 30 June 2024. During the year the
business has continued its significant growth, both organic and through acquisitions and has continued to grow
revenues above previous year levels. Underlying market trends are driving demand in the 4Com focused segment
due to the accelerated shift to more flexible working solutions, as well as longer term drivers such as the
upcoming switch off of PSTN in 2027. The Group’s business mode! is to continue to offer unified communications
to SMEs, creating long-lasting revenue streams from a growing customer base, as well as securing an upgrade
pathway to customers to move to new technologies when launched. This model is supported by a panel of asset-
based finance providers. in addition, the Group has demonstrated significant revenue synergies through its
acquisition model, completing a further 11 acquisitions during FY24, growing recurring revenue and delivering on
associated cost synergies.
The share capital of 4com Technologies Limited was acquired post year end by Daisy Midco Limited on the 23
September 2024. This has led to a change in ultimate parent and controlling party. Subsequently the Term Loan
and RCF facility at a 4com Group Limited level were repaid, and new facilities were drawn, which included a £70m
Term Loan Facility, and a £12m RCF facility.
On 23 September 2024 Daisy Midco Limited acquired 4Com Group (4com Technologies Limited and its
subsidiaries). Following completion of this transaction, the debt within the 4Com Group will be ring-fenced and
subject to a separate Adjusted leverage covenant test on its stand-alone metrics on substantively the same basis
as the existing Daisy Group (which will also retain its covenant test on stand-alone metrics on the same basis as
described above). However, as an event of default in either banking group would trigger a mandatory prepayment
event in the other banking group, the Group directors have also considered the projected cash flows of the Daisy
Group in making their assessment of the going concern of the Group and the Company. In light of the directors
considering the Daisy Group cashflows and covenant compliance, the risk of a default event is deemed remote
within the going concern assessment period. .

Going concern (continued)
The major term lender has continued to support the Group during the year, providing a new £70m Term Loan
Facility post year end during September 24, which is repayable in June 2028. The Group is subject to covenant
compliance in relation to the senior debt facilities. The Group has met all covenants during the year ended 30
June 2024 and is expected to continue to meet the covenants throughout the going concern period. In addition the
Group has access to an undrawn £0.8m of trade loan facility and a £12m RCF facility, of which £6m is drawn. The
RCF facility was renewed post year end during September 24, which is repayable March 2028. Our base case
scenario used in the going concern assessment does not rely on the use of the RCF facility.
The Group closely monitors and manages its funding position, preparing regular forecasts with sensitivity
analysis, to ensure it has sufficient headroom to fund operations and continue to invest in discretionary R&D
projects. To assess the Groups resilience to more adverse outcomes, its forecast was sensitised to reflect
scenarios based on the Group's principal risks and the downside prospects of a potential UK recession. The
Group assessed the going concern by comparing a base case scenario (being the Group's forecast for the
relevant going concern period) to a downside scenario, and in the case of the downside scenario taking into
consideration relevant mitigation steps. Our downside scenario was performed reflecting a change in .
assumptions in relation to significant changes in customer demand, leading to a reduction in selling price by

. 10%, which may lead to lower than expected cashflows. However the downside scenario would expect to be
mitigated by headcount rationalisation. .

: The mitigation that would be applied in the downside scenario relate to pay cost and headcount which are within
the control of the Group to implement quickly in response to any downward trends should they be necessary. .
While these additional mitigating actions could be implemented more quickly, they have only been forecast within
the going concern assessment period to 31 March 2026 for the purposes of the going concern assessment.
Under the downside scenario assessed, the Group would remain cash positive and compliant with debt
covenants throughout the whole of the going concern assessment period. The mitigation actions have not been
applied in the downside scenario as they are deemed not required.

The directors have considered a reverse stress test scenario, being assumed cash flow forecasts during the
going concern assessment period that result in a breach of banking covenants. A 29% decline in Group EBITDA
was applied, which took into account changes in customer demands, before banking covenants were breached
(assuming no mitigating actions had been taken to offset this). We consider these scenarios to be highly unlikely,
in addition we have the mitigations applied being the same as referred to in the downside scenario. These
mitigations are fully within the control of the Group.
The directors, in concluding on the going concern assessment, have not relied on a parent letter of support from
Daisy to meet their liabilities as they fall due over the going concern assessment period, as the 4com Group can
generate sufficient liquidity as a standalone group of companies.
A material uncertainty statement formed part of the prior year financial statements due to the ongoing transaction
with Daisy Midco Limited had not been completed by the time the financial statements were issued. The directors
are comfortable that no material uncertainty exists in preparing the current year going concern assessment, as
the transaction has been fully executed post year end. .
After reviewing the Group's net asset position and Company's net asset position, their cash balances, borrowing
facilities, projected cash flows and incorporating a severe but plausible downside sensitivity analysis, which
assumes no growth and no mitigating actions taken, together with reviewing the same in relation to the Daisy
Group with a severe but plausible downside sensitivity analysis that assumes no revenue synergies arising on
the combination and no mitigating actions taken, the directors believe that the Group and the Company have
adequate resources to continue operations for the foreseeable future through to 31 March 2026. For this reason,
they continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Company is part of the 4Com Group whose financial risks are managed on a group basis. The Group's
operations expose it to a variety of risks that could impact the achievement of the long-term strategy. The
Group's overall risk management programme focuses on the impact from economic factors as well as

. commercial factors and seeks to minimise potential adverse effects on the Group's financial performance, its
business resilience and continuity of services to customers.
General economic risk
The Group continually assess the impact of macro-economic uncertainty, potentially leading to a downturn in the
UK economy and stress tests its forecasts, including impact from UK business confidence. The Group mitigates
this risk by continuously updating credit scores prior to approaching customers to ensure high acceptance levels
are maintained. The customer base is mainly SMEs and therefore there is no significant concentration of credit
tisk, with any exposure spread across a large and diverse customer base with no concentration to any industry
sector. The Group has experience of trading through economic downturns delivering robust revenue trends, as
UC and telecoms are a critical part of business, irrespective of economic conditions.
Credit risk
The Group's principal financial assets are bank balances and trade and other receivables. The Group's credit
risk is primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of
allowances for doubtful receivables. An allowance for impairment is made where there is an identified loss event
which, based on previous experience, is evidence of a reduction in the recoverability of the cash flows. The
Group has in place policies to ensure that sales of products are made to customers with an appropriate credit
history. Where this is not possible, appropriate credit limits are set in place after taking into account the
customers’ financial strength. The customer base is mainly SMEs and therefore there is no significant
concentration of credit risk, with any exposure spread across a large and diverse customer base. For system
installation revenue third-party finance companies are used to purchase the equipment and provide operating
leases to customers. Funds are received on the day of the installation with the third-party funder bearing the
credit risk with the customer. The credit risk on liquid funds is limited because the counterparties are banks with
strong credit-ratings assigned by international credit-rating agencies.
Liquidity risk
In order to maintain liquidity to ensure that sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations and future
developments, the Group uses a Revolving Credit Facility (RCF) and a Term Loan Growth Fund. Compliance
with the covenants is monitored on a monthly basis as part of the treasury process.
Price risk
The Group is exposed to price risk due to normal inflationary increases in the purchase price of the goods and
services purchased in the UK. The Group has no exposure to equity securities price risk as it holds no listed or
other equity investments.
interest rate cash flow risk
The Group has both interest-bearing assets and interest-bearing liabilities. Interest bearing assets include only
cash balances, which earn interest at variable rates. The Group's working capital facility bears interest at a
variable rate. The Group continues to stress test effects of interest rate increase and serviceability of debt. In
relation to its other facilities, interest rates remain unhedged.
In addition to financial risks, the Group is exposed to a number of commercial risks which we seek to identify and
manage as they arise, as well as building contingency options into our plans and processes.

Explanation as to what extent the audit was considered capable of detecting irregularities, including

Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures
in line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect irregularities, including fraud. The risk of not detecting a
material misstatement due to fraud is higher than the risk of not detecting one resulting from error, as fraud may
involve deliberate concealment by, for example, forgery or intentional misrepresentations, or through collusion.
The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud is detailed below.
However, the primary responsibility for the prevention and detection of fraud rests with both those charged with
governance of the entity and management.
Our approach was as follows:
: e We obtained an understanding of the legal and regulatory frameworks that are applicable to the
company and determined that the most significant are those that relate to the reporting framework
: (FRS 102’ and the Companies Act 2006) and the relevant tax compliance regulations in the United
Kingdom. In addition, the company has to comply with laws and regulations, including employee
legislation, health and safety, data protection, anti-bribery and corruption.

e We understood how 4Com Network Services is complying with those frameworks by making enquiries
of management to understand how the company maintains and communicates its policies and
procedures in these areas and corroborated this by reviewing minutes of the Board meetings held,
verifying employee policies, employee handbooks and communication with the employees. .

e We assessed the susceptibility of the company’s financial statements to material misstatement,
including how fraud might occur by through internal team discussions and enquiry of management and
those charged with governance. We considered the risk of management override and assessed
revenue cut-off as a fraud risk.

e To address our fraud risk on revenue cut off, we selected samples of revenue transactions around the
year-end date and performed detailed testing, including understanding the nature of the revenue
transactions and agreeing to source documentation, including the certificate of acceptance from
customers, to validate that the revenue had been recognized in the appropriate period.

e To address our fraud risk of management override of controls, we tested specific journal entries
identified by applying risk criteria. For each journal selected, we tested specific transactions back to
source documentation to confirm that the journals were authorised and accounted for appropriately.

e Based on this understanding we designed our audit procedures to identify noncompliance with such
laws and regulations. Our procedures involved had a focus on compliance with the accounting
framework through obtaining sufficient audit evidence in line with the level of risk identified and with
