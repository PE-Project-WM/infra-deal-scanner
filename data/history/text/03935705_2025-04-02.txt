GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2024

The directors present their Strategic Report of the Company and the Group for the year ended 31 March 2024.
Business review
The Group has posted strong revenues at £12.6m (2023: £12.0m) and remained EBITDA positive with £0.6m
EBITDA in the last financial year (2023: £1.4m).
The loss for the year, after taxation, amounted to £0.1m (2023: £0.6m profit) and the Group ended the year with
£3.3m (2023: £4.5m) cash balances. :
In November 2023 the Group acquired the assets and trade of FITTS Limited a company in administration. The
acquisition also include the shares in FITTS Kenya Limited based in Nairobi. This acquisition increased the
group's capabilities in selling modern workplace, cloud and security solutions and brought in nine employees in
the UK and 18 in Kenya. The Group continues to integrate the business and implement a turnaround plan for
the acquired business.
Average customer contract term was 2.4 years (2023: 2.4 years) and the Group has a high level of recurring
revenues (96.6% of total revenues), from a mix of public and private business sectors.
Order backlog of business awaiting commissioning at the end of the year was £2.0m (2023: £1.5m). The
increase reflects improved levels of orders from customers. In addition, there has been a continued change in
product and service sets with more cloud computing and security making up over 39% of orders; these orders
being delivered in weeks rather than months. ,
In order to remain competitive and gain access to new markets, the Group maintains 1S027001 Information
Security Management, ISO9001 Quality Management, ISO20000 Service Management and 1SO14001
Environmental Management certifications.
During the year the Group also published a revised Carbon Reduction plan with the aim of becoming net zero by
2030 and the Board continues to emphasise achieving this target.
The Group serves customers across the United Kingdom and Kenya in the private, public and not for profit
sectors. The Group has been successful in winning larger, multi-site and multi-service orders.
In the UK public sector the Company holds certification on the Network Services 2 (and its successor Network
Services 3 Framework Agreements, awarded in August 2023). These agreements provide simplified access to
network and telecommunications services for Public Sector organisations with a more competitive range of
suppliers. .
In November 2023 a place on Cloud Compute 2 was also awarded, allowing Public Sector organisations to
competitively acquire cloud compute services in the Platform as a Service (“PaaS”) and Intrascture as a Service
(“laaS”) categories.
The Company continues to invest in acquiring relevant accreditations and is accredited the Health and Social
Care Network ("HSCN") to further enhance the public and health sector opportunities.
Principal risks and uncertainties

: Risk acceptance and risk management is addressed through a framework of policies, procedures and internal
controls. Compliance with regulation, tegal and ethical standards is a high priority for the Group. many of these
are covered under the Group's ISO accreditations and subject to regular internal audit and external annual audit
and periodic re-accreditation.
The principal risks for our business arise from:
- Increasing inflation impacting our input prices with the Group possibly not being able to recover the increased
costs leading to reduced profitability;
- The actions of third parties initiating cyber and security attacks on our, or our suppliers, networks and systems
- Changes in the political and regulatory conditions that may have an impact on the willingness of prospects and
customers to invest in IT infrastructure impacting sales;

Strategy
Revenue growth is expected to come organically from increased product offerings and enhanced routes to
market. In the period, the Group has increased its offering in Cloud Services including private, public and hybrid
cloud services.
The Group continues to market and develop its range of cloud compute, security, secure access, high-speed
telecoms and related IT services to business and public sector customers in the UK and East Africa. The
substantial and continuous investment in cloud and network services ensures the Group continues to enhance
its standing in the marketplace whilst successfully gaining larger, multisite and multi-service customer orders.
The Group partners with key cloud service providers including Microsoft, AWS, Veeam and Fortinet and virtually
all the large telecommunications companies in the UK, and a country specific subset in Kenya and the wider East
African market, to provide customers with a wide choice of solutions with high performance at economic rates.
In addition to organic growth the Group continues to review carefully selected acquisition targets as a way of
. increasing revenue, product sets and routes to markets.
Future developments
The Group will continue to roll out enhanced cloud compute, security and secure access network services by
focusing on the provision of secure, flexible connectivity and collaboration platforms. It will continue to build the
foundations of its comprehensive set of services. The Group purchased additional capacity and functionality for
its private cloud and storage platforms which was delivered in Summer 2024 and has invested in significant
professional consulting capacity to assist clients to develop their cloud security and service implementation.
The Group rebranded from hSo to Syntura in November 2024. Further development of the brand will continue
into 2025.
The Board remains vigilant in ensuring measures are being taken to protect the business, the staff and its
customers in the current business environment. We are very grateful to the Management and staff for their
efforts to meet the challenges of the ever-changing services situation and continues to support their professional
development.
Key performance indicators (KPIs)
The Board monitors performance against budget and prior year throughout the year by reference to full
Management Accounts and KPIs namely New customers numbers, Total Contract Value of new orders, Total
Contract Value of customer re-sign orders, Revenue, Gross Margins, Earnings Before Interest Tax Depreciation
and Amortisation ("EBITDA"), Net Income, Capital Expenditure, Cash and Indebtedness.

Directors’ responsibilities statement
The directors are responsible for preparing the Group strategic report, the Directors’ report and the consolidated
financial statements in accordance with applicable law and regulations.

Group will continue in business.

Company and the Group and to enable them to ensure that the financial statements comply with the Companies
Act 2006. They are also responsible for safeguarding the assets of the Company and the Group and hence for
taking reasonable steps for the prevention and detection of fraud and other irregularities.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the Company and its subsidiaries (together "the Group") is that of the provision of
professional and managed services for modern workplace and cloud compute and of integrated

virtualisation and wide area network solutions.

2.6 Revenue
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Group and the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes. The following criteria must also be met before revenue is recognised:
Sale of goods
Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised when all of the following conditions are satisfied:
e the Group has transferred the significant risks and rewards of ownership to the buyer;
. the Group retains neither continuing managerial involvement to the degree usually associated

with ownership nor effective control over the goods sold;
. the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
e it is probable that the Group will receive the consideration due under the transaction; and
e the costs incurred or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be measured reliably.
Rendering of services
Revenue from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are
provided in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all of the following
conditions are satisfied:
e the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
. it is probable that the Group will receive the consideration due under the contract;
. the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured
reliably, and ~

. the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.

The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Income

the Group operate and generate income. :
Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not
reversed by the balance sheet date, except that:
° The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits; and
° Any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
allowances have been met.
Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of

2.10 Intangible assets
Goodwill
Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and
the acquirer's interest in the fair value of the Group's share of its identifiable assets and liabilities of
the acquiree at the date of acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost
less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a
straight-line basis to the Consolidated statement of comprehensive income over its useful economic
life.
Other intangible assets
Intangible assets are initially recognised at cost. After recognition, under the. cost model, intangible
assets are measured at cost less any accumulated amortisation and any accumulated impairment
losses.
These are amortised in equal annual instalments over 5 to 6 years. The Group undertakes a review
for impairment of an intangible asset if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying
amount of the intangible asset may not be recoverable. To the extent that the carrying amount
exceeds the recoverable amount, that is, the higher of net realisable value and value in use, the
intangible asset is written down to its recoverable amount.

2.12 impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting end date, the Company and the Group reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible
and intangible fixed assets to determine whether there is any indication those assets have suffered
an impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in
order to determine the extent of impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the
recoverable amount of an asset, the Company and the Group estimates the recoverable amount of
the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and the value in use. In assessing
value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax
discount rate which reflects the current market assessment of the time value of money and the risks
specific to the asset for which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset is estimated to be less than its carrying amount, the carrying
amount of the asset is reduced to its recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised
immediately in the Income Statement, unless that relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in _
which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.
Recognised impairment losses are reversed if the reasons for the impairment loss have ceased to
apply. Where an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the assets is
increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but so the increased carrying amount
does not exceed the carrying amount which would have been determined had no impairment loss
. been recognised for the asset in prior years. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised
immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case
the reversal of the impairment is treated as a revaluation increase.
