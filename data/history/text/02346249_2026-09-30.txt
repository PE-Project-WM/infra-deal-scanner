Strategic report
For the year ended 31 December 2025
Introduction
The directors present the Strategic Report for Sota Solutions Limited for the year ended 31 December 2025. The Company provides managed IT services spanning support, cloud, AI, cyber security, connectivity, unified communications and consultancy.
Business review and performance
2025 was a year of building for growth. Following leadership transition and a review of strategic priorities, the Company focused on strengthening its commercial and operational foundations while maintaining customer service, recurring revenues and financial resilience.

Building for growth
During the year, the Company progressed a program of digital transformation designed to improve scalability, efficiency and customer value. The Company also continued to evolve its cloud, cyber security and managed-service propositions to meet changing customer requirements.
Alongside technology investment, the Company strengthened leadership, specialist capability and employee development. 
Principal risks and outlook
The principal risks are changing customer demands and rapid technology change; cyber security and service resilience; and access to specialist skills and delivery capacity. The directors mitigate these risks through the development of differentiated services, phased adoption of new technology, recognised security and resilience controls, and continued recruitment, training and knowledge sharing.
The directors enter 2026 with a positive outlook and a clear focus on converting the foundations established in 2025 into improved organic growth and profitability. Priorities include completing the next stages of internal digital transformation, increasing automation, and bringing scalable cloud, cyber security and artificial intelligence solutions to customers.

Financial risk management
The company's operations expose it to a variety of financial risks, including credit risk, liquidity risk. The company has in place a risk management framework to monitor and manage these exposures on an ongoing basis. The key financial risks and management policies are summarised below:
Credit risk
The company is exposed to credit risk from its trade receivables and cash deposits. Credit exposure is managed by setting credit limits and monitoring the creditworthiness of customers. The company does not have significant concentrations of credit risk.
Liquidity risk
The company maintains sufficient cash and credit facilities to meet its short-term obligations. Cash flow forecasts are regularly prepared and reviewed to ensure adequate liquidity is maintained.

Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. 
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in the profit or loss.
