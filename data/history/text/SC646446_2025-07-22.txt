STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Fair review of the business
Highlands Bidco Limited was incorporated to facilitate the acquisition of Commsworld Holdings Limited, the parent
company of the Commsworld Group. Commsworld Group (‘Commsworld' or 'the Group’) is a leading UK
Telecommunications Network Provider and ISP. It operates in the business to business market and its client base
includes small, medium and large organisations in both the private and public sectors. The business has a broad
portfolio of Cloud, Security and Voice solutions, supported by in-house service & support capabilities. The trading
entity is Commsworld Limited.
The quality of service provided has been a key factor in helping the Group secure large public sector contracts in
both public and private sectors. Our support staff are ITIL qualified and we have iSO9001 and ISO27001
accreditations. The Group further developed it’s ESG strategy in 2024 by completing a carbon reduction plan and
have introduced various initiatives that will enable the business to reduce it’s carbon footprint over time.
The results for 2024 were as follows:
© Turnover was £36.9m (2023: £36.3m)
* Adjusted EBITDA* was £7.0m (2023: £6.6m)
* Loss before tax amounted to £6.8m (2023: £6.6m)
*Adjusted EBITDA is retained earnings before interest, taxation, depreciation, amortisation and other abnormal one-
off costs and arrangement fees and monitoring costs of £0.5m (2023: £1.1m).
The business has continued to establish itself as a viable alternative to the larger service providers that have held a
dominant position with corporate and public sector organisations and achieved growth with an expanded public
sector customer base in 2024 as new contracts commenced.
The business continues to develop several large public sector opportunities that can enable the business to
continue on its growth journey. Our people play a key part in delivering a world class service to our customers and
they continue to demonstrate a strong customer service mentality.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risk that the Group faces is the technological changes in the communications industry. The Group
continuously reviews the products and services that it offers, working closely with its key customers and suppliers,
to ensure that our capabilities and product offering are up to date in order to minimise this risk.
Other risks and uncertainties
Competition risk - the Group has established and longstanding strong relationships with each of its key customers
and works with diligence and flexibility to continue these relationships when renewal dates or new opportunities
arise.
Debtors - the Group has established credit control parameters and maintains strong relationships with each of its
key customers.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The Group uses various financial instruments which include cash and various items, such as trade debtors and
trade creditors that arise directly from its operations. The main purpose of these financial instruments is to raise
finance for the Group's operations. The existence of these financial instruments exposes the Group to a number of
financial risks, these are described in more detail below.
Liquidity risk
The Group seeks to manage financial risk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable needs.
The objective is to ensure a mix of funding methods offering flexibility and cost effectiveness to match the needs of
the Group. Currently the company has no loans or overdrafts and makes use of hire purchase and finance leases
where possible.
interest rate risk
The Group has external debt through bank borrowing and overdrafts and thus is exposed to interest rate risk. The
Group's policy is to arrange bank borrowings at an agreed margin. The company does have balances held in
deposit accounts at times, however, the interest received from these are immaterial.
Currency risk
The Group is exposed to foreign exchange risk. The Group's policy is to minimise the use of complex financial
instruments and foreign exchange is not hedged. Foreign exchange differences on retranslation of these assets and
liabilities are taken to profit and loss. The overall impact of foreign exchange is not material to the Group.

Turnover
Turnover is recognised at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable for goods and services

For network service call charges, revenue is recognised in the month in which the calls occur. As customers
are billed one month in arrears the final month of the years network service call charges are included in
accrued income at the balance sheet date.
For network service charges, revenue is recognised in the month in which network service is delivered. As
certain customers are billed in advance for network service charges, the element of revenue relating to the
next financial year is recognised within deferred income at the balance sheet date. Training and maintenance
contracts are recognised over the life of the contract.
The Group has secured several large, multi-year contracts, where the end customer is a public sector body.
These contracts can involve the delivery of multiple services. In determining the appropriate policy to be
applied in relation to revenue recognition, the directors aim to establish the elements of the contract that are
of a one-off nature and the element of the contract that relates to the provision of ongoing services. The
policies applied aim to ensure that commercial rates are applied to each element of the contract, where the
amounts charged are for more than one product or service. Revenue is recognised on the supply of goods or
the provision of a one-off service, when the goods are supplied or the service is provided. In relation to
recurring services, revenue is recognised when the customer is charged, resulting in accrued and deferred
income being recognised at the balance sheet date.
Certain multi-year contracts may contain embedded operating leases. For these contracts the embedded
operating lease feature usually involves the company operating as a lessee and lessor. It follows that the

intangible fixed assets - goodwill
Goodwill represents the excess of the cost of acquisition of a business over the fair value of net assets
acquired. It is initially recognised as an asset at cost and is subsequently measured at cost less accumulated
amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is considered to have a finite useful life and is
amortised on a systematic basis over its expected life, which is 10 years.
For the purposes of impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the cash-generating units expected to benefit
from the acquisition. Cash-generating units to which goodwill has been allocated are tested for impairment at
least annually, or more frequently when there is an indication that the unit may be impaired. If the recoverable
amount of the cash-generating unit is less than the carrying amount of the unit, the impairment loss is
allocated first to reduce the carrying amount of any goodwill allocated to the unit and then to the other assets
of the unit pro-rata on the basis of the carrying amount of each asset in the unit.

Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting period end date, the group reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible
assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any
such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the
impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an individual asset,
the company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in use,
the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for which the
estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying
amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. An
impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued
amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.
