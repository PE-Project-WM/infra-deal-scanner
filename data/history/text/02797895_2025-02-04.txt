Principal risks and uncertainties
Like all businesses. Chess faces a range of risks and uncertainties that could impact the delivery of our
long-term strategy. spanning operational, finance, and regulatory areas. The principal risks and
uncertainties facing the Group are monitored with a Risk Register. The board meets once a month to
identify. evaluate and manage our exposure to known risks to all divisions of the business. For the risks
within our control, the board puts appropriate processes and controls in place to mitigate them. The
principal risks and uncertainties facing the business are set out below.
Acquisition integration risk .
Acquisitions have driven the significant growth of the Group over the past few years and remains a key part
of our future strategy. Acquisitions by their nature have the potential to cause operational, cultural and
system issues during the integration phase. all of which can impact the ability of the business to deliver on
the expected financial performance. To mitigate this risk, there is a detailed planning and due diligence
process run by a dedicated acquisitions team prior acquisition and board level approval is required on
acquisition agreements and payments.
During the integration phase, there are regular peer-to-peer senior management meetings to respond to
day-to-day complications with the assimilation process, as well as consultation meetings with new People
and a strategy focused away day to ensure we deliver on performance. Where possible, an element of the
acquisition consideration is contingent upon performance of the acquisition, which encourages all parties to
work together to overcome any issues that arise as soon as possible.
Market risk
The ICT and Telecommunications markets are constantly evolving, and failure to keep abreast of emerging
product trends could impact financial performance and growth. There is a risk that the business is unable to
deliver on customers’ requirements or that we invest in areas that do not generate the expected demand.
Chess monitors market developments closely by having active dialogue with suppliers to keep abreast of
market changes and training our People on the latest technological developments. We also undertake
strategic reviews of the broader market periodically, as part of our budgetary process.
Chess puts significant internal resource into new product development with a team of People focused in
this area and regularly launches new products based on customer feedback and needs.
Liquidity and interest rate cash flow risk
The Group's policy is to finance its operations and expansion through working capital. The interest rate on.
long-term loans used to finance acquisitions is linked to SOFR and therefore the Group is exposed to
interest rate risk on the loan. The Group's banking facilities also includes a financial leverage covenant, to
manage this risk the Group regularly reviews forecasts to ensure continued compliance with the financial
covenants and continued availability of the banking facilities. :
Credit risk
The Group's credit risk is primarily attributed to its trade debtors. The amounts presented in the balance
sheet are net of any allowance for doubtful debtors, estimated by the directors. The Group has no
significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread across our large and diverse customer base.
The trade receivable balances are monitored on an ongoing basis to ensure that the Group's exposure to
bad debt is not significant and appropriate provision for bad debt risk is made. The Group also requires
appropriate credit checks on potential customers before sales are made.

Data protection and cyber security

The Group holds a significant volume of personal information, both confidential and sensitive in nature.
Failure to comply with data privacy regulations, such as GDPR, and adopting best practices across
information network security may result in potential data breach(es) across our business, which would
cause detrimental brand damage to us and loss of confidence and privacy to our customers. The Group
has a dedicated Information Security Officer to oversee operational compliance towards all data privacy
regulations and to instil the security best practices across our IT Network infrastructure, most notably
information security and Cyber Essentials accreditations. Our Group holds the ISO 27001 standard and
subsequently carries out regular penetration testing, and system upgrades to meet the audit requirements.
Cyber Essentials has been implemented across our key operational sites and is subject to regular and
frequent audits as to its effectiveness. The Group also holds the ISO 9001 accreditation which drives
continual improvement, regulatory compliance and customer satisfaction across the business, our internal
audit team are responsible for the ongoing maintenance of the quality standards and raise opportunities for
improvement. should any nonconformities arise. .

General economic risk

The Company. like other businesses. recognises that the current economic uncertainty is likely to continue.
Therefore, the Group continually reviews its forecasts including any impact on consumer confidence to
monitor the general economic climate.

Connection commissions
Connection commissions are receivable from mobile network operators when the Group

the Group over the period of service required by the commission agreement. When the Group
: assesses that there is an ongoing obligation beyond connection, commission is recognised

recognised within “Amounts due from network operators’ as part of the overall debtors balance.
- This balance due from network operators is typically recoverable over the term of the contract

: the extent that it is highly probable that a significant reversal shall not occur in the future.

historical experience and adjusts commissions income accordingly.

Positive goodwill arising on acquisitions is capitalised as an asset on the Balance Sheet and

Where it arises through a trade and asset acquisition it is classified as Purchased Goodwill.
Where the fair value of the assets and liabilities acquired exceed the cost of the acquisition,
negative goodwill arises. The Group after consideration of the assets, liabilities and contingent
consideration acquired and the cost of the combination, recognises negative goodwill on the
Balance Sheet. This is released to the profit and loss over the period in which the benefit is .
expected to arise up to a maximum of 5 years.

2.23 Provisions for liabilities
. Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the Group a legal or constructive
obligation that probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, and a reliable
estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation.
Provisions are charged as an expense to profit or loss in the year that the Group becomes aware
of the obligation. and are measured at the best estimate at the balance sheet date of the
expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account relevant risks and uncertainties.
When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the Balance
Sheet.
2.24 Financial instruments
Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised in the Balance Sheet when the Group
becomes a party to the contractual provisions of the instrument.
Trade and other debtors and creditors are classified as basic financial instruments and
measured at initial recognition at transaction price. Debtors and creditors are subsequently
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method. A provision is established
when there is objective evidence that the Group will not be able to collect all amounts due.
Cash and cash equivalents are classified as basic financial instruments and comprise cash in
hand and at bank, short-term bank deposits with an original maturity of three months or less and
bank overdrafts which are an integral part of the Group's cash management.
Financial liabilities and equity instruments issued by the Group are classified in accordance with
the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into and the definitions of a financial . .
liability and an equity instrument. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual
interest in the assets of the Group after deducting all of its liabilities. Equity instruments issued
by the Group are recorded at the proceeds received, net of direct issue costs.
Interest bearing bank loans, overdrafts and other loans which meet the criteria to be classified
as basic financial instruments are initially recorded at the present value of cash payable to the
: : bank, which is ordinarily equal to the proceeds received net of direct issue costs. These liabilities
are subsequently measured at amortised cost. using the effective interest rate method.
