Introduction
Grain deploys and operates fibre-to-the-premises (“FTTP”) networks in targeted urban communities and new
build housing developments across Great Britain.
Grain is also an Internet Service Provider (“ISP”), retailing its high speed FTTP connectivity directly to consumers
and small to medium sized businesses.
Grain’s strategy is to build, connect and operate the lowest cost network in the industry, so that it maintains a .
strategic long-term cost advantage over the rest of the industry.

Strategic overview and outlook
Grain’s overall goai for the financial year was to build on its strategy to expand financial superiority across all
aspects of the business. It has been a year of transformation for Grain in which the financial position started in a
goad position and got stronger throughout the year.
Building Superiority — Network Build
Grain is focussed on the continued expansion of its FTTP network,:targeting new build developments and urban
areas covering more than 50 towns and cities across Great Britain.
During the year Grain has continued to expand its coverage, investing into its footprint across both urban
developments and new build housing with over 237,000 homes passed, and 204,000 homes ready for service.
Unlike Openreach and other Alternative telecommunications networks (“Alt-nets”) which describe a home ready
for service as one which has a live network at the top of a pole or in a chamber, somewhere near a premise,
(sometimes over 100 metres away), a home ready for service for Grain is one that has a live network at the
boundary of each premise.
The majority of Grain’s network deployment has been in Urban areas, in which it has spent £435 of capital
expenditure per home passed. This covers the end-to-end build of the urban network, including all expenditure
required to deliver a live fibre connection from the boundary of every premise connecting the site to Grain’s
datacentres. :
Grain has full ownership of its end-to-end access network. The majority of other Alt-nets don’t own their access
network but rather rent access to Openreach’s infrastructure via Physical Infrastructure Access (“PIA”).
At this time, Alt-nets aren't capitalising the embedded lease of PIA into their build costs. The equivalent of a £1
per month PIA rental (assuming 3% inflation with a 10% discount rate) is the equivalent of £178 of embedded
capital, which is a cost that Grain does not have to incur. In addition, this means that Grain does not carry any
regulatory risk on future PIA pricing or access. Grain has built one of the most efficient networks in the industry,
delivering Homes Ready for Service ("RFS") for significantly less than the majority of the industry.
. Grain has a low net debt per home passed relative to the industry, which was only £147 at the end ‘of the year.
Building Superiority —- Network Connection
The approach that Grain has developed, live network at the boundary of each premise rather than to a pole or
chamber that could be over 100 metres away from the home, enables a very efficient installation approach.
During the financial year, Grain has been able to deliver customer connections a number of time in under 40
minutes from sign-up to completion of the customer install. Grain recently set a new record of 29 minutes from
order to a customer enjoying a broadband service over a brand new Full Fibre fine installed into their property. .
Openreach reports an average install time of eight working days; over 400 times longer than our record.
In December 2023, Openreach published a business briefing which shared that they spend £300 per home
converting a home ready for service to a live connection, which excludes sales, marketing and router costs that .
are borne by their retailers. The best monthly equivalent that Grain set during the course of the year was £54, c.
6 times less than Openreach spends, achieving cost superiority across the industry.
Building Superiority — Operating Costs
_ Grain’s strategy is to be a low-cost operator providing low prices to our customers over the long-term and in
order to underpin that is building the lowest cost operation in the industry.
The efficient operating model that Grain has built enabled it to achieve peak EBITDA investment in May 2023
: when we had only c.14,000 customers. After achieving peak EBITDA investment in May 2023, by the end of the
financial year Grain had grown its customer base to over 27,000 customers and during that time, total monthly
operating costs (cost of sales, plus administrative expenses, less depreciation, amortisation and exceptional
costs) only grew by 0.2%.

Customer acquisition ,
Grain offers customers value for money with super-fast, reliable, and affordable broadband packages to local
communities. Fairness and transparency has been at the core of Grain's customer acquisition strategy. Grain
grew its customer base in the financial year to over 27,000 customers by the end of the year.
Moving house is one of the key points of time when customer switch their utility provider and new build housing
developments by their nature have everyone moving house on first occupation. This means we achieve our
terminal rate of take-up much earlier in new build projects. On sites where Grain competes with Openreach
FTTP and all of their resellers, Grain has achieved c.50% market share. In its Urban business where customers
are predominantly people already living in their houses when our network arrives, we compete in the natural
switching market. During the financial year Grain continued to grow its market share in the footprint it operates in
(number of customers, divided by number of homes RFS) on a monthly basis, achieving a similar market share
to TalkTalk within six months of a project going live, a similar market share to Sky in under two years and similar
market share to BT & Virgin in around three years. During the financial year the fastest urban project to achieve
20% take-up was achieved in under 5 months. :
Further equity investment
In November 2023, Grain secured a £12million equity investment from Equitix.
Sustainability >
With our market leading cost to convert a home passed into a customer, resulting in customers paying back in
their first 12 months, the business is forecasting to become profitable (on an EBITDA basis) in the second half of
the next financial year (ended 31 March 2025). The only substantial ongoing cash requirement is for building out
and expanding our footprint meaning that Grain has already created a sustainable business which is forecast to
generate cash for investors over the long-term with no requirement for further funding.

Principal risks and uncertainties ,

The principle risks and uncertainties of the business are as follows:

Interest rates

The rise in interest rates during 2023 continued into 2024 and has impacted the cost of borrowing, which impacts :
future debt funding availability to Grain. Grain has mitigated this through the use of interest rate derivatives to
limit further increases and will continue to do so for further debt borrowings.

Foreign Exchange

The principal foreign exchange risk for the business is the US Dollar. Foreign exchange risk arises from
commercial transactions. Grain seeks to minimise its exposure to foreign exchange risk and all cash balances in
foreign currency that are not required for short term working capital monetary needs are converted into pounds.
Access to funding

Grain requires significant capital expenditure funding in order to achieve its network roll-out goals, and until it
achieves maturity in its customer penetration, is not in a position to self-fund this growth. Access to further equity
funding was obtained in November 2023, and Grain intends to maintain its operational and financial performance
to secure further funding in future years.

- Competition ,
Competition arises from BT, Virgin, Sky, TalkTalk and new entrants to the market. Grain has taken steps to
ensure that it maintains both a low cost, efficient build, and an efficient operating model, in order to pass the
benefits back to customers in the form of a low price, allowing it to be more competitive than other fibre
providers.
