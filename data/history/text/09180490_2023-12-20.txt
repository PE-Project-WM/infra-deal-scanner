The directors present their strategic report for the period 1 January 2022 to 30 December 2022. 
REVIEW OF BUSINESS
Ice Telecommunications Ltd (ICE Comms) is an award winning B2B provider of telecommunications solutions to 
businesses to drive efficiency and performance.
With over 50,000 mobile phone connections and several more thousand of landline / VOIP and hosted solutions to over 
2,500 industry agnostic, disparate businesses - Ice Telecommunications Ltd has created a brand that is now widely 
=== page 9 ===
recognised as a brand that delivers on value and solutions.
The performance of Ice Telecommunications Ltd over the past few years has been remarkable with significant growth 
and recognition within the industry as shown by the multiple awards given to ICE.
2022 has been a year of consolidation and change in strategy and approach. Some key changes and transformation 
occurred during the year:
-
Partial asset base sale to Daisy to reinvest back into the business.
-
Change in focus and strategy to be more customer focussed.
-
Changing commercials to provide customers with even more savings.
-
Creating and setting up new revenue streams to drive cross selling of new products.
-
Different focus on people due to recruitment challenges.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
1) Cost of living impact
The current economic climate and labour shortage is anticipated to put additional pressures on employment costs, which 
will require planning and management to ensure these are effectively controlled.
2) Recruitment
To continue the growth of the company to match its aspirations - recruitment is a key aspect to delivering this. Both 
quantity and quality. The job market has been difficult to navigate as the company has to be compete with more 
organisations - as jobs that were historically enviable for candidates - are now genuine options due to work flexibility 
and working from home.
This has been mitigated to a certain extent by offering flexible working, market rate salary and to create our USP for 
recruitment - our staff are customer focussed, have fun and leading levels of non financial benefits.

3) Market Competition
There has been significant movement in the industry with big organisations making moves to enter the market with large 
scale consolidations. This increases the competition within that sector and we have had to differentiate itself not just on 
price but also its customer support and offering. The Group welcomes this as it drives us further to drive improvements 
in our offerings and our commercial approach.
There has been significant investment across the business as a whole to drive and improve customer support.
This has been reflected in achieving the revenue figures we did.
4) General economic activity
Ice Telecommunications operates in the business to business sector and where economic activity shows a downward 
trend, this might be perceived as a potential risk to the operations of the Company. However, experience has shown that 
revenues tend to be robust, irrespective of activity as telecoms are an essential part of business, irrespective of the 
economic conditions. Additionally our offering is to save customers money and realise operational efficiencies - hence 
as a business Ice Telecommunications Ltd has done well.
5) Lack of diversification
The Company is heavily dependent on telecoms and will continue to investigate other areas such as business energy 
supply, IT, phone cover etc. In 2022 - further investment made in the Hosted and Energy teams  - both have had a stable 
start and as knowledge and experience improves we expect these to be major contributory revenue streams within ICE.
6) Regulatory changes and price risk
In the past, regulatory changes have affected gross telephony revenues and prices and the Company monitors the effects 
of this on a regular basis and where applicable will devise and implement strategies and policies to address and minimise 
the impact of any commercial pressures or compliance requirements.
7) Changes in technology
The speed of technological advancement and customer expectations in telecommunications is regularly monitored by the 
Company. One of Ice Comms' strengths, through its independence, is its ability to adapt to change and willingness to 
offer a variety of products to the UK total communications market.
8) Liquidity and cashflow risk
The Company mitigates this risk by profit retention and a strong cash balance, alongside policies with customers where 
incentives are paid on a spread basis. The Company regularly reviews cash flow projections and has invested in new 
Finance team to improve cash flow management. The company is also investigating new products that improve cash 
flow.
9) Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk that one party to a financial instrument will cause a financial loss for the other party by failing to 
discharge an obligation.
Company policies are aimed at minimising such losses and require that deferred terms are only granted to customers 
=== page 11 ===
who demonstrate an appropriate payment history and satisfy credit worthiness procedures. A large proportion of the 
mobile customer credit risk is borne by O2 and other carriers rather than the company as they receive their revenue share 
from them rather than collecting cash from customers.

BUSINESS OUTLOOK
Although 2022 revenue had not increased versus prior year- the investments made for the growth of the business will 
reap rewards as we aim to continue this focus on growth.
This will be achieved through organic growth and improved performance, new products that will be introduced that will 
complement what we do as well as growth with our customers.
We will do this through:
-
developing a CRM system that automates the end to end process of a sale to customer - creating a great customer 
journey as well as operational in house efficiencies
-
Develop effective partnerships with businesses to act as referrers
-
Invest in people to expand and retain the best people to achieve our growth plans
-
Execute a compelling cross sell and upsell strategy through to our customer base to develop our new products 
across utilities and energy
In April 2023 there was an industry wide increase applied to mobile phone charges as those contracts are linled to the 
CPI and the company has benefited from a material uplift in sales and cash inflow from its existing customers during 
2023.
Our aim is to be the company businesses go to when looking to drive down costs, improve efficiency and create value..
In conclusion, 2022 was a tough year and as we finalise 2023 - the investment in people, process and systems made in 
2022 are already realising benefits in 2023.

Adverse opinion
We have audited the financial statements of Ice Telecommunications Ltd (the 'company') for the period ended 
30 December 2022 which comprise the Statement of Comprehensive Income, Statement of Financial Position, Statement 
of Changes in Equity and Notes to the Financial Statements, including a summary of significant accounting policies. The 
financial reporting framework that has been applied in their preparation is applicable law and United Kingdom 
Accounting Standards, including Financial Reporting Standard 102 'The Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the 
UK and Republic of Ireland' (United Kingdom Generally Accepted Accounting Practice). 
In our opinion, because of the significance of the matters described in the basis for adverse opinion section of our report, 
the company's financial statements:
-
do not give a true and fair view of the state of the company's affairs as at 30 December 2022 and of the 
company's loss for the year then ended;
-
have not been properly prepared in accordance with United Kingdom Generally Accepted Practice; and
-
have not  been prepared in accordance with the Companies Act 2006.
Basis for adverse opinion
During the course of our audit work we have encountered the following issues:
-
In the absence of  sufficient, appropriate  evidence to the contrary, we have concluded  that two  material debtors 
are irrecoverable and should be provided against;
-
We have identified material errors on sales cut-off that have not been corrected;
-
A change in the company's income recognition policy during the period in respect of upfront revenue from 
indirect contracts should have been but has not been accounted as a prior period adjustment; and 
-
No accruals were  made for late purchase invoices invoices received post year end but relating to the current 
financial year.
-
In the absence of sufficient, appropriate evidence to the contrary, we have concluded that a number of sales 
ledger balances are irrecoverable and should be provided against
In addition the strategic report does not consider the effect of failing to correct these errors.

Trade debtors
The company engages its customers into contracts with third party suppliers, which are subject to rights of 
cancellation.  The timing difference between the company earning its right to income, the date the contract is

Long term contracts

Revenue recognition
Revenue is recognised to the extent that the company obtains the right to consideration in exchange for its 
performance.  Revenue is measured at the fair value of the consideration received, excluding discounts, rebates, 
VAT and other sales taxes or duty.
Customer contracted services
Revenue from the provision of project services  is recognised by reference to the stage of completion of a 
project. The stage of completion of a project is measured by comparing the costs incurred to date to the total 
estimated project costs. Revenue is only recognised to the extent of recoverable expenses when the outcome of a 
project cannot be estimated reliably. Revenue in excess of amounts billed to the customer are shown in 
prepayments and accrued income within debtors: amounts falling due within one year.
Commissions from customer contracts with Network operator
Contracts revenue is recognised on completion of the contract established between the customer and the network 
service provider.  All current and future contractual costs associated with that customer network deal are 
recognised in full immediately.  Additional revenue entitlement based on customer use or clawback are 
recognised at the time of occurrence.
Call revenue
Call revenue is recognised in the month in which calls are made.  Calls made in the year, but not billed by the 
year-end are accrued within debtors as accrued income.
