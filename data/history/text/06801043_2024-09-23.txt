STRATEGIC REPORT
The Directors present their Strategic report on Creative Technologies Ltd (the ‘Company’ or ‘Creative Technologies’) for the year ended 31 December
2023.
Results and review of the business
The profit for the year, after tax was £0.6 million (2022: £1.1 million profit after tax).
Revenue increase by £0.1 million to £13.1 million (2022: £13.0 million) and Gross profit increased by £0.2 million to £10.7 million (2022: £10.5 million).
Administrative expenses increased by £1.0 million to £10.4 million (2022: £9.4 million).
The Company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Benchmark Capital Limited. Benchmark Capital Limited and its subsidiaries (collectively the ‘Benchmark
Group’) are part of the Schroders pic group (the ‘Schroders Group’).
The principal activity of the Company is that of a technology development firm providing software licenses and business support services to Financial
Advisers based in the UK.
Throughout the year, the Company continued to make significant investments in its market leading technology and bespoke software solutions.
The Directors consider the results and the Company's financial position at 31 December 2023 to be satisfactory.
The Company and Schroders Group have remained resilient and there has been no significant impact on business operations. The Company remains
vigilant and well placed to weather future challenges. .
Directors’ dutles - compliance with section 172 of the Companies Act 2006
Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 requires a director of a company to act in the way he or she considers, in good faith, would most likely promote
the success of the Company for the benefit of its members as a whole. In doing this section 172 requires a director to have regard, amongst other
matters, to the:

- likely consequences of any decisions in the long-term;

- need to foster the Company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;

~ impact of the Company's operations on the community and environment;

~ desirability of the Company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct, and

~ need to act fairly as between members of the Company.
To discharge their section 172 duties the Directors had regard to the factors set out above in making the principal decisions taken by the Company.
This year's budget was approved by the Board following a comprehensive review of strategic priorities and risks to the business. Whilst plans are
ambitious, they will position the Company and wider Benchmark Group well against longer-term strategic visions, honouring commitments to
stakeholders.
The board of directors considered changes to its composition during the year and considered the resignation of one director.
Due to the structure of the Schroders Group stakeholder engagement also took place using Schroders Group resources, in line with agreed
delegations. For details of the engagement that takes place with the Company's stakeholders at the Schroders Group level, please refer to the
Schroders plc annual report and accounts for the year ended 31 December 2023 (‘the Schroders Report’). The Schroders Report does not form part of
this report.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The board of Directors has determined that the Company's three main areas of risk are regulatory risk, the risk arising from competition and financial &
economic risk.
Whilst the Company's activities are not directly regulated, it develops technology that the Company's clients apply to regulated activities. As a result,
the Company remains exposed to the risk of inadequate and insufficient controls which its customers rely upon and failure to demonstrate standards
that are deemed acceptable by the financial services sector. Failure to meet the requirements gives rise to a number of risks which may progress to
litigation and redress payments to customers. The board recognises and mitigates these risks through a rigorous controls framework that includes the
arrangement of internal and external training of staff.
The Company continues to strive to be ahead of its competition through a combination of technological integration and efficiency. A culture of continued
client focus by management has emanated through the development team which has allowed the Company to react quickly to changes in regulation
and client needs. Attracting quality financial adviser firms, providing ongoing training and support to employees, together with a culture of excellence
puts the firm in a strong position to be competitive in the market.
The Company builds, hosts and maintains secure back office systems which help run its customers’ businesses. It has responsibility to ensure that its
customers’ data is secure, and therefore is exposed to risk of security breach either through intentional hacking of its systems, or error by its staff.
These risks are mitigated by industry standard practices and safeguards such as regular penetration testing, coding tools, staff training, user access
controls and partnering with industry experts who offer risk mitigation and intrusion detection services.

Going concern
Whilst the Company has made a profit this year it has net liabilities as at 31 December 2023. The Directors note that the net liability position is driven
primarily by amounts owed to entities within the Benchmark Group, the majority of which is owed to its immediate parent company, Benchmark Capital
Limited.

subsidiaries will not demand repayment of the Company's outstanding obligations unless and until the Company has sufficient resources to meet these
liabilities and its ongoing working capital requirements.
Taking all the above factors into consideration, including the nature of the Company and its business, the Directors are satisfied that, at the time of
approving the financial statements, there is a reasonable expectation that the Company has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for
12 months from the date the Annual! Report and Accounts is signed.

11 - FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT
The risk management processes of the Company are aligned with those of the Schroders Group as a whole. Details of the Schroders Group's risk
management processes are outlined in the Business review section and in the Schroders Report. Sensitivities are measured against market risk
movements which the Benchmark Group believes could reasonably occur within the next calendar year.
The Company's specific risk exposures are explained below.
{a) Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk that a counterparty to a financial instrument will cause the Company financial loss by failing to discharge an obligation.
The Company has exposure to credit risk from its normal activities where the risk is that a counterparty will be unable to pay in full amounts when due.
The Company’s counterparties are predominately its related parties and therefore there is limited credit risk exposure outside the Schroders Group on
these balances. The balances are monitored regularly and historically, default levels have been nil.
Fee debtors are monitored regularly. Historically default levets have been insignificant.
Expected credit iosses are calculated in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) 9 on all of the Company's financial assets
that are measured at amortised cost and all debt instruments that are measured at fair value through other comprehensive income. The gross carrying
values are adjusted to reflect these credit losses.
The Company has internal processes designed to assess the credit risk profile of its financial instruments, and to determine the relevant stage for
calculating the expected credit losses. These processes include consideration of internal, extemal, historic and forward-looking information about
specific loans and securities as well as market data.
For financial assets held with rated counterparties (such as cash and cash equivalents), the Company calculates expected credit losses based on ,
default information published by rating agencies and considers any known factors not yet reflected in this information. The Company's cash and cash
equivalents is invested primarily in current accounts and on deposit with an AA- rated UK bank (2022: AA- rated).
Factors considered in determining whether a default has taken place include how many days past the due date a payment is, deterioration in the credit
quality of a counterparty, and knowledge of specific events that could influence a counterparty’s ability to pay.

(b) Liquidity risk ,

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Company cannot meet its obligations as they fall due or can only do so at a cost. The Benchmark Group's liquidity

policy is to maintain sufficient liquidity to cover any cash flow funding, meet all obligations as they fall due and maintain solvency. The Company has.

access to sufficient liquid funds to cover normal operating requirements. Outside the normal course of business the Company can request to borrow

through intra-Group foans to maintain sufficient liquidity. Overall liquidity of the Benchmark Group's capital (and for each entity) is monitored on a

regular basis.

(c) Capital management

The Company holds capital required to meet the Company's working capital requirements. The policy of the Company is to hold sufficient capital, keep

an appropriate standing with counterparties and meet working capital requirements. Where this is surplus to immediate working capital requirements it

is managed by the Schroders Group Treasury function and may be distributed to the shareholder.

{d) Foreign exchange risk

Foreign exchange risk is the risk that'the fair value of future cash flows of financial instruments will fluctuate because of changes in foreign exchange

rates,

Transactions in foreign currencies are recorded at the rate ruling at the date of the transaction. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign

currencies are retranslated at the rate of exchange ruling at the Statement of Financial Position date.

Foreign exchange gains and tosses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the translation at period-end exchange rates of monetary

assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are recognised in the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income.

The Company's policy in relation to currency exposure is not to hedge as this is considered part of the business.
