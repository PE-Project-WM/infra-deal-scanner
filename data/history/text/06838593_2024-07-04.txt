The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2023.
The Company was incorporated on 6 March 2009 and the company continues its principal activity of providing telecommunications services.
Review of the business
The Company has been providing telecommunication solutions since 2009.
Market
Industry Post Pandemic
The telecommunications industry has experienced spectacular growth in some product lines during the Covid pandemic. Video conferencing and remote working have now become part of the new way of working. This period has seen the increased adoption of unified communications products, such as those sold by Firstcom Europe, to provide the necessary tools to accommodate the “new normal”.
UK 
Legacy Switch Off
The pandemic accelerated the take up of unified communications products, however there is still further growth to be experienced in the market via those companies that are late adopters of new technology who will be forced to change their technology as the legacy network switch-off occurs in 2025 in the UK. The approach of Firstcom Europe has been to adopt a conversion strategy, buying legacy databases and moving customers from old to new technology, with a focus on subscription-based pricing.
Services
The Company’s primary focus is to deliver cloud based Unified Communications solutions that bring together chat, voice, video and document collaboration and instant messaging through the one system. These products operate through either fixed line phones, mobile handsets or computers and comprise a suite of advanced features such as all-call recording, contact centre functionality, interactive voice response systems and text to voice messaging. 
The range of products sold by the Company is extensive and solutions are designed to fit the individual company needs. Products in the portfolio comprise voice encryption, all-call recording, contact centre real time statistics, mobile services and fixed phones as well as on-premise PBX’s. 
Customers
The Company’s management teams have extensive experience inside the communications industry. They manage teams of professionals with a wide range of complementary skills who develop solutions for customers that can be operated and configured in their companies without requiring expensive outside assistance. The sales channels used by the Company is an in-house direct sales teams. Despite serving some very large companies using different products in the portfolio, the target for Firstcom Europe’s products are SME clients (small and medium-sized enterprises). The unique selling point of our services is that they are easy to use and can be operated and configured by non-technical staff.

Mitigants
Software Failure
The Company’s business could be severely damaged if an error or failure occurred in providing any of its products if this caused an interruption on its customer’s operations.

Information Security Failure

In many customer engagements, the Company is reliant upon a partner product that is sold to the customer. If problems occur with this product the consequences and reputational impact will be directly on Firstcom Europe.

Cyber Attack Risk
The Company is exposed to risk of cyber-attacks, in the same way as almost all companies that operate with any level of IT infrastructure. The risk is significant and could create significant business disruption and/or financial loss.
The Company has a cyber security policy which is monitored on a weekly basis, and it uses its own specialist internal team and external consultants to implement and continually monitor its policy and cyber-attack risk.

COVID-19, Ukraine War and UK Economy
Prior year’s trading had to consider the impacts of the global pandemic on the global economies as well as the Company’s trading.  The belief is that the impacts and threat was minimal throughout 2023, however there were still some symptoms of the pandemic being felt across the world in 2023, but to a much lesser extent than 2021 and 2022.  Shortages in supply of products for manufacturing and supply chain challenges in many industries, including some effects on hardware in the telecoms sector, combined with continued inflationary pressures being the main lasting impacts. These continued to increase cost of goods and services across the world, further exaggerated by some supply shortages which naturally creates upwards price pressure.
Ukraine War:
The start of the war in Ukraine in early 2022 has only added to the longer-term pandemic impacts outlined above, possibly with narrower supply chain issues on specific areas and industries, rather than more broadly as seen from the pandemic. However, if has certainly created price pressures on the energy and power sectors as well as oil and petrol prices, all of which has a knock-on effect to the operating costs of almost all companies and households.
UK Macro-Economic Impacts:
With the Company trading in the UK it is always subjected to the impacts of the UK’s macro-economic situation. The UK economy has experienced a slowdown in growth since mid-2022 and throughout 2023, with high inflation reaching double digit percentage levels between July ‘22 and March 2023, leading to the Bank of England increasing interest rates to the highest rates since the financial crisis in 2008. Huge energy prices combined with the high inflation and interest rates has created a cost-of-living crisis, leading to very little growth in the UK economy since early 2022. From the start of 2024, energy prices have reduced slightly and inflation dropped to 2.3% in April 2024, with some economic advisers speculating that the Bank of England might therefore look at one to three interest rates reductions before the end of the year, suggesting some easing of the economic pressures could be on the horizon.
Despite these macro-economic situations, the Company’s results fared well throughout 2023. At the date of finalisation of these accounts, the Company has continued to operate in a satisfactory manner despite the disturbance caused by the macro-economic impacts, all of which are constantly being monitored and evaluated by the Directors.

The Board has overall responsibility for the determination of the Company's risk management objectives and policies. 
The overall objective of the Board is to set policies that seek to reduce risk as far as possible without unduly affecting the Company's competitiveness and flexibility. Further details regarding these policies are set out below:
a) Credit risk
The Company's principal financial assets are cash and cash equivalents and trade and other receivables. Credit risk refers to the possibility that a financial loss will occur as a result of a customers' inability to meet its financial obligations and arises principally from the Company's receivables from customers.
Historically, the cash collection profile has been very good. Debt ageing and collections are monitored on a regular basis and new customers subjected to credit verification procedures before credit terms are granted.
The credit risk on liquid funds is limited because the counterparties are banks with high credit ratings assigned by international credit rating agencies. The Company has no significant concentration of credit risk.

Going concern
The financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis which assumes that the Company will continue in business for the foreseeable future being for a period of at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements.
Firstcom Europe Limited is an indirect subsidiary of Firstcom Europe Group Holdings Limited, which has agreed in writing to provide financial support, if required, to enable the company to meet its liabilities as they fall due for a period of twelve months from the signing of the financial statements.
The company directors have performed sensitivity analysis using revenue reduction with no cost reduction for the twelve months following sign-off of these financial statements to demonstrate cash availability. In addition, at a Group level, regular reviews of forecasts for sales, costs and cash flows are undertaken, projecting forwards 12 months ahead and more.
Group level detailed stress testing has been undertaken on group cash flow forecasts, in order to consider how much the forecasts have to reduce by to cause cash constraints, and also to consider the likelihood of these scenarios occurring. The Group’s revenue is approximately 78% recurring which minimises the possibility of these sensitised scenarios occurring. The Group has also looked at how these scenarios affect financial covenants of its loan facilities to ensure there is not a breach that is likely to occur in the forecast period, or, under extreme sensitised scenarios, when a breach might occur. The final consideration has been to evaluate what mitigating factors can be implemented to offset the effects of these sensitised scenarios, including group cost control across several overhead items, such as employee costs, travel expenses and marketing activity. Considering all the analysis, the Directors are comfortable that a scenario which would cause the Group, and ultimately Company cash restrictions and breach of Group loan covenants is deemed remote and therefore not a realistic outcome to consider.
The Group financial statements included a material uncertainty regarding going concern as the 
l
oans held with Kartesia Management SA are due for repayment on 17 August 2025 and should the Directors be unable to refinance the loans, the Group will require additional funding which is not yet secured.
As the Company is reliant on the Group for financial support and the Group consolidated financial statements included a material uncertainty related to going concern which may have a direct impact on the Group’s ability to provide required financial support to the Company, this event or condition indicates the existence of a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the Group and Company’s ability to continue as a going concern and therefore they may be unable to realise their assets and discharge their liabilities in the normal course of business. 
The Directors of Firstcom Europe Group Holdings Limited are confident that the refinance will be successful, and therefore the Directors of the Company consider that the going concern basis of preparation is deemed appropriate.  The financial statements do not include the adjustments that would result if the Company were unable to continue as a going concern.

1.2
Going concern
The financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis which assumes that the Company will continue in business for the foreseeable future being for a period of at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements.
Firstcom Europe Limited is an indirect subsidiary of Firstcom Europe Group Holdings Limited, which has agreed in writing to provide financial support, if required, to enable the company to meet its liabilities as they fall due for a period of twelve months from the signing of the financial statements.
The company directors have performed sensitivity analysis using revenue reduction with no cost reduction for the twelve months following sign-off of these financial statements to demonstrate cash availability. In addition, at a Group level, regular reviews of forecasts for sales, costs and cash flows are undertaken, projecting forwards 12 months ahead and more.
Group level detailed stress testing has been undertaken on group cash flow forecasts, in order to consider how much the forecasts have to reduce by to cause cash constraints, and also to consider the likelihood of these scenarios occurring. The Group’s revenue is approximately 78% recurring which minimises the possibility of these sensitised scenarios occurring. The Group has also looked at how these scenarios affect financial covenants of its loan facilities to ensure there is not a breach that is likely to occur in the forecast period, or, under extreme sensitised scenarios, when a breach might occur. The final consideration has been to evaluate what mitigating factors can be implemented to offset the effects of these sensitised scenarios, including group cost control across several overhead items, such as employee costs, travel expenses and marketing activity. Considering all the analysis, the Directors are comfortable that a scenario which would cause the Group, and ultimately Company cash restrictions and breach of Group loan covenants is deemed remote and therefore not a realistic outcome to consider.
The Group financial statements included a material uncertainty regarding going concern as the 
l
oans held with Kartesia Management SA are due for repayment on 17 August 2025 and should the Directors be unable to refinance the loans, the Group will require additional funding which is not yet secured.
As the Company is reliant on the Group for financial support and the Group consolidated financial statements included a material uncertainty related to going concern which may have a direct impact on the Group’s ability to provide required financial support to the Company, this event or condition indicates the existence of a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the Group and Company’s ability to continue as a going concern and therefore they may be unable to realise their assets and discharge their liabilities in the normal course of business. 
The Directors of Firstcom Europe Group Holdings Limited are confident that the refinance will be successful, and therefore the Directors of the Company consider that the going concern basis of preparation is deemed appropriate.  The financial statements do not include the adjustments that would result if the Company were unable to continue as a going concern.

Revenue recognition
Revenue, which is stated net of value added tax, represents sales from products and services to third parties. Revenue is recognised when it is probable that economic benefits associated with a transaction will flow to the company and the amount of revenue and associated costs can be reliably measured. The recognition criteria for different streams of revenue are as follows:
System Installations and Hardware Sales
Revenue from the installation of services and systems are recognised when the significant risks and rewards of ownership of the goods have passed to the buyer (usually on completion of installation), the amount of revenue can be measured reliably, it is probable that the economic benefits associated with the transaction will flow to the entity and the costs incurred or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be measured reliably. Where invoices have been raised in relation to installs prior to completion, for example, deposits, this revenue should be deferred and recognised in the period the installation is complete
.
Maintenance Agreement
Revenue from maintenance contracts is recognised over the term of the agreement in line with the economic benefits of the transaction. Where a long term fee has been raised revenue is deferred and released over the term of the agreement. Where the group offers a 
free of charge (
FOC
)
 period, an adjustment is made to recognise the associated cost of servicing this FOC period at the end of each financial year end.
Calls and Service charges
Revenue from calls and service contracts are recognised in the period to which they relate and recognised where both revenue and costs can be measured reliably. Where service charges are billed in advance revenue is deferred into the period the charges relate. Where calls are billed in arrears, revenue is accrued and recognised in the period the call revenue relates.
Termination fees
Revenue in relation to termination and cancellation fees are recognised when the value can be reliably measured, which is principally on notice of cancellation from a customer and where the fees are in line with terms and conditions with the contract.
Rebates
Revenue from rebates are recognised in line with the services rendered where the amount of revenue can be measured reliably, it is probable that the economic benefits associated with the transaction will flow to the entity.
