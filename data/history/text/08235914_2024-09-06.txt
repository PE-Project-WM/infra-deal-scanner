The Directors present their strategic Report for the year ended 31 October 2023.
Principal activities
The company remains focused within the Data Centre Sector, specialising in providing technical fit-out services,
bespoke manufactured containment solutions, and managed services. These services support Global High-
Performance Cloud Computing Clients and Global Data Centre Providers.
The company has an industry-leading reputation for delivering its work and projects responsibly. It boasts a large
specialist offsite manufacturing facility, further enhancing its capabilities.
Operating as a global business with a local presence, the company maintains a balanced portfolio of projects
within the Global Data Centre Sector. This includes large, strategically important projects for some of the world’s
largest cloud computing companies, as well as smaller projects delivered by regional branches. The company
continues to build its credentials in the sector, seeking to expand into new regions and areas.
Business review
The Group experienced challenging conditions to the start of the year due to significant inflationary pressures,
the lingering effects of the pandemic and political uncertainty. These factors created some of the toughest
market conditions for the Data Centre Industry.
Despite a slight easing of inflationary pressures, trading conditions remain difficult. High interest rates, coupled
with ongoing economic and political uncertainty, have made customers cautious, impacting demand. Additionally,
the industry continues to grapple with a labour and skills shortage, as well as material disruptions affecting
supply chains stil! suffering from the effects of COVID-19 and Brexit.
With a refreshed leadership team, a renewed focus on governance, quality margins and risk management, the
Group was able to deliver substantial increases to both turnover and profits.
The Group has reported a turnover of £116.5m (2022: £52.6m) and a pretax profit of £8.1m for the year ended
31 October 2023 (2022: £1.3m).
In response to current market conditions, The Group has made significant investments in contract risk
management. This initiative aims to identify and address potential risks proactively rather than reactively. The
Group continuously refreshes and strengthens its approach at all stages of contract lifecycles to ensure robust
tisk mitigation and management strategies are in place.
The medium to long-term outlook within the sector remains strong with increased demand globally for data
centres. With the increased investment in Al, the data centre industry is increasingly looking for new sites and
regions to build and base their infrastructure. The Group continues to monitor the expansion of our customers
and ensure that we grow with them by the continued servicing of their needs.
One of our biggest challenges throughout our sector is a structural skills shortage, with the recruitment and
retention of people being crucial for the delivery of our future growth plans. The Group has an excellent track
record of retaining its employees. We aim to be a great place to work through market-competitive remuneration,
training and development. The company continues to invest and explore new ways to attract, retain and develop
its greatest asset. We are actively looking at new schemes including apprenticeships, internships and graduate
schemes.
The Group’s balance sheet has strengthened with net assets of £12.8m (2022: £7.6m). In the current trading
environment, it is increasingly important that our customers, supply chain and employees can rely on the
continued financial strength and stability of the Group.

The year was marked by substantial growth and strategic accomplishments for DPI, reflected in the significant
increase in revenue from £52.6 million to £116.5 million. The top five projects alone generated £57 million,

Principal risks and uncertainties
Health and Safety
For the Group, Health and Safety is not an add-on but an integral part of our day-to-day business. With the
stakes so high in the Data Centre industry in regard to H&S on the shop floor, and the possible adverse
consequences to DPI’s reputation shoutd a critical event occur, we are committed to a consistent and effective
approach to managing health and safety.
Due care and consideration for all our SHE (Safety, Health, and Environment) undertakings are paramount. Our
dedicated SHE department advocates a holistic and comprehensive approach to SHE management, consistently
promoting the Health & Safety message: ‘Health is our mission, Safety is critical.’
DPI has numerous initiatives to promote SHE and wellbeing, including a monthly in-house designed Poster
Campaign based on topical or nationwide awareness campaigns. Each poster is accompanied by a specific
toolbox talk. Additionally, SHE Alerts & Bulletins with important messages are issued directly to employees and
shared with our key supply chain members.
We use a QR code and Helmet Sticker QR code reporting system, allowing any SHE issues, suggestions, or
matters arising to be reported and addressed promptly. The information generated provides metrics to identify
potential positive and negative trends, enabling us to address issues before they impact the company. While our
SHE procedures are robust and extensive, we also advocate a ‘Hearts & Minds’ philosophy, empowering
employees to stop a task and seek assistance if something does not feel right.
Regarding wellbeing, the Group is proud of our impressive ratio of Mental Health First Aiders and Mental Health
Champions who support our workforce. We fully support all our employees in their mental health needs.
Effective and demonstrable leadership is a key component of our SHE strategy. We passionately believe that the
strength and commitment of the Senior Leadership Team (SLT) determine how safe and healthy a workplace
can be. Senior leaders are committed to the belief that it is simply unacceptable for any person to suffer harm or
ill health from their work activities and ensure the highest standards of health and safety on any given project.
Market Conditions
The company’s services are susceptible to economic downturns and fluctuations in the private sector. The
Group’s strategy focuses on recession-resilient sectors, particularly critical infrastructure. Projections indicate
that the global Data Centre market will grow at a compound annual growth rate of 14.4% from 2024 to 2030.
Leveraging its global subsidiary presence, the Group is well-positioned to capitalise on these opportunities.
Notably, both the current UK government and opposition parties have publicly committed to accelerating the
development of critical infrastructure for energy, transport, and technology, recognising its importance for
economic growth.
Despite easing inflationary pressures during 2023/24, market conditions remain challenging due to persistently
high inflation and ongoing economic and political uncertainty in the UK. The construction industry has been
particularly hard-hit, with prices rising faster than in other sectors, putting pressure on operational delivery and
trading margins. The combined effects of the pandemic, Brexit, the Ukraine conflict, uncertainty in the Middle
East, and the cost-of-living crisis have exacerbated material and labour shortages and led to supplier
insolvencies. These challenges can result in project delays or cancellations and operational difficulties in cost
management and delivery.

Market Conditions (continued)
The company continues to monitor the impact of these challenging market conditions and has developed plans
to respond to a range of scenarios. These plans consider various factors, including market conditions, workforce
availability, changes in productivity, and the availability and pricing of materials.
Considering the tough environment, the company has refreshed its “bid no bid" processes, estimation, and
procurement strategies for new work. The commercial approach to agreeing on terms and conditions now
includes clearly defined parameters to ensure that the company is highly selective, winning work at margins that
reflect the associated risks. Additionally, we continue to focus on securing work directly with end clients rather
than through main contractors.
While inflation has eased during the period, the company remains vigilant in managing cost inflation. Strategies
include offering short pricing windows, incorporating inflationary provisions into contracts, and ensuring
advanced purchasing once a job is secured. A significant proportion of the company’s sales are protected
against inflationary pressures through contractual provisions or advance purchases on secured projects. We are
committed to only taking on work where the impacts of inflation can be acceptably managed.
Competition
The Group operates in a highly competitive sector, where price significantly influences contract awards. The
Group balances quality, capability, stability, and reputation with cost considerations. To manage these dynamics,
the Group rigorously evaluates bids, ensuring margins align with risk. This approach is crucial in today's
environment, where some competitors accept lower margins to secure turnover despite inflation-related risks.
A large proportion of next year’s sales are already secured, supporting the company’s selective approach.
Quality and profitability take precedence over chasing turnover. The Group's focus on cost efficiency, .
investments in people and technology, and strong financial position contribute to its competitive edge. High
customer retention and repeat business reflect consistent excellence.
Customers and Supply Chain
The construction industry is facing growing risks of insolvency, driven by rising costs from high inflation, supply
chain disruptions, and high interest rates. In response, DPI remains focused on managing its cashflows through
strict contract administration and routine credit checks on customers and suppliers.
To enhance supply chain resilience, the company builds strong relationships with suppliers and subcontractors,
ensuring high-quality standards at competitive prices while avoiding over-reliance on any single supplier or
subcontractor. Our commercial, procurement, and project teams are dedicated to meeting forecast targets,
maintaining financial stability amid these challenges.

Financial instruments
The company’s principal financial assets consist of cash and trade debtors, with credit risk primarily associated
with trade debtors and amounts recoverable on contracts. To manage this risk, the financial strength of
customers is assessed before entering into contracts and regularly reviewed throughout the contract's duration
-to monitor exposure.
Liquidity risk management is achieved through careful monitoring and forecasting of cash flow, aligning creditors
and debtors with contractual obligations, and implementing effective cash collection techniques. The company
mitigates liquidity constraints by closely managing cash flows, including daily cash level monitoring and monthly
cash flow forecast reviews. Accurate forecasting, retained profits, and project delivery modelling contribute to
effective liquidity management.
Research and development activities .
As demand for Data Centres continues to grow rapidly, new technologies and methods are being implemented to
deliver compliant and efficient solutions within tight deadlines. Data Centre providers are constantly exploring
innovative ways to meet market capacity needs. In response, DPI actively engages in R&D activities to support
the bespoke builds required by our clients. These custom projects often involve extensive testing and
development to ensure that the solutions align with the specific structural needs of each Data Centre.
in the past year, DPI invested £3.3 million (2022: £3.3 million) in R&D expenses, reflecting our commitment to
advancing our capabilities and supporting our customers in future innovations. We anticipate continuing this level
of support as we contribute to the evolving demands of the industry.

2.4 Revenue
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Group and the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes. The following criteria must also be met before revenue is recognised:
Rendering of services
Revenue from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are
provided in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all of the following
conditions are satisfied:
. the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
. it is probable that the Group will receive the consideration due under the contract;
. the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured
reliably; and
° the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.
Construction contracts
The Group's primary activity is the construction of data centres. Its revenue is recognised by
reference to the stage of completion of the contract at the reporting date.
Revenue from long term contracts includes the amount initially agreed in the contract plus any
variations in contract work to the extent that it is probable that the variation will result in revenue that
can be reliably measured.
Revenue is recognised on construction services over time as the benefit is transferred to the
customer. The Group uses the surveys of work performed method to determine the stage of
completion of construction contracts.
Where the outcome of the contract cannot be estimated reliably, contract revenue is recognised to
the extent of contract costs incurred where it is probable they will be recoverable. Contract costs are
recognised as expenses in the period in which they are incurred. Contract costs include costs that
relate directly to the specific contracts and costs that are attributable to contract activity in general
and can be allocated to the contract. Full provision is made for all known or expected losses on
individual contracts immediately, once such losses are foreseen.
The gross amount due from customers from contract work, including retentions, is shown as a
receivable. The gross amount due comprises costs incurred plus recognised profits less the sum of
recognised losses and progress billings. Where the sum of recognised losses and progress billings
exceeds costs incurred plus recognised profits, the amount is shown as a liability.

method so that the amount charged is at a constant rate on the carrying amount. Issue costs are
initially recognised as a reduction in the proceeds of the associated capital instrument.
2.8 Pensions .
Defined contribution pension plan
The Group operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a
pension plan under which the Group pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the Group has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not paid
are shown in accruals as a liability in the Balance Sheet. The assets of the plan are held separately
from the Group in independently administered funds.
2.9 Share-based payments
Where share options are awarded to employees, the fair value of the options at the date of grant is
charged to profit or loss over the vesting period. Non-market vesting conditions are taken into
account by adjusting the number of equity instruments expected to vest at each balance sheet date
so that, ultimately, the cumulative amount recognised over the vesting period is based on the number
of options that eventually vest. Market vesting conditions are factored into the fair value of the options
granted. The cumulative expense is not adjusted for failure to achieve a market vesting condition.
The fair value of the award also takes into account non-vesting conditions. These are either factors
beyond the control of either party (such as a target based on an index) or factors which are within the
control of one or other of the parties (such as the Group keeping the scheme open or the employee
maintaining any contributions required by the scheme).
Where the terms and conditions of options are modified before they vest, the increase in the fair
value of the options, measured immediately before and after the modification, is also charged to profit
or loss over the remaining vesting period.
Where equity instruments are granted to persons other than employees, profit or loss is charged with
fair value of goods and services received.

2.10 Current and deferred taxation
The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in profit or loss
except that a charge attributable to an item of income and expense recognised as other
comprehensive income or to an item recognised directly in equity is also recognised in other
comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date in the countries where the company and
the Group operate and generate income.
Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not
reversed by the balance sheet date, except that:
. The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits;
. Any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
. allowances have been met; and
° Where they relate to timing differences in respect of interests in subsidiaries, associates,
branches and joint ventures and the Group can control the reversal of the timing differences
and such reversal is not considered probable in the foreseeable future.
Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of
business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair values of
assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences between the fair
values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax. Deferred tax is determined
using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.

2.11 intangible assets
Goodwill
Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and
the acquirer's interest in the fair value of the Group's share of its identifiable assets and liabilities of
the acquiree at the date of acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost
less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a
straight-line basis to the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income over its useful economic
life.
Other intangible assets
Intangible assets are initially recognised at cost. After recognition, under the cost model, intangible
assets are measured at cost less any accumulated amortisation and any accumulated impairment
losses.
At each reporting date the company assesses whether there is any indication of impairment. If such
indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is determined which is the higher of its fair
value less costs to sell and its value in use. An impairment loss is recognised where the carrying
amount exceeds the recoverable amount.
All intangible assets are considered to have a finite useful life. If a reliable estimate of the useful life
cannot be made, the useful life shall not exceed ten years.
Computer software is amortised over its estimated useful life of 5 year on a straight-line basis.
2.12 Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of
operating in the manner intended by management.
Depreciation is charged so as to allocate the cost of assets less their residual value over their
estimated useful lives, using the straight-line method.
Depreciation is provided on the following basis:
Long-term leasehold property - 20%
Plant and machinery - 20%
Motor vehicles - 33%
Fixtures and fittings - 20% Computer equipment, a subset of fixtures and
fittings, is depreciated at 33%
The assets’ residual values, useful lives and depreciation methods are reviewed, and adjusted
prospectively if appropriate, or if there is an indication of a significant change since the last reporting
date.
Gains and losses on disposals are determined by comparing the proceeds with the carrying amount
and are recognised in profit or loss.

2.13 Impairment of fixed assets and goodwill
Assets that are subject to depreciation or amortisation are assessed at each balance sheet date to
determine whether there is any indication that the assets are impaired. Where there is any indication
that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset (or cash-generating unit to which the
asset has been allocated) is tested for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised for the amount
by which the asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the
higher of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs to sell and value in use. For the purposes of
assessing impairment, assets are grouped at the lowest levels for which there are separately
identifiable cash flows (CGUs). Non-financial assets that have been previously impaired are reviewed
at each balance sheet date to assess whether there is any indication that the impairment losses
recognised in prior periods may no longer exist or may have decreased.
2.14 Valuation of investments ,
Investments in subsidiaries are measured at cost less accumulated impairment.
2.15 Stocks
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and net realisable value, being the estimated selling price less
costs to complete and sell. Cost is based on the cost of purchase on a first in, first out basis. Work in
progress and finished goods include labour and attributable overheads.
At each balance sheet date, stocks are assessed for impairment. If stock is impaired, the carrying
amount is reduced to its selling price less costs to complete and sell. The impairment loss is
recognised immediately in profit or loss.
2.16 Debtors
Short-term debtors are measured at transaction price, less any impairment. Loans receivable are
measured initially at fair value, net of transaction costs, and are measured subsequently at amortised
cost using the effective interest method, less any impairment.
2.17 Cash and cash equivalents
Cash is represented by cash in hand and deposits with financial institutions repayable without penalty
on notice of not more than 24 hours. Cash equivalents are highly liquid investments that mature in no
more than three months from the date of acquisition and that are readily convertible to known
amounts of cash with insignificant risk of change in value.
In the Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows, cash and cash equivalents are shown net of bank
overdrafts that are repayable on demand and form an integral part of the Group's cash management.
2.18 Creditors
Short-term creditors are measured at the transaction price. Other financial liabilities, including bank
loans, are measured initially at fair value, net of transaction costs, and are measured subsequently at
amortised cost using the effective interest method.
