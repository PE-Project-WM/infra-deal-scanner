STRATEGIC REPORT
BUSINESS REVIEW
The Company is wholly owned by Airspan Networks Inc., registered in Delaware, USA, and ultimately
owned by Airspan Networks Holdings Inc. (“Airspan Group”), listed on the New York Stock Exchange
American. The Company is a designer and producer of wireless network equipment for 4G and 5G networks
for both mainstream public telecommunications service providers and private network implementations.
The Company provides Radio Access Network (“RAN”) products based on Open Virtualized Cloud Native
Architectures that support technologies including 5G new radio (“5G NR”), Long-Term Evolution (“LTE”),
and Fixed Wireless standards, operating in licensed, tightly-licensed and unlicensed frequencies.
The market for the Airspan Group’s wireless systems includes mobile carriers, other public network
operators and private and government network operators for command and control in industrial and public
safety applications such as smart utilities, defence, transportation, mining and oil and gas. Its strategy
applies the same network technology across all addressable sectors.
The Airspan Group’s main operations are in Slough, United Kingdom; Mumbai and Bangalore, India;
Tokyo, Japan; Airport City, Israel; and the Company’s corporate headquarters are in the United States
(“U.S.”) in Boca Raton, Florida.
The Company has historically incurred losses from operations that have been financed by Airspan Networks
Inc., the parent company, by intercompany loans or equity share purchases.
FINANCIAL OVERVIEW
The Company reports its financial statements and the notes to the accounts in United States dollars (“$”).
The Company’s operating loss after tax for the year ended 31 December 2022 was $18.6 million, (2021:
$7.3 million loss, restated). Turnover at $21.2 million in 2022 decreased 31% from $30.8 million in 2021,
as restated, with gross margins at 67% and 70% for 2022 and 2021, respectively. The directors have
considered the going concern position of the Company and have a reasonable expectation that the Company
can continue to adopt the going concer basis of preparation.
GOING CONCERN
The Company incurred a loss for the year of $18.6 million, and had total liabilities at 3] December 2022 of
$264.8 million, of which $185.6 million is due to the parent company. These events and conditions, along
with other matters as set forth in Note 1, indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant
doubt on the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern.
In order to address the need to satisfy the Company’s continuing obligations and minimise losses incurred
by the Company and realize its long-term strategy; the management of the Airspan Group and the
management of the Company has taken several steps and is considering additional actions to improve its
their operating and financial results. Specific actions that are being undertaken by the management of the
Company includes including the following:
© focusing the Company’s efforts to increase sales in additional geographic markets;
© continuing to develop 5G product offerings that will expand the market for the Company’s
products;
focusing the Company’s efforts to improve days sales outstanding to provide additional liquidity;
® continuing to implement cost reduction initiatives to reduce non-strategic costs in operations and
expand the Company’s labour force in lower cost geographies, with headcount reductions in higher
cost geographies, and
© obtaining additional funding from parent company to support increase in sales and expansion of
products. In addition, the management of the Company obtained a letter of non-recall from its
parent company.
The Company’s profitability and cash flows is also dependent on the transfer pricing agreements with the
Airspan Group, particularly Airspan Networks Inc. and Airspan Networks Limited, wherein the Company
shares revenue, losses and funding with these companies.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES FACING THE COMPANY
Technology Risk
The Company relies on technology for day-to-day operations that has potential system vulnerabilities,
failure due to hacking, phishing and ransomware and potential data loss. All modern companies operate
with this risk and the Company addresses this risk with a robust attention to system vulnerabilities and
education of users. The Company has strict controls in place and regutar communications to staff members
to remain cautious and vigilant to the threats.
Supply Chain Risk
The Company is reliant on key suppliers for components that are from a single source. An interruption of
supply from these key suppliers could disrupt or halt production for products that are designed using these
key components.
Key Staff Risk
The Company is reliant on the wealth of expertise of its staff, and in some cases particular staff members,
to research, develop and improve the product line. Losing these key employees could risk the development
of the products and cutting-edge technologies.
Funding Risk
The Company is reliant and funded through its parent company, which has provided the financial support
for the Company to grow to where it is now. The directors believe there is a reasonable expectation that the
Company will continue to be supported and meet its strategic targets.

Deferred taxation

Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the

balance sheet date where transactions or events have occurred at that date that will result in an obligation to

pay more, or a right to pay less or to receive more, tax with the following exceptions:

* provision is made for deferred tax that would arise on remittance of the retained earnings of overseas
subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures only to the extent that, at the balance sheet date, dividends
have been accrued as receivable;

« — deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that the directors consider that it is more likely
than not that there will be suitable taxable profits from which the future reversal of the underlying
timing differences can be deducted.

Deferred tax is measured on an undiscounted basis at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the periods :
in which timing differences reverse, based on tax rates and laws enacted or substantively enacted at the
balance sheet date.
Foreign currency translation
Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency using the average rates in effect at
the dates of the transactions.
At each period end, foreign currency monetary items are translated using the closing rate. Non-monetary
items measured at historical cost are translated using the exchange rate at the date of the transaction and
non-monetary items measured at fair value are measured using the exchange rate when fair value was
determined.
Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the translation at
period-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are
recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.
Foreign exchange gains and losses that relate to borrowings and cash and cash equivalents are presented in
the Statement of Comprehensive Income within Other Charges.
Revenue recognition
Revenue is recognised when all of the following conditions are met:
* an arrangement exists with the customer,
« delivery has occurred or services have been rendered,
« the price for the product or service is fixed and determinable, and
« collection of the receivable is reasonably assured.
Revenue from product sales, including sales to distributors and resellers, is generally recognised at the time
the product is shipped to the customer. Billings are classified as deferred revenue if customer acceptance is
required, rights of return exist, or other significant obligations remain that are essential to the functionality
of the delivered products. Once these conditions have been satisfied, revenue is recognised. The estimated
cost of any post-sale obligations, including basic product warranties, is accrued at the time revenue is
recognised based on historical experience.

Revenue from time and material service contracts is recognised once the services have been performed.

Revenue from service contracts that relate to a period of cover is recognised ratably over the given

contract period.
