The directors present their Strategic report for Sabio Ltd (“the Company”) and its subsidiaries the (“Sabio
Group”) for the year ended 30 September 2024. References to the Company's intermediate parent company,
Seattle Holdco Limited and its subsidiaries are hereafter known as “the Group”.
Principal activities
The Sabio Group is a leader in digital customer experience (CX) transformation, supporting enterprise clients
across the UK, Spain, France, and the Benelux region. We deliver Al-powered solutions and expert consulting
that streamline customer interactions, reduce costs, and improve satisfaction. By integrating technologies such
as conversational Al, automation, and analytics, we help organisations achieve measurable outcomes and
long-term value. Sabio partners closely with clients to drive continuous improvement and sustainable business
performance.
Business review
Following the reappointment of Andy Roberts as CEO in April 2023, Sabio advanced its strategic
transformation in FY24, positioning the Sabio Group for long-term, sustainable growth amid a dynamic and
evolving market landscape. The focus areas of the transformation were:
* Strengthening the leadership team.
* Directing commercial resources towards the enterprise target customer acquisition strategy.
+ Investment in helping clients to leverage Al to drive performance improvement. .
* Responsible trading to drive an increase in operational efficiency.
* Simplification of portfolio and investment in the provision of high value services.
* Positioning the Sabio Group to secure a financial partner to support the next phase of business growth.
New ownership and future outlook
On 22 July 2025 majority ownership of the Group was acquired by funds and accounts managed and advised
by CVC, a leading global private markets manager with €202 billion in assets under management. As part of
this transaction the Group's capital structure has been substantially modified with total debt reduced to £69
million with maturities extended to fall between 30 June 2030 and 30 December 2031.
The change of ownership marks a significant milestone and reflects the strength of Sabio’s underlying business
model, quality of our client relationships, and our long-term potential in an exciting market. The transaction
provides Sabio with a significantly strengthened financial position, enhanced liquidity, and the financial
flexibility to accelerate investment in innovation, talent, and strategic growth initiatives.
The Group's proforma go-forward balance sheet summarised below ensures that Group's improved financial
position will allow it to invest in future growth and deliver exceptional outcomes for clients. In addition, the
Group’s annual interest expense will more than halve.

Operational Efficiency
Sabio’s strategic transformation in FY24 emphasised operational efficiency as a core pillar of its growth and
resilience strategy. This focus was evident in both structural decisions and performance outcomes
undertaken.
During the audited and subsequent assessed period Sabio has undertaken initiatives to drive business
: performance including:
. Divestment of non-core division;
. Evolving the team size, skills and locations to match changing client requirements and ensure cost
competitiveness;
. Strategic hiring in low-cost geographies;
. Continued focus on the provision of high-value services;
. Investment in Al-powered automation to improve customer outcomes and streamline operations.
The improvement in operational metrics signifies a meaningful uplift in both productivity and profitability per
employee. Adjusted EBITDA) per headcount (excluding the discontinued SSP operation) increased by 4%,
from £25,000 in FY23 to £26,000 in FY24, highlighting Sabio's successful operational scaling and enhanced
cost efficiency.
Performance
In FY24, Sabio demonstrated strategic resilience and operational discipline in the face of significant market
headwinds. Revenue decline to £126.5 million from £145.8 million in FY23 was anticipated due to known
changes in the way in which clients are choosing to consume consulting, recurring Professional Services
and annuity services; the deliberate shift away from mid-market to enterprise customers; discontinuation of
the non-core division (Salesforce delivery practice); and the impact of the Genesys announcement in
October 2022 which reduced professional services spend on the Genesys Engage product portfolio.
Despite this, Sabio maintained robust profitability, with Adjusted EBITDA“) holding steady at £20.0 million
{FY23: £20.8 million), reflecting the strength of its business model coupled with improvements in business
efficiency.
; Future Outlook ;
Sabio anticipates continued momentum in the transition to cloud-based technologies, with increasing
adoption of Contact Centre as a Service (CCaaS) platforms and the growing application of Agentic Al-
powered automation to manage simpler customer experience (CX) interactions. These market dynamics
represent a significant long-term opportunity for Sabio given its positioning as a trusted partner and warrant
focused action across each of our strategic pillars. :
Culture - We remain committed to investing in the development of our colleagues through our structured
career pathways aligned to future capability needs. In parallel, we will continue to optimise our onshore and
offshore resource mix to enhance contribution margin while delivering market-leading service at a
competitive cost.
Proposition - As legacy enterprise managed services naturally decline within our client base, we expect
these to be progressively replaced by high-margin Agentic Al automation solutions and a growing demand
for client success management services.
Customers - Execution of our Top 100 strategy will remain a key priority. We will continue to target the
most complex and bespoke enterprise clients, where Sabio’s deep domain expertise delivers maximum
value. Our role will be to support these organisations through their cloud migration journeys and then enable
them to differentiate through our high-value strategic services.
Performance - Revenue growth is expected to resume during FY25, underpinned by a strong pipeline of
opportunities with the Sabio Group maintaining strong EBITDA margins and key metrics around unit
economics.

ESG strategy and culture
Our commitment to sustainability, inclusivity, and social responsibility advanced further in FY24, underscoring
our belief that a strong corporate culture is essential to delivering exceptional customer experience (CX)
transformations.
Environmental Sustainability :
Environmental progress was driven by our Sustainability Steering Committee, with notable achievements
including EcoVadis Silver certification - affirming our sustainable business practices and enhancing our appeal
to environmentally conscious clients. Our Net-Zero strategy is fully integrated with our intelligent automation
solutions, helping clients reduce their own carbon footprints.
Carbon emissions fell by 22% compared to FY23. Scope 2 emissions dropped by 70% through strategic energy
Management and renewable energy adoption. Scope 3 emissions declined by 12%, reflecting supply chain
and operational efficiencies. Scope 1 emissions rose by 17%, attributed to increased client facing business
activity as the industry recovered from covid driven remote working culture. These results are particularly
significant given the concurrent rollout of Al-powered solutions that promote resource efficiency.
Diversity, Inclusion & Talent Development
Diversity and inclusion remain central to our culture. The launch of our Women in Tech Group in FY24 fostered
gender diversity in Al and data science, gaining momentum and expanding in early FY25. Our gender pay gap
reporting shows a 28% improvement, with the median hourly gap narrowing from 13.7% to 9.8%, driven by
internal career progression and equitable compensation practices.
Women now represent 30% of our global workforce - exceeding the industry average of 25% - and 33% of
entry-level roles, reflecting targeted recruitment and strong internal mobility programmes. Over 50% of roles
are filled internally, supporting career advancement and leadership development.
Community Engagement .
Our sponsorship of a youth football team in Scotland exemplifies our innovative approach to community
engagement. Sabio provided Al-powered technology to analyse player activity combining technology with
grassroots sports development. This initiative introduces Al concepts to young people encouraging interest in
: STEM subjects.
Our Volunteering Days programme remains a cornerstone of our culture, enabling employees to support
causes they value. Activities range from supporting CHAS (Children’s Hospices Across Scotland) to
participating in PRIDE events, showcasing our team’s broad commitment to social impact.
Disclosure of Principal Risks and Uncertainties
The Sabio Group's operations expose it to a variety of risks that include but are not limited to the loss of key
customers, the loss of key suppliers, outsourcing, business continuity and reliance on key IT systems, together
with risks associated with growth of the business and underlying economic environmental factors affecting
overall business confidence.
These risks are formally reviewed by the Board at least annually and appropriate processes put in place to
monitor and mitigate their effects. Customer risk is managed by focusing on the diversification of the customer
portfolio and customer retention strategies, as well as reviewing the financial stability of new and existing
customers. Supplier risk is managed through regular assessment of existing key suppliers, looking at factors
such as financial stability, resource availability, product lifecycle, product relevance to our customers and their
exposure to market risk. Regular forums are held with our customers to ensure we understand their needs,
and our suppliers are critically appraised to ensure their products meet customer needs. In addition, regular
evaluations of alternate suppliers, solutions and technologies are made to minimise the risk in this area. The
Sabio Group has established formal business continuity and disaster recovery plans, and these are reviewed
at Board level at least annually and tested bi-annually. The risk to the growth of the business and the underlying
economic environment is managed by aligning the Sabio Group's product and service offering with its core
competencies, whilst retaining and winning customers from a diverse cross-section of size, sectors and
geographic locations.

Going concern (continued)
As part of their review the directors have considered the implications of the current economic conditions on
the going concern assumption. This has also included sensitivity analyses which consider a severe but
plausible downside. As disclosed in note 30 (Events after the reporting period), the Group’s capital structure
has been substantially modified with total debt reduced to £69 million and maturities extended. In addition,
certain terms and conditions in the existing facility agreement were reset. The directors are confident that the
new capital structure provides a solid financial foundation for the Group.
The cashflow forecasts used by management in both base and severe but plausible downside scenarios were
used to calculate forecast covenant compliance under the reset terms and conditions in the going concern
period.
The Group’s management base case assumes revenue remains relatively flat for most of the going concern
period, with no material deterioration in gross margins or working capital. Annuity renewal rates remain in line
with previous years and productivity improvements in delivering additional professional services backlog are
achieved. The Group’s management base case also includes management's best estimate of other costs
which may be incurred by the Group.
The severe but plausible downside scenario includes sensitivities which result in reduced adjusted EBITDA
and cash generation.
The conclusion is that sufficient funds are expected to be generated within the Group to meet the liabilities of
the Group as they fall due, and that the Group will not breach its covenants within the going concern period.
The directors have concluded that the going concern basis of accounting remains appropriate with respect to
both the Sabio Group and the Company.

Revenue recognition
A contract involves agreement between two or more parties that creates enforceable rights and
performance obligations. The Sabio Group recognises revenue when an enforceable performance
obligation is satisfied, and it is identifiable separately from other promises in the contract. A performance
obligation involves the transfer of control of distinct goods and services to the customer where a
customer can benefit from the good or service on its own or together with readily available resources in
the market.
The Sabio Group allocates a transaction price to each performance obligation on the basis of the stand-
alone selling prices of each distinct good or service promised in the contract. The Sabio Group uses an
estimated price when stand-alone prices are not observable. Discounts, rebates, and consideration that
are generated through the synergy of the contract as a whole should be allocated to one or more, but
not all performance obligations.
For performance obligations satisfied over time, Sabio Group recognises revenue over time by selecting
an appropriate method for measuring the Sabio Group's progress towards completion of that
performance obligation.
i) Annuity services in respect of service, support and maintenance income is typically invoiced in .
advance, termed deferred income. Revenue is recorded on a straight-line basis over the relevant period;
in accordance with IFRS 15, a time-based method to measure progress is assessed to be appropriate
as the performance obligation is satisfied evenly over a period of time and/or Sabio Group has an active
obligation to perform over a period of time. In a number of Annuity contracts, Sabio Group has a right
to consideration from customers in an amount that corresponds directly with the value to the customer
of Sabio Group’s performance completed to date; in particular in relation to call and usage charges. In
: that instance Sabio Group recognises revenue in relation to those call and usage charges for the
amount to which the entity has a right to invoice. .
ii) Professional services revenue is in respect of project service income from consultancy, installation
. and training services on telephony and data systems. It is recognised when performance, provision or
delivery of the service has occurred, based on measured input hours, to the degree that the associated
hours are recoverable, and when the contractual right to consideration has been earned. If any of these
services are invoiced and paid for in advance of being delivered, the income is termed deferred income,
and then recorded as revenue when.under this accounting policy the Sabio Group is entitled to
recognise the revenue.
iii) Technology revenue (revenue from the sale of software and hardware) is recognised when goods
are delivered, or invoiced and the Sabio Group has an unconditional right to consideration before it has
satisfied a performance obligation.
iv) The Sabio Group considers itself principal in all the above revenue categories as the majority of its
projects and services involve the implementation, maintenance and support of complex customer
experience IT solutions, comprising multiple technologies — both third party and proprietary — that often
require specific configuration for customers’ existing IT infrastructures and processes. Sabio Group
manages all interactions with its customers and provides 1st and 2nd level support services to its
customers. .
Investments in subsidiary undertakings
Investments in subsidiary undertakings are recorded at cost plus incidental expenses less any provision
for impairment. Impairment reviews are performed by the directors when there has been an indication
of potential impairment.
