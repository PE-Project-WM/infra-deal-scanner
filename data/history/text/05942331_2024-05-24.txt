Strategic report

For the year ended 31 December 2023

The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2023.

Principal activities

The principal activity of the group continued to be that of the provision of visual communication and other

workplace services. The group's services and products focus on the development, project management,
. implementation, maintenance and customisation of video conferencing and other workplace systems. The group

specialises in providing turn-key end-to-end solutions and maintenance services tailored to the special needs of

individual clients.

Business review of performance and position

In the year ended 31 December 2023 York Telecom Limited saw a 21% increase in revenue and a 38% increase in

gross profit. Revenue in 2023 grew due to winning some material contracts which included service delivery which

has a higher gross profit.

Administrative expenses in 2023 were only £10,000 higher than in 2022.

York Telecom Limited will continue to be reliant on support from York Telecom Corporation.

Strategy

Group and company strategy is fivefold: nurture existing clients, acquire new customers, enter new markets,

introduce new products or service and support York Telecom customers on a global basis. Historically the

company has been successful at retaining existing customers, so additional focus is being given to acquiring new

customers. Also, the company is looking to diversify its portfolio by increasing the volume of services being

introduced and sold.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Covid-19 - The effects of the disruption caused by the Covid-19 virus had minimal impact on performance in

2023. Another pandemic may happen and York Telecom Limited is confident it now has a mix of physical and

remote services that means it will be able to manage any future pandemics.

Supply chain - As the group and company does not manufacture itself it is reliant on third parties who may

choose to cease trading through partners. The impact of the chip shortage disrupting the global supply chain

was minimal in 2023. The group and company has mitigated this risk by working with a variety of different

manufacturers and has worked with clients to adapt solutions to work with the products that are available.

New technology - New technology could be introduced to the market that would cause some of the existing

video services to become obsolete. The group and company works with new suppliers of technology to

integrate them with their services.

The economy - A low level of economic activity may cause other industries to be at risk, but the group and

company believes that as it emphasises cost savings and efficiency associated with its technology it can mitigate

this risk.

Brexit - The group and company continues to monitor the impact of Brexit and has seen growth in its Irish

subsidiary. The group and company believes that it has mitigated the impact of Brexit as the group and

company works with a network of manufacturers, distributors and logistics companies to deliver outside the

UK.

Financial instruments
Cash flow risk
The group makes use of an invoice discounting facility to mitigate any cash flow risk. This is in addition to controls
around credit risk and working closely with suppliers where it is known there may be a need for extended credit
terms due to the nature of the project undertaken.
Liquidity risk
The group is exposed to some liquidity risk as it may need to pay suppliers for goods before the group has received
payment from customers. Where the group is aware of this possibility in advance the group will negotiate
extended terms with suppliers. On occasion the group has received short term funds from its parent company York
Telecom Corporation.
Interest rate risk
The directors do not consider the group to be exposed to interest rate risk as the group only pays a fixed interest
rate on the group's banking facilities.
Currency risk
The group buys and sells products and services in sterling, US dollars and euros. To date risk exposure has been
maintained as we align purchases and sales, but this position is monitored. Where historic support has been
provided by York Telecom Corporation this support was provided in US dollars which means the group has risk
when there is an adverse movement in sterling to US dollars. The exchange movement in 2023 related to the
historic support from York Telecom Corporation, to date the exchange loss has not been realised.
Credit risk
The group deals with a large portfolio of customers so is not exposed to a concentration of credit risk. The
customers with whom the group has extensive dealings tend to be large multinationals with strong credit ratings.
New customers are subject to credit reviews and where there are concerns, deposits are taken.
Price risk
The group produces bespoke pricing for most jobs therefore there is little exposure to risk of supplier prices
changing adversely that are not covered by an equivalent change in sales price. Competition may resultin adecline ~
in margins as the group must respond to market trends.

Auditor's-responsibilities for the audit of the financial statements
Our objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the group and parent company financial
statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an
auditor's report that includes our opinion. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee
that an audit conducted in accordance with ISAs (UK) will always detect a material misstatement when it exists.
Misstatements can arise from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually or in the aggregate, they
could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of these financial
statements.
Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures
in line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect material misstatements in respect of irregularities,
including fraud. The specific procedures for this engagement and the extent to which these are capable of :
detecting irregularities, including fraud are detailed below.
Identifying and assessing risks related to irregularities:
We assessed the susceptibility of the group and parent company’s financial statements to material misstatement
and how fraud might occur, including through discussions with the directors, discussions within our audit team
planning meeting, updating our record of internal controls and ensuring these controls operated as intended. We
evaluated possible incentives and opportunities for fraudulent manipulation of the financial statements. We
identified laws and regulations that are of significance in the context of the group and parent company by
discussions with directors, communication with component auditors and by updating our understanding of the
sector in which the group and parent company operates.
Laws and regulations of direct significance in the context of the group and parent company include The Companies
Act 2006 and UK Tax legislation.
Audit response to risks identified
We considered the extent of compliance with these laws and regulations as part of our audit procedures on the
related financial statement items including a review of group and parent company financial statement disclosures.
We reviewed the parent company's records of breaches of laws and regulations, minutes of meetings and
correspondence with relevant authorities to identify potential material misstatements arising. We discussed the
parent company's policies and procedures for compliance with laws and regulations with members of ©
management responsible for compliance.
During the planning meeting with the audit team, the engagement partner drew attention to the key areas which
might involve non-compliance with laws and regulations or fraud. We enquired of management whether they were
aware of any instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations or knowledge of any actual, suspected or
alleged fraud. We addressed the risk of fraud through management override of controls by testing the
appropriateness of journal entries and identifying any significant transactions that were unusual or outside the
normal course of business. We assessed whether judgements made in making accounting estimates gave rise to a
possible indication of management bias. At the completion stage of the audit, the engagement partner’s review
included ensuring that the team had approached their work with appropriate professional scepticism and thus the
capacity to identify non-compliance with laws and regulations and fraud.
As group auditors, our assessment of matters relating to non-compliance with laws or regulations and fraud
differed at group and component level according to their particular circumstances. Our communications with the
component auditors included a request to identify instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations and
fraud that could give rise to a material misstatement of the group financial statements in addition to our risk
assessment. :
There are inherent limitations in the audit procedures described above and the further removed non-compliance
with laws and regulations is from the events and transactions reflected in the financial statements, the less likely
we would become aware of it. Also, the risk of not detecting a material misstatement due to fraud is higher than
the risk of not detecting one resulting from error, as fraud may involve deliberate concealment by, for example,
forgery or intentional misrepresentations, or through collusion.
