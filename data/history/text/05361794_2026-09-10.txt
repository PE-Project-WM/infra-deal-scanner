Strategic Report for the Year Ended 31 December 2025
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025. 
Principal activity
The principal activity of the company is that of the wholesale distribution of electronic parts and equipment.
Objectives and strategies to achieve
At Purdicom, our mission is to deliver innovative wireless, cloud and security solutions that help our customers design, deploy and operate reliable communications infrastructure. We remain focused on providing technical expertise, responsive service and long-term value to our customers, whilst maintaining strong and sustainable relationships with our vendor partners.
Our strategy continues to be centred on profitable growth through a combination of portfolio expansion, market development and investment in technical capability. We remain committed to making Purdicom easy to do business with and to providing customers with a level of support that differentiates us within the market.
During 2025, we continued to strengthen our presence across the UK and Europe, supporting a broader customer base and expanding our engagement across multiple technology sectors. The Company remains focused on achieving sustainable growth whilst preserving the financial discipline that has been a key contributor to our long-term success.
Progress during the year has moved the business materially closer to its strategic objective of achieving annual revenues in excess of £50 million. Whilst market conditions remain competitive, the Board remains confident that the Company’s diversified customer base, broad product portfolio and strong financial position provide a solid platform for future growth.
Business model
Purdicom operates specialist sales and technical teams focused on wireless networking, security, cloud and associated infrastructure technologies. Our teams support a broad range of customers, including telecommunications operators, internet service providers, managed service providers, systems integrators and value-added resellers.
The Company’s business model combines distribution expertise with technical consultancy, enabling customers to access both products and specialist knowledge throughout the lifecycle of their projects. This approach has continued to strengthen customer relationships and has supported growth across both existing and new markets.
Professional services remain an important component of our offering, providing customers with additional design, deployment and technical support capabilities where required. This strengthens our position as a trusted technology partner rather than solely a product supplier.

Trends
The technology sector continued to experience rapid change throughout 2025, driven by increasing demand for digital infrastructure, enhanced cyber security and the growing adoption of artificial intelligence technologies.
Whilst the broader economic environment remained characterised by cautious business spending in certain sectors, organisations continued to invest in secure, high-performance connectivity and cloud-enabled solutions that support operational efficiency and digital transformation initiatives.
Purdicom remains well positioned to benefit from these long-term market trends through its established vendor relationships, technical expertise and diversified solution portfolio. The Company continues to assess emerging technologies carefully and align its offering with evolving customer requirements whilst maintaining a disciplined commercial approach.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Macroeconomic Risk: Inflation, Interest Rates & Recession
Although inflationary pressures moderated during 2025 compared with recent years, economic uncertainty, interest rates and business confidence continued to influence customer purchasing decisions. The Company mitigates these risks through diversification of customers, technologies and geographic markets.
Political and Regulatory Risk
Changes in UK and European regulatory, tax and trade policies may impact elements of the markets in which the Company operates. Purdicom continues to monitor developments and adapt accordingly where necessary.
Geopolitical Risk
Ongoing geopolitical tensions continue to have the potential to affect global supply chains, logistics and component availability. The Company maintains close relationships with suppliers and actively manages sourcing strategies to minimise disruption.
Exchange Rate Risk
As an international business with significant foreign currency purchasing activity, Purdicom remains exposed to movements in exchange rates. The Company continues to utilise appropriate hedging arrangements and monitors currency exposure closely to protect profitability.
Credit Risk
Maintaining strong credit management remains a priority. The Company continues to utilise credit insurance and external credit assessment agencies alongside internal monitoring procedures to manage customer credit exposure effectively.
Technology and Market Risk
Technology markets continue to evolve rapidly. Purdicom mitigates this risk through ongoing investment in technical expertise, vendor relationships and market-led product development to ensure its solutions remain aligned with customer requirements.
Development and performance

Sales Performance Overview
2025 represented a year of strong recovery and growth for Purdicom. Turnover increased to £45.4 million from £38.4 million in the prior year, representing growth of approximately 18%. Gross profit increased to £7.7 million, whilst operating profit improved to £1.9 million. Profit after tax increased to £1.45 million from £1.25 million in 2024. These results reflect improved trading conditions, strong execution across the business and continued demand for the technologies and services that we supply.
Growth was achieved while maintaining the disciplined operational approach established during the previous year. The Company continued to focus on profitable opportunities, customer retention and operational efficiency, resulting in improved profitability and a stronger overall financial position.
Operational Efficiency & Cost Management
Throughout the year, the Company maintained focus on operational efficiency whilst selectively investing in areas that support future growth. Administrative costs increased in line with business activity; however, profitability improved as revenue growth outpaced the increase in operating expenses.
The Company also invested in fixed assets and infrastructure during the year to support future operational requirements and maintain service quality for customers and vendor partners. Tangible fixed assets increased from £0.16 million to £0.33 million by year end.
Balance Sheet
=== page 7 ===
Purdicom’s balance sheet remains strong and continues to provide a solid foundation for future growth. Shareholders’ funds increased to £14.8 million at 31 December 2025, compared with £13.7 million at the end of the previous year. Cash balances remained robust at £3.8 million despite increased investment in working capital and fixed assets.
As business activity expanded during the year, stock levels increased to £8.5 million and trade debtors increased to £7.0 million. These increases reflect higher trading volumes and continued support for customer demand. The Company continues to maintain prudent working capital controls and active management of customer credit exposure.
The Board believes that the Company’s strong capital base, healthy liquidity position and long-standing relationships with suppliers, customers and finance providers leave it well positioned to capitalise on future opportunities whilst remaining resilient to potential market challenges.

Accounting policies
Summary of significant accounting policies and key accounting estimates
 The principal accounting policies applied in the preparation of these financial statements are set out below. These policies have been consistently applied to all the years presented, unless otherwise stated.
Statement of compliance
These financial statements were prepared in accordance with Financial Reporting Standard 102 'The Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the United Kingdom and Republic of Ireland and the Companies Act 2006'.
Basis of preparation
These financial statements have been prepared using the historical cost convention except that as disclosed in the accounting policies certain items are shown at fair value.
Prior period adjustments
The comparative figures have been restated to recognise the Company's investment in Selcoms Limited, which was omitted from financial statements as at 31 December 2024. As a result, fixed asset investments and other creditors as at 31 December 2024 have each increased by £1. There is no impact on the reported profit or cash flows for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Revenue recognition
Turnover comprises the fair value of the consideration received or receivable for the sale of goods and provision of services in the ordinary course of the company’s activities. Turnover is shown net of sales/value added tax, returns, rebates and discounts.
Foreign currency transactions and balances
Transactions in foreign currencies are initially recorded at an exchange rate which is updated periodically (but not daily) throughout the year. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are retranslated into the respective functional currency of the entity at the rates prevailing on the reporting period date. Non-monetary items carried at fair value that are denominated in foreign currencies are retranslated at the rate on the date when the fair value is re-measured.
 Non-monetary items measured in terms of historical cost in a foreign currency are not retranslated.
Tax
The tax expense for the period comprises current tax payable and deferred tax.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date in the countries where the company operates and generates taxable income.

Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences between taxable profits and profits reported in the financial statements.
 Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised when it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
 Deferred tax is measured using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.

Tangible assets are stated in the balance sheet at cost, less any subsequent accumulated depreciation and subsequent accumulated impairment losses.

Trade debtors
Trade debtors are amounts due from customers for merchandise sold or services performed in the ordinary course of business.
Stocks
=== page 15 ===
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell. Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out (FIFO) method.
 The cost of finished goods and work in progress comprises direct materials and, where applicable, direct labour costs and those overheads that have been incurred in bringing the inventories to their present location and condition. At each reporting date, stocks are assessed for impairment. If stocks are impaired, the carrying amount is reduced to its selling price less costs to complete and sell; the impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss.
Trade creditors
Trade creditors are obligations to pay for goods or services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of business from suppliers.
Provisions
Provisions are recognised when the company has an obligation at the reporting date as a result of a past event, it is probable that the company will be required to settle that obligation and a reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation.
Share capital
Ordinary shares are classified as equity. Equity instruments are measured at the fair value of the cash or other resources received or receivable, net of the direct costs of issuing the equity instruments. If payment is deferred and the time value of money is material, the initial measurement is on a present value basis.
Dividends
Dividend distribution to the company’s shareholders is recognised as a liability in the financial statements in the reporting period in which the dividends are declared.
Defined contribution pension obligation
A defined contribution plan is a pension plan under which fixed contributions are paid into a pension fund and the company has no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions even if the fund does not hold sufficient assets to pay all employees the benefits relating to employee service in the current and prior periods.
 Contributions to defined contribution plans are recognised as employee benefit expense when they are due. If contribution payments exceed the contribution due for service, the excess is recognised as a prepayment.
