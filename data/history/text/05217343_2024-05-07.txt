Introduction

. North PB Limited (the 'Company’) is a member of the North Group (the 'Group'). North PB Limited is a subsidiary
of North PB Group Limited, with Perth Topco Limited as the Ultimate Parent Company.
The Company's principal activity is to design, deliver, and maintain integrated networking and ‘smart place’
solutions; and provides customers with the ability to partner with a single provider that has expertise relating to
physical infrastructure, networking & connectivity and deployment of !oT, safety & security sensors and
applications.
Whilst North Group reported record revenues it also experienced margin pressure, particularly in the first half of
the financial year 2022-2023, in large part relating to inflationary cost pressures and a tight labour market.
Improvement plans were implemented from the second half of financial year 2022-2023 which have successfully
improved margins, operating profitability and cash generation. .
The Group currently employs over 300 staff with multiple offices across the UK.
The Company & Group is focused on developing multi-year relationships with organisations across the UK and
on further developing capabilities to provide end-to-end networking and ‘smart place’ solutions.
Business review
The North Group & the Company has a long-standing strategy to become a leading force in the integrated
networking and smart places market. This is captured by “NORTH Stronger Networks, Smarter Places” which
was trademarked in March 2024.
North delivers end-to-end design, built off an intimate understanding of customers' business goals and of the
current technologies being deployed; this is then followed by a pragmatic integration of multiple technologies and
data feeds working with customers to ensure that the expected benefits are realised.
North PB Limited is well positioned to succeed in this market due to its breadth of expertise and experience

. including the integration of high quality infrastructure installation within a networking & connectivity solution
together with the deployment of software: it is this combination that makes places ‘smart’.
Development and performance
Employee engagement
North PB Limited's key asset is its people, and their development is critical to our success. North PB Limited
conducts employee technical, Health & Safety and personal development training based on a robust
performance appraisal system. As an independent assessment of its performance with its people, North utilises
Investors in People to conduct regular surveys and audits. North PB Limited has retained its silver Investors in
People accreditation throughout the year.
The company places great value on employee engagement and keeps them regularly informed on company
developments through a comprehensive two-way communications plan, encompassing all company emails,
virtual and face-to-face meetings, as well as employee forum meetings.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Competition
The Company operates in a competitive marketplace. Its chosen strategy is to work in the value-add sector by
deploying knowledge & experience to integrate technologies to differentiate itself. This differentiation is enhanced
by North’s strong focus on customer engagement and relationships.
Eunding and working capital
North PB Limited has sufficient cash reserves to fund working capital requirements as the business continues to

Management of growth
The ability of the Company to deliver its projected growth requires it to deliver against its strategy. To this effect,
management control systems are in place together with effective planning tools and management information.
Market developments
North PB Limited monitors the developments within its market through its vendor engagements, industry press

Financial risk management objectives & policies

Financial key performance indicators
Contracted recurring revenues and margins grew strongly in the year, with revenues up £11.5m in the current
year. A proportion of this growth was due to customers moving between legal entities within the overall North
Group, as we commence our business restructuring and simplification programme to consolidate the Group’s
businesses into a more efficient operating model.
Administrative expenses also increased as centralised resource moved into the Company as part of this
reorganisation. This was in part offset by other operating income for management recharges. The Company's
operating profit (before non-recurring administrative expenses) was £1.1m for the period, slightly up on the prior
year of £0.5m.
The Company’s non-recurring administrative expenses relate to re-organisation costs to allow for the Group to
‘align and realise future business synergies.
The Company's Statement of Financial Position continued to remain resilient during the reorganisation of the
North Group, with net current assets of £1.3m (2022 - £1.2m), and a positive cash balance of £1.4m (2022 -
£0.5m).
Other key performance indicators
The North Group & the Company have continued to grow its customer base in focused markets allowing
measurements of numbers of customers, average customer spend and contract SLA’s to be tracked. Further
clarity was enabled by the organisational restructure in July 2023 to a market Sector based organisation. To
independently assess its customer satisfaction, the North Group measures its ‘Net Promoter Score’ which is a
management tool that can be used to gauge the loyalty of a firm's customer relationships.
ESG
North PB Limited is committed to working in a sustainable and responsible way throughout our operations and to
promoting social, economic and environmental improvements. This includes mitigating our environmental
impact, ensuring social responsibility, and maintaining strong corporate governance practices.
We choose to work sustainably and responsibly because we care about the communities we work and operate
in. We believe that everyone at North PB Limited has a role to play and have a strategy, framework and policies
that span all dimensions of ESG to ensure it is at the core of business operations and values and that we
educate and support employees in their strategic decisions and daily work and operations.
North PB Limited aligns with both UK and Global sustainability targets. In line with the UK Government's Net
Zero target and the UN Paris Agreement, we understand the urgent need to decarbonise and reduce our
environmental impact across business activities, with the goal of achieving Net Zero.
As a carbon-neutral and sustainably conscious business, we publish an annual ESG Impact and SECR Report
and have an ESG Policy and training in place to demonstrate our commitment to working towards a more
sustainable future.

Economic impact of global events
UK businesses are currently facing many uncertainties such as environmental sustainability and geopolitical
events such as the Russian invasion of Ukraine and the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. These uncertainties
have contributed to an environment where there exist a range of issues and risks, including inflation, changing _
interest rates, labour shortages, disrupted supply chains and new ways of working.
The Directors have carried out an assessment of the potential impact of these uncertainties on the Company,
including the impact of mitigation measures, and have concluded that the greatest impact on the business is
expected to be from the economic ripple effect on the global economy. The Directors have taken account of
these potential impacts in their going concern assessment.
The Company continues to work with its partners to minimize any impacts of these events to maximize the
realization of any opportunities they may provide to the business.

2.4 Foreign currency translation
Functional and presentation currency
The Company's functional and presentational currency is GBP.
Transactions and balances
Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency using the spot exchange
rates at the dates of the transactions.
At each period end foreign currency monetary items are translated using the closing rate. Non-
monetary items measured at historical cost are translated using the exchange rate at the date of the
transaction and non-monetary items measured at fair value are measured using the exchange rate
when fair value was determined.
Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the
translation at period-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign
currencies are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

2.5 Revenue
The revenue is attributable to the principal activities of the Company. The revenue shown in the
Statement of Comprehensive Income represents sales to external customers at invoice amounts
together with the value of work carried out on long term contracts to the reporting date less value
added tax and discounts allowed.
Long term contracts are assessed on a contract by contract basis and are reflected in the Statement
of Comprehensive Income by recording revenue and related costs as the contract progresses.
Where the outcome of each long term contract can be assessed with reasonable certainty before its
conclusion, the profit is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income as the difference
between the attributable turnover and related costs for the contract. Attributable revenue is based on
the state of completion with reference to the fair value of the goods or services provided as a
proportion of the fair value of the contract. Full provision is made for losses on all contracts in the
period in which the loss is first foreseen.
Revenue in respect of maintenance costs is recognised over the period of the contract in equal
monthly instalments.

2.7 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The Company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a

contributions have been paid the Company has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in the Statement of Comprehensive Income when
they fall due. Amounts not paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the Statement of Financial
Position. The assets of the plan are held separately from the Company in independently administered
funds.

2.9 Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting date, the Company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible assets to determine
whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any such
indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of
the impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an
individal asset, the Company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which
the asset belongs.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its
carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its
recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised immediately in the Statement of
Comprehensive Income, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the
impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.

2.10 Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary far it to be capable of
operating in the manner intended by management.
At each reporting date the Company assesses whether there is any indication of impairment. If such
indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is determined which is the higher of its fair :
value less costs to sell and its value in use. An impairment loss is recognised where the carrying
amount exceeds the recoverable amount.
Depreciation is charged so as to allocate the cost of assets less their residual value over their
estimated useful lives, using the straight-line method.
Depreciation is provided on the following basis:

Fixtures & fittings - 10 years
Computer equipment - 5-20 years

The assets’ residual values, useful lives and depreciation methods are reviewed, and adjusted
prospectively if appropriate, or if there is an indication of a significant change since the last reporting
date.
Gains and losses on disposals are determined by comparing the proceeds with the carrying amount
and are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

2.11 Stocks
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and net realisable value, being the estimated selling price less
costs to complete and sell. Cost is based on the cost of purchase on a first in, first out basis.
At each reporting date, stocks are assessed for impairment. If stock is impaired, the carrying amount
is reduced to its selling price less costs to complete and sell. The impairment loss is recognised
immediately in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

2.15 Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the Company a legal or constructive
obligation that probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, and a reliable estimate
can be made of the amount of the obligation.
Provisions are charged as an expense to the Statement of Comprehensive Income in the year that
the Company becomes aware of the obligation, and are measured at the best estimate at the
reporting date of the expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account relevant risks
and uncertainties.
When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the Statement of
Financial Position.
