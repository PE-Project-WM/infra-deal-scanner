Strategic Report
Introduction
Gigaclear Limited ("Gigaclear") has been designing, building, and operating new gigabit, full fibre networks since 2011
during which time it has firmly established itself as the largest rural, full fibre alternative network (altnet) in the UK.
With its headquarters in Abingdon, Oxfordshire the company employs ¢.600 people, serving more than 160,000 customers
across 26 counties. Gigaclear's vision is to be the best rural fibre provider in England, using its network to generate economic
and social value for the communities and customers it serves.
Business Review
In February 2024, Gigaclear marked the departure of Gareth Williams as CEO and lan Wade as CFO and welcomed Nathan
Rundle as its new CEO. Having been Gigaclear’s delivery director and then COO for some five years, Nathan came to the
role with a deep understanding of the business and the market. In November 2024, Hugo Eales was appointed as Gigaclear's
CFO, bringing a wealth of experience from the telecoms sector and beyond.
The change in leadership has provided an opportunity to look at how the different functions of Gigaclear are led and
managed, an exercise which will continue into 2026 and onwards. This will drive a more customer focused company which
will help to drive cost efficiencies. It also accelerated the examination of Gigaclear's wider strategy, in the context of a
market where priorities were shifting from large build volumes to an increased emphasis on revenue generated by
connecting customers.
In response to this, Gigaclear has shifted its own resources to focus its build expertise on its area of strength, operating in the
most rural and hard to connect areas of the country, whilst ramping up all aspects of its order-to-cash process. The results
show the success of that strategy, with customer additions increasing by 45%.
The capital market in the altnet sectors has proven to be tightening and challenging. To combat the challenges ahead,
Gigaclear has continued its attention to delivering to the most rural communities and slowdown its capital expenditure
investment in network infrastructure in 2025 and beyond. Consequently, the Group has downsized its build team as a cost
saving measure. These have helped to preserve cash and relieve liquidity situations.
_ Despite these headwinds, the Company entered into productive dialogue with its lenders, who remained supportive of the

: business throughout the process, to look for a longer-term solution to the Company's liquidity. In this regard, a recapitalisation
transaction is agreed with the lenders, which culminated in the entry into the Lock-up Agreement ("LUA") in November 2025.
The LUA provides a dual-track framework to allow for M&A processes or lender-led transactions.
As part of the dialogue with the Company's lenders, Gigaclear secured a £80,000,000 Super Senior Facility, with a further
£20,000,000 of uncommitted funding available under the Super Senior Facility in December 2025. This enabled Gigaclear to
secure adequate funding requirements and to be fully funded in the first quarter of 2027.
In January 2026, the lenders agreed not to pursue an M&A process at this stage. The lenders will instead be taking control of
the business in 2026 through an agreed consensual lender led transaction. This involves a substantial deleveraging and
reduces the debt service requirement.
Given the challenges ahead in the wider FITP market, the Company is required to perform an impairment assessment review
under IAS 36 and concluded an impairment of £110.2m on the property, plant and equipment (refer fo note 14 for details).
This does not have any impact to the operation of the Company as Gigaclear has already secure fundings necessary and
to be fully funded.
Financial Review
2024 saw Gigaclear's revenues increase from £33.8m to £47.2m, an increase of 39%. This was driven by the continued growth
in customers numbers joining our network throughout the year.
The loss for the year, excluding fair value change of interest rate swaps, widened to £242.6m from £114.5m primarily due to
an impairment of property, plant and equipment amounting to £110.2m (refer to note 14 for details), higher finance costs
contributed by an increase in net debt and more deprecation from the expanding network investment. This was offset by
improvement in revenue from increased customer numbers together with better network cost efficiency. The loss for the year
narrowed to £217.8m from £138.3m resulting from fair value gain of interest rate swaps as compared to a loss in 2023.

Engaging with Suppliers
Gigaclear manages a significant amount of procurement across all parts of its business and seeks to maintain high standards
in its dealings with suppliers. The establishment of a professional procurement function in 2024 supports the Group's
engagement with its supply chain and seeks to drive efficiency, cost reduction and reliability as well as offering suppliers a
consistent and well managed service with which to do business. 2024 has seen a reduction in the volume of network build
when compared to the previous year. Those suppliers engaged in new network build have been required to ramp down
delivery levels, with management seeking to achieve this in a collaborative way which supports the supply chain in managing
its forward planning. In contrast, suppliers engaged in customer installations have been required to increase performance in
volume terms, to support the significant increase in customer numbers. Improvements in efficiency have been a key
requirement through the year to drive improvements in the time between a customer placing an order for service and being
installed and activated.
Engaging with Employees
Gigaclear employed 697 people at the end of 2024, which represented a 16% reduction in overall headcount, primarily
affecting the teams delivering new network build, as the customer facing teams continued to grow. This change reflects the
strategic decision to focus on delivering more customer connections and ‘ultra-rural' builds. There was a significant exercise
in consultation with a large number of potentially affected employees. Management focused on conducting this difficult
exercise with sensitivity, with employee welfare a key consideration and succeeded in achieving the necessary reduction in
the workforce with the majority accepting voluntary redundancy.
Engagement with employees continues to be a key focus for management, with 2024 targeting individual learning and
development goals. 80% of employees have engaged positively with this initiative. The human resources team have
continued their work on healthy workplaces and developing a good culture and have looked at more ways fo engage with
those teams who have previously had lower levels of participation.
Engaging with Customers and Communities
Early in 2024, Gigaclear was able to mark a significant milestone, connecting its 100,000th customer. Gigaclear realised its
ambitious plans to increase its revenue and penetration in 2024, and finished the year with over 130,000 customers. This 45%
growth in customers resulted from strong ongoing sales and marketing execution of ‘rural champion’ retail brand as well as
a step up in installation performance.

. Improvements in the customer installation journey were borne out of clear feedback from customers giving a renewed focus
for both internal and external resources on achieving successful and efficient installations. In addition the business launched
One Touch Switching as part of an industry wide initiative to make it easier for customers to switch between providers.

2024 also saw significant improvements in the service provided to existing customers. Customer service has extended its
opening hours, invested in dedicated teams for technical support and business customers, and focused on ‘first contact
resolution’ to get it right first time for customers. Customers are kept informed with a customer magazine and have the
opportunity to give feedback through regular customer surveys. Gigaclear’s retail loyalty team has a successful record of
customer retention and overall, Gigaclear's scoring on Trustpilot reached an all-time high of 4.7 during 2024 and its customer
Net Promoter Score improved to a healthy +28.
Environment Social and Governance
The safety of workers, employees and the environment is a priority in all Gigaclear's activities and Gigaclear takes its
responsibilities to the environment and the wider community seriously. Gigaclear has clear policies on reducing the impact
of its activities on the environment and has implemented a carbon reduction plan, including comprehensive rules for its supply
chain to follow, enforced by regular and rigorous inspection. 2024 saw a trial of electric plant on site, further progress on
reporting of Scope 3 emissions and significant progress in waste management across all Gigaclear's activities.
Gigaclear also seeks to have a positive social and economic impact on the communities it serves, believing that its desire to
bridge the digital divide in rural areas has an inherent benefit for the homes and businesses which might otherwise suffer from
being left behind. This is enhanced with specific initiatives within communities such as its successful sponsorship programme
for rural sports teams, community engagement events and free connections for local community buildings. Gigaclear also
participates in the government apprenticeship scheme with 19 apprentices engaged in 9 separate schemes across the
business in 2024. The business also engages with a local school to support its careers programme to encourage new entrants
into the industry.

Opinion
We have audited the financial statements of Gigaclear Limited (the ‘company’) for the year ended 31 December 2024, which
comprise the statement of comprehensive income, the statement of financial position, the statement of cash flows, the
statement of changes in equity, and notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policy information. The
financial reporting framework that has been applied in their preparation is applicable law and UK-adopted international
accounting standards.
In our opinion:

e the financial statements give a true and fair view of the state of the company's affairs as at 31 December 2024 and of its

e the financial statements have been properly prepared in accordance with UK-adopted international accounting

Basis for opinion
We conducted our audit in accordance with International Standards on Auditing (UK) (ISAs (UK)] and applicable law. Our
responsibilities under those standards are further described in the ‘Auditor's responsibilities for the audit of the financial
statements’ section of our report. We are independent of the company in accordance with the ethical requirements that are
relevant to our audit of the financial statements in the UK, including the FRC’s Ethical Standard, and we have fulfilled our other
ethical responsibilities in accordance with these requirements. We believe that the audit evidence we have obtained is
sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our opinion.
Material uncertainty related to going concern
We draw attention to note 2 in the financial statements, which indicates that, following agreement by investors to pursue a
lender led transaction, that there is an inherent material uncertainty following the imminent change in ownership of the
Company regarding the Board composition and the plan of the lenders. As stated in note 2, these events of conditions
indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt over the company's ability to continue as a going
concer. Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter.
In auditing the financial statements, we have concluded that the directors’ use of the going concern basis of accounting in
the preparation of the financial statements is appropriate.
Our responsibilities

2 Going concern
The ability of the Company to continue as a going concern is dependent on the ability of the Company to generate
sufficient cash flows, and have the necessary funds, to meet the requirements of the Company's operations to settle
its liabilities as and when they fall due. To assess the Company's status as a going concern, the Directors assessed the
Company’s ability to fund its business for 12 months from the signing of these accounts. As part of this process, the
Directors considered the Company's key factors relevant to the Company's viability as a going concern and prepared
acash flow forecast for the review period.
The Company's primary operational focus has been in providing broadband services to existing customers,
onboarding new customers and investing in its network infrastructure. These rely on its ability to raise its funds through
debt and equity to satisfy its cash requirements. As such, the Company would require additional funding before it
becomes cash generative.
The Company has been working on financing its cashflow in the first three quarters of 2025, including crystalising interest
rate swaps, delaying interest payments by entering into the AD, etc (refer to note 32 for details}. To provide a longer-
term solution to the Company's liquidity position, the Company enters into productive dialogue with its lenders, who
are supportive of the business throughout the process, in relation to a recapitalisation transaction, which culminated
in the entry into the Lock-up Agreement ("LUA") being executed on 5 November 2025, between the Company and
all of the lenders.
As part of the LUA framework, lenders and the Company entered into a Super Senior Facility Agreement (“SSFA") on 2
December 2025, which provided an £80,000,000 facility to the business, with a further £20,000,000 of uncommitted
funding available under the facility. This has been implemented by an Amendment and Restatement Deed (“ARD")
on 2 December 2025 to the existing debt facility signed on December 2023, establishing the SSFA and amending
relevant financial covenants.
In mid-January 2026, the lenders determined that the M&A processes would not be pursued and instead a lender-led
solution would be implemented. The lender-led recapitalisation transaction involves a substantial deleveraging of the
business to facilitate its transition to lender ownership, and the SSFA remains available. This will improve the debt-to-
equity ratios of the Company and reduces the debt service requirement. Whilst the terms of the transaction are agreed
as between the lenders, the existing shareholder and the Company, the recapitalisation transaction is expected to
become effective once certain regulatory processes have completed, in first half of 2026.
The Directors acknowledge that is an inherent material uncertainty following the imminent change in ownership of the
Company, in particular regarding any future plans the lenders (as new owners) may have. However, given the
productive dialogue that the Directors have with the lenders, the Directors are confident that the Company has the
full support of the lenders to continue with its current business plan.
Management have produced detailed cash flow forecasts under the lender-led transaction, which project that with
the £80,000,000 available under the SSFA, the Company has adequate cashflow up to the first quarter of 2027 and by
then the business will be cash generative. The base case scenanio is consistent with the historical performance on key
factors, such as customer growth, penetration rates, and ARPU.
The key financial covenant under the amended facilities is the minimum liquidity requirement. This requires the
Company to maintain not less than £10,000,000 of cash and available facilities under the SSFA at the applicable test
date. The finance documents also include interest cover ratio, leverage ratio and “premises connected” financial
covenants, but these ratios will not be tested until 30 June 2028. With that in mind, sensitivity analysis has been
performed on ARPU change by 10%. The results demonstrate it would fall below the £10,000,000 minimum liquidity
threshold required. This risk could be mitigated via liaising with the lenders to further drawdown the £20,000,000 of
uncommitted funding available under the SSFA and noting that the Company will be in lender ownership following
completion of lender-led transaction, which links to the inherent material uncertainty mentioned above.
The Directors are satisfied that, taking into consideration the measures and development above, there is a reasonable
expectation that the Company would continue to operate as a going concern, for a minimum of 12 months from the
date of signing of these accounts.

leases
The Company considers whether a contract is, or contains a lease, at inception of the contract. A lease is defined as
a ‘contract, or part of a contract, which conveys the right to use an asset (the underlying asset) for a period of time
in exchange for consideration’. To apply this definition the Company assesses whether the contract meets three key
evaluations which are whether:
« the contract contains an identified asset, which is either explicitly identified in the contract or implicitly
specified by being identified at the time the asset is made available to the Company;
« the Company has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from use of the identified asset
throughout the period of use, considering its rights within the defined scope of the contract:
« the Company has the right to direct the use of the identified asset throughout the period of use.
Measurement and recognition of leases as a lessee
At lease commencement date, the Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability on the balance
sheet. The right-of-use asset is measured at cost, which is made up of the initial measurement of the lease liability, any
initial direct costs incurred by the Company, an estimate of any costs to dismantle and remove the asset at the end
of the lease, and any lease payments made in advance of the lease commencement date (net of any incentives
received).
The Company depreciates the right-of-use assets on a straight-line basis from the lease commencement date to the
earlier of the end of the useful life of the right-of-use asset or the end of the lease term. If a lease transfers ownership
of the underlying asset or the cost of the right-of-use asset reflects that the Company expects to exercise a purchase
option, the related right-of-use asset is depreciated over the useful life of the underlying asset. The Company also
assesses the right-of-use asset for impairment when such indicators exist.
At the commencement date, the Company measures the lease liability at the present value of the lease payments
unpaid at that date, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease if that rate is readily available or the
Company's incremental borrowing rate.
Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability are made up of fixed payments (including in
substance fixed), variable payments based on an index or rate, amounts expected to be payable under a residual
value guarantee and payments arising from options reasonably certain to be exercised.
After initial measurement, the liability will be reduced for payments made and increased for interest. It is remeasured
to reflect any reassessment or modification, or if there are changes in in-substance fixed payments.
When the lease liability is remeasured, the corresponding adjustment is reflected in the right-of-use asset, or profit and
loss if the right-of-use asset is already reduced to zero.
The Company has elected to account for short-term leases and leases of low-value assets using the practical
expedients. Instead of recognising a right-of-use asset and lease liability, the payments in relation to these are
recognised as an expense in profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

Revenue
Revenue consists of internet connectivity fees, customer connection fees, commission earned on the sale of VOIP
services to customers by an external service provider and other income from the sale of stock and network build
carried out for customers who do not have an ongoing contract with the Company.
To determine whether to recognise revenue, the Company follows a 5-step process:
] Identifying the contract with a customer
2 Identifying the performance obligations
3 Determining the transaction price
4 Allocating the transaction price to the performance obligations
5 Recognising revenue when/as performance obligations(s) are satisfied.
Revenue is recognised either at a point in time or over time. when (or as) the Company satisfies performance
obligations by transferring the promised goods or services to its customers.
The Company recognises contract liabilities for consideration received in respect of unsatisfied performance
obligations and reports these amounts as other liabilities in the statement of financial position (see Note 24). Similarly,
if the Company satisfies a performance obligation before it receives the consideration, the Company recognises
either a contract asset or a receivable in its statement of financial position, depending on whether something other
than the passage of time is required before the consideration is due.
Internet connectivity fees
Revenue from the monthly fees charged to customers for providing access to the internet is recognised in line with the
time period for which the connection is provided. Fees are charged and payable monthly in advance and revenue
for dates beyond the current accounting period is reported in the statement of financial position as a contract liability.
Customer connection fees
Connection fees are one-off fees charged to customers at the outset of the contract to cover set up and installation
services. These fees are included in the client's first invoice and are payable together with the first monthly connectivity
fee. As such, the Company judges that connection services form a part of a bundle of services to deliver internet
connectivity services. The revenue from the connection fees is therefore recognised over the same time period as the
customer's internet connectivity services contract. Revenue which relates to the portion of the customer's contract
which is beyond the current accounting period is reported in the statement of financial position as a contract liability.
Customer contracts vary in length, the standard residential contract is 18 months, business contract is 24 months, and
wholesale customer contract is 12 months.
VOIP Commission
VOIP services are provided to the Company's customers by an external service provider. The Company receives
commission for each customer who subscribes to a VOIP service. The commission is received monthly in arrears and
is recognised in the period for which the commission is paid.
Other Income
Otherincome relates to sales of stock and network build activity carried out for customers who do not have a contract
with the Company for the provision of ongoing services. The revenue is recognised in the financial statements when
the stock has been transferred or the building work is complete.
