Strategic report
The directors present their strategic report on the Group for the year ended 31 December 2024.
The Group consists of the parent Sonovate International Holdings Limited (‘the Company’) and its wholly owned
subsidiaries Sonovate Limited, Sonovate US LLC, two dormant entities Sonovate Business Services Limited and
Sonovate US Servicing LLC (“the Subsidiaries”) and a Special Purpose Vehicle (“SPV”) set up in the previous year
for funding purposes (see below).
The Group is an innovative financial services technology company that supports recruitment agencies and HR
Technology businesses in the contingent labour market. Since 2015, it is providing highly valued financial products
and user experience to its clients.
Review of the business
The audited consolidated financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2024 is set out on pages 14 to 34. The
Group continued to evolve and grow its business in the UK. The Operating loss for the financial year to 31 December
2024 was £7,879, 138 (2023: loss of £11,892,791), The statutory loss after tax for the year was £10,108,146 (2023:
loss £14,713,188). These losses are a result of continued investment into software development and personnel. At the
balance sheet date, the Group had total net liabilities of £7,691,552 (2023: net liabilities £2, 583,792) as a result of the
losses during the year.
Over the course of the year ended 31 December 2024, the Group processed and funded £1,258,841,922 of invoices
(2023: £1, 192,123,731) an increase of 5.6% (2023: 8.0%) over the previous year, Cumulatively to 31 December 2024
the Group has provided over £6bn of funding to businesses.
Trade debtors, which include outstanding amounts on invoices funded, increased from £141,655,835 as at 31
December 2023 to £151,670,161 as at 31 December 2024 representing an increase of 7.1% (2023: decrease 8.1%).
Consolidated revenue which includes other & interest income was £23,603,600 in the year to 31 December 2023 in
comparison to £25,216,079 for the year to 31 December 2024 representing an increase of 6.8% (2023: 32.6%). The
continued growth in revenue is due to significant investment within sales, marketing, and tech and a fee review for
existing clients and subscriptions fees following the new platform going live during the year.
The Group continues to invest considerable resources into the development of software which enables
recruitment agencies and other providers of contractors and contingent workers to process contracts, invoices,
payments, and the provision of working capital funding with the objective of becoming the supplier of choice
for such services. Its technology allows it to offer its services via an API in a “Lending as a Service” type
product. During the year, the Group released a new enhanced software platform and will slowly phase out the
old platforms. This new platform was essential to handle the Group’s projected growth and to provide existing
and new customers an improved experience and allow the group to open up new revenue streams. The Group
provides significant liquidity into UK small businesses and are an enabler of UK small business growth.
Funding and liquidity
In June 2022, the Group had made a significant investment to expand the sources of funding by refinancing existing
facilities with a private securitisation facility and setting up a “bankruptcy-remote” Irish special purpose vehicle
(“SPV”), Sonovate Trade Receivables Designated Activity Company. This is a single purpose entity established for
the securitisation facility and is considered “bankruptcy-remote” as its sole purpose is to administer the securitisation
of the company’s receivables and is prohibited from incurring other debt or obligations. The Company’s subsidiary,
Sonovate Limited, now securitises certain receivables assets via the sale of these assets to the SPV, which in turn
issues asset-backed securities to investors. During the year, the Group agreed a loan syndication for the senior loan
facility. An additional loan provider was required to support the company’s growing volumes.
In support of this initiative, a state-of-the-art data warehouse was built to enable the Group to fund thousands of
invoices on a daily basis. This effort is already yielding fruit as it has significantly reduced the blended cost of funds
as well as increased the advance rates (i.e. loan to value) that allows Sonovate Limited to scale up its own operations.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Group is subject to principal risks and uncertainties as detailed below:

Continued access to funding and liquidity

In order to lend into UK small business, the Company requires access to a committed funding line either through
. equity or debt capital. This is managed by the group and as detailed above the Senior Financing Facility, the private

securitisation facility of £150m of senior notes held by a large European Bank, a £40m mezzanine notes facility issued

to a UK asset manager which are both now held in a special purpose vehicle ‘Sonovate Trade Receivables Designated

Activity Company’, and the £30m revolving loan facility were all extended subsequent to the year end.

Credit risk

The Group is exposed to the risk that debtors do not settle debts in a complete and/or timely manner. The Group has

in place credit risk models to ensure that lending levels are appropriate for the credit risk profile of the debtor. The

vast majority of funding is credit insured which significantly mitigates the risk of non-collection. This risk is

monitored closely in terms of the delinquency of the invoices funded and the proportion of debt that is uninsured. The

Group has recorded a bad debt charge in the period ended 31 December 2024 of £1,363,077 (2023: £547,385) against

debtors that are unlikely to be recoverable. £1,755,511 of unrecoverable debts have been written off during the year;

for debts that have not been written off at 31 December 2024 attempts are continuing to recover the sums due.

Financial Crime risk .

The Group is exposed to a number of financial crime risks, with the most significant exposure to fraud risk. The most

likely fraud risk to the Group is the financing of fake invoices. The Group mitigates this risk through a proprietary

software which uses a combination of public and non-public data sources to assess the potential risk for fraudulent

invoices on a continuous basis.

: Operational risk

Operational Risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems or from

external events. As a result of these risks, the Group may have a negative impact to reputation with its stakeholders,

face fines or damages and loss of future business. The Group mitigates this risk through regular internal review

processes and the maintenance of an internal risk register.

Competition

The provision of finance into the temporary worker sector is a competitive marketplace. While the Group remains

significantly ahead in terms of its technology, the entrance of multiple and / or well backed competitors with

significant financial resources and a focus on technology could result in more competitive pricing and / or a reduced

rate of market share capture. The market is monitored regularly.

Currency risk ;
The Group transacts in USD, EUR, CHF, CAD, DKK, SEK, NOK and AUD although the vast majority of business
is in GBP. In cases where the Group funds customers in GBP, USD or EUR (Matched Currencies), the £200m private
warehouse allows it to draw funds in matching currency which mitigates any potential currency risk. For the Non-
matched Currencies, the Group draws against the SPV’s GBP note and deals with the currency exposure by reserving
a generous amount in cash to mitigate this risk. However, with the debt turn standing at 40 days, the likely deviation
in currency pairs in that term is rather small.

Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that is it probable that they will
be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.

2.4 ° Share based payments
Equity-settled arrangements are measured at fair value (excluding the effect of non— market based vesting conditions)
at the date of the grant. The fair value is expensed on a straight-line basis over the vesting period. The amount
recognised as an expense is adjusted to reflect the actual number of shares or options that will vest. Where equity-
settled arrangements are modified, and are of benefit to the employee, the incremental fair value is recognised over the
period from the date of modification to date of vesting. Where a modification is not beneficial to the employee there is
no change to the charge for share-based payment. Settlements and cancellations are treated as an acceleration of vesting
and the unvested amount is recognised immediately in the profit and loss. The group has no cash-settled arrangements.
