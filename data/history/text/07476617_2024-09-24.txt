Gigaclear has been designing, building, and operating new gigabit, full fibre networks since 2011 during which time it has
firmly established itself as the largest rural, full fibre alternative network (altnet} in the UK.
With its headquarters in Abingdon, the company employs ¢.800 people from eight office locations, serving more than

Strategic Report
CEO review
Such has been Gigaclear's growth during the past 12 months that by the end of the current financial year, to December
2023, it had propelled itself to within touching distance of several significant milestones. Having reached ‘500,000 premises
passed’ during 2023, we achieved our stated aim of having 500,000 properties Ready for Service (RFS) and welcoming our
100,000th customer in Q1 2024 - both of which point to very positive signs of progress.
2023 saw 160,000 properties being RFS'd taking the total RFS number to 480,000 compared to 98,000 in 2022, an increase of
63%. This was achieved against a slightly lower Cost Per Premises Passed (CPPP) figure. As always, ensuring we build at the
right cost is key to securing the company's long-term future. The past 12 months also saw announcements that we had
extended our full fibre network into rural East Sussex, Norfolk and Yorkshire, bringing to 26 the number of counties that can
now offer a Gigaclear service.
Our customer base grew by 24,000 new customers, a 41% increase on the 17,000 in 2022. By year end, the total number of
Gigaclear customers stood just over 92,000. During the same period, our Trustpilot score increased from 3.8 to 4.5 ‘Excellent’,
a positive reflection of the emphasis we put on delivering customer satisfaction.
2023 saw us secure our first two contracts under the Government's flagship £5 billion Project Gigabit programme, set up to
enable hard-to-reach communities’ access gigabit-capable broadband. By year-end, we learned we had also won a third
contract in Gloucestershire, with £16.5m in subsidy to connect c.5000 hard-to-reach rural premises in the east of the county.
Furthermore, 2023 saw a 28% increase in the revenue received under the UK Gigabit Voucher (UKGV) scheme. The scheme
enables us to assess the practicalities of infilling at a more commercially viable manner and we shall continue to monitor
the availability of eligible properties that fall within its scope throughout 2024 and beyond.
Momentum that had been building up throughout the year resulted in several other records being broken in November.
These included: most gross orders taken in a month, the highest number of hard installations completed in a day and the
highest monthly revenue achieved. Gigaclear continues to add muscle to support its ambitious targets.
Confidence in Gigaclear's progress - as well as a recognition of its unique position in the rural roll-out landscape - was
underlined during the year by both new and existing shareholders as well as by the market. In June, against the backdrop
of a squeeze in industry funding, we secured up to £420m in equity from Equitix and, in December, we announced we had
raised a new £1.5bn debt facility.
These investments will not only help drive forward the latest leg of our full fibre journey - to provide brilliant broadband to
more than one million premises by 2027 - but puts us in a robust position as the sector continues to consolidate.

Policy and Regulatory developments
The UK Government is still targeting 85% of UK households having access to a gigabit-capable broadband network by
2025. To help achieve this, the government and Ofcom have implemented and are continuing to develop policy measures
designed to support the rollout of full fiore networks:

¢ The Department of Science Innovation and Technology (DSIT) launched two consultations late in 2023 to solicit input
on the policy underpinning the Broadband Universal Service Obligation (USO) and on improving broadband for Very
Hard to Reach premises (VHTR) to understand how industry might be able to work with DSIT and Ofcom to participate
in and support these objectives. These challenges apply within Gigaclear’s current and planned FTTP footprint.

« The Deparment of Science Innovation and Technology (DSIT) and Department for Transport (DfT) officials are just
starting trials, working with industry and local highways bodies for a more flexible highways permitting system. Once
implemented, this could significantly reduce our administrative burden in this area.

« Progress has been made towards the amended General Conditions for communications providers to develop and
operate a new “one touch” switching process for all UK residential customers who switch landline and broadband
services, removing the need for customers to contact their current provider when looking to switch to a new provider.
Gigaclear has completed interoperability testing with a major ISP and awaits the launch of the new process.

* The PSTN switch off driven by Openreach continues to offer an opportunity for Gigaclear with its retail digital voice
offering to support rural customers transitioning away from the PSTN network.

¢ Ofcom launched a consultation on their proposed Plan of Work for 2045/25 focussing on 4 priority outcomes with the
final plan to be published in March 2024. The priority, ‘Internet we can rely on’, is a key capability of Gigaclear and
as such we will continue to engage with Ofcom's programme of works to deliver this outcome in the year ahead, with
particular focus on the following projects:

o Beginning the Wholesale Fixed Telecoms Market Review, and the Wholesale Voice Markets Review to define the

regulatory framework from April 2026 through to March 2031

o Promoting network security and resilience, notably under the Telecoms Security Act.

o Managing the migration from legacy services and old technologies.

o Monitoring consumer protection rules, protecting vulnerable consumers and monitoring the affordability of

telecoms services.
Gigaclear continues to closely monitor and carefully implement all new regulations. Where appropriate, Gigaclear engages
proactively in consultation with government, the regulator and industry bodies to help shape and develop new policy and
regulation, ensuring that our unique perspective on the challenges of rural deployment is given proper consideration.
Financial Review
Gigaclear finished 2023 securing additional equity and a new £1.5bn debt facility. These two key transactions help to secure
the future of Gigaclear and allows Gigaclear to continue on its path to deliver full fibre broadband to one million rural homes.
2023 saw Gigaclear's revenues increase from £25.7m to £33.8m, an increase of 32%. This was driven by the continued increase
in customers joining our network throughout the year.
The Loss for the year was £138.3m (2022: £21.8m). This year's loss was made worse from a market-to-market loss on our interest
rate swaps of £23.8m (2022 had a £46.8m profit). Without this loss, our loss for the year would have been £11 4.5m, this is in line
with our forecasts. While we continue to focus our efforts on building new network areas, Gigaciear will carry the cost of a
significant network delivery organisation. During this period of highly capital-intensive growth, we expect to realise net
operating losses.

colleagues in our in-house maintenance, installation, and network delivery team (called the Direct Labour (DL) team), and
in our Network Build Team, which designs and manages day-to-day deployment of our networks. We continued to invest in
our IT & Systems department, further enhancing our capabilities in deploying PON (passive optical networks) and PIA (passive
infrastructure access) networks and investing in systems to assist us in detivering our networks more efficiently. 2023 saw a
large investment in our ISP (internet Service Provider) teams enlarging our customer acquisition and customer care
departments. We expec? to see these customer facing departments grow as we seek to expand our customer base.
These various initiatives brought additional costs into the business but are required to allow Gigaclear to scale up its network
construction and customer acquisition activities. Consequently, our operating expenses for the year increased to £91.9m

Engaging with Customers
There was a rapid growth in Gigaclear's customer base in 2023. At year end, Gigaclear had 92,000 customers having grown
33% over the last 12 months from 69k. This growth was driven by heightened customer demand for ultrafast broadband
services, and increasingly effective sales and marketing strategies. We delivered a major marketing refresh in mid-2023
including a new TV advert which accelerated sales in the second half of the year This, plus continuous improvement of sales
channels under-pinned a strong year for sales, with resultant customer install finishing the year with a monthly run rate of over
3000. We are delighted that over 15% of these customers now buy home phone as well as broadband from Gigaclear, via
it’s VoIP telephony serve.
Gigaclear made several investments in customer operations in 2023 to continue to improve its customer experience. The
senior management team of customer operations has been strengthened in the year, including with a new training manager
to develop the quality of customer service agents. In parallel we have continued to develop the Customer Loyalty team to
retain and recontract customers, Gigaclear customer churn remains at industry low levels, with very few leavers outside of
movers out of network areas.
Our customer experience measures have shown the benefit of these investments as evidenced by a Trustpilot score which
went from 3.8 to 4.5 across 2023, and a Google Review score rapidly catching up now at 4.1. This positive sentiment translates
into happy customers with a high propensity to recommend the service to friends and family, which we have harnessed with
arecommend a neighbour scheme in 2023.
Alongside this scale growth of new marketing, enhanced customer service and new products, Gigaclear remains true to its
roots as a rural supplier on a mission to empower communities with better broadband. In 2023 Our Community Engagement
Team launched a local sports club sponsorship initiative to bring the brand and local funding to: over 50 sports clubs in
Gigaclear communities. We are working with ex-England cricketer Darren Gough as an ambassador for this initiative. Similarly,
our local business sates team helped connect over 900 local businesses to better broadband, from independent shops and
restaurants to large public sector and corporate clients. We now have over 3k business clients and see this as an area of
exciting future growth.

Health, Safety, Environment and Quality
The building and maintenance of our network requires thousands of workers every day carrying out work activities in our
offices, people's homes and communities in often challenging environments. The safety, health and wellbeing of our
employees, contractor supply chain workers and the public is a top priority for Gigaclear and its Board who are committed
to ensuring the welfare of all stakeholders is constantly considered and protected.
Gigaclear's HSEQ Department has in place a robust strategic plan tailored around our "Safety First" program which focuses
on assessing, managing and improving the quality and compliance of our operations, ensuring the protection and welfare
of our workforce and those that may be affected by our activities. Key objectives include regularly engaging with
contractors on requirements and performance, training and education of our employees, risk management on the
assessment and control of risks, clear communications on HSEQ standards and requirements, workshops and behavioural
culture sessions and a utility strike campaign focusing on actions to mitigate or prevent the risk of utility strikes, on-site
inspections and monitoring for compliance with over 59k inspections recorded in 2023. The education and drive for
compliance of all stakeholders is critical for creating positive behavioural change for individuals, the contractor supply chain
and the wider fibre industry.
Throughout 2023 we have continuously reviewed, updated and improved working practices and policies to ensure we
provide our employees, partners and the public a safe working environment and a positive experience when engaging with
the organisation. Despite the industry challenges with the regular movement of contractor personnel and the developing
safety culture we have demonstrated an improvement in the safe operation and compliance of our live sites, and this is
evidenced not just from inspections and audits undertaken by Gigaclear personnel but also by external regulatory authorities.
Gigaclear is proud to be a founding member of the fibre industry safety group SHIFT which is the first and only of its kind in the
UK created for fibre asset owners to engage and collaborate on establishing and implementing universal standards to
improve the safety of all those that work in, or may be affected by, our industry activities. Gigaclear's HSEQ team are also
part of many unique working groups including NUAR the government's new platform for accessing and locating all utility

Environment
We are pleased to report that, as a result of the Company's efforts, SECR reporting of our Scope | and 2 emissions for 2023
shows a significant decrease in our ‘Intensity Ratio' which measures the tones of carbon emitted per premises built from 0.20
at year end 2022 to 0.14 at year end 2023 (see page 13 for details). This means that despite the increase in our build activity
we have not seen a significant impact on our carbon emissions. We attribute this to a variety of factors, including a campaign
to improve driving style and reduce speeding and an improvement in network resiliency with a focus on improved battery
health, requiring less intervention with diesel generators. We have also achieved efficiencies in the deployment of engineers
to reduce the number of miles travelled.
There are several environmental initiatives that the company has implemented during 2023:
. Trials of an electric excavator set up to commence on the Oxfordshire Project Gigabit contract work
° A new waste management supplier has been onboarded to manage waste across the company with a target to

We have started work on gathering data on our Scope 3 emissions. The first target was to audit the electricity consumption
at our two data centres, which revealed that both were able to certify 100% renewable energy use. A new requirement for
our supply chain to monitor its energy usage and report monthly to the company commenced in Q4 2023 and will enable
more detailed reporting for year end 2024.

Discharge of Directors’ Duties - $172 Statement
The directors have a duty to promote the success of the company for the benefit of the members as a whole, but this duty
must take into account the wider context in which the company operates: its stakeholders, its market and regulatory
framework and the balance of interests between members. Decision making must also reflect a long-term view of the
success of the Company.
The directors consider the Company's stakeholders to be the Company's shareholders, employees, customers, suppliers,
regulators and government partners and the wider public in the communities where the Company builds and operates.
The directors endeavour to have the interest of all these groups in mind whilst recognising that some decisions may affect
one group more than others. The Board includes representatives of the major shareholders as well as management who
seek to ensure that all shareholders' interests are fairly represented. Monthly business performance review meetings facilitate
information sharing and strategic decision making, together with Board meetings held at least quarterly. Where appropriate,
direct engagement with stakeholders is used to inform the board, including detailed reporting to the Board's Corporate Risk
and Culture Committee and reporting of the regular employee engagement surveys and the net promoter score surveys
done for both customers and employees.
Engagement with both central and local government is a constant focus for the Company, as a result of its participation in
the rollout of the Superfast broadband scheme and in the wider consultation exercises for the ongoing rollout of gigabit
capable broadband. The Company also maintains a close relationship with the industry regulator, Ofcom.
The directors have an ongoing focus on health and safety, with the interests of the Company's employees, suppliers and
the wider public at the heart of the issue. Health & safety reporting is a standing agenda item at each board meeting and
the directors have challenged and endorsed the Company's health & safety strategy and will continue to monitor
performance in this area closely.
The Directors continue to encourage the development of the Company's ESG agenda, with a particular focus on the
Company's environmental impact, diversity in the workplace and the importance of keeping information and personal data
secure and well-managed. At the heart of the ESG programme is the need to understand and respond to the needs of
stakeholders and the Company's position in the wider economy.
The Directors believe in the highest standards of business ethics and will seek to ensure that these standards inform all the
Company's decision making for the benefit of ail stakeholders.

Impairment of financial assets
IFRS 9's impairment requirements use forward-looking information to recognise expected credit losses - the “expected
credit loss (ECL) model.” This replaces IAS 39's “incurred loss model.” Instruments within the scope of IFRS 9 include trade
receivables.
The Company considers a broad range of information when assessing credit risk and determining expected credit losses.
The information reviewed includes including past events, current conditions, reasonable and supportable forecasts that
affect the expected collectability of the future cash flows of the instrument.
In applying this forward-looking approach, a distinction is made between:
e — financial instruments that have not deteriorated significantly in credit quality since initial recognition or that
have low credit risk ("Stage 1") and
e financial instruments that have deteriorated significantly in credit quality since initial recognition and whose
credit risk is not low ("Stage 2").
“Stage 3" would cover financial assets that have objective evidence of impairment at the reporting date.
"12-month expected credit losses" are recognised for the first category while “lifetime expected credit losses” are
recognised for the second category.
Measurement of the expected credit losses is determined by a probability-weighted estimate of credit losses over the
expected life of the financial instrument.
Trade and other receivables and contract assets
The Company makes use of a simplified approach in accounting for trade and other receivables and records the loss
allowance as lifetime expected credit losses. These are the expected shortfalls in contractual cash flows, considering the
potential for default at any point during the life of the financial instrument. In calculating expected losses, the Company
uses its historical experience, external indicators and forward-looking information to calculate the expected credit losses
using a provision matrix.
The Company assesses impairment of trade receivables on a collective basis {as they possess shared credit risk
characteristics) and are grouped based on days past due. Note 28 provides a detailed analysis of how the impairment
requirements of IFRS 9 are applied.

Leases

The Company considers whether a contract is, or contains a lease, at inception of the contract. A lease is defined as a
‘contract, or part of a contract, that conveys the right to use an asset (the underlying asset) for a period of time in exchange
for consideration’. To apply this definition the Company assesses whether the contract meets three key evaluations which
are whether:

e the contract contains an identified asset, which is either explicitly identified in the contract or implicitly specified by

being identified at the time the asset is made available to the Company: ~
e the Company has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from use of the identified asset
throughout the period of use, considering its rights within the defined scope of the contract:

¢ the Company has the right to direct the use of the identified asset throughout the period of use.

Measurement and recognition of leases as a lessee

At lease commencement date, the Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability on the balance sheet.
The right-of-use asset is measured at cost, which is made up of the initial measurement of the lease liability, any initial
direct costs incurred by the Company, an estimate of any costs to dismantle and remove the asset at the end of the
lease, and any lease payments made in advance of the lease commencement date (net of any incentives received).
The Company depreciates the right-of-use assets on a straight-line basis from the lease commencement date to the
earlier of the end of the useful life of the right-of-use asset or the end of the lease term. If a lease transfers ownership of
the underlying asset or the cost of the right-of-use asset reflects that the Company expects to exercise a purchase option,
the related right-of-use asset is depreciated over the useful life of the underlying asset. The Company also assesses the
right-of-use asset for impairment when such indicators exist.

At the commencement date, the Company measures the lease liability at the present value of the lease payments unpaid
at that date, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease if that rate is readily available or the Company's
incremental borrowing rate.

Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability are made up of fixed payments (including in substance
fixed), variable payments based on an index or rate, amounts expected to be payable under a residual value guarantee
and payments arising from options reasonably certain to be exercised.

After initial measurement, the liability will be reduced for payments made and increased for interest. It is remeasured to
reflect any reassessment or modification, or if there are changes in in-substance fixed payments.

When the lease liability is remeasured, the corresponding adjustment is reflected in the right-of-use asset, or profit and loss
if the right-of-use asset is already reduced to zero.

The Company has elected to account for short-term leases and leases of low-value assets using the practical expedients.
Instead of recognising a right-of-use asset and lease liability, the payments in relation to these are recognised as an
expense in profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

On the statement of financial position, right-of-use assets have been included in property, piant and equipment and lease
liabilities have been included in trade and other payables.

Internet connectivity fees
Revenue from the monthly fees charged to customers for providing access to the internet is recognised in line with the time
period for which the connection is provided. Fees are charged and payable monthly in advance and revenue for dates
beyond the current accounting period is reported in the statement of financial position as a contract liability.
Customer connection fees
Connection fees are one-off fees charged to customers at the outset of the contract to cover set up and installation services.
These fees are included in the client's first invoice and are payable together with the first monthly connectivity fee. As such,
the Company judges that connection services form a part of a bundle of services to deliver internet connectivity services.
The revenue from the connection fees is therefore recognised over the same time period as the customer's internet
connectivity services contract. Revenue which relates to the portion of the customer's contract which is beyond the current
accounting period is reported in the statement of financial position as a contract liability. Customer contracts vary in length,
the standard residential contract is 18 months, business contract is 24 months, and wholesale customer contract is 12 months.

VOIP services are provided to the Company's customers by an external service provider. The Company receives

recognised in the period for which the commission is paid.

In December 2023 the Company signed a 7-year new debt facility to refinance the existing debt package and provide
additional capital to support future build activities. This facility is repayable as a bullet in September 2030. The new facility is
comprised of an Initial Term Facility, Capex facilities and a revolving facility with a combined value over £960m and a
provision for an accordion facility worth over £500m. The carrying value of the facilities are stated net of unamortised finance
costs of £20.1m (2022: £10.5m), see net debt statement for additional detail.
The facilities are subject to an interest rate of 3.75% - 5.00% above SONIA. The margin is determined by reference to the age
of the facility. The margin applicable in 2024 is 3.75%. Customary commitment fees are applicable on the Facilities.
