for the Year Ended 30 September 2023

The Chairman presents his Strategic report on the Company and the Group for the year ended

30 September 2023.

Principal activities

Sabio Limited (hereafter referred to as “the Company") and its subsidiaries (hereafter referred to as "the Sabio

Group") are Expert Services companies, that brings together expertise, technology and insight to guide large

Enterprise customers to realisation of benefits from its investment in Al (Automation) Data and Contact Centre

technology by focussing on customer experiences.

We specialise in cloud migration and the application of technologies to deliver sustainable, long-term value to

our customers.

Refer to note 13 for detail on group structure. Sabio Limited is a member of the Seattle Topco Limited group

(hereafter referred to as “the Seattle Group’).

Business review

FY23 proved to be a challenging yet steady year for the Sabio Group amidst economic and industry headwinds.

Revenue remained flat at £145.8 million, compared to £147.5 million the previous year, however EBITDA saw

a slight increase of 1% to £20.4 million, up from £20.1 million in 2022. The Sabio Group ended the year with

net liabilities of £4.8 million (2022: net assets £14.8 million).

Business review (continued)
Despite those external challenges, we successfully completed our transition from a perpetual to a SaaS model
and the Sabio Group managed to maintain steady annuity business growth, with our annuity business reporting
6% year-on-year growth to £104 million.
Through diligent cost control measures such as headcount changes, this helped to align our cost base with
external trading conditions and maintained Sabio’s EBITDA.
While this year presented myriad challenges, we succeeded in delivering slight EBITDA growth while
continuing to transition the business model. Our annuity revenue streams also showed promising growth.
Increasing interest rates in FY23 negatively impacted cashflows resulting in additional investor funding being
required and contributed to a higher loss for the year.
With an eye on prudently managing costs, we are confident of continuing to weather any ongoing economic
and market uncertainty while providing value for our customers and partners.
Proposition

: The reporting period represented a year of strategic realignment for the business, as we refreshed our

. leadership team and doubled down on our mission of becoming the leading specialist in customer experience
technology and expert services.
Under the leadership of returning CEO Andy Roberts, we instilled a renewed focus on building a people-centric
culture, improving visibility and engagement of our executive management and leadership teams across all of
our locations. This proved invaluable in guiding our workforce through the ongoing challenges, both in regard
to the CX market and economically.
We strengthened our executive team with several key appointments, including loan MacRae as Chief Revenue
Officer with Darren Hayward and Mark Betts being promoted to Chief Financial Officer and Chief Experience
Officer respectively. Both the Chief Revenue Officer and Chief Experience Officer roles are new to the Sabio
Group leadership.
At a regional level, these changes were complemented through the appointment of new managing directors in
two key regions. Danny Seaborne was named MD for the UK & South Africa and Nicolai Ginge was named
MD for the Nordic region; a reinforcement of our commitment to that region following expansion of the Sabio
Group a few months earlier in the reporting period. This expansion extended both our reach and capability in
Europe.
On the technology front, we continued enhancing our own IP, improving our Sabio Console, Interaction
Analytics/IC &A and Wellbeing Companion solutions. Our strategy of specialising in the intersection of contact
centre, Al and data technologies was validated by major players like Salesforce and Genesys moving closer
together. As their ecosystems converge, our expertise provides essential value to clients seeking maximum
ROl.
To support our credibitity in the market, we published 12 new case studies and actively increased our public
references across sectors like telecoms, healthcare and financial services. We were also added to key UK
public sector frameworks like G-Cloud 13 and the Crown Commercial Services Network, opening up new
government opportunities.
Like many businesses, we faced macroeconomic headwinds this year as growth in cloud contact centre
migration began to slow. With pressure on expenditures, some clients are delaying their transition timelines.
However, we see this as an opportunity for the business. Migrating complex on-premise infrastructure to the
cloud has proven more difficult than anticipated for many organisations and, as a result, our specialist guidance
and support services are now needed more than ever through every step of the transformation process.

Our people are fundamental to achieving Sabio's strategy of achieving and maintaining our position as the

Future Outlook
As we closed out the 2023 financial year, Sabio Group stood at over 900 employees globally, with operations
across 11 offices supporting more than 500 organisations in 65 countries.
Within our substantial client footprint, we manage over 220,000 contact centre seats and handle 1.5 billion

These metrics demonstrate the scale of our customer experience technology implementations and our

As we look ahead, we remain committed to growing our capabilities and footprint without losing sight of our

endeavour to extend our position as a leading specialised provider of customer and employee experience
solutions worldwide.
Disclosure of Principal Risks and Uncertainties
The Sabio Group’s operations expose it to a variety of risks that include but are not limited to the loss of key
customers, the loss of key suppliers, outsourcing, business continuity and reliance on key IT systems,
together with risks associated with growth of the business and underlying economic environmental factors
affecting overall business confidence.
These risks are formally reviewed by the Board at least annually and appropriate processes put in place to
monitor and mitigate their effects. Customer risk is managed by focusing on the diversification of the
customer portfolio and customer retention strategies, as well as reviewing the financial stability of new and
existing customers. Supplier risk is managed through regular assessment of existing key suppliers, looking
at factors such as financial stability, resource availability, product lifecycle, product relevance to our
customers and their exposure to market risk. Regular forums are held with our customers to ensure we
understand their needs, and our suppliers are critically appraised to ensure their products meet customer
needs. In addition, regular evaluations of alternate suppliers, solutions and technologies are made to
minimise the risk in this area. The Sabio Group has established formal business continuity and disaster
recovery plans, and these are reviewed at Board level at least annually and tested bi-annually. The risk to
the growth of the business and the underlying economic environment is managed by aligning the Sabio
Group’s product and service offering with its core competencies, whilst retaining and winning customers from
a diverse cross-section of size, sectors and geographic locations.
Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting (“SECR”)
The Sabio Group has partnered with two organisations - Carbon Responsible and Social Value Portal (SVP)
- to allow us to monitor and action Carbon Reduction initiatives, understand our ESG (Environmental, Social
and Governance) responsibilities as well as the value the Sabio Group adds to society. As a consequence,
and due to the Sabio Group's increased focus in this area, the methodology with respect to SECR has been
improved for the purposes of the current year to 30 September 2023 to more accurately represent the Sabio
Group's environmental impact for the financial year.
We have now established a cycle of quarterly reporting to understand the impact of the various initiatives
undertaken.
Our commitment to a greener environment is embedded within the Sabio One strategy and the business is
now looking at longer-term mechanisms to enable us to reduce our direct and indirect environmental impact.
The Group has commenced a number of initiatives including a sponsorship arrangement with Ecologi, an
online platform focused on real climate action. Ecologi are planting trees on behalf of the Sabio Group for each
completed customer satisfaction survey that Sabio receives. During 2023, we are further defining our
sustainability targets and strategy which form an important element of stakeholder attraction and retention.

Going concern

The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis which forecasts that the Sabio Limited
Group and the Company will have sufficient liquidity to meet their financial obligations for a period of at least
12 months from the date of these financial statements.

The directors have prepared a consolidated liquidity forecast at the Seattle Topco Limited group level (the
Company’s ultimate parent) (‘the Seattle Group”) which includes Sabio Limited and its subsidiaries (the Sabio
Group”) for the going concern period, being a period to 31 December 2025, which is at least 12 months from
the date of approval of these financial statements.
As part of their review the directors have considered the implications of the current economic conditions on
the going concern assumption. This has also included sensitivity analyses which consider a severe but
plausible downside. As disclosed in note 30 (Events after the reporting date) of the Sabio Group’s consolidated

reached agreement with its lenders to reset certain terms and conditions under its existing lending facilities.
The cashflow forecasts used by management in both base and severe but plausible downside scenarios were
used to calculate forecast covenant compliance under the reset terms and conditions in the going concern
period.

Revenue recognition
A contract involves agreement between two or more parties that creates enforceable rights and
performance obligations. The Sabio Group recognises revenue when an enforceable performance
obligation is satisfied, and it is identifiable separately from other promises in the contract. A performance
obligation involves the transfer of control of distinct goods and services to the customer where a
customer can benefit from the good or service on its own or together with readily available resources in
the market.
The Sabio Group allocates a transaction price to each performance obligation on the basis of the stand-
alone selling prices of each distinct good or service promised in the contract. The Sabio Group uses an
estimated price when stand-alone prices are not observable. Discounts, rebates, and consideration that
are generated through the synergy of the contract as a whole should be allocated to one or more, but
not all performance obligations.
