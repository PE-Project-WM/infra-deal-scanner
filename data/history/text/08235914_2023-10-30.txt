DATALEC POWER INSTALLATIONS LIMITED
GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 OCTOBER 2022
Introduction :
The directors are pleased to report that Datalec Power Installations Limited (DPI) Group has continued to grow
particularly with its operations across Europe and with the successful relaunch of its Ireland Operations. The
whole industry, still recovering from the impact of Covid, saw something of a slow start to the financial year.
, : During this period, the Group continued to invest heavily in staff and systems. The staff base grew significantly,
. not only to provide the much-needed construction resource but also to provide specialists to improve our
awareness of wider subject matter that will go on to become new sources of revenue for the future.
Notwithstanding this, a robust pipeline converted to contracted work has resulted in an annual performance
largely in line with expectations.
M&A
During the year DP! acquired Technivo, a specialist technical cleaning and contamination management
specialist. DPI had worked in partnership with Technivo for several years and given the synergies and expertise
that had developed in that period, the shareholders at DPI and Technivo believed the time was right to join
forces. This acquisition is an important development and is fully aligned with the Group's managed services and
annuity-based revenue ambitions. Technivo operates as a wholly owned subsidiary to DPI.
Finally, the Board would like to take this opportunity to acknowledge the continued support of our valued
customers and suppliers and of course the hard work and Joyal support of staff including our delivery teams
support services.
Business review
The Group has reported a turnover of £52.6m (2021: £45.2m) and a pretax profit of £1.3m for the year ended 31
October 2022 (2021: £2.9m). The Group has total shareholder funds of £7.6m as at 31 October 2022 (2021:
£6.0m).
In the year, turnover at £52.6m was £7.4m (17%) greater than the previous year.
Cash held at year end is £3.7m up £0.9m from 2021(£2.85m). :
Dividends paid in the year amounted to £1,472,000 (2021: £1,580,000) .
The Group has continued to invest heavily in staff and systems to deal with anticipated revenue growth and
extended geographical reach in FY23 and beyond. Demand in the industry continues to outstrip supply and this
has created a vacuum within which the Group maneuvered to ensure revenue and profit growth for the
forthcoming years. The Data Centre industry is fast developing beyond the Tier 1 geographies as hyperscale and .
large enterprises both deliver and demand better distributed services.
Enormous investments are being made in Tier 2 and 3 cities and countries taking account of power availability,
demographics, ESG considerations and network availability. The Group is building an organisation capable of
addressing these future demands. The DPI focus remains centred around Quality, this remains our most
important market differentiator; we are a Group in pursuit of perfection. The consequence can easily be
demonstrated through multiple repeat customers, our fundamental growth denominator.

Future developments

The Group's strategy is to continue focusing on the Data Centre sector in the medium to long term. Acquiring

Technivo allows the business to expand services offered and feverage current relationships and customers to

grow the business.

The Group will continue to pursue tactical acquisitions that provide improved product of geographical reach. The

principal growth is expected to result from organic operations and business development pursuits.

Plans are being made to expand further into new geographic regions including the Middle East and America.
See note 31.

By leveraging Aisle Containment and Security Caging capabilities, the business plans to establish a foothold in
these regions and further expand capabilities to increase market share.

Summary

This past year saw a top line growth of 17%. The UK continues to be the country that creates the most revenue
at £25.1m with the combined European countries contributing revenues of £27.5m. We believe this will change
for the 2022-23 FY with most of the revenue being completed in Europe, Germany being the main contributor.
The Group grew in line with expectations in the year to end October 22. Our employee base rose from around
90 in October 2021 to 158 in Octeber 2022 and our average number of employees grew from 66 to 134.
Augmenting construction revenues, our now well-established managed services portfolio, including Intelligent
Hands, Technical Cleaning, Project Management as a Service and Managed Migration Services, provide
customer satisfaction and are accretive to revenue and profits. These annuity-based revenue streams are
already seeing promising traction and it is expected that, over the coming few years, they will make up around
20% of our overall top line but with a proportionally greater impact on our profitability. .
Focused on geographical reach, DPI has recently incorporated in the Middle East in pursuit of the substantial
regional growth in digital infrastructure. DPI is ‘applications orientated’ and in FY22 and FY23 further investment
has been made into a bespoke Group-wide SharePoint platform; a highly developed salesforce.com application
to manage customer relations, sales pipeline, tenders and marketing initiatives and the transition for all finance
and commercial operations to Eque2, a Microsoft Dynamics platform with open API integration to SharePoint
and salesforce.com. PeopleHR, our staff cloud-based application, is in widespread use throughout the Group
and manages all HR related matters including Timesheets, Expenses, Holidays and Group Policies. These
investments will ultimately allow DPI to compete at any level and in any geography. :
DPi has again invested heavily in R&D in FY22. We are constantly looking at our new modular and offsite
construction capabilities and designing multiuse cabinets, new security caging and lighting. Responding to
customer demand for quality, plans to develop and implement a paint line were completed in FY22 and in the .
same period it was fully commissioned and set to work.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Generating sufficient cash from operations is a key health metric for DPI. This is monitored closely, and we
manage the business accordingly.

. There is a widespread shortage of data centre construction expertise, this continues to drive costs up and
availability down. Alongside its very successful trainee and apprentice program, DPI continues to develop a
strategy to promote talent from within — we work hard to identify our own rising stars and make sure they have
the mentorship and encouragement necessary to reach into the management layer. Resource and recruitment
are a constant workstream and efforts this year will double down again over last year’s focus in this area

in our opinion the financial statements: .
. give a true and fair view of the state of the Group's and of the parent company’s affairs as at 31 October
2022 and of the Group's profit for the year then ended;

Practice; and

Basis for qualified opinion
We were not appointed as auditor of the company until 25 November 2021 and thus did not observe the counting
of stock at 31 October 2021. We were unable to satisfy ourselves by alternative means concerning the group
stock quantities held at 31 October 2021, which are included in the consolidated statement of comprehensive
income at £154,714, by using other audit procedures. Since opening stock enters into the determination of
financial performance and cash flows, in respect of profit for the year reflected in the consolidated statement of
comprehensive income or in the net cash flows from operating activities in the cash flow statement, we were
unable to determine whether any adjustment to this amount was necessary.
We conducted our audit in accordance with International Standards on Auditing (UK) (ISAs (UK)) and applicable
law. Our responsibilities under those standards are further described in the Auditors’ responsibilities for the audit
of the financial statements section of our report. We are independent of the Group in accordance with the ethical
requirements that are relevant to our audit of the financial statements in the United Kingdom, including the
Financial Reporting Council's Ethical Standard and we have fulfilled our other ethical responsibilities in :
accordance with these requirements. We believe that the audit evidence we have obtained is sufficient and
appropriate to provide a basis for our opinion. . .
Conclusions relating to going concern
in auditing the financial statements, we have concluded that the directors’ use of the going concern basis of
accounting in the preparation of the financial statements is appropriate.
Based on the work we have performed, we have not identified any material uncertainties relating to events or
conditions that, individually or collectively, may cast significant doubt on the Group's or the parent company’s
ability to continue as a going concern for a period of at least twelve months from when the financial statements

2.4 Revenue
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the

Rendering of services
Revenue from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are

. the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
. it is probable that the Group will receive the consideration due under the contract; ~
. the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured

. the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.
Construction contracts
Revenue is recognised on construction services over time as the benefit is transferred to the

2.12 Intangible assets
Goodwill
Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and
the acquirer's interest in the fair value of the Group's share of its identifiable assets and liabilities of
the acquiree at the date of acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost
less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a
straight-line basis to the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income over its estimated useful
economic life of two years.
Other intangible assets
Intangible assets are initially recognised at cost. After recognition, under the cost model, intangible
assets are measured at cost less any accumulated amortisation and any accumulated impairment
losses.
At each reporting date the company assesses whether there is any indication of impairment. If such
indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is determined which is the higher of its fair
value less costs to sell and its value in use. An impairment loss is recognised where the carrying
amount exceeds the recoverable amount.
All intangible assets are considered to have a finite useful life. If a reliable estimate of the useful life
cannot be made, the useful life shall not exceed ten years.
Computer software is amortised over its estimated useful life of 5 years on a straight-line basis.
2.13 Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of
operating in the manner intended by management.
Depreciation is charged so as to allocate the cost of assets less their residual value over their
, estimated useful lives, using the straight-line method.
Depreciation is provided on the following basis: :
Long-term leasehold property - 20%
Plant and machinery - 20%
Motor vehicles - 33%
Fixtures and fittings - 20% Computer equipment, a subset of fixtures and
fittings, is depreciated at 33%
The assets’ residual values, useful lives and depreciation methods are reviewed, and adjusted
prospectively if appropriate, or if there is an indication of a significant change since the last reporting
date.
Gains and losses on disposals are determined by comparing the proceeds with the carrying amount
and are recognised in profit or loss.

2.19 Financial instruments
The Group only enters into basic financial instrument transactions that result in the recognition of
financial assets and liabilities like trade and other debtors and creditors, loans from banks and other
third parties, loans to related parties and investments in ordinary shares.
Debt instruments (other than those wholly repayable or receivable within one year), including loans
and other accounts receivable and payable, are initially measured at present value of the future cash
flows and subsequently at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Debt instruments that
are payable or receivable within one year, typically trade debtors and creditors, are measured, initially
and subsequently, at the undiscounted amount of the cash or other consideration expected to be paid
or received. However, if the arrangements of a short-term instrument constitute a financing
transaction, like the payment of a trade debt deferred beyond normal business terms or in case of an
: out-right short-term loan that is not at market rate, the financial asset or liability is measured, initially
at the present value of future cash flows discounted at a market rate of interest for a similar debt
instrument and subsequently at amortised cost, unless it qualifies as a loan from a director in the
case of a small company, or a public benefit entity concessionary loan.
Financial assets that are measured at cost and amortised cost are assessed at the end of each
reporting period for objective evidence of impairment. If objective evidence of impairment is found, an
impairment loss is recognised in the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
For financial assets measured at amortised cost, the impairment toss is measured as the difference
between an asset's carrying amount and the present value of estimated cash flows discounted at the
asset's original effective interest rate. If a financial asset has a variable interest rate, the discount rate
for measuring any impairment loss is the current effective interest rate determined under the
contract.
For financial assets measured at cost less impairment, the impairment loss is measured as the
difference between an asset's carrying amount and best estimate of the recoverable amount, which is
an approximation of the amount that the Group would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the
balance sheet date.
Financial assets and liabilities are offset and the net amount reported in the Balance Sheet when
: there is an enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle ona
net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

Any excess of the cost of the business combination over the Group's interest in the net fair value of
