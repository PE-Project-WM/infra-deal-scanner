Introduction
. Mycom (UK) Limited (“the Company”) is part of the Mycom OSI group. Mycom OSI was formed in 2014 by

combining two leading Telecom Industry Service Assurance software providers — Mycom and OSI. The heritage of
Mycom (Est. 1997) is in Network Performance Management, and OSI (Est. 1989) in Fault Management, with both
offering Service Management capabilities. In 2018 the Mycom OSI group was purchased by the Magnate group.
Today the group's portfolio of applications are cloud native, integrated and provide real time assurance powered by
automation, analytics and artificial intelligence / machine learning.
With this powerful suite of applications, the group continues to grow its portfolio of tier 1 Communications Service
Providers ("CSPs’), including some of the world’s largest. The Mycom OSI technology helps manage the
experience of over 2bn tier 1 CSP customers globally.
Mycom OSI is headquartered in London, UK with 250 employees in offices worldwide serving customers in all
continents.
Strategy, objectives and business model
The Company's strategy is to be the leading specialist assurance software provider in the global telecommunications
market. The Company commits significant investment in its people and application portfolio to ensure the Mycom OSI
platform drives real outcomes for its customers globally and aligns with their strategic, operational and commercial
objectives.
The Company provides flexible commercial models including subscription-based offerings which provide predictability
of spend for customers as part of an end-to-end service.
The Company has technically skilled and dedicated employees in centres of excellence around the world. These
teams ensure the successful delivery of the Mycom OSI suite of products to its customers.
The board regularly review the group's strategy to ensure it reflects the opportunities identified in the markets in which the
group operates, as well as emerging markets where the group can use its experience and technology to expandits
business model. Mycom OSI is at the forefront of the market in providing artificial intelligence services to its
customers. The Company is focused on growing the opportunity pipeline and with continued investment in its sales
and marketing teams the Company continues to develop opportunities to grow the order book and revenues.
The business is well positioned to target growth opportunities across the broader market, including targeting

: customers looking for end-to-end services in the mobile and private network markets. In the current year, the
Company has delivered organic growth from its existing customer base where customers are now purchasing the full

. suite of Mycom OSI products with opportunities to deliver additional services.
Core values
Whilst the Company uses technology and processes, the core of its business is its people who strive to keep the
group at the forefront of its markets with their passion for the innovation and customer service.
The Company could not succeed without the skill and dedication of its workforce. They embody the culture and
values and ensure that the Company delivers the excellent products and service on which its success depends.
. The directors express their appreciation to all the group’s employees for their hard wark and dedication.
As a values led organisation, corporate responsiblity is a consistent theme. Acting responsibly enables the
Company to be a better, more efficient business and a good corporate citizen.
The Company's approach to responsible business influences the way it manages and develops its people. It also
underpins the delivery of excellent customer service by ensuring all colleagues are kept safe and secure, and
improves the Company’s efficiency by encouraging a reduction in energy and resources used to mitigate the impact
on the environment.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Competitive environment
The Company operates in a competitive environment with competition from a broad range of organisations. Failure
to continue innovating could reduce the value of the Company's customer proposition and therefore adversely
impact its leading competitive position.
To mitigate this risk the Company invests to continue the innovation of its suite of assurance applications and to
keep at the forefront of an ever-changing market.
Economic environment
The cost base of the Company has been subject to increased price pressure due to the prevailing economic
environment, alongside customers looking to reduce their cost base. In order to protect growth and margins we
work with our customers to offer the best value for money propositions for their business to retain revenue streams
and grow the customer base. The Company implemented an internal cost saving review to identify measures and
cost savings to manage the cost base.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Failure to manage reputation
Failure to manage reputation will mean that customers will be less likely to award the Company new business or
renew existing business. It will also impact on the Company’s ability to attract and retain high quality people as
maintaining the right staffing with the appropriate technical and administrative skills is critical to meeting the needs
of customers. To mitigate this risk, the business has implemented a Customer Success Programme to focus on
customer satisfaction, value realisation and user adoption.
Major information security breach or cyber attack
A major information security breach resulting in the loss or compromise of sensitive information (including personal
or customer) or the loss of service is likely to cause significant reputational damage, financial penalties, and loss of
customer confidence is a key risk to the business.
The Company continues to make significant investments in its cyber security systems and processes for both
customer solutions and internal systems. In addition, the Company has implemented a security training programme
for employees.
With phishing and ransomware attacks increasing, new security measures have been implemented including two
factor authentication, training and internal IT alerting systems.
Contract non-compliance
Failure to deliver contractual obligations or to meet agreed service performance levels and report against them
accurately may lead to significant financial penalties or legal notices.
Any failure in the Company’s software products or solution delivery to customers could also result in increased
project delivery costs and an adverse impact on reputation risk. To address this risk, regular performance reviews
are conducted for all material contracts.
Significant failure of suppliers
A significant failure in a platform deliverable hosted by a supplier may expose the Company to a risk of failure to
meet its customer obligations, perform critical business operations or win new business.
Supply chain risk is carefully managed for critical customer deliverables. The Company works closely with suppliers
to understand their systems and deliverables and engage with them regularly to ensure compliance and key
delivery dates are adhered to.
Material legal and regulatory compliance failure
Failure to comply with laws and regulations could result in significant loss to the Company including exposure to
prosecution and fines, reputational damage and the potential loss of license and authorisations, all of which may
adversely impact the prospects for future contracts.
All key contracts are reviewed on a monthly basis by the management team to ensure performance is in line with
contractual obligations and to ensure any remedial actions are delivered.
Financial control failure
Financial control failure may result in an inability to accurately report timely financial results and meet contractual
financial reporting obligations, increase risk of error and fraud, and adversely impact management's ability to make
business decisions and forecast accurately.
The Company is focused on maintaining an effective and well monitored control environment that minimises
financial risk and ensures the effectiveness of systems for internal control and financial reporting.
Foreign exchange risk
The Company's transactions are primarily carried out in British Pounds and US Dollars. The Company is able to net
the US Dollar amounts with other subsidiaries in the group where sufficient cash balances are maintained so that
transactional risk across the group is managed.

Going concern
The Company is part of the wider Magnate Midco Limited group (“the Group”) as detailed in note 21. The Group as a
whole is managed as a single cash generating unit, with resources deployed across the Group as needed in order to meet
individual company obligations as they fall due.
The Group and Company continue to deliver strong revenue performance and underlying operating cash flow generation.
The Directors are confident the Group and Company will maintain and grow existing customer contracts with a sales
pipeline to support winning new customer contracts in its target market to grow revenue.
Subsequent to the balance sheet date, the Group have entered into loan agreements with [nflexion Buyout V Investments
LP, a related party, totalling £16.1 million to support the ongoing working capital requirements of the Group and to repay in
full the $9.6 million revolving credit facility which was repayable on 14 February 2024. The loans are repayable in full on
11 August 2026 and are not subject to any loan covenants.
In addition, the Group has obtained a signed letter of support from Inflexion Buyout Fund V GP Guernsey Limited which
confirms that they will continue to support the operating activities of the Group and Company for a period of at least 12
months from the date of approval of these financial statements.
The Group has a term bank loan of £45.5 million including accrued interest, which is due in-full on 14 December 2024. The
Directors are confident that this term loan will be settled as it falls due. This may be through a refinancing of the debt,
shareholder support or a combination of these. Management are currently in the process of refinancing the debt, however,
these circumstances represent a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the Group’s ability to continue as a
going concern for the foreseeable future.
The Directors are, based on detailed financial projections and the ongoing support of Inflexion Buyout Fund V GP
Guernsey, of the opinion that that the Company has adequate working capital funding to continue as a going concern for the
foreseeable future and, in particular for a period of at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial
statements. Therefore, the financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis.

A right-of-use asset is recognised at the commencement date of a lease. The right-of-use asset is measured at

Where the lease term is twelve months or less or the leased asset has a value of $5,000 or less, and the lease

Straight-line basis over the lease term.
A lease liability is recognised at the commencement date of a lease. The lease liability is initially recognised at
the present value of the lease payments to be made over the term of the lease, discounted using the interest
rate implicit in the lease or, if that rate cannot be readily determined, the company's incremental borrowing rate.
Lease payments comprise of fixed payments less any lease incentives receivable, variable lease payments that

Lease liabilities are measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. The carrying amounts are

Critical accounting judgements
Revenue
The company's contracts with customers may include promises to transfer multiple products and services to a
customer. Determining whether products and services are considered distinct performance obligations that
should be accounted for separately versus together may require significant judgement.
For arrangements with multiple performance obligations, the company allocates the contract transaction price,
including discounts, to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Judgement is
tequired to determine the standalone selling price for each distinct performance obligation.
The company determines standalone selling prices based on prices charged to customers in observable
transactions and uses a range of amounts to estimate standalone selling prices for each performance obligation. The
company maximises the use of observable inputs by maintaining pricing analysis that includes the group’spricing
policies, historical standalone sales when they exist, and historical renewal prices charged to customers. It was
concluded that the standalone selling prices of certain performance obligations, specifically the standalone selling
prices for software licenses and SaaS arrangements, are highly variable. In these instances, the group
estimates the standalone selling prices using the residual approach, determined based on total transaction price
minus the standalone selling price of other performance obligations promised in the contract.
Lease accounting — determining the lease term
The company determines the lease term as the non-cancellable term of the lease, together with any periods covered
by an option to extend or terminate the lease. In making this judgement, the company evaluates whether it is
reasonably certain to exercise the option to renew or break the lease term.
That is, it considers all relevant factors that create an economic incentive for it to exercise the renewa! and the
circumstances and facts for each lease including past experience to determine the likely lease term and whether the
break option is likely to be exercised. This includes an assessment on the length of time remaining before the option
is exercisable, current trading conditions and future trading forecasts on the ongoing profitability of the business.
After the lease commencement date, the company reassesses the lease term if there is a significant event or change
in circumstances that is within its control and affects its ability to exercise (or not to exercise) the option to renew (for
example, a change in business strategy).
Estimation uncertainty
Lease accounting — determination of the appropriate rate to discount the lease payments
The company uses the wider groups incremental borrowing rate as the discount rate for determining its lease
liabilities at the lease commencement date since the rate implicit in the lease cannot be readily determined. The
calculation of the incremental borrowing rate involves estimation and is based on the average interest rate of all
wider group debt outstanding at the time the lease began.
