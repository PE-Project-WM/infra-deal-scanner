STRATEGIC REPORT

FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 SEPTEMBER 2024

Introduction ,

The directors present their Strategic Report of I.P. Integration Limited (the ‘Company’) for the year ended 30

September 2024.

Principal activity

The principal activity of I.P. Integration is through delivering digital transformation to the market, enabled by a

suite of Contact Centres, Customer Engagement and Al solutions to mid-market, enterprise, and public sector

organisations. In conjunction with these technology solutions, IPI provides consulting services, managed

services, and network connectivity services that enable organisations to optimise the customer journey and the

customer experience. These solutions and services are now delivered as cloud services.

Business review

The directors are pleased to report that the company had another successful year with continued double-digit

growth across revenue and gross profit. This success has been achieved despite the recent economic

challenges posed by the 2024 budget. The knock-on impact of National Insurance increases has culminated in a

number of our clients fast tracking focus on technology transformation projects that will help with business

efficiency and cost reduction initiatives. However, decision making is taking slightly longer for clients in terms of

signing off on larger transformation project budgets. This transition to becoming a cloud and Al first company

delivering digital transformation across the customer experience environment has helped us to take a strong

foothold with new logo wins across a number of vertical markets such as insurance, utilities and the retail sector.

In the year, overall revenues grew to £36.4m, representing a 12% increase on 2023, with recurring revenue for

the period increasing significantly to £33.5m (2023: £28.9m) representing 16% in year growth and 92% of total

turnover. The recurring growth is attributable to securing multi-year subscription fee contracts for CCaaS

solutions together with attached revenues of connectivity and network services which are typically secured in .

conjunction with the wider suite of our Customer Experience (CX) solutions on multiyear contracts whilst

remaining focused on industry leading levels of customer retention through our purpose of exceptional customer

contact. The non-recurring element of revenue which historically would have been made up from the sale of

hardware and perpetual software licenses with associated professional services is essentially now just down to

our professional services where we continue to expand our service offerings such as consulting, training and
_ enablement, applications development and customer success management.

This year there was an operating profit of £1.8m (2023: loss of £3.4m)

The gross margin saw a decrease from 47.8% to 44.7%. This is attributable to our sales success and the

winning of some large CCaaS new logos where the margins are lower than our historical averages but add

significant growth to our contract base as can be seen in the Gross Profit increase. 11 new logos were

onboarded within the financial year.

Now with 92% recurring revenues, the strategy for the company is to increase quality of GM% through further

investment in the ongoing development and global sales of our own Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) product

and services portfolio with several new products and new channels being successfully launched through

FY23/24 which we expect will deliver strong and profitable growth opportunities in the coming years. This is

enabling us to provide some of our services to clients and partners globally with some key wins for our Cloud PCI

solutions in North America.

People
In the year, the company continued to focus on its programme of investing and developing its people to create
an exceptional culture and work environment. Underpinning the strategy, is the company’s participation in the
Best Places to Work accreditation scheme. In 2023 the company achieved the 2-star rating for ‘Outstanding

overall position of 18th place. This continued investment and focus on building and developing talent has helped
the company onboard some highly skilled talent in the area of R&D, helping to fuel more innovation and product
development. During the year average headcount was 134 employees.
Investment activity
Investment in the fiscal year was principally focused on the continued development of our own IPR across 3 core
areas: Cloud Platform: building out additional capacity together with the introduction of new platform services. In
house applications; building on the success of launching ‘IPI Cloud PCI" into the global market place through
partnerships with Genesys, Nice and Avaya providing channel access and global coverage. The ongoing:
investment and feature enhancements to fully embed generative Al capability into our own Elastic CX CCaaS
platform has resulted in key new logo wins. This focus on investing in our own technology capability coupled
with the ongoing investment in our digital transformation consulting services group is helping us to open new
market opportunities for the company but also driving up customer retention and gross margin improvement
across our services functions. The IPI Innovation team are building a robust roadmap for the continual
enhancement of our technology propositions; including a full suite of Artificial Intelligence (Al) applications that
are being embedded into our key CX propositions together with adjacent security offerings as part of our DX IT
services and cloud business.

2.6 Turnover
Turnover represents net invoiced sale of goods and services and management fee recoveries,
excluding value added tax.
Turnover from the provision of goods is recognised when the risks and rewards of ownership of those
goods have transferred to the customer. The risks and rewards of ownership are deemed to be
transferred when the goods are shipped to the customer.
Turnover in respect of services provided is recognised in the accounting period in which the services
are rendered. For fixed price service contracts the revenue is estimated based on a percentage :
completion basis. Percentage completion is determined by the time cost incurred by the relevant
personnel delivering the contract, as a percentage of the total contract value. For contracts that are
not completed by the year end, an assessment is made regarding the expected the time cost to fulfil
. the contract and the recognised turnover is adjusted accordingly to reflect the adjusted percentage
completion. Estimates of revenues, cost or extent of which progress towards completion are revised
if circumstances change and are reflected in the Statement of Comprehensive Income in the period
in which the circumstances have arisen.
Management fees are recognised when resources are utilised by group companies and are
determined based on time spent and costs incurred.
Turnover from the provision of goods and services is only recognised when the amounts to be
recognised are fixed or determinable and recovery is reasonably assured.
2.7 Operating leases: the Company as lessee
Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to the Statement of Comprehensive Income on a
straight-line basis over the lease term.
Benefits received and receivable as an incentive to sign an operating lease are recognised on a
straight-line basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is representative of the time
pattern of the lessee's benefit from the use of the leased asset.
2.8 Leased assets: the Company as lessee
. Assets obtained under hire purchase contracts and finance leases are capitalised as tangible fixed
assets. Assets acquired by finance lease are depreciated over the shorter of the lease term and their
useful lives. Assets acquired by hire purchase are depreciated over their useful lives. Finance leases
are those where substantially all of the benefits and risks of ownership are assumed by the

allocated to future periods. The finance element of the rental payment is charged to the Statement of

2.9 Research and development - :
In the research phase of an internal project it is not possible to demonstrate that the project will
generate future economic benefits and hence all expenditure on research shall be recognised as an
expense when it is incurred. Intangible assets are recognised from the development phase of a
project if and only if certain specific criteria are met in order to demonstrate the asset will generate
probable future economic benefits and that its cost can be reliably measured. The capitalised
development costs are subsequently amortised on a straight-line basis over their useful economic
lives, which is considered by management to be 10 years.
If it is not possible to distinguish between the research phase and the development phase of an
internal project, the expenditure is treated as if it were all incurred in the research phase only.

The contributions are recognised as an expense in the Statement of Comprehensive Income when
they fall due. Amounts not paid are shown in other creditors as a liability in the Statement of Financial
Position. The assets of the plan are held separately from the Company in independently administered .

2.12 Share based payments
Where share options are awarded to employees, the fair value of the options at the date of grant is
charged to profit or loss over the vesting period. Non-market vesting conditions are taken into
account by adjusting the number of equity instruments expected to vest at each reporting date so
that, ultimately, the cumulative amount recognised over the vesting period is based on the number of
: options that eventually vest. Market vesting conditions are factored into the fair value of the options
granted. The cumulative expense is not adjusted for failure to achieve a market vesting condition.
The fair value of the award also takes into account non-vesting conditions. These are either factors
beyond the control of either party (such as a target based on an index) or factors which are within the
control of one or other of the parties (such as the Company keeping the scheme open or the
employee maintaining any contributions required by the scheme).
Where the terms and conditions of options are modified before they vest, the increase in the fair
value of the options, measured immediately before and after the modification, is also charged to profit
or loss over the remaining vesting period.
Where equity instruments are granted to persons other than employees, profit or loss is charged with
fair value of goods and services received.

The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Statement

The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date in the countries where the Company operates

Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not

. The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will be

Financial assets are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting date.
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the
estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The :

the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the
impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying
amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The

3. Judgements in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In applying the Company's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements,
estimates and assumptions in determining the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities. The
directors’ judgements, estimates and assumptions are based on the best and most reliable evidence
available at the time when the decisions are made, and are based on historical experience and other
factors that are considered to be applicable. Due to the inherent subjectivity involved in making such
judgements, estimates and assumptions, the actual results and outcomes may differ.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to
accounting estimates are recognised in the year in which the estimate is revised, if the revision
affects only that year, or in the year of the revision and future years, if the revision affects both current
and future years.
3.1 Critical judgements in applying the Company’s accounting policies
(i) Assessing indicators of impairment
In assessing whether there have been any indicators of impairment of assets, the directors have
considered both external and internal sources of information such as market conditions, counterparty
credit ratings and experience of recoverability. There have been no indicators of impairments
identified during the current financial year.
3.2 Key sources of estimation uncertainty
The key assumptions concerning the future, and other key sources of estimation uncertainty, that
have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying amounts of assets and
liabilities within the next financial year are discussed below.
(i) Determining useful economic lives of goodwill and intangible assets
On acquisition, the group determines a reliable estimate of the useful life of goodwill and intangible
assets based upon factors such as the expected use of the acquired business, forecasts of expected
future results and cash flows, and any legal, regulatory or contractual provisions that can limit useful
life.
At each subsequent reporting date the directors consider whether there are any factors such as
technological advancements or changes in market conditions that indicate a need to reconsider the
useful life of goodwill and intangible assets.
(ii) Determining the percentage completion of service based revenue
Revenue arising from fixed price professional services contracts is estimated using a percentage of
completion method. The percentage of completion method is based on the actual service performed
as a percentage of the total services to be provided. Estimates of revenues, cost or extent of which
progress towards completion are revised if circumstances change and are reflected in the Statement
of Comprehensive Income in the period in which the circumstances have arisen.
