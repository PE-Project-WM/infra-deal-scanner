Group Strategic Report
for the year ended 31 March 2023
The directors present their Strategic Report of the Company and the Group for the year ended 31 March 2023.
Review of business
The Group has posted strong revenues at £12m (2022 £12.9m) despite the significant impacts noted above
and remained EBITDA positive with £1.4m EBITDA in the last financial year (2022 - £2m).
The profit for the year, after taxation, amounted to £621,000 (2022 - £1,271,000) and the Group ended the
year with £4,501,000 (2021 - £4,343,000) cash balances.
The Group has historically benefited from signing customer contracts with average contract terms of over 2.5
years but due to COVID-19 and the war in Ukraine average contract term was 2.4 years (2022 - 1.9 years)
years and has a high level of recurring revenues (96% of total revenues), from a mix of public and private
business sectors. ~
Order backlog of business awaiting commissioning at the end of the year was £1.5m (2022 - £2.8m). The
decrease reflects improved levels of deliveries to customers and a change in product and service sets with
more cloud computing and security making up over 50% of orders; these orders being delivered in weeks
rather than months.
In order to remain competitive and gain access to new markets the Group maintains 1S027001 Information
Security Management, ISO9001 Quality Management, ISO20000 Service Management and !SO14001
Environmental Management certifications in the light of many public body tenders requiring them. During the
year the Group also published a Carbon Reduction Plan with the aim of becoming net zero by 2030.
The Group serves customers across the United Kingdom including enterprise and public sector organisations.
The Group developed and enhanced services particularly in Cloud Compute and Security in response to
market trends with the acceleration of a shift to cloud-based computing, particularly in the enterprise market.
The Group has had success in selling these services to customers including listed companies and large not
for profit organisations. The Group enhanced its standing in the enterprise marketplace, successfully winning
larger, multi-site and multi-service orders. :
The Company was awarded a place on the Network Services Framework Agreement (RM1045) ("RM1045")
in 2015 as a supplier to the UK Public Sector. This Agreement ran until July 2019. The Company was
successful in securing a place on the Network Services 2 Framework Agreement (RM3808) ("RM3808"), the
RM1045 replacement. These agreements provide a simplified access to network and telecommunications
services. They are designed to help connect Public Sector organisations with a more competitive range of :
suppliers, including more small and medium-sized businesses. hSo operates in the following sections of
RM3808 - Lot 1 - Data Access Services and Lot 3 - Traditional Telephony Services.
RM3808 expires the new financial year and the Group has already tendered for a place on its successor
Network Services 3 RM6116, with the Parent company being awarded a place in August 2023.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Risk acceptance and risk management is addressed through a framework of policies, procedures and internal
controls. Compliance with regulation, legal and ethical standards is a high priority for the Group. Many of
these are covered under the Group's ISO accreditations and subject to regular internal audit and external
annual audit and periodic re-accreditation.
The principal risks for our business arise from:
- Increasing inflation impacting our input prices with the Group possibly not being able to recover the increased
costs leading to reduced profitability;
- Changes in the political and regulatory conditions that may have an impact on the willingness of prospects
and customers to invest in IT infrastructure impacting sales;
- The competitive pricing environment and hence our ability to compete with major UK corporations; and
- Radical change in product sets requiring significant investment in new infrastructure.
The Board considers these risks at regular Board meetings.
Strategy
Revenue growth is expected to come organically from increased product offerings and enhanced routes to
market e.g. channel expansion. In the period the Group has increased its offering in Cloud Services including
private, public and hybrid cloud services. The Group continues to review developments in this area and has
committed to significant expenditure in the coming months.
The Group continues to market and develop its range of high-speed telecoms and IT services to business and |
public sector customers nationally. The substantial and continuous investment in cloud and network services
ensures the Group continues to enhance its standing in the marketplace whilst successfully gaining larger,
multi-site and multi-service customer orders. The Group partners with virtually all the large
telecommunications companies in the UK to provide customers with a wide choice of solutions with high
performance at economic rates.
In addition to organic growth the Group continues to review carefully selected acquisition targets as a way of
increasing revenue, product sets and routes to markets.
Future developments
The Group will continue to roll out enhanced network services by focusing on the provision of secure, flexible
connectivity and collaboration platforms and continue to build the foundations of its comprehensive set of
services in three main areas: Managed Networks; IP Communications and Cloud Computing. The Group has
committed to purchase additional capacity and functionality for its private cloud platforms which were delivered
in summer 2023.
The Board remains vigilant in ensuring measures are being taken to protect the business, the staff and its
customers in the current uncertainty. We are very grateful to the Management and staff for their efforts to cope
with the recent events and their continuing abilities to meet the challenges of the ever-changing situation.

this report the number of the Group's customers that have failed or that are in financial difficulties is minor
and the Group has either agreed deferred payment plans or in the case of some Charities maintained
services with reductions in charges

Basis of consolidation
Subsidiaries are consolidated using the acquisition method. Their results are incorporated from the date
that control passes. The difference between the cost of acquisition of shares in subsidiaries and the fair
value of the separable net assets acquired is capitalised as purchased goodwill and amortised through
the Statement of Comprehensive Income over its estimated economic life. Provision is made for any
impairment. All financial statements are made up to 31 March 2023. All intragroup transactions,
balances and unrealised gains on transactions between group companies are eliminated on
consolidation. Unrealised losses are also eliminated unless the transaction provides evidence of an
impairment of the asset transferred.
Telecommunication circuit charges
Initial telecommunication circuit connection charges from telecommunication carriers are expensed to
the Income Statement spread over the period of the initial contract. The recurring access fees are
expensed in equal instalments over the circuit contract term.
Related party exemption
The Company has taken advantage of exemption, under the terms of Financial Reporting Standard 102
'The Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and Republic of Ireland’, not to disclose related
party transactions with wholly owned subsidiaries within the Group.

Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting end date, the Company and the Group reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible
and intangible fixed assets to determine whether there is any indication those assets have suffered an
impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order
to determine the extent of impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable
amount of an asset, the Company and the Group estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-
generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and the value in use. In assessing value
in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount
rate which reflects the current market assessment of the time value of money and the risks specific to
the asset for which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset is estimated to be less than its carrying amount, the carrying
amount of the asset is reduced to its recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised immediately
in the Income Statement, unless that relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the
impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.
Recognised impairment losses are reversed if the reasons for the impairment loss have ceased to
apply. Where an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the assets is increased
to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but so the increased carrying amount does not exceed
the carrying amount which would have been determined had no impairment loss been recognised for
the asset in prior years. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss,
unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the reversal of the impairment
is treated as a revaluation increase.

{ii) Trade and other receivables
Trade and other receivables are initially recognised at the transaction price, including any transaction

provision for impairment. Amounts that are receivable within one year are measured at the

Where the arrangement with a debtor constitutes a financing transaction, the debtor is initially measured
at the present value of future payments discounted at the market rate of interest for a similar debt
instrument and subsequently measured at amortised cost.
At the end of each reporting period, the Group assesses whether there is objective evidence that a
receivable amount may be impaired. A provision for impairment is established when there is objective
evidence that the Group will not be able to collect all amounts due according to the original terms of the
receivables. The amount of the provision is the difference between the asset's carrying amount and the
present value of the estimated future cash flows, discounted at the effective interest rate. The amount
of the provision is recognised immediately in the Income Statement.
