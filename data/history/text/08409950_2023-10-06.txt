The directors present their report with the audited Financial Statements of Ascensos Limited (the “Group” and/or
“Ascensos’) for the year ended 31 December 2022.
Introduction :
Ascensos is one of Europe's leading independent providers of Customer Experience Management (CXM).
Our specialty is providing excellence in customer service and our leading-edge digital technology ensures the
utmost agility and security. Our owner-led team move fast to provide intelligent and future-proof solutions, bespoke
to every client.
Business review :
2022 represented an important year in the development of the group, and one that has set solid foundations for
strong growth in 2023 and in subsequent years.
After the significant increase in volumes that we experienced during 2021, as expected, activity returned to more
normal levels and Covid specific activity came to an end. This resulted in a 2.8% decline in revenue to £63m
(2021: £65m) as we both repositioned our UK business and continued to invest for the future on the back of
significant account wins.
During the year we increased our stake in Ascensos South Africa (formerly Digital Tribe) to 75.1% as well as our
capacity there, and, during the first quarter of 2023, established a new operation in Trinidad. These investments
leave us well placed to grow our offshore business and take advantage of both current and future opportunities
that exist as a consequence of having a presence in these countries together with customer demand and allied to
our reputation for excellent customer service delivery.
Evaluating the impact of the recent advances in Al is a key focus not only for Ascensos but also for our clients
and our market sector in general. Through our recent investments in both technology and talent we are very well
positioned to respond positively to these rapidly emerging opportunities.

. The reduction in revenues in conjunction with the inflationary impact on costs and our continuing growth plans
resulted in a pretax loss of £0.7m (2021: £4m profit). We view this as a one-off correction and expect to see a
sharp improvement in profitability during 2023/4.

We chose to write off a deferred tax asset in the UK during the year, which resulted in the reported loss after tax
for 2022 (£1.2m) being at a higher level than the pre-tax loss position noted above.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Group’s operations expose it to a variety of financial risks. The Group has exposure to the main risk areas of
liquidity risk and customer credit exposure. To a lesser extent the Group is also exposed to interest rate risk and
foreign exchange. The Group has a tight monitoring system, which allows the directors to act to limit any effects
—___--_-—_—-of-adverse-movements-in-any-of-the-above-risk-factors:—-—£_—
i) Liquidity Risk
The board regularly reviews the Group's liquidity projections to ensure the Group has enough funds to run its
operation. .
ii) Customer Credit Exposure
The Group may offer credit terms to its clients which allow payment of the debt after the delivery of the
services. The Group is at risk to the extent that a customer may be unable to pay the debt on the specified
due date. This risk is mitigated by strong on-going customer relationships, an assessment of an individual
company’s credit risk prior to commencing business with them and finally ongoing monitoring of credit rating
of existing clients via an established third-party service.
iii) Interest Rate Cashflow Risk
The Group has both interest-bearing assets and liabilities. The interest-bearing assets only include cash
balances. The Group borrows from its bankers using either overdrafts or term loans whose tenure depends
on the nature of the asset and management's view of the direction of interest rates.
iv) Foreign Exchange Transactional Currency Exposure
The Group is exposed to currency exchange risk due to a proportion of its payables, receivables and operating
expenses being denominated in non-sterling currencies.
The Group operates hedging instruments to reduce the impact of fluctuations in foreign currency exchange
rates.
v) Loss of akey client .
The Group works to ensure that there is no reliance on one or two key clients alone, and this risk is further
mitigated by strong ongoing customer relationships. As the Group evolves, and the client base broadens out,
this risk is reduced year on year.
vi) Operational Risks
The Group operates a quarterly risk and information management review (RIM), an output of which is a
regularly maintained operational risk register.
Financial key performance indicators
The Group monitors performance through the measurement of Revenue, Gross Margin, EBITDA, EBIT, Cash and
Employee satisfaction data together with a range of client specific measures. These measures will continue to be
the focus of the Group to ensure it delivers for its employees, clients, and shareholders.

Energy efficiency actions
We have previously noted our relationship with a UK based partner who specialises in IT lifecycle management.
This arrangement means that when the devices come to the end of their life with Ascensos, 97% of them will be
refurbished and reused rather than disposed of, thus lowering carbon footprint and e-waste.
Within the office environment, we operate with LED lighting as a standard, resulting in less power usage than that
of normal lighting.
Our buildings operate with sensors meaning that we are not unnecessarily using electricity.
We continually monitor the most efficient use of office space and hybrid working has resulted in being able to
teduce operational space in use at various points with an ability to restore office operations at short notice.
We continue to invest in the latest Video Conferencing (VC) equipment across all sites to further reduce travel.
We endeavour to use where possible, local suppliers as key providers of services to new sites in South Africa and
Trinidad and Tobago to reduce the need for travel by support teams.
Energy efficiency considerations for 2023 and beyond
We are looking to implement a 400-panel solar solution to Motherwell HQ, being the start of a concerted effort to
bring renewable energy as primary supply to all sites possible.
We are currently exploring the potential to offer a salary sacrifice Electric vehicle scheme for senior employees.

The Group operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a
pension plan under which the Group pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the Group has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not paid
are shown in accruals as a liability in the Statement of Financial Position. The assets of the plan are
held separately from the Group in independently administered funds.

Interest income is recognised in profit or loss using the effective interest method.

2.23 Borrowing costs
Alf borrowing costs are recognised in profit or loss in the year in which they are incurred.

2.24 Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the Group a legal or constructive
obligation that probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, anda reliable estimate
can be made of the amount of the obligation.
Provisions are charged as an expense to profit or loss in the year that the Group becomes aware of
the obligation, and are measured at the best estimate at the Statement of Financial Position date of
the expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account relevant risks and uncertainties.
When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the Statement of
Financial Position.
- 2.25 Business combinations
Acquisitions of subsidiaries and businesses are accounted for using the purchase method. The cost
of the business combination is measured at the aggregate of the fair values (at the date of exchange)
of assets given, liabilities incurred or assumed, and equity instruments issued by the Group in
exchange for control of the acquiree, plus costs directly attributable to the business combination.
2.26 Taxation

Current tax is recognised for the amount of income tax payable in respect of the taxable profit for the
current or past reporting periods using the tax rates and laws that that have been enacted or
substantively enacted by the reporting date.
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences at the reporting date, except as otherwise
indicated. Deferred tax assets are only recognised to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits. If and when all
conditions for retaining tax allowances for the cost of a fixed asset have been met, the deferred tax is
reversed.
Deferred tax is calculated using the tax rates and laws that that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the reporting date that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.
Deferred tax liabilities are presented within provisions for liabilities and deferred tax assets within
debtors.
