GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT 
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MAY 2025
INTRODUCTION
The directors are pleased to present the Group Strategic Report for the financial year ended 31 May 2025, which 
consolidates the activities of Green Frog Connect Limited and PHD Electrical Contractors Limited. The period under 
=== page 7 ===
review was characterised by continued delivery of complex 66kV and 132kV grid connection projects, alongside a 
deliberate investment in our workforce and delivery controls. Financial performance was adversely affected by a one-off 
equipment procurement issue impacting a small number of projects, as well as the completion of certain legacy 
underperforming contracts. Further buoyed by PHD's added capabilities in electrical contracting.
REVIEW OF BUSINESS
The Group's principal activities centre on delivering comprehensive Independent Connection Provider (ICP) and 
Balance of Plant (BoP) solutions in the renewable energy and storage sectors, primarily through GFC. During the year 
GFC completed six major 66kV and 132kV projects, commenced works on two further projects and continued delivery 
on others, alongside a number of 33kV schemes. This portfolio reflects the Company's strategic focus on higher-voltage 
work and technically demanding delivery.
For the year ended 31 May 2025 the group reported turnover of £24.8m (FY2024: £47m) and a loss before tax of £1.1m. 
The loss was driven principally by costs of approximately £1.76m arising from a one-off equipment procurement issue 
which largely crystallised in FY2025, despite the majority of revenue on the affected projects having been recognised in 
prior periods. Absent this one-off issue, the directors believe the Company would have been profitable for the year, 
notwithstanding some margin erosion from the close-out of a small number of legacy underperforming contracts.
One-off project cost impacts: FY2025 results were adversely affected by one-off costs arising from quality and 
compliance concerns identified in certain specialist equipment supplied to a small number of projects. Group undertook 
remedial works to ensure safe and compliant energisation; these works are now complete. The Company has notified its 
insurers and any potential recovery has been treated as a contingent asset.
Alongside these matters, the GFC continued to invest in capability and resilience. Following the rapid scale-up 
experienced in FY2024, the directors prioritised strengthening the delivery organisation, including expanding the 
commercial and project controls function, improving contract review discipline and reinforcing civil subcontractor 
management. These actions are intended to reduce delivery risk and improve margin stability on current and future 
projects.
While certain projects faced heightened complexities and resource pressures, our continuous investment in people, 
systems, and processes has helped to maintain overall resilience. These lessons in resource planning and execution will 
underpin stronger margins and operational efficiencies in subsequent periods.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The directors recognise that the group operates in a delivery environment where outcomes are influenced by programme 
pressures, long-lead specialist equipment and the allocation of contractual risk. GFC's principal risks and uncertainties 
therefore relate primarily to the successful delivery of complex, fixed-price infrastructure projects and the associated 
operational, commercial and supply chain exposures. During FY2025 the directors further strengthened the Company's 
commercial governance, project controls and delivery capability, and these improvements form a core part of the 
mitigation approach outlined below.
A key risk remains project delivery and fixed-price exposure, particularly where scope definition, programme 
dependencies, ground conditions and subcontractor performance can materially affect cost and schedule. The Company 
mitigates this through strengthened contract and commercial reviews at bid and mobilisation stages, improved project 
controls and reporting, and earlier identification and escalation of delivery risk. In particular, the Company has increased 
focus on civil subcontractor strategy and management, alongside tighter cost control and change management processes, 
to reduce the likelihood and impact of cost overruns.
The Company is also exposed to supply chain risk, including equipment quality and lead times, which can affect 
programme certainty and cost, especially for specialist high-voltage components and other long-lead items. Mitigations 
include enhanced supplier due diligence, improved technical acceptance and testing processes, diversification of supply 
where feasible, and proactive procurement planning and engagement to manage lead times and reduce disruption.
Contract terms and the risk of claims and disputes are an inherent feature of the markets in which the Company operates, 
with some clients seeking to impose aggressive risk transfer. The Company mitigates this through increased focus on 
securing fair contractual positions, clear internal escalation routes for high-risk terms, disciplined change control and 
record keeping, and early engagement with clients to resolve issues before they develop into formal claims or disputes.
The directors also consider talent retention and delivery capacity to be a principal risk given the competitive market for 
skilled engineering, commercial and project delivery personnel. The Company mitigates this through continued 
investment in recruitment and development, clearer career pathways and progression opportunities, and active workload 
and capacity planning to maintain sustainable utilisation and support consistent delivery performance.
Finally, the Company's ability to execute its pipeline can be influenced by liquidity and bonding requirements, including 
client expectations around performance security. The Company manages this through strong cash controls, pursuing 
appropriate upfront payment structures where possible, close monitoring of working capital by contract, and early 
engagement with clients and advisers regarding security requirements (including performance bonds) to avoid 
constraints to delivery.
FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
Looking ahead, the directors remain optimistic about market fundamentals across renewable generation, energy storage 
and grid infrastructure, and the groups positioning in higher-voltage and niche connection projects. For FY2026, the 
directors expect a resilient level of activity with materially improved margins, supported by strengthened delivery 
controls, increased selectivity in bidding, and a continued focus on fair contractual terms and appropriate risk allocation. 
The groups strategy is to avoid 'race-to-the-bottom' pricing and to prioritise work where its reputation, technical 
capability and delivery record justify appropriate returns.
The group has also commenced investment in a new headquarters facility, supporting future growth and improved 
collaboration. This activity began towards the end of FY2025 and did not have a material impact on the results for the 
=== page 8 ===
year.
MARKET POSITION AND COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE
The year continued to see consolidation within the ICP and grid connections market. The directors believe GFC's 
independence, agility and specialist capability in higher-voltage connections remain key differentiators, particularly as 
connection queue constraints and programme pressures place a premium on experienced delivery partners. The 
Company's strong pipeline in renewable and energy storage projects reflects this positioning.

EMPLOYEES
The groups success depends on the commitment, capability and safety performance of its people. During the year the 
GFC and PHD continued to invest in its workforce to support sustainable delivery at scale. GFC employed 47 people at 
the year end (49 at the date of this report) and expects further growth in headcount to support its pipeline. The directors 
would like to thank all employees and subcontract partners for their contribution during a challenging year.
ENVIRONMENT AND SUSTAINABILITY
The group supports renewable generation, energy storage and grid stabilisation projects and recognises its responsibility 
to manage environmental impacts from its operations and supply chain. During FY2025 progress in emissions 
measurement and reporting was constrained by resource and data limitations; however, GFC has since appointed a 
full-time sustainability and data analyst to strengthen data capture and reporting, and to support the  groups longer-term 
decarbonisation objectives.
CONCLUSION
FY2025 was a challenging year financially due to a significant one-off equipment procurement issue and the close-out of 
certain legacy underperforming contracts in GFC. Notwithstanding this, the directors believe the underlying demand for 
GFC's services remains strong and that the actions taken to strengthen delivery controls, commercial governance and 
workforce capacity position the Company for improved profitability and sustainable growth in FY2026 and beyond.

ACCOUNTING POLICIES
Basis of preparing the financial statements
These financial statements have been prepared in accordance with Financial Reporting Standard 102 "The Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and Republic of Ireland" and the Companies Act 2006. The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention.
Significant judgements and estimates
In the application of the company's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates 
and assumptions about the carrying amount of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other 
sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are based on historical experience and other factors that are 
considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates 
are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised where the revision effects only that period, or in the 
period of the revision and future periods where the revision affects both current and future periods.
Key sources of estimation uncertainty
=== page 21 ===
The estimates and assumptions which have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying 
amount of assets and liabilities are as follows;
- Estimation of stage of completion of construction contracts - The level of revenue recognised for each project 
is directly related to the stage of completion recognised by both the company's own internal quantity surveyors 
together with the customer's own agent.
Turnover
Turnover, which all derives from UK activities, is recognised at the fair value of the consideration received or 
receivable for goods and services provided in the normal course of business, and is shown net of VAT and other 
sales related taxes. The fair value of consideration takes into account trade discounts, settlement discounts and 
volume rebates.
Revenue from the sales of goods is recognised when the significant risks and rewards of ownership of the goods 
have passed to the buyer (usually on dispatch of goods), the amount of revenue can be measured reliably, it is 
probable that the economic benefits associated with the transaction will flow to the entity and the costs incurred 
or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be measured reliably.
Revenue from contracts for the provision of construction of plant and related professional services is recognised 
by reference to the stage of completion when the stage of completion, costs incurred and costs to complete can 
be estimated reliably. The stage of completion is calculated by comparing costs incurred, mainly in relation to 
contractual hourly staff rates and materials, as a proportion of total costs. Where the outcome cannot be 
estimated reliably, revenue is recognised only to the extent of the expenses recognised that it is probable will be 
recovered.
Goodwill
Goodwill, being the amount paid in connection with the acquisition of a business in 2022, is being amortised evenly over its estimated useful life of one years.
Intangible assets
Intangible assets are initially measured at cost. After initial recognition, intangible assets are measured at cost 
less any accumulated amortisation and any accumulated impairment losses.

Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of 
impairment at each reporting end date.
Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that 
occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If 
an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of 
the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is 
recognised in profit and loss.
If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from the event occurring after the impairment was 
recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed 
what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment 
reversal is recognised in profit and loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flow from the asset expires or are 
settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership 
to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has 
transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual 
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of 
the company after deducting all of its liabilities.
Basic financial liabilities
Basic financial liabilities, including creditors, bank loans and loans to fellow group companies, are initially 
recognised at transaction price unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the debt 
instrument is measured at the present value of the future payments discounted at a market rate of interest. 
Financial liabilities classified as payable within one year are not amortised.
Trade creditors are obligations to pay for goods or services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of 
business from suppliers. Amounts payable are classified as current liabilities if payment is due within one year or 
less. If not, they are presented as non-current liabilities. Trade creditors are recognised initially at transaction 
price and subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method.
Taxation
Taxation for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Consolidated Income 
Statement, except to the extent that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in 
equity.
Current or deferred taxation assets and liabilities are not discounted.
Current tax is recognised at the amount of tax payable using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or 
substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.

Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance

timing difference.
Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they 
will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
Employee benefits
