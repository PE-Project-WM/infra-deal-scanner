STRATEGIC REPORT

FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 SEPTEMBER 2023

Introduction

The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 September 2023.

Principal activities

The principal activity of I.P. Integration is the provision of Contact Centres and Customer Engagement solutions

to mid-market, enterprise, and public sector organisations. In conjunction with these technology solutions, IP!

provides consulting services, managed services, and network services that enable organisations to optimise the

customer journey and the customer experience. These solutions and services are now delivered as cloud

services and framed as Contact Centre as a Service (CCaaS).

Business review

The directors are pleased to report that the company had another successful year with strong growth across

revenue and gross profit. This success has been achieved despite the macro-economic challenges post Covid to

include inflation, supply shortages and wars in Europe. The strategy of the company, of moving away from one-

off products & services sales to a recurring revenue model built on cloud services, CCaaS solutions and IPI's

own innovation applications has in the most part been accomplished with recurring revenues now reaching 89%

of overall revenue. This transition to delivering cloud focused applications and services enabling our customers

to benefit from rapid deployment of flexible and business impacting solutions is essential in today’s ever

changing and challenging economic environment.

In the year, overall revenues grew to £32.5m, representing a 19% increase on 2022, with recurring revenue for

the period increasing significantly to £28.9m (restated 2022: £24.1m) representing 20% in year growth and 89%

of total turnover. The recurring growth is attributable to securing multi-year subscription fee contracts for CCaaS

solutions together with attached revenues of connectivity and network services which are typically secured in

conjunction with the CCaaS solution on multiyear contracts whilst remaining focused on industry leading levels of

customer retention through our purpose of exceptional customer contact. The non-recurring element of revenue

which historically would have been made up from the sale of hardware and perpetual software licenses with

associated professional services is essentially now just down to our professional services where we continue to

expand our service offerings such as consulting, training and enablement and applications development and

customer success management.

This year there was an operating loss of £3.4m. However, the directors draw your attention to share based

payment expense of £4.9m being recognised (per note 25) following minority investment by Ethos Partners and

that Net Assets and Capital and Reserves have in fact shown growth in the year. This was achieved despite the

Company recognising a customer being overbilled which also required a prior year adjustment as covered off in

note 2.5.

The gross margin saw a marginal decrease from 51.9% to 47.8%. This is attributable to our sales success and

the winning of some large CCaaS new logos where the margins are lower than our historical averages but add

significant growth to our contract base as can be seen in the Gross Profit increase. 17 new logos were

onboarded within the financial year.

Now with 89% recurring revenues, the strategy for the company is to increase quality of GM% through further

investment in the ongoing development and global sales of our own Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) product

and services portfolio with several new products and new channels being successfully launched through

FY23/24 which we expect will deliver strong and profitable growth opportunities in the coming years.

The increased visibility of both future revenue and future profit is very reassuring to the Directors particularly

whilst there remains uncertainty around the macro-economic environment heading into FY24 and future years.

With 89% of revenues being secured under contract, together with the fact that the services provided by the .

company are critical for our customers, the Directors remain committed to continued investment in the cloud
* platform, our IPR, our people, and our Customer Success Programme for FY24.

People
In the year, the company continued to focus on its programme of investing and developing its people to create
an exceptional culture and work environment. Underpinning the strategy, is the company’s participation in the
Best Places to Work accreditation scheme. In 2023 the company achieved the 2-star rating for ‘Outstanding
levels of workplace engagement’. This continued investment and focus on building and developing talent has
helped the company manage through the challenges of the post Covid workplace and meeting the need to adopt
a more flexible remote working environment whilst still managing to maintain strong engagement and
communication with its people. During the year average headcount was 128 employees.
Investment activity
Investment in the fiscal year was principally focused on the continued development of our own IPR across 3 core
areas: Cloud Platform: building out additional capacity together with the introduction of new platform services. In-
house applications; building on the success of launching ‘Pauseable’ onto the App Foundry Programmes of
Genesys, Nice and Avaya providing channel access and global coverage. Elastic CX; Development of own in-
house CCaa& offering for the Mid-Market which was launched in Q1 FY23. This focus on investing in our own
IPR will not only open new market opportunities for the company but will also assist with customer retention and
gross margin improvement. The IPI Innovation team are building a robust roadmap for the continual
enhancement of our proposition; including the launch of a new analytics proposition at the end of the year and
new adjacent security offerings as part of our DX IT services and cloud business.

2.7 Turnover
Turnover represents net invoiced sale of goods and services and management fee recoveries,
excluding value added tax.
Turnover from the provision of goods is recognised when the risks and rewards of ownership of those

2.9 Leased assets: the company as lessee
Assets obtained under hire purchase contracts and finance leases are capitalised as tangible fixed
assets. Assets acquired by finance lease are depreciated over the shorter of the lease term and their
useful lives. Assets acquired by hire purchase are depreciated over their useful lives. Finance leases
are those where substantially all of the benefits and risks of ownership are assumed by the company.
Obligations under such agreements are included in creditors net of the finance charge allocated to
future periods. The finance element of the rental payment is charged to the Statement of
Comprehensive Income so as to produce a constant periodic rate of charge on the net obligation
outstanding in each period.
2.10 Research and development
In the research phase of an internal project it is not possible to demonstrate that the project will
generate future economic benefits and hence all expenditure on research shall be recognised as an
expense when it is incurred. Intangible assets are recognised from the development phase of a
project if and only if certain specific criteria are met in order to demonstrate the asset will generate
probable future economic benefits and that its cost can be reliably measured. The capitalised
development costs are subsequently amortised on a straight-line basis over their useful economic
lives, which range from 3 to 6 years.
If it is not possible to distinguish between the research phase and the development phase of an
internal project, the expenditure is treated as if it were all incurred in the research phase only.

2.13 Share based payments
Where share options are awarded to employees, the fair value of the options at the date of grant is
