FOR THE YEAR ENDED 28 FEBRUARY 2026
Introduction
The Directors present their Strategic Report for VEA Group Limited together with the audited financial statements for the year ended 28 February 2026.
During the year, the Company remained focused on telecommunications and utility infrastructure services. Through VEA Telecoms, it delivered end-to-end turnkey design, build, pre-enablement, home installation and maintenance services for telecom networks. VEA Civils also expanded its offering to support new clients across the wider multi-utility sector.
Business review
VEA Group continued to strengthen its position in the Fibre to the Home (FTTH) market. During the year, integrated FTTH operations exceeded 140,000 premises passed. By February 2026, maintenance services covered around 1.28 million premises and are expected to exceed 1.4 million by July 2026.
VEA Civils delivered substantial civil engineering and pole access services and expanded its pre-enablement offering to additional clients. During the year, the division secured and completed its first project in the water sector. It also recorded encouraging order book growth for FY27, supported by stronger tender activity and improved win rates.
During the year, the Company launched VEA FM and VEA Highways to pursue opportunities in the Facilities Management and Roads and Highways sectors. Both divisions have made a positive start.
The Company reported turnover of £32.56 million (2025: £21.06 million) and operating profit of £9.80 million (2025: £3.29 million). Net assets increased to £11.56 million (2025: £4.13 million), reflecting continued investment and strong operational delivery.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Directors have considered the key risks and uncertainties facing the business. A summary of the principal risks and the steps taken to mitigate them is set out below:
Contract Management
The Company’s operations are supported by long-term contracts that are periodically renewed or amended. Failure to renew these contracts, or reductions in scope, could create commercial risk.
To mitigate this, the Company focuses on quality delivery, strong health and safety performance, and proactive client engagement, supported by a robust bidding and negotiation framework that has delivered positive outcomes to date.
Skilled Workforce and Supply Chain Dependence
Continued growth depends on access to skilled employees and dependable supply chain partners across the UK. The Company has invested in training, development and succession planning, and its recruitment strategy combines domestic and international hiring for key roles, supported by competitive pay and performance incentives.
The Company also maintains strong relationships with preferred suppliers and subcontractors, pays promptly, and monitors performance closely to protect continuity of delivery.
Health and Safety
The Company undertakes inherently high-risk work, including confined space entry, working at height and handling hazardous materials. A strong safety-first culture is embedded across the business through rigorous policies, procedures and best-practice standards. Safety, Health, Environmental and Quality (SHEQ) teams operate within each division to provide oversight and assurance, while regular reporting keeps the Directors informed of safety performance and compliance.
The Company successfully passed Stage 1 audits for ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 and aims to achieve full certification following the Stage 2 audits scheduled for June 2026.
Economic Environment
Economic volatility may affect client investment decisions, which could in turn influence the volume of work awarded and the commercial terms agreed. The Company maintains regular dialogue with clients to identify and respond to emerging risks, while continuing to diversify its client base and service offering to strengthen resilience.

Basic financial assets

Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. 
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
