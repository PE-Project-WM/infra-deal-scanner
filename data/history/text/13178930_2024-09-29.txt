Strategic report
The directors present their strategic report on the Group for the year ended 31 December 2023.
The Group consists of the parent Sonovate International Holdings Limited (‘the Company’) and its wholly owned
‘ subsidiaries Sonovate Limited, Sonovate US LLC, two dormant entities Sonovate Business Services Limited and
Sonovate US Servicing LLC (‘‘the Subsidiaries”) and a Special Purpose Vehicle (“SPV”) set up in the previous year
for funding purposes (see below). Sonovate Business Services Limited did not trade during the previous year and was
dissolved as of 6 September 2022. . :
The Group is an innovative financial services technology company that supports recruitment agencies and HR
Technology businesses in the contingent labour market. Since 2015, it is providing highly valued financial products
and user experience to its clients.
Review of the business
The audited consolidated financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2023 is set out on pages 14 to 34. The
Group continued to evolve and grow its business in the UK. The Operating loss for the financial year to 31 December
2023 was £10,770,824 (2022: loss of £6,084,323). The statutory loss after tax for the year was £14,713,188 (2022:
: “loss £6,796,954). These losses are a result of continued investment into software development and personnel. At the
balance sheet date, the Group had total net liabilities of £2,583,792 (2022: net assets £11,529,173) as a result of the
losses during the year. .
Over the course of the year ended 31 December 2023, the Group processed and funded £1,192,123,731 of invoices
(2022: £1,103,640,859) an increase of 8.0% (2022: 58.3%) over the previous year. Cumulatively to 31 December
2023 the Group has provided over £5bn of funding to businesses.
Trade debtors, which include outstanding amounts on invoices funded, reduced from £154,213,892 as at31 December
2022 to £141,655,835 as at 31 December 2023 representing a decrease of 8.1% (2022: 47.4%). This decrease in trade
debtors with an increase of invoices funded is a result of investment and restructure of the credit control department.
Consolidated revenue was £17,812,105 in the year to 31 December 2022 in comparison to £23,613,600 for the year
to 31 December 2023 representing an increase of 32.6% (2022: 51.9%). The continued growth in revenue is due to
significant investment within sales, marketing, and tech and a fee review for existing clients and subscriptions fees
following the new platform going live during the year. :
The Group continues to invest considerable resources into the development of software which enables recruitment
agencies and other providers of contractors and contingent workers to process contracts, invoices, payments, and the
provision of working capital funding with the objective of becoming the supplier of choice for such services. Its .
technology allows it to offer its services via an API in a “Lending as a Service” type product. During the year, the
Group released a new enhanced software platform and will slowly phase out the oid platforms. This new platform was
essential to handle the Group’s projected growth and to provide existing and new customers an improved experience
and allow the group to open up new revenue streams. The Group provides significant liquidity into UK small
businesses and are an enabler of UK small business growth.

Funding and liquidity ,
During the previous year, in June 2022, the Group has made a significant investment to expand the sources of funding
by refinancing existing facilities with a private securitisation facility and setting up a “bankruptcy-remote” Irish
special purpose vehicle (“SPV”), Sonovate Trade Receivables Designated Activity Company. This is a single purpose
entity established for the securitisation facility and is considered “bankruptcy-remote” as its sole purpose is to
administer the securitisation of the company’s receivables and is prohibited from incurring other debt or obligations. :
The Company’s subsidiary, Sonovate Limited, now securitises certain receivables assets via the sale of these assets to
the SPV, which in turn issues asset-backed securities to investors. During the year, the Group agreed a loan syndication
for the senior loan facility. An additional loan provider was required to support the company’s growing volumes.
In support of this initiative, a state-of-the-art data warehouse was built to enable the Group to fund thousands of
invoices on a daily basis. This effort is already yielding fruit as it has significantly reduced the blended cost of funds
as well as increased the advance rates (i.e. loan to value) that allows Sonovate Limited to scale up its own operations. .
As at 31 December 2023, as a result of the securitisation and the loan syndication the Group is funded by the following
facilities:
© Senior facility £200m (£100m each) - the Irish SPV has issued class A senior debt notes to BNP Paribas and
Lloyds Bank with a maximum 82% advance rate paying 1.5% over Sterling Overnight Index Average
(SONTA).
© Mezzanine facility £40m — the Irish SPV issued class B mezzanine notes to M&G Investments for up to 93%
advance rate and a coupon of 9% over SONIA.
e =A revolving £30m loan facility agreement between Sonovate International Holdings Limited and BCI Capital
Limited which is used to fund the vehicle’s subordinated notes for the remainder 7% advance rate as well as
ongoing operational expenses.
Principal risks and uncertainties .
The Group is subject to principal risks and uncertainties as detailed below:
Continued access to funding and liquidity
In order to lend into UK small business, the Company requires access to a committed funding line either through
equity or debt capital. This is managed by the group and as detailed above the Senior Financing Facility was
refinanced in June 2022 with a private securitisation facility for £150m of senior notes held by a large European Bank, :
which was subsequently increased to £200m in August 2023 through a note syndication and a £40m mezzanine notes
facility issued to a UK asset manager which are both now held in a special purpose vehicle ‘Sonovate Trade
Receivables Designated Activity Company’.
A £20m revolving loan facility was also agreed in June 2022 (extended to £30m in May 2023) with an expiry date of
June 2024. This was extended to June 2025 subsequent to the year end.
The Group refinanced again in June 2024 extending the maturity of ‘the commitment until June 2025.
Credit risk
The Group is exposed to the risk that debtors do not settle debts in a complete and/or timely manner. The Group has
in place credit risk models to ensure that lending levels are appropriate for the credit risk profile of the debtor. The
vast majority of funding is credit insured which significantly mitigates the risk of non-collection. This risk is :
monitored closely in terms of the delinquency of the invoices funded and the proportion of debt that is uninsured. The
Group has recorded a bad debt provision in the period ended 31 December 2023 of £547,385 (2022: £111,522) against
debtors that are unlikely to be recoverable. No debts have been written off during the year; for debts that have not
been written off at 31 December 2023 attempts are continuing to recover the sums due.

The principal activity of the Group during the year was the provision of a tech-enabled solution to recruitment agencies
and other providers of contractors and contingent workers to process contracts, invoices, payments and provide

The financial performance is set out on pages 14 to 34 and is discussed in the Strategic Report. The directors do not

Political and charitable contributions ,
The Group made no political and charitable donations during the period (2022: £nil).
Going concern
The performance of the Group is driven by its ability to attract new customers onto the platform, enable customers to
grow organically through the platform, effectively manage the performance of the debtor portfolio, manage equity
and debt funding requirements and associated costs, and manage operational costs.
As a provider of financing to customers, the funding requirements of the business is a key management activity as it
is required to grow, and therefore be renewed, on a continual basis as the business scales. The Group and its
management team are therefore focused on continuing to monitor, forecast and discuss with lenders and investors,
both existing and potential.
In June 2022, the Senior Financing Facility was refinanced with a private securitisation facility for £150m of senior
notes held by a large European Bank (increased to £200m in August 2023 through a loan syndication) and £30m
mezzanine notes issued to a UK asset manager (extended to £40m in August 2023) both running until a renewal date
of 30 June 2025 and ultimate maturity date of 27 December 2025. In addition, on 29 June 2022, the group entered
into a new 24-month revolving loan facility of £20m (extended to £30m in May 2023). .
As well as the funding facilities above, additional equity of £10m was raised from existing investors in May 2022.
This funding and enlarged facilities was required to support the Group’s forecast volumes. However, the Group is
currently preparing for the next refinancing round which will extend again the tenor of the facility as well as selectively
discussing with potential investors a potential equity capital injection to enable the next stage of growth. This is
expected to complete in early 2025.
At the time of approving the financial statements, the process to renew existing facilities as well as raise additional
equity and/or commitment has commenced but has not been secured. Therefore, these events and conditions, along
with the other matters explained in note 1.3, constitute a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the
Group’s and the parent Company’s ability to continue as a going concern.
Further consideration is set out in note 1.3 to the financial statements.

Financial risk management
The Group’s activities expose it to a variety of financial risks: credit risk, liquidity risk and market risk (including
interest rate risk and currency risk).
Interest rate risk
The Group’s exposure to interest rate risk is minimal. While the Group’s financing is linked to underlying interest rate .
. benchmarks, it has the ability to reprice and adjust its income based on a number of factors including movement in
underlying interest rates.
Liquidity and capital risk
The Group manages liquidity and capital risk through its access to various sources of debt and equity funding. During
the year the Group agreed a loan syndication to increase the senior financing facility to £200m and increased the
revolving loan to £30m.
The Group is currently working on the next refinance and launching their Series C equity fundraising which will
enable the next stage of growth.
Currency risk
The Group transacts in USD, EUR, CHF, CAD, DKK, SEK, NOK and AUD although the vast majority of business
is in GBP. In cases where the Group funds customers in GBP, USD or EUR (Matched Currencies), the £200m private
warehouse allows it to draw funds in matching currency which mitigates any potential currency risk. For the Non-
matched Currencies, the Group draws against the SPV’s GBP note and deals with the currency exposure by reserving
a generous amount in cash to mitigate this risk. However, with the debt turn standing at 40 days, the likely deviation
in currency pairs in that term is rather small. .

24 Share based payments :
Equity-settled arrangements are measured at fair value (excluding the effect of non— market based vesting conditions)
at the date of the grant. The fair value is expensed on a straight-line basis over the vesting period. The amount
recognised as an expense is adjusted to reflect the actual number of shares or options that will vest. Where equity- .
settled arrangements are modified, and are of benefit to the employee, the incremental fair value is recognised over the
: period from the date of modification to date of vesting. Where a modification is not beneficial to the employee there is
no change to the charge for share-based payment. Settlements and cancellations are treated as an acceleration of vesting
and the unvested amount is recognised immediately in the profit and loss. The group has no cash-settled arrangements.
