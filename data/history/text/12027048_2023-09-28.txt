Strategic Report for the Year Ended 29 November 2022
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 29 November 2022. 
Principal activity
The principal activity of the group is is to act as a holding company.
The principal activities of the subsidiary companies are the supply, distribution and support to business of high quality voice and data solutions.
Fair review of the business
During the year ended 29 November 2022, the plans of Trust Distribution Ltd    (“Trust”) and SOS Communications Ltd ("SOS") continued to be impacted by the pandemic. Our overriding priority has continued to be the stability of our sales and profitability, this year was when we felt the full impact of Panasonic leaving the telecoms market permanently and Mitel stopping production of the MiVoice250 phone system.  This was our best seller, and we unfortunately lost a lot of customers to other vendors due to this, vendors which we didn’t have in our portfolio.  We also have seen our market shrink by approx. 100 resellers as the 6/7 large telecoms channel companies embarked on their acquisition journeys.
=== page 11 ===
Trust has seen an accumulative £8million downturn in revenue 2020-2022 as a result of the disruption detailed above.  However we added in new revenue of £2million in 2022 by finding new products and services to take to market.  This came at a large cost as we invested heavily in new products and infrastructure, to guide the business into its new “hybrid” sales, not just “on premises” sales but moving into Cloud based recurring revenue products and services. Interest rates increased as did our borrowings, reflecting again the disruption detailed above and as shareholders and directors we continue to keep a tight control on the companies finances.
Despite the continued disruption as seen in 2020/21, Trust, in 2022, produced another credible year of trading with turnover of £12.7m. with gross margin being £1.9m (down from 2021).  SOS has experienced a similar impact.
It’s important to point out that as we publish these 2022 Accounts that the 1st 7 trading months of 2023 the group has seen a dramatic positive change we have almost halved the 2020-2022 profit deficit, and the group’s profitability is on a steep upward trajectory as we see the 2022 investments into new products and services start to come to fruition.  In addition through natural progression staffing levels have lowered adding to reduced monthly salary costs.  The company has had to transition quickly into the new hybrid world, of less hardware sales and more cloud based sales to mitigate the unseen impacts the pandemic caused and the investments costs/turnover and profit losses we incurred in 2022 unfortunately is still a direct result of the pandemic, however, as stated, 2023 has become a year of seeing that turn around.
Our new Cloud based and Licence services are growing well as are our Hospitality Phones sales, Trust is the exclusive distributor of Vtech Hospitality phones across EMEA…. 
We have also seen our SIP and Cloud services company TruSIP Ltd grow at a remarkable rate, and as a 60% shareholder, The group is experiencing the benefit of that too……….2023 will finish strong 
We continue to maintain strong relationships with our banks and suppliers.  These relationships, combined with the support provided by the shareholders through the year, is key to the ongoing success of the group .

Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risks and uncertainties facing the business are explained in more detail above, but in summary relate to: 
Market disruption
Reference the notes above, the group is looking to mitigate these risks by developing new revenue streams and customer bases.
Credit risk
The directors are involved in the operation of the group on a day to day basis, and as such are well placed to keep a firm control on all debt, and maintain close relationships with all customers.
Liquidity risk
Likewise the close involvement of the directors in all aspects of the group’s operations means they are well placed to monitor the cost basis, and maintain close relationships with all suppliers and funders.
Interest rate risks
The rise in interest rates has inevitably impacted the reported results.
Key performance indicators
The directors are closely involved in the operations of the business, and as such do not require formal reporting of key performance indicators.
The indicators of most relevance in current market conditions relate to revenue and margins, as referred to in the review above.

Leases
Leases in which substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership are retained by the lessor are classified as operating leases. Payments made under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the period of the lease.
 Leases are classified as finance leases whenever the terms of the lease transfer substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee.
 Assets held under finance leases are recognised at the lower of their fair value at inception of the lease and the present value of the minimum lease payments. These assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis over the shorter of the useful life of the asset and the lease term. The corresponding liability to the lessor is included in the statement of financial position as a finance lease obligation.
 Lease payments are apportioned between finance costs in the income statement and reduction of the lease obligation so as to achieve a constant periodic rate of interest on the remaining balance of the liability.

 Contributions to defined contribution plans are recognised as employee benefit expense when they are due. If contribution payments exceed the contribution due for service, the excess is recognised as a prepayment.
