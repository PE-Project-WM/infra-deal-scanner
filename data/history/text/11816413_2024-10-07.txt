STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2023

Introduction

This strategic report provides an overview of the performance, position, and prospects of Expereo UK, a

subsidiary of Expereo International B.V. for the year ended 31 December 2023.

Expereo UK is the leading provider of managed network and cloud connectivity solutions for enterprise and

wholesale channel.

Business review

On 5 August 2022 (the "Valuation Date”) Expereo UK Ltd acquired a 100% stake in Breeze Networks Ltd and

subsequently hived up the trade and net assets, then on the 7 September 2023 Breeze Networks Ltd was

wound up voluntary. For further details on the acquisition, please refer to note 13.

Our business model is to deliver reliable, secure, and flexible network and cloud connectivity solutions to our

customers, leveraging our global network of partners and suppliers, our proprietary software platform, and our

local expertise and support. Our solutions include:

¢ Managed internet access and SD-WAN: We provide high-speed, cost-efficient, and resilient internet access
and software-defined wide area network (SD-WAN) solutions that enable our customers to connect their
offices, data centers, and cloud applications across the globe,’

e Managed cloud acceleration and optimization: We offer cloud acceleration and optimization solutions that
enhance the performance, security, and visibility of our customers’ cloud applications and services

« Managed security and compliance: We deliver security and compliance solutions that protect our
customers’ network and cloud infrastructure, data, and users from cyber threats and ensure compliance
with industry standards and regulations.

Our strategy is to grow our business by:

e« Expanding our customer base and retention: We aim to attract and retain more customers from various
sectors and sizes, by offering competitive pricing, superior quality, and excellent customer service,

¢ Diversifying our product portfolio and innovation: We seek to diversify our product portfolio by introducing
new and innovative solutions that address the evolving needs and challenges of our customers,

e Enhancing our operational efficiency and agility: We strive to enhance our operational efficiency and agility
by optimizing our processes, systems, and resources, and by leveraging our global network of partners and
suppliers, our proprietary software platform, and our local expertise and support.

Our key achievements and challenges in 2023:

« We increased our revenue vs previous year by 30%, 15% it's related to the growth we registered based on
our former customers and the rest it's due to new customers onboarded over 2023, .

¢ We improved our profitability vs previous years, mainly due to our operational efficiency and cost savings,

, as well as our value-added services and cross-selling opportunities,

e We expanded our customer base vs previous years, including several large and prestigious accounts,
such as Unilever, Carlsberg and HSBC.

However, we also faced some challenges in 2023, such as:

¢ The increased competition and price pressure from other network and cloud service providers, both local
and global, which required us to differentiate and innovate our offerings and value proposition,

* The emerging and evolving cyber threats and regulatory requirements, which required us to invest and
enhance our security and compliance capabilities and measures.

Future developments
In 2024, Expereo UK will continue to pursue its vision of becoming the leading provider of global managed
network services and cloud connectivity solutions. We will focus on four key areas of development: expanding
our global footprint, enhancing our product portfolio, improving our customer experience, and strengthening our
social and environmental responsibility.
Expanding our global footprint: We will leverage our strategic partnerships with over 3.000 local service
providers and our presence in 190 countries to extend our reach and coverage to new and existing markets. We
will invest in building and upgrading our network infrastructure, including our 50+ cloud acceleration hubs, to
ensure high performance, reliability, and security for our customers. We will also explore new opportunities for
mergers and acquisitions, joint ventures, and alliances with other players in the industry, to increase our market
share and competitive advantage.
Enhancing our product portfolio: We will continue to innovate and develop our product offerings, to meet the
evolving needs and expectations of our customers. We will launch new solutions for software-defined wide area
network (SD-WAN), internet backbone, cloud access, and network intelligence, as well as enhance our existing
solutions for internet access, ethernet, virtual private network (VPN), and mobile data. We will also leverage our
expertise and experience in cloud-native technologies, artificial intelligence, and automation, to deliver agile,
scalable, and cost-effective solutions for our customers.
Improving our customer experience: We will strive to provide the best possible customer experience, from pre-
sales to post-sales, across all touchpoints and channels. We will invest in upgrading our customer relationship
management (CRM) system, enhancing our online portal and mobile app, and providing more self-service
options and tools for our customers. We will also improve our customer support, by increasing our staff
capacity, training, and quality standards, and by implementing customer feedback mechanisms and satisfaction
surveys. We will aim to achieve high levels of customer retention, loyalty, and advocacy.
Strengthening our social and environmental responsibility: We will uphold our commitment to being a
responsible and sustainable business, by aligning our operations and activities with the United Nations
Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the Paris Agreement on climate change. We will implement
policies and practices to reduce our environmental impact, such as minimizing our energy consumption, carbon
emissions, and waste generation, and increasing our use of renewable energy sources and recycled materials.
We will also support our social impact, by fostering a diverse, inclusive, and healthy workplace culture, by
promoting ethical and fair business practices, and by contributing to the communities where we operate,
through charitable donations, volunteering, and sponsorships.
We are confident that these future developments will enable us to achieve our strategic objectives and financial
targets, and to create long-term value and impact for our shareholders, customers, employees, partners, and
society at large.

2.8 Finance costs
Finance costs are charged to the Statement of income and retained earnings over the term of the
debt using the effective interest method so that the amount charged is at a constant rate on the
carrying amount. Issue costs are initially recognised as a reduction in the proceeds of the associated
capital instrument.
2.9 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a
pension plan under which the company pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the company has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not
paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the Statement of financial position. The assets of the plan
are held separately from the company in independently administered funds.
2.10 Current and deferred taxation
The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Statement
of income and retained earnings except that a charge attributable to an item of income and expense
recognised as other comprehensive income or to an item recognised directly in equity is also
recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date in the countries where the company operates
and generates income.
Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not
reversed by the reporting date, except that:
° The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will
be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits; and
° Any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
allowances have been met.
Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of ;
business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair values
of assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences between the
fair values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax. Deferred tax is
determined using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the
reporting date.

2.11 Intangible assets
Goodwill
Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and
the acquirer's interest in the fair value of its identifiable assets and liabilities of the acquiree at the
date of acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost less accumulated .
amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a straight-line basis to
the Statement of income and retained earnings over its useful economic life.
All intangible assets are considered to have a finite useful life. If a reliable estimate of the useful life
cannot be made, the useful life shall not exceed ten years.
2.12 Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable
of operating in the manner intended by management.
Depreciation is charged so as to allocate the cost of assets less their residual value over their
estimated useful lives, using the straight-line method.
The estimated useful lives range as follows:
Fixtures and fittings - 2.5 years straight line

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 “Basic Financial Instruments” of FRS

Financial instruments are recognised in the company's Statement of financial position when the

Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include trade and other receivables, cash and bank balances, are
initially measured at their transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried
at their amortised cost using the effective interest method, less any provision for impairment, unless
the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the
present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.
Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial. The company's cash and cash
equivalents, trade and most other receivables due with the operating cycle fall into this category of
financial instruments.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting date.
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the
estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The
impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of
the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the
impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying
amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in the profit or loss.
