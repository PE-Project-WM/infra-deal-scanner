Business review 
Chess continued to focus on gross profit growth and cash generation, helping our customers and partners transition their business with modern secure workplace solutions. Our focus on driving operational efficiency continued, supporting our vision to be a great place to work and a great place to be a customer. Our year-end run rate performance was enhanced by the completion of acquisitions towards the end of the year. 
Gross profit for FY23 of £25.5m (39.7%) compares favourably with £25.3m (36.7%) in FY22, highlighting Chess’ growth in profitability as we continue to transition our customers from connect to cloud revenues. Improved margins are being achieved on device and mobile sales whilst, pleasingly, our digital business continues to grow.
Cyber Security licence revenue and gross profit reduced by £2.4m and £150k respectively, as expected with the annual renewal cycle profile. However, it was encouraging to see growth in our specialist penetration testing business and licence revenues forecast to rise in line with future renewal timings.
Cloud revenues grew as we continue to transition our customers in advance of the PSTN switch off in 2025, the decrease in connect revenues an expected trend, albeit part of an overall gp% improvement across our portfolio of products and services.
Wage inflation in the year of £1.5m resulted in a temporary reduction in EBITDA, prior to the full benefit of the operational efficiencies made impacting our results, driving continued improvement in FY24 to date. 
EBITDA to operating cash generation of 96% highlights the strength of our recurring revenue streams, further benefited by larger consultancy, installation and hardware sales and the resulting profit and cash inflows. 
Chess people remain our most important asset and we were delighted to achieve Top 100 ‘Best Company’ status again for the 15th consecutive year, being awarded 1st place in North West and 2nd place in the UK large business categories. We continue to invest in our people through our learn2earn scheme, retaining great people who develop skills and accreditations, enabling us to collaborate with our customers on their long-term growth strategy. We maintained our ISO27001 and ISO9001 status. 
Our people continue to drive our ESG agenda, a framework of projects agreed during the year, reviewed in monthly meetings with main board representation. We are passionate about providing opportunity and working with our suppliers and customers to deliver a better future.

Principal activities

∙

Financial risk management objectives and policies
=== page 12 ===
The Group is exposed to a variety of financial risks that include the effects of changes in debt market prices, credit risk, liquidity risk and interest rate risk (see principal risks and uncertainties). The Group has in place a risk management programme that seeks to limit the adverse effects on the financial performance of the Group by monitoring levels of debt finance and the related finance costs. Given the size of the Group, the directors have not delegated the responsibility of monitoring financial risk management to a sub-committee of the board. The policies set by the board of directors are implemented by the Group's Finance department.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Like all businesses, Chess faces a range of risks and uncertainties that could impact the delivery of our long-term strategy, spanning operational, finance, and regulatory areas. The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Group are monitored with a Risk Register. The board meets once a month to identify, evaluate and manage our exposure to known risks to all divisions of the business. For the risks within our control, the board puts appropriate processes and controls in place to mitigate them. The principal risks and uncertainties facing the business are set out below.
Acquisition integration risk
Acquisitions have driven the significant growth of the Group over the past few years and remains a key part of our future strategy. Acquisitions by their nature have the potential to cause operational, cultural and system issues during the integration phase, all of which can impact the ability of the business to deliver on the expected financial performance. To mitigate this risk, there is a detailed planning and due diligence process run by a dedicated acquisitions team prior acquisition and board level approval is required on acquisition agreements and payments.
During the integration phase, there are regular peer-to-peer senior management meetings to respond to day-to-day complications with the assimilation process, as well as consultation meetings with new People and a strategy focused away day to ensure we deliver on performance. Where possible, an element of the acquisition consideration is contingent upon performance of the acquisition, which encourages all parties to work together to overcome any issues that arise as soon as possible.
Market risk
The ICT, Telecommunications and Security markets are constantly evolving, and failure to keep abreast of emerging product trends could impact financial performance and growth. There is a risk that the business is unable to deliver on customers' requirements or that we invest in areas that do not generate the expected demand. Chess monitors market developments closely by having active dialogue with suppliers to keep abreast of market changes and training our People on the latest technological developments. We also undertake strategic reviews of the broader market periodically, as part of our budgetary process.
Chess puts significant internal resource into new product development with a team of People focused in this area and regularly launches new products based on customer feedback and needs.
Liquidity and interest rate cash flow risk
The Group's policy is to finance its operations and expansion through working capital. The interest rate on long-term loans used to finance acquisitions was linked to LIBOR ( this was migrated to a SOFR as at the end of December 2021) and therefore the Group is exposed to interest rate risk on the loan. The Group's banking facilities also includes a financial leverage covenant, to manage this risk the Group regularly reviews forecasts to ensure continued compliance with the financial covenants and continued availability of the banking facilities.

Credit risk
The Group's credit risk is primarily attributed to its trade debtors. The amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of any allowance for doubtful debtors, estimated by the directors. The Group has  no significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread across our large and diverse customer base. The trade receivable balances are monitored on an ongoing basis to ensure that the Group's exposure to bad debt is not significant and appropriate provision for bad debt risk is made. The Group also requires appropriate credit checks on potential customers before sales are made.
Data protection and cyber security
The Group holds a significant volume of personal information, both confidential and sensitive in nature. Failure to comply with data privacy regulations, such as GDPR, and adopting best practices across information network security may result in potential data breach(es) across our business, which would cause detrimental brand damage to us and loss of confidence and privacy to our customers. The Group has a dedicated Information Security Officer to oversee operational compliance towards all data privacy regulations and to instil the security best practices across our IT Network infrastructure, most notably information security and Cyber Essentials accreditations. Our Group holds the ISO 27001 standard and subsequently carries out regular penetration testing, and system upgrades to meet the audit requirements.
Cyber Essentials has been implemented across our key operational sites and is subject to regular and frequent audits as to its effectiveness. The Group also holds the ISO 9001 accreditation which drives continual improvement, regulatory compliance and customer satisfaction across the business, our internal audit team are responsible for the ongoing maintenance of the quality standards and raise opportunities for improvement. should any nonconformities arise.
General economic risk
The Company, like other businesses, recognises that the current economic uncertainty is likely to continue. Therefore, the Group continually reviews its forecasts including any impact on consumer confidence to monitor the general economic climate.

Section 172(1) statement
In performing their duties under section 172 (1) (a) to (f) of the Companies Act 2006, the directors of the Group, whilst aiming to promote the success of the business, also remain conscious of the impact their decisions have (amongst others) on:
a) the likely consequences of any decision in the long-term;
b) the interests of the Company's employees;
c) the need to foster the Company's business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;
d) the impact of the Company's operations on the community and the environment;
e) the desirability of the Company's maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct: and
f) the need to act fairly as between members of the Company.
The board understands the views of all stakeholders and have considered the matters set out in section 172(1) (a) to (f) of the Companies Act 2006 so as to act in the interest of all stakeholders and make sure all parties are treated fairly.
The following examples explain how the directors fulfil their duties in respect of these matters:
Our People
The health, safety and wellbeing of our People is paramount to the way we operate as a business. We have facilitated a 'home first philosophy', moving all of our people to online working from home, with hybrid options.
Chess recognises that the quality and commitment of our People is our greatest asset. they have been fundamental to our success as a business and are key to the delivery of our long-term strategy. Chess provides opportunities for our People to make positive contributions  to the  development  of the business and the wider community. We are committed to cultivating performance and nurturing talent and we aim to be a responsible employer in our approach to the pay and benefits our People receive. Chess recognises the importance of physical and mental Wellbeing, opportunities for personal growth and inclusion of all. Our 3- star 'world class to work for' Best Companies status, is an honour we share with our People and one we feel immensely proud of.
Stakeholders
The board are committed to openly engaging with our stakeholders, as we recognise the importance of a continuing effective dialogue, whether with our investors, suppliers, customers,  or our People. The board are committed to fully communicating the Group's strategy, vision, governance, and performance with all its stakeholders, and always tries to ensure that it has visibility of these relationships so that it can consider its' stakeholders when making key decisions for the business.
Culture, values, and standards

2.5
Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that the Group obtains the right to consideration in exchange for its performance. Turnover is measured at the fair value of the consideration received, excluding discounts, rebates, VAT and other sales taxes or duty.
Call turnover
Call turnover is recognised in the month in which calls are made. Calls made in the year, but not billed by the year-end are accrued within debtors as accrued income.
Line rental
Line rental income is recognised during the month of rental.
Handsets
Turnover and related costs from the sale of handsets are recognised on the date of connection.
Connection commissions
Connection commissions are receivable from mobile network operators when the Group connects or retains end users to their network. The commissions are recognised as income by the Group over the period of service required by the commission agreement. When the Group assesses that there is an ongoing obligation beyond connection, commission is recognised evenly over the period of the obligation. When the Group assesses that there is no ongoing obligation beyond connection, the full commission expected to be recovered over the life of the contract is recognised upon connection and an associated accrued income balance is recognised within "Amounts due from network operators" as part of the overall debtors balance. This balance due from network operators is typically recoverable over the term of the contract as commission payments are received from the mobile network operators.
The full commission to be received over the life of the contract is dependent on usage, and so related spend, by the consumer. As such, the consideration receivable by the Group is variable and involves estimation (see note 3 where this has been disclosed as a key source of estimation uncertainty and how this estimate is derived). Variable consideration is recognised as revenue to the extent that it is highly probable that a significant reversal shall not occur in the future.
In some instances commission income is subject to adjustment by the mobile network operator. Where this is the case, the Group assesses the likelihood of adjustment by reference to historical experience and adjusts commissions income accordingly.
Long-term contracts
These contracts are accounted for in accordance with the percentage of completion guidance in Section 23 of FRS 102. The only contracts that are accounted for in this manner are support service contracts, which typically have a term of between 12 months and 60 months.
The percentage of completion is measured by reference to the number of days completed on a contract at the balance sheet date, compared to the total expected number of days required on that contract. When it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue the expected loss shall be recognised as an expense immediately with a corresponding provision for an onerous contract.

2.25
Impairment of non-financial assets
At each balance sheet date non-financial assets not carried at fair value are assessed to determine whether there is an indication that the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) may be impaired. If there is such an indication the recoverable amount of the asset (or asset's cash generating unit) is compared to the carrying amount of the asset (or asset's cash generating unit).
The recoverable amount of the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) is the higher of the fair value less costs to sell and value in use. Value in use is defined as the present value  of the future  cash flows before interest and tax obtainable as a result of the asset's (or asset's cash generating unit) continued use. These cash flows are discounted using a pre-tax discount rate that represents the current market risk free and the risks inherent in the assets.
If the recoverable amount of the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) is estimated to be lower than the carrying amount, the carrying amount is reduced to its recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised in the profit and loss account.
If an impairment loss is subsequently reversed, the carrying amount of the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but only to the extent that the revised carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of depreciation or amortisation) had no impairment loss been recognised in prior periods. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised in the profit and loss account.
Goodwill is allocated on acquisition to the cash generating unit expected to benefit from the synergies of the combination. Goodwill is included in the carrying value of cash generating units for impairment testing.

Judgements in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In the application of the Group's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions about the carrying amount of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are based on historical experience and other factors that are considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised if the revision affects only that period, or in the period of the revision and future periods if the revision affects both current and future periods.
The directors consider that the following judgements including key sources of estimation uncertainty have had the most significant effect on amounts recognised in the financial statements:
Commissions 
Judgement is applied to determine whether commission contracts contain performance obligations beyond connection. Management forms this judgement based on the terms of the contract. The revenue recognition policy note details the treatment applied in the cases where there are ongoing performance obligations and also where all obligations are complete upon connection.
The commission recognised as receivable from network operators and the commission payable to partners, are based on expected spend on the mobile contract by the end user over the life of the contract.
When the Group assesses that there is not an ongoing obligation beyond connection, commission is recognised upon connection. This is therefore initially based on an expectation of what the future spend will be by end user. Where available, such as on a customer renewing a contract, this estimate is based on historic information specific to that customer. Where it is a new customer, the estimate is based on the average revenue per user over the entire mobile base. As such there is inherent estimation in the amounts recognised and this reconsidered by management throughout the life of the contract.
Share-based payments 
The Group measures the cost of equity-settled transactions with employees by reference to the fair value of the equity instruments at the date at which they are granted. Judgement is required in determining the most appropriate valuation model for a grant of equity instruments, depending on the terms and conditions of the grant. Management is also required to use estimation in determining the most appropriate inputs to the valuation model including expected life of the option; volatility and dividend yield. The assumptions and models used are disclosed in the notes to the financial statements.

Intangible assets and goodwill carrying values

The recoverable amount is the higher of an assets fair value less costs to and its value in use. The value in use calculation requires estimation of future discounted cash flows.

Contingent consideration relating to acquisitions is included based on management estimates of the most likely outcome. In January 2016 the Company established an employees' joint share ownership plan ('the JSOP') under which selected employees of the Company were invited to acquire interests, as joint beneficial owners upon the terms of a joint ownership agreement ('JOA'), in a total of 600,962 shares in the Company held by the Trust. ∙
the initial acquisition of the employee's interest, as joint Owner, in the Jointly Owned Shares: and
∙
the division of the proceeds of sale, as between the employee and the Trust, when vested Jointly Owned Shares are sold or otherwise disposed of.
In particular, the JOA provides that the proceeds of sale of vested Jointly Owned Shares will be divided unequally between the employee and the Trust so that the employee will receive all of the growth in value of the Jointly Owned Shares above a threshold amount of £24.96 per share (less a fixed rate of interest on their initial value), and the Trust will be entitled to the balance, i.e. the threshold amount of the Jointly Owned Shares plus the interest calculated on that amount ("the  Hurdle Amount'). If the disposal proceeds are Less than the Hurdle Amount, the Trust will be entitled to the whole of the proceeds (less a nominal amount).

If an employee's interest in Jointly Owned Shares does not become 'vested', the Trust will have the right to buy back the interest for a nominal consideration.

32.
Post balance sheet events
On 16 June 2023, the Group headed by Chess Limited disposed of its 100% ownership in two subsidiaries, Chess Cybersecurity Limited and Armadillo Sec Limited, to Chess T2 Limited, a company under common control incorporated in England and Wales. The total consideration received in respect of these disposals was £2.96m, realising a profit of £862k in the financial year ended 30 April 2024.
On 31 January 2024, the directors refinanced all facilities, with a revised termination date of 31 January 2026. The renewed facilities include a further option for both parties to extend the termination date by one year to 31 January 2027, and subsequently for a further year to 31 January 2028, subject to ongoing compliance with relevant adjusted leverage covenants. The Barclays loan facilities consist of a non-amortising loan of £15m related to the JSOP and a non-amortising £5m revolving facility.
