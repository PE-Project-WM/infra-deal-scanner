Going concern
The company continues to see significant growth in customer connections on its full fibre network in line with its business plan. The directors are satisfied with progress to date, notwithstanding the loss sustained in the year to 31 December 2025 of £27,562,984 (2024: £25,039,256), the net current liabilities at 31 December 2025 of £969,301 (2024: £23,670,906) and the deficit on shareholders funds of £135,824,403 (2024: £109,938,945).
Net current liabilities includes £nil (2024: £1,987,000) of shareholder loans. The net deficit also includes gross borrowings of £nil (2024: £167,151,139) from Aviva Investors Infrastructure Income No. 6 Limited which owns shares in the company, refer to notes 17 and 21. It also includes associated accrued loan interest of £nil (2024: £23,886,146).
In the current year, as part of the group reorganisation, the shareholder loans, the borrowing from Aviva Investor Infrastructure No. 6 Limited and accrued interest payable were transferred to Truespeed Communications Group Limited creating an intercompany creditor.
The directors have prepared a detailed business plan and rolling cash flow forecasts. The company (as part of the new Freedom Truespeed Group) has the support of its new investor, Infrabridge, which has committed to fund the company through to cash breakeven. On this basis the directors have concluded that the company will be able to meet its liabilities as they fall due for the foreseeable future and hence it is appropriate to continue to adopt the going concern basis in the preparation of these financial statements.
Demand for gigabit-capable broadband services continues to increase, driven by higher-quality video streaming, more connected devices per home, and new use cases such as Cloud services, smart-home devices, and gaming.
Post balance sheet events
Subsequent to the year end, the group to which the company belongs combined with Freedom Fibre Limited, a further full-fibre broadband provider, to create the Freedom Truespeed Group. This transaction further extends the group's network reach and customer base, and is consistent with the directors' strategy of consolidation within the sector. As the combination occurred after the reporting date, it is treated as a non-adjusting post balance sheet event and has had no impact on the amounts recognised in these financial statements.

1.6
Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting 
period 
end date, the 
company 
reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible
and intangible assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an individual asset, the 
company 
estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised immediately in 
profit
 or 
loss
