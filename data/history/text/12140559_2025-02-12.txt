Introduction
We are pleased to present the Strategic Report for Volt Technologies Holdings Limited ("Volt") for the year
ended 31 December 2023.
This report accompanies the audited financial statements and provides an overview of the Company's strategy,
performance, and future prospects.
Business review
Volt continues to be a provider of open banking solutions which enable seamless, secure, and efficient financial
data sharing between banks, third-party providers, and consumers.
Since incorporation in August 2019, Volt has predominantly grown and continues to grow its revenue through
providing Payment Initiation Services (PIS).
In order to broaden Volts product offering, Volt secured an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence from the
United Kingdom's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) in Q3 2023. In addition, Volt also secured a Payment
institution licence from Poland's Financial Supervision Authority (KNF). Volt’s licences has enabled it to evolve its
cash management product from which Volt virtual accounts will now be issued to merchants.
As well as moving forward with revenue derived from the payments arm of the business, these accounts receive
account-to-account payments from merchants’ customers and provide real-time payment confirmation, giving
merchants visibility and control across the payment lifecycle.
In July 2023, Volt announced new investment into the business in the form of a $60m Series B funding round led
by institutional Venture Partners (IVP). Of which, $10m of previously held loan notes were converted to equity.
To execute on the Group business plans and meet capital requirements, Volt will continue to draw down on the
$60 million dollars from this funding round.
This funding has allowed Volt to further its operations in Europe by opening a branch based in Berlin, Germany
as well as continue to develop its product offering.
Strategy and Objectives
Volt's organisational vision is to drive real time payments everywhere where individuals and businesses are
seamlessly connected. Volt believes to achieve this, the key elements to drive forward the vision and mission
are:
1. Product Innovation: Create a payments product that meets and exceeds the functionality and user

experience of card payments.
2. Geo Expansion: Serve the global payments economy with a vast network in key markets for our merchants

to tap into.
3. Scalable Growth: Build a sustainable growth organisation, demonstrate product-market fit, create value for

our merchants and our end-users at scale.
4. Merchant Success: Deliver for our merchants, serving their needs fast, with quality, and at scale.
5. Talent Excellence: Attract and retain global talent.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Company faces several risks and uncertainties, including:
: 1. Financial Risks: Including both liquidity risk and currency risk.
2. Regulatory Risk: The risk that a change in regulations or legislation will have an adverse effect on Volt's
ability to operate in the current environment.
3. Cybersecurity Threats: As an open banking provider, we are exposed to cybersecurity risks and
potential breaches in security.
4. Market Competition: The competitive nature of the fintech industry requires ongoing innovation and
differentiation to maintain our market position.
5. Macroeconomic Risks: Includes the risk the economic turmoil may have an adverse effect on Volt's
performance.
6. Technology Risk: Includes failures or security breaches within Volt's technology systems.
Each of these risks are addressed in further detail below:
Financial risks
This incorporates both liquidity risk and currency risk and is mitigated by Volt by:
Preparing and monitoring regular cash flow forecasts to review liquidity requirements and preparing detailed
plans covering the next 12-24 months of trading. These plans are reviewed by the Senior Leadership Team and
approved at Board Level.
Regulatory risk
As Volt operates in a highly regulated industry and is currently regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority
. (FCA), there are a large amount of reporting and statutory obligations Volt must adhere to.
Volt has dedicated resources are in place to ensure continued and ongoing compliance with these regulatory
requirements. These include but are not limited to governance requirements such as Anti-money laundering
(AML) checks and regular Risk and Compliance Committee meetings as well as in depth reviews and
compliance checks during onboarding of new customers.
Volt's Compliance and Risk Teams ensure they stay up to date with any changes to this regulatory environment
to pre-empt anything that might cause disruption to Volt's ecosystem.
Cybersecurity threats
Cybersecurity risks and potential breaches or losses of confidential information or IT systems and security are
mitigated through robust security measures and protocols.
By leveraging a technology stack and building out an Information Security team headed by a Chief Information
Security Officer, Volt is able to operate in a secure and safe IT environment.
Market competition
Given the fast growth and highly competitive nature of the sector Volt operates in, it is imperative Volt stays
ahead of the market by continual product innovation and investment back into its own suite of products, offerings
and markets.
As such, Volt continues to invest heavily in its own technology as well as looking ahead to expand both
geographically and in its product offering as will be case now with the EMI license.

Volt's business is sensitive to global macroeconomic conditions as Total Processed Volume (TPV) and the
revenue generated from initiation of these payments is linked to the number and value of users transactions
using Volt's open banking network. TPV measures the gross value of payments successfully processed through
a payments platform over a given timeframe.
Technology risk
Volt has always sought to employ inhouse developers who are at the forefront of their craft in a bid to ensure the
Volt framework remains robust and ever evolving.
As above, Volt also ensure it partners with established providers of IT infrastructure as well as ensuring ongoing
risk and information security is managed by the internal IT security team.

Future Developments

Going Concern
The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. The Directors and Management Team
have prepared projected cash flow and financial information in excess of 12 months from the date of their
approval of these financial statements.
Management have additionally considered two stress scenarios whereby the going concern basis was tested
under a 25% reduction in revenue vs forecast and a 50% reduction in revenue vs forecast with an additional 5%
increase in the cost base.
The Company has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the period of at least 12 months
from the date of approval of these financial statements. Even in the two above stress scenarios the Group held
adequate resources to continue in operation for at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial
statements.

2.19 Share-based payments
Where share options are awarded to employees, the fair value of the options at the date of grant is
charged to profit or loss over the vesting period. Non-market vesting conditions are taken into
account by adjusting the number of equity instruments expected to vest at each balance sheet date
so that, ultimately, the cumulative amount recognised over the vesting period is based on the number
of options that eventually vest. Market vesting conditions are factored into the fair value of the options
granted. The cumulative expense is not adjusted for failure to achieve a market vesting condition.
The fair value of the award also takes into account non-vesting conditions. These are either factors
beyond the control of either party (such as a target based on an index) or factors which are within the
control of one or other of the parties (such as the group keeping the scheme open or the employee
maintaining any contributions required by the scheme).
Where the terms and conditions of options are modified before they vest, the increase in the fair
value of the options, measured immediately before and after the modification, is also charged to the
consolidated profit and loss account over the remaining vesting period.
Where equity instruments are granted to persons other than employees, profit or loss is charged with
fair value of goods and services received.
