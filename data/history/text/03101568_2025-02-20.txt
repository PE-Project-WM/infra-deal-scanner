The directors present the strategic report and financial statements for the year ended 30 September 2024.

Fair review of the Business .

Principal activity

The principal activity of the Company is the provision of connectivity and cloud services to both its residential and
business customers. The connectivity services provided include broadband, voice, ethernet, private and wide-area
networks, hosting and associated services. ;

Purpose

Zen's ultimate purpose is to deliver happiness to our people, customers, partners and suppliers, in other words do
right for people and planet. We choose not to be constrained by short-term financial targets but to strive for long-
term goals aligned to our pursuit of happiness.

Founded on principles of quality service, integrity, and customer satisfaction, Zen operates in the competitive
telecommunications and broadband industry, specialising in delivering internet, hosting, and IT services to
residential and business customers. The Company remains committed to providing solutions that meet customer
needs while prioritising technological innovation, environmental sustainability, and customer service excellence.
Strategic objectives and business model

1. Be a greater place to work: continue to build on our ‘People First’ culture, maintaining high levels of
engagement by continued focus and improvement of people development and !eadership opportunities,
adoption of new ways of working and the continued promotion of our health and wellbeing and diversity,
equity, inclusion and belonging agendas.

2. Positive impact on society and the environment: by doing business the right way, continue to work
towards our Net Zero goals, improve our B Corp status and continue to support our local community

3. Provide the best service in the UK: Maintain high customer satisfaction levels by focusing on quality
service, technical reliability, and responsive customer support.

4. Digital transformation and innovation: Invest in digital tools, artificial intelligence and innovative
solutions that streamline customer experiences, support scalable growth and result in operational
efficiencies.

5. Expand our network reach: increase our market share and geographical coverage through strategic
partnerships with full fibre Alternative Network providers.

6. Financially self-sufficient: continue to grow, trade profitably and invest for the long term.

Zen operates in both the UK consumer and business markets. For the year under review, demand for our services
has remained strong across Zens’ four trading divisions, Consumer, Business, Enterprise and Partner. The
Business and Enterprise divisions combined with effect from 1 October 2024.

Zen's strategic focus remains on Ultrafast FTTP connectivity services, and a broad range of Cloudbased
networking and communications services.

¢ Ultrafast connectivity: Zen now delivers 88% of its FTTP services over its own network infrastructure and
continues to invest to expand its network reach and the quality of service delivered. Geographic coverage
will further improve following a recent partnership with AltNet Trooli. Supplementary AltNet partnerships are
targeted during FY25.

* Cloud-based networking and communications: Continued growth in Cloud services is based on a strong
partnership with Amazon Web Services. Delivery against the strategy is progressing well and the business
continues to be recognised in the UK marketplace for the quality and resilience of the service it provides.

Zen continues to invest in its people to ensure it is in a position to take advantage of the opportunities that lie
ahead.

Financial overview

Full year revenues of £121m (2023 - £110m) show growth of 9% (2023 - 9%) once again above market rates.
Growth came from broadband, voice, leased line and cloud services. Growth is underpinned by the continuing
commitment and focus on providing excellent customer service.

Gross profit percentage reduced in the year to 18.9% (2023: 20.2%). The two key reasons for the reduction in
gross profit percentage are firstly the result of cost increases from the Company's supply chain that it was unable to
pass through to its customer base due to its price promise guarantees and secondly the move in sales mix from the
Company’s proprietary cloud platform to Amazon Web Services. The impact of these two factors was partially offset
by the first full year margin benefit resulting from the migration of existing circuits and the placing of new circuits on
to the Company's own infrastructure.

Zen’s Consumer broadband base grew by 19% and reflects the demand for full fibre services and the success of
Zen’s partnership with Alternative Network provider (AltNet), CityFibre. This impressive growth was partially offset
by a number of customer losses in the Partner division where price competition is particularly aggressive. Overall,
the broadband base grew by 5% (2023: 7%).

The results for the year are EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) of £4.6m (2023
£3.9m) and an operating profit of £1.9m (2023 £0.4m). The improvement in EBITDA and operating profit is a result
of new business, the migration of existing circuits onto its own network, tight control of people, marketing and
administrative spend. Profit before tax of £0.9m (2023 £0.4m) includes a loss resulting from a write down to the fair
value of the investment property of £1.1m due to the effects of the wider property market.

Existing cash balances together with EBITDA and good cash conversion enabled the business to invest £2.9m
(2023: £6.1m) of capital into its core business. Investment in intangible assets of £1.6m splits between a £0.8m
purchase of IPv4 addresses and £0.8m on third party and inhouse developed software. The year-end cash balance
is £5.4m (2023: £3.5m), the Company remains debt free. The net asset position of the business has improved to
£28.4m (2023: £28m). The investment property valuation of £6.0m (2023 £5.9m) reflects management's best
estimate of the element of the Rochdale Head Office rented to tenants; the valuation is based on current market
rates.

There has been a small growth in both trade debtor and trade creditor balances. However, debtor days have
decreased by 2 days to 25 days (2023: 27 days) and creditor days have increased by 2 days to 25 days (2023: 23
days). Both debtor and creditor days remain within our standard commercial terms. There are a small number of
minor balance sheet movements. Stock increased by £0.3m as the Company prepares for the launch of multi-
gigabit broadband products, other creditors increased by £0.5m and reflects funding received in advance from a
strategic supplier to support marketing spend planned for FY25.
A 5-year revolving credit facility of £5m was in place and unutilised during the year; the facility expired on 30
September 2024.
Taking the above factors into account the directors are satisfied with the financial performance of the business
during the year.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The Company makes little use of financial instruments other than an operational bank account, bank and other
loans and the use of hire purchase agreements when required so its exposure to price risk, credit risk, liquidity risk
and cash flow risk is not material for the assessment of the assets, liabilities, financial position and profit or loss of
the Company.
The management's objectives are to:

* retain sufficient liquid funds to enable it to meet its day-to-day obligations as they fall due whilst maximising

returns on surplus funds;
* match the repayment schedule of any external borrowings or overdrafts with the expected future cash
flows expected to arise from the Company's trading activities.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The Directors consider the following risks to be the most material or significant for the business. These issues do
not purport to be a compiete fist or an explanation of all the risks facing the Company. In particular, the Company's
performance may be affected by changes in the market and/or economic conditions and changes in legal,
regulatory or tax requirement legislation. Additional risks and uncertainties, not presently known by the Company or
that the Company currently deems immaterial, may impact the business.
: Market
Description
The main risk facing the business is competition within the market and ensuring that the Company's proposition
keeps abreast of technological change. Competition within the UK market remains strong as established players
look to grow their customer and revenue base and new entrants look to gain a foothold in the market through price
competition. Legacy services will be withdrawn, Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) and Integrated
Services Digital Network (ISDN) services will be switched off by Openreach by January 2027 and accordingly
Wholesale Line Rental (WLR) based broadband services will no longer be supported.
Potential impact :
Growing competition may dilute the addressable market and slow down the rate of growth. If the Company does not
at least keep pace with the evolving market in terms of product and service development, then its plans for revenue
and margin growth may be negatively impacted. Existing customers using legacy services need to be migrated to
data only broadband services or risk being lost to competitors and/or their services becoming unsupported or in the
extreme case switched off by Openreach.
Mitigating actions
A clear and competitive pricing strategy is in place with ongoing monitoring of pricing position and market
proposition. Plans to move existing customers from legacy services to data only or full fibre products continues at
pace. The company has invested in targeted marketing focusing on its reputation for superior customer service as
the core differentiator to the competition. In addition the company is partnering with leading AltNets and reselling
their services to its consumer and business customer bases.
Security

Mitigating actions
This risk is mitigated through procedures designed to identify and neutralise such threats; this includes the constant
monitoring of unusual traffic patterns by sophisticated applications. Zen continues to adapt its governance structure
to ensure best practice is followed in the identification and management of information and cyber security risks.
This includes increased frequency and broadened scope of both routine and bespoke penetration testing;
continuous compliance checks; security simulations; integrated security behaviours training, which is mandatory for
all employees; and dedicated security roles to track how cyber threats are evolving and are best detected and
mitigated
Board visibility is provided via the internal risk governance process. The Company is represented in various
industry forums to ensure it is fully aware of new areas of risk, methods employed by malicious actors and best
practice in the identification and mitigation of risk. Zen’s core infrastructure and operating capability is certified
under ISO 27001 for security. The company has mandatory staff training on data protection and cyber hygiene
practices alongside a rolling refresher programme.
Service availability
Description
Reliable, high-quality voice, data and cloud services are critical to any customer and are the core components of
Zen's products and strategy. Therefore, maintaining very high levels of service availability is central to its credibility,
competitive positioning, and its financial performance.
Potential impact :
if Zen’s products and services perform below the market's expectations, then this could have a direct impact on
product and revenue growth through reputational impact and could also result in increased operating costs.
Mitigating actions
Zen operates a comprehensive operational governance framework to manage the availability and performance of
its services. This includes the design and architecture of the network for resilience, product platforms, capacity
planning, change management and security. Business continuity planning is a component of the governance
framework. This governance is subject to external audit via our ISO 27001.
The core network is designed to be resilient. Should part of the network be interrupted, traffic is routed through
alternative paths. Underlying network equipment and circuits are subject to a continual upgrade programme,
whereby key equipment is replaced and upgraded before its end of life and core capacity is increased in line with
the forecast growth in traffic and the demands of new ultrafast technologies.
There is an Incident Management process that is rehearsed on a regular basis. This capability is available
24x7x365 and ensures the business can respond immediately to events that may impact the performance of the
services provided to customers.
The Company has an established communications process which is initiated during any major service incident. This
ensures that the Company maintains effective communication both internally and externally with customers and
suppliers.

Environment and Climate Change
Description
The impact of Climate Change on the environment has both immediate and progressive effects on the risk profile of

Potential impact
The impact of climate change risks on Zen is assessed as low. Extreme weather risks are mitigated via existing

Supply Chain Disruptions

Description

Zen relies on external suppliers for equipment, technology, and infrastructure components. Disruptions in the
supply chain, due to global factors or specific supplier issues, may lead to service delays and increased costs.
Potential Impact .

Delays in infrastructure expansion, reduced ability to take on and provision new customers and increased input
costs could impact service delivery and customer satisfaction.

Mitigating Actions

Diversification of suppliers to reduce dependency on any single source. Holding strategic inventory reserves for
critical components. Regular assessment of supplier performance and risk to anticipate and address potential
disruptions. Engaging with suppliers on long-term contracts to ensure stable supply.

Economic Environment and Inflationary Pressures

Description

Uncertain economic conditions, inflation, and rising operational costs (e.g., energy, salaries, and supplies) can
negatively affect Zen’s cost structure and profitability.

Potential impact

Zen is subject to supply chain increases, inflationary pressures on salaries and the unpredictability of prices in the
current energy market. Zen’s competitive position in the market and future profitability are subject to its ability to
absorb cost increases or to pass these through to customers.

Mitigating actions

The Company mitigates the impact of supply chain cost increases by passing these through to customers on those
services not subject to price guarantees. Energy costs are hedged to minimise exposure to major fluctuations and
again where possible have been passed through to customers.

on people, customers, suppliers, the community and the environment. Since incorporation in 1995, Zen has

board of directors take their responsibilities under the BCorp regimen very seriously.
