INTRODUCTION
The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31st January 2025 (“FY25”).
The Company (JHG") notes the requirement for the board to take into account the factors set out in section 172 of
the Companies Act 2006 when completing their strategic report. This report takes into account that requirement.
The principal activity of the Company during the period is that of a Holding Company.
The Shamrock Topco Group comprises of the parent company and 10 wholly owned subsidiaries.
The range of products and specialist services offered by the subsidiaries covers telecommunications infrastructure
installation, power infrastructure installation, associated engineering services, civil engineering, highways
diversionary works and traffic management services.
Going forward, the Company will continue to provide support services to the ongoing trading entities within the Group
and carry out its role as a holding company.
BUSINESS REVIEW

. In line with our primary duty under section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 to “promote the success of the company”
the directors have developed a strategy that is clear and fully aligned with the members wishes and requirements
for the company over the next three years. . :
Strategic Update
In FY25 the Group provided a complete range of Tier 1 Telecoms infrastructure services to all the major players in
the UK Telecoms market: Virgin Media, Openreach, Vodafone, the National Grid and Gigaclear.
JHG offers a strategic mix of new fibre services along with national network maintenance and network assurance
services. JHG provides each client with their own set of specialist services, from husiness connectivity, cell site on-
net connections and full fibre build programmes to residential feeds, network upgrades and broadband links. JHG
also has a strong reputation for delivering Civil Engineering services across the UK.
The company is currently operating in a large and dynamic market; the UK's fibre rollout phase is estimated to be
cumulatively worth £27 billion between the years 2024 and 2030, and the recurring maintenance market, post roll-
out, will be worth £2 billion per year through to 2035 and beyond.
Market demand for Tier 1 Telecoms infrastructure services slowed during the calendar years 2023 and 2024,
primarily due to a significant reduction in Altnet activities. Penetration is low across the industry and competition from
both Openreach and VM has been significant. This, together with the dramatic rise in the cost of money, meant that
some funders slowed the pace of investment.
With the slowdown in funding, Builds have been left stranded, and this means that the ‘Land Grab’ phase is now
over. The Altnet industry belatedly recognises that commercial enterprises need to have paying customers that
provide profits, as opposed to being mostly engaged in large-scale build activities funded by cheap debt and equity.
Many observers expect substantial industry change and consolidation in the UK in the next 18 months. In fact, the
Telecoms market is still in a state of flux and somewhat in the doldrums due to this expected shake-out.
Accordingly, as JHG focuses on the fixed networks and has low exposure to Altnets, the company remains well
placed to benefit from these uncertain market conditions and, although clearly impacted in FY25, it is not feeling the
fierce cold winds of the dramatic reductions in build works across the industry in the same way as others have.
Having said that, in FY25 there was also a reduction in Build programmes in Openreach and Virgin Media causing
widespread disruption to the UK Telecoms supply chain. The consequences of these reductions were identified
quickly by JHG which enabled Management to take appropriate early actions in order to mitigate the impact of the
market changes. The resulting performance contrast between H1 and H2 is clearly noticeable and the recovery is

. measurable for all to see.

Whilst the headwinds impacted on JHG revenues for the year, the gross margins have been maintained. Meanwhile,
the company restructured the overhead base in order to shape the business so as to be fit for the future. Such
changes protected profitability and a focus on cash collection and working capital improvements helped deliver a
sound financial performance in the year.
Whilst the build phase may have stalled in the UK Altnet market, the two major Telecoms network providers in the
UK are continuing their own commitments to upgrade their high-speed broadband capability, driven partly by the
consumer demand for ever-increasing symmetrical broadband capacity. JHG maintain and upgrade networks so
naturally they are better positioned than most to take advantage of changes to the fixed networks, and will continue
to do so for some time to come.
Diversification
The Director's view remains that Telecoms is a critical, national infrastructure priority across all businesses, homes,
workspaces, transport, and open areas, that it needs to be treated as such, and will continue to be so. However,
changes in the nature and pace of development in the UK Telecoms market since 2023, along with the rapid
development of infrastructure services demand for Power connectivity, led the Board to review their longer-term
strategy and bring forward their diversification plans.
There is presently a significant requirement to upgrade the electrical grid capacity for the UK and not enough
suppliers to service the demands for the expansion of the Power Industry. The drivers for this demand include the
capacity needs of data centers; the development of Al; and the political commitments to reach net zero; all of which
require a large scale upgrading of the electricity infrastructure in the UK. JHG will take advantage of these market
trends by supplying complementary services to the Power industry.
JHG is well placed to expand its specialist services into adjacent sectors, bringing decades of Civils expertise into
the Power sector which struggles for Civils capability and capacity. Having solid revenues of £50m, enduring profits,
and excellent cash generation provides a robust cornerstone from which to launch into a diversification strategy that
can seamlessly move into Power and Highways in the next few years and other Infrastructure Services after that.
Going Forward ,
Once again, the business plan for the year to 31* January 2026 aims to improve the quality of the revenues and
margins, maintain a name for Excellence, reduce overheads as a percentage of revenues, increase the Earnings
Before Interest, Taxation, Depreciation and Amortisation (“EBITDA”), and deliver high levels of cash generation.
Cash generation and conversion of profits into cash has, historically, been very strong and in the eight years between
FY17 to FY25 the Group generated net cash from Operations of £44.8m :
The Company continues to believe that any tier 1 provider of scale with repeat revenues, multi- disciplinary
offerings, a strong pipeline of opportunities, enduring profitable growth, good cash generation and a strong
management team, will continue to attract interest from investors and trade parties alike.
JHG is a cash generative, Tier 1 provider of scale to the Telecoms industry with all those attributes on show in the
financial year to 318t January 2025 and operates in markets with healthy growth prospects for many years to come.
Review of FY25
In the year to 31st January 2025 the Group delivered full year revenues of £51.0m, gross profit of £10.0m (20%) and
an operating profit of £2.2m.
After a good financial performance in FY24, in line with most operators in the market, FY25 was a relatively difficult
year, particularly in H1. However, there was a strong recovery in H2 and the ‘core continuing’ activities within the
business continue to perform well. This group of ‘core continuing’ activities deliver strong underlying gross margins
that underpin the financial health of the company.

Competition

The Company believes that its strong, embedded and longstanding relationships with clients are not easily replicated
by competitors, but the business still remains wary of competition and competitive risk.

Having said that market intelligence suggests that many competitors have been hit much harder by the downturn in
Build activity in the sector and that although their short-term financials will suffer, many competitors could be tempted
to cut margins in order to win work thereby reintroducing competitive pressure on margins in order to keep work.
Whilst this is a risk, JHG believe that there is risk to a client in removing a long-standing and valued partner merely
based on price.

The Company continues to encounter competition from long-standing competitors, regional players and new
entrants to the market and we expect this to continue in the future. The Directors regularly monitor performance to
ensure that any new competitive threats are being addressed, and to ensure that positive relationships with key

, accounts and customers are being maintained.

The Directors remain convinced that improving the quality of delivery, and the health and safety standards, is the
best way to stay ahead of the competition and satisfy the demands of its customers.

SECTION 172 (1) STATEMENT - “Promoting the success of the company”

The Company notes the requirement for the board to take into account the factors set out in section 172 of the
Companies Act 2006 when completing their strategic report and this statement is intended to reflect how the directors
have met their duties under section 172. The directors have considered FRC and BEIS guidance when considering
the production and content of this statement. ,

The directors have also considered in detail how each must act in the way they consider, in good faith, would be
most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole and, in doing so, having
regard to; the likely consequences of any decision in the long term; the interests of the company’s employees; the
need to foster the company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others; the impact of the
company’s operations on the community and the environment; the desirability of the company maintaining a
reputation for high standards of business conduct; and the need to act fairly as between members of the company.

value (in line with an extract from a House of Commons debate on the draft legislation in February 2006). The
directors consider “For the benefit of its members as a whole” to mean “promoting the common interests of all
“members”.

The directors recognise that the long-term success of the company is dependent on the sustainability of its business
model and its management of risk. The directors accept that decisions made by the board will have a direct impact
on the viability of the company over differing time periods. Accordingly, all strategic decisions are made in close
association with the members and with regular and controlled reporting procedures in place. Planning, preparation,

papers and reports are supported by appendices, with granular detail providing ample opportunity to assess and
highlight areas of risk on an ongoing basis.

Annual business plans result from detailed and comprehensive input from all areas of the business. Using bottom
up preparation and a top-down review process, input is challenged and considered to ensure that strategic ambition
and day to day practicalities are blended to deliver well thought through and thoroughly tested business plans. The
company also produces 3-year strategic plans consistent with delivering “Success” through a long-term increase in
value.

2.4 TANGIBLE FIXED ASSETS

2.18 GOING CONCERN
The company has net current assets of £25.3m, including cash of £3.8m. After a good financial performance in
FY 24, in line with most operators in the market, FY25 was a relatively difficult year, particularly in the first half of
the year. The company underwent a programme of restructure in early 2024 which impacted trading performance
reducing a small amount of headcount and cost. However, there was a strong recovery in the second half and the
‘core continuing’ activities within the business continue to perform well. This graup of ‘core continuing’ activities
deliver strong underlying gross margins that underpin the financial health of the company.
A strong focus on working capital and cash collection, together with profits for the year turning to cash, meant that
despite the challenges, the business generated £10.1m of cash in the year, a significant improvement on FY24.

profit £5.8m and the Adjusted EBITDA was £3.5m giving a run-rate Adjusted EBITDA of £7.0m on an annual basis
when reviewing in line with the first half of FY26.

FY26, as mentioned above.

continue in operational existence at current levels with headroom within operating facilities for planned growth in
