The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 29 April 2024.
Introduction
North PB Limited (the ‘company’') is a member of the North Group (the 'group’). North PB Limited is a subsidiary of
North PB Group Limited, with Perth Topco Limited as the ultimate parent company.
The company's principal activity is to design, deliver, and maintain integrated networking and ‘smart place’
solutions. North Group provides customers with the ability to partner with a single provider that has expertise
relating to physical infrastructure, networking & connectivity, safety & security and deployment of Internet of Things
sensors and applications.
A reorganisation of the capital structure of the Group took place in July 2023. This included new capital being
introduced by our investors, a change in debt facilities with our lenders, and a debt for equity swap to strengthen
the balance sheet and support the strategic growth plans of the business.
North Group reported strong performance for the financial year 2023-24 delivering improved profitability, margins
‘ and cash generation. A number of improvement plans were implemented in the second half of financial year
2022-2023, the benefits of which were seen in the improved performance for the financial year 2023-24; these
included
« Implementation of a market sector aligned organisational model, each headed by a Managing Director
accountable for their respective Sector. This has delivered clearer accountabilities and greater proximity to
the marketplaces that North competes in and customers that we serve.
* {mprovement of the ‘One North’ operating platform to realise operating efficiencies across all sectors
* Refresh & refocus of the North Brand including the trademarking of “NORTH Stronger Networks, Smarter
Places”
* Identification of industry segments where North has a high ‘right to win’ and that are underpinned by a
range of repeatable propositions revolving around ‘Stronger Networks, Smarter Places’
* Exit of low profitability customer segments and products
¢ Robust pricing reviews to ensure that North is market competitive
¢ Enhanced focus on working capital management resulting in a significant reduction in the value of
outstanding debt and a significant optimisation relating to 3rd party spend.
The Group employs over 300 staff with multiple offices across the UK providing nationwide services. The Group
also delivers international projects for existing customers.
North is focused on developing multi-year relationships with organisations across the UK and on further
developing capabilities to provide end-to-end networking and ‘smarter place’ solutions.
Business review .
North has a long-standing strategy to become a leading force in the integrated networking and ‘smarter places’
market. This is captured by “NORTH Stronger Networks, Smarter Places” which was trademarked in March 2024.
North places great emphasis on understanding customers’ goals and requirements beyond specific tender
requirements which results in an end-to-end design that aligns with a customer’s wider technology and outcomes
roadmap. The success of this approach was illustrated in a recent tender win: “The evaluation panel were
unanimous that the winning tender demonstrated an excellent understanding of our requirements and expectations”
Having understood the required outcomes, North is then able to work on a pragmatic integration of multiple
technologies and data feeds, some existing and others new, working with customers to ensure that the expected
benefits are realised. This often requires a close working relationship with customers and flexibility to ensure that
the intended outcomes are achieved.
Following the project phase, North typically takes ongoing accountability for the monitoring, support & maintenance
of the network and smarter places solutions implemented and will work with customers to ensure that outcomes are
delivered and that solutions are optimised over time.
North is well positioned to succeed in this market due to its breadth of expertise and experience which includes
: expert design skills covering a wide range of wired & wireless networking and safety & security technologies,
dependable project management & installation, deployment of software & analytics tools, and nationwide resources
to provide ongoing maintenance support. It is this combination that enables North to make networks stronger and
places smarter.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Exposure to UK economic conditions
Adverse economic conditions could lead customers to reduce spending.
North is, to a degree, protected from this risk since its business activities underpin critical infrastructure and

. solutions for customers. In addition, the majority of North's revenue comes from a blue-chip customer base. North
has also exited industry sectors such as retail & hospitality that are more sensitive to macro-economic conditions.
Market developments
North monitors the developments within its market through its close’ customer and vendor engagements, as well as

_ Monitoring industry press and attending industry shows and seminars.
By being close to the market, the company has historically been strong at adapting to market and technology
changes in an agile manner, often resulting in new opportunities. Recent examples include the anticipation of the
increasing importance of loT [internet of things] solutions to enable improved services at lower costs which resulted
in the successful bid for and implementation of the loT Scotland network and subsequent public & private sector
customer wins. Equally North was early to understand the implications of, and opportunities for, the rising
imperative for public space safety combined with the advances in IP technologies and video analytics solutions.
Competition
North operates in a competitive marketplace. At the ‘bottom end’, there are many small regionally focused resell &
installation competitors which are competitive for small and lower complexity work; and at the ‘top end’ there are a
small number of telecoms operators and systems integrators which are competitive for large scale customers and
programmes. :
North is clear on where it has a high ‘right to win’ and differentiates based on deep customer centricity combined
with the ability to design, implement, and maintain multi-technology solutions with in-house expertise.
In addition, in each Sector North has significant recurring business from long term frameworks and contracts where
competition is limited to a select few.
Management of growth
The ability of North to deliver its projected growth ambitions requires it to deliver against its strategy of selling &
delivering integrated networking and smarter places solutions, be able to efficiently scale whilst maintaining close
customer relationships, and to continually replenish, and add to, a pipeline of new projects.
To this effect, management control systems are in place together with effective planning tools and management
information.
In addition, North’s focus on larger customers and provision of ongoing managed & maintenance services results in
an increased proportion of contracted and recurring revenue streams and in an increased level of direct award
project work.
Funding and working capital ,
The Directors deem the company to have has sufficient cash reserves to fund working capital requirements as the
business continues to grow.
North pursues a capex light strategy and seeks to maintain a low fixed overhead to minimise the risk of high
operating leverage.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
Detailed financial and cash flow forecasts are prepared and reviewed by the Group, on a monthly basis covering
updates from the sales, operations and finance departments. Cash balances are reviewed weekly with a detailed
forecast produced every two weeks.
Financial control measures and financial risks are reviewed regularly and a programme of continuous improvement
against any risks identified is in place.
Health & Safety
North operates in high-risk environments, including operating at height, operating within asbestos present buildings,

. operating in high voltage environments and, on occasion, operating in hazardous materials environments. Each of
these presents a health & safety risk to North colleagues and subcontractors and/or to the general public.
North deploys a robust operating controls framework to minimise such risks, holds all required industry certifications
and conducts regular training and awareness activities.
Cyber Security
All organisations face cyber-security risks. North minimises this risk by operating its systems almost exclusively
from the cloud and by deploying a full range of Microsoft cyber security tools. In addition, regular training & .
awareness interventions are made across the business alongside a range of regular security audits and tests.
North records, monitors and manages all systems interfaces with suppliers and customers to minimise 3rd party
risks.
Financial key performance indicators
Contracted recurring revenues and margins grew strongly in the year, with revenues up £3.3m in the current year.
A proportion of this growth was due to customers moving between legal entities within the overall North Group, as
we commence our business restructuring and simplification programme to consolidate the group's businesses into
amore efficient operating model.
The company's operating profit (before non-recurring administrative expenses) was £1.9m for the period, up on

. the prior year of £1.1m.

: The company's non-recurring administrative expenses relate to re-organisation costs to allow for the group to
align and realise future business synergies.
The company's Statement of Financial Position continued to remain resilient during the reorganisation of the North
Group, with net current assets of £2.5m (2023 - £1.3m), and a positive cash balance of £1.0m (2023 - £1.4m).
Other key performance indicators
One of the key non-financial performance indicator for North is its customer 'Net Promoter Score’ which is formally
measured annually to gauge customer engagement and advocacy. The most recent survey was conducted in
March 2024 and resulted in leading-class feedback: the average NPS from ‘customer decision makers’ was +48
and from ‘customer day-to-day contacts’ was +59. Common themes highlighted included North's high degree of

- customer focus, flexibility and technical expertise.
In addition, North has a range of operating KPls including sales pipeline progression, programme delivery
milestone & quality metrics, service desk response & resolution times, and technical training & skills achievement.
Staff attrition is also measured monthly together with the reasons for departure.

Future developments

North continues to develop its capabilities and market propositions to design, build & support technology solutions

that make networks stronger and places smarter for its customers.

Since July 2023 North has been aligned behind 4 clear customer Sectors: Enterprise, Public Services, Defence &

Justice, and Financial Services. These are defined based on customers organisational, operating and tendering

requirements. North will continue to focus developing new and existing customer relationships within these Sectors

with organisations seeking a multi-year relationship.

The majority of North’s customers have similar technical demands for stronger networks and smarter places (be that

smarter campuses, smarter buildings, smarter cities or smarter bases). North addresses these demands via a set of

repeatable propositions that are tailored to fit the needs of specific sectors and customers. North will continue to

evolve these propositions, working with a core group of vendor partners, to provide referenceable case studies,
: market leading subject expertise and an efficient deployment model.

North has always ensured it has leading edge technical expertise and this continues to be the case. Specific

capability development is underway to further enhance North’s network security, loT, private 5G, data analytics and

software platform technical expertise: all of which are key to provide stronger networks and smarter places.

To deliver to customers’ demands, North plans to recruit more sales and technical solutions colleagues and plans to

review all sub-contractors to ensure that they maintain the technical and operating capabilities required.

Turnover
The turnover is attributable to the principal activities of the company. The turnover shown in the Statement of
Comprehensive Income represents sales to external customers at invoice amounts together with the value
of work carried out on long term contracts to the reporting date less value added tax and discounts allowed.
Long term contracts are assessed on a contract by contract basis and are reflected in the Statement of
Comprehensive Income by recording turnover and related costs as the contract progresses. Where the
outcome of each long term contract can be assessed with reasonable certainty before its conclusion, the
profit is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income as the difference between the attributable
turnover and related costs for the contract. Attributable turnover is based on the state of completion with
reference to the fair value of the goods or services provided as a proportion of the fair value of the contract.
Full provision is made for losses on all contracts in the period in which the toss is first foreseen.
Turnover in respect of maintenance costs is recognised over the period of the contract in equal monthly
instalments.
Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets are initially measured at cost and subsequently measured at cost or valuation, net of
depreciation and any impairment losses. .
Depreciation is recognised so as to write off the cost or valuation of assets less their residual values over their
useful lives on the following bases:
Fixtures and fittings 10 years
Computer equipment 5-20 years
The gain or foss arising on the disposal of an asset is determined as the difference between the sale
proceeds and the carrying value of the asset, and is credited or charged to profit or loss.
Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting period end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible assets to
determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any such
indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the
impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an individual asset,
the company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in use,
the-estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for which the
estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying
amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. An
impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued
amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.
Cash and cash equivalents .
Cash is represented by cash in hand and deposits with financial institutions repayable without penalty on
notice of not more than 24 hours. Cash equivalents are highly liquid investments that mature in no more
than three months from the date of acquisition and that are readily convertible to known amounts of cash
with insignificant risk of change in value.

Financial instruments
The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12
‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments.
Financial instruments are recognised when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the .

Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when

Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include trade and other debtors, amounts due from group undertakings and cash
and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are
subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes
a financing transaction, where the financial asset is measured at the present value of the future receipts
discounted at a market rate of interest.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of
impairment at each reporting end date.
Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that
occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected.
{f an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present
value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss
. is recognised in profit or loss.
If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was
recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not
exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or
are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of
ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of
the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the
assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.
Basic financial liabilities
Basic financial liabilities, including trade and other creditors and amounts due from fellow group undertakings
are initially recognised at transaction price unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where
the debt instrument is measured at the present value of the future payments discounted at a market rate of
interest.
Debt instruments are subsequently carried at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method.
