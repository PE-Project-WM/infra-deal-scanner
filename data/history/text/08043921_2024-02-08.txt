The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 September 2023.
Business review
Aerial Direct operates in the telecommunications sector and resells telecommunications services including the
provision of mobile and fixed line services and the installation and maintenance of fixed line systems and
handset financing for over 150,000 customers across the UK.
Aerial Direct has reported solid growth with revenue of £39.7M (up 5% on 2022) and EBITDA of £10.6M (up
7% on 2022).
The business has continued to invest in technology and people to achieve its goal of becoming the leading
data driven mobile and unified communications provider in the UK. On 22nd November 2023 Aerial Group
Limited completed the acquisition of 51% of Layered Technology Limited. Layered Technology Limited
(trading as Vantage Cloud) is a trailblazing provider of telecom solutions known for its fully automated
framework. This framework offers instant pricing from multiple providers, streamlined service agreements with
digital signatures, and access to over 190 industry APIs. The extensive API coverage checker from Vantage
Cloud is revolutionising the telecom landscape, enabling businesses to receive hundreds of quotes in less than
60 seconds. This strengthens Aerial Direct's position as a leader in the industry and sets a new standard for
innovation and service excellence.
Through continued investment in its data warehouse and reporting tools, Aerial Direct is able to better manage
its core KPls delivering improved performance and operational efficiency across the business. Furthermore,
cohorting of both target customers and existing customers into 6 target segments across multiple industries
has allowed Aerial Direct to improve lead generation methods whilst improving conversion rates for new
business and customer re-signs. Investment and consideration into new products and supplier agreements has
broadened the products set, with further targeting into the customer segments.

These products include:

« Mobile — Aerial Direct has grown from a strong mobile heritage. In 2023 Aerial Direct have continued to
develop strategic partnerships to broaden the mobile proposition.

« Unified communications — Aerial Direct primarily resells IP Cortex, a company which was acquired in
2019. The IP Cortex platform is nearing completion of a major rewrite incorporating best in class
technologies and using the latest containerisation platforms. This allows components of the platform to be
easily upgraded where technologies have been superseded. The first release of the product will be
launched in Q3, which benchmarked with the leading IP telephony platforms in the world will become a
market leader IP Telephony and Omni channel support platform for the SME sector.

« IT solutions, private cloud hosting and cyber security — Aerial Direct have developed a comprehensive IT
managed service offering that can be tailored for all sizes of customers. These solutions were fully
launched in October 2023. Aerial has already seen significant wins in this area and completed numerous
free IT audits to existing and new customers. This has highlighted significant weaknesses in their IT
infrastructure which have been mitigated through the deployment of our solution.

« Connectivity — During the year, in addition to the majority stake in Layered Technology Limited trading as
Vantage Cloud, Aerial Direct have developed several strategic partnerships that will allow Aerial to offer
some of the most complete and competitive connectivity solutions in the country.

Employee involvement
At Aerial Group, we recognise that our employees are the cornerstone of our success. Their dedication,
creativity, and passion drive our business forward, and fostering their engagement is central to our ethos.
We employ a comprehensive approach to encourage employee involvement, through various channels for
feedback and collaboration. This approach involves structured bi-annual appraisals, company wide townhalls,
and an open-door policy to management.
Structured Bi-Annual Appraisals: Our commitment to employee development is reflected in our bi-annual
appraisal process. Through structured discussions, employees and managers collaboratively review
performance, set goals, and identify opportunities for growth. This not only ensures alignment with
organisational objectives but also empowers employees to take ownership of their professional development.
Companywide Townhalls: Transparency is important in our communication strategy. Regular companywide
townhall meetings provide a platform for leadership to share updates, insights, and strategic direction directly
with all employees. These gatherings foster a sense of community, encourage open discussion, and reaffirm
our Collective commitment to success.
Open-Door Approach to Management: Our leadership team maintains an open-door policy, welcoming
employees to engage in open discussions at any time. This accessibility nurtures a culture of trust,
transparency, and collaboration, where every vaice is heard and valued.
By encouraging a culture of engagement and empowerment, we create an environment where information
flows freely, ideas flourish, and innovation thrives. Together, we propel Aerial Group towards our shared vision
of success.

At Aerial Group, we are dedicated to ensuring an inclusive workplace where equal opportunities flourish. We

2.4 Revenue

Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the

company and the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the

consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes. The following criteria must also be met before revenue is recognised:

e network commissions revenue is recognised with reference to the stage of completion of the
service under the individual contract with the Mobile Network Operator (‘MNO’), as outlined in
network commissions section;

« revenue from the sale of of goods is recognised at the point of sale or, where later, upon
delivery to the customer and is stated net of returns;

e revenue earned from ongoing performance obligation to provide customer support is
recognised over the term of the contracts when the company delivers the performance of its
contractual obligations. At the end deferred income predominantly related to deferred customer
support revenues; and

e revenue génerated from the provision of fixed asset and mobile network services is recognised
as it is earned over the lives of the relevant customer contracts.

Network commissions

The company operates under contracts with a MNO, Over the life of these contracts the service

provided by the company to the MNO is the procurement of connections to the MNO's network.

The individual consumer enters into a contract with the MNO for the MNO to supply the ongoing

airtime over that contract period.

The company earns a commission for the service provided to each MNO (‘network commission’).

Revenue is recognised with reference to the stage of completion of the service under the individual

contract with the MNO.

A key judgement associated with this recognition is the unit of account used in recognition. The

company has determined that the number and value of consumers provided to the MNO in any

given month represents the best output measure of stage of completion of each contract. The level
of network commission earned is based on a share of the monthly payments made by the
consumer to the MNO. The total consideration receivable is determined by both fixed (monthly line
rental) and variable elements (being out of bundle and out of contract revenue share). The

company recognises all of the fixed revenue share expected over a consumers contract when a

consumer is connected to the MNO. This gives rise to accrued income being recognised, which is

collected over the consumer contract terms.

Estimating in advance variable elements of revenue is subject to significant judgements and is

dependent on consumer behaviour after the point of recognition. The company does not consider

that the amount of out of bundle and out of contract revenue can be measured reliably in advance,
and therefore these revenues are recognised in the month they are received.

The method of measuring the fair value of the revenue and associated receivables in the month of

connection is to estimate al! future cash flows that will be received from the network and discount

these based on their timing of receipt.

2.5 Research and development
In the research phase of an internal project it is not possible to demonstrate that the project will
generate future economic benefits and hence all expenditure on research shall be recognised as an
expense when it is incurred. Intangible assets are recognised from the development phase of a
project if and only if certain specific criteria are met in order to demonstrate the asset will generate
probable future economic benefits and that its cost can be reliably measured. The capitalised
development costs are subsequently amortised on a straight line basis over their useful economic
lives, which range from 3 to 10 years.
If it is not possible to distinguish between the research phase and the development phase of an
internal project, the expenditure is treated as if it were all incurred in the research phase only.
Research and development tax credits are included within other operating income within the
statement of comprehensive income.

Grants of a revenue nature are recognised in the statement of comprehensive income in the same

2.11 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is
a pension plan under which the company pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the company has no further payment obligations.

they fall due. Amounts not paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the statement of financial

2.13 Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable
of operating in the manner intended by management.
Depreciation is charged so as to allocate the cost of assets less their residual value over their
estimated useful lives, using the straight-line method.
Depreciation is provided on the following basis:
Long-term leasehold property - 4% per annum
Motor vehicles - 25% per annum
Fixtures and fittings - 20% - 33% per annum
Office equipment - 33% per annum
The assets’ residual values, useful lives and depreciation methods are reviewed, and adjusted
prospectively if appropriate, or if there is an indication of a significant change since the last
reporting date.
Gains and losses on disposals are determined by comparing the proceeds with the carrying amount
and are recognised in the statement of comprehensive income.
2.14 Stocks
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and net realisable value, being the estimated selling price
less costs to complete and self. Cost is based on the cost of purchase on a actual basis. Work in
progress and finished goods include labour and attributable overheads.
At each statement of financial position date, stocks are assessed for impairment. If stock is
impaired, the carrying amount is reduced to its selling price fess costs to complete and sell. The
impairment loss is recognised immediately in the statement of comprehensive income.
2.15 Debtors
Short-term debtors are measured at transaction price, jess any impairment. Loans receivable are

2.16 Cash and cash equivalents
Cash is represented by cash in hand and deposits with financial institutions repayable without
penalty on notice of not more than 24 hours. Cash equivalents are highly liquid investments that

2.17 Creditors

2.18 Financial instruments
The company only enters into basic financial instrument transactions that result in the recognition
of financial assets and liabilities like trade and other debtors and creditors, loans from banks and
other third parties, loans to related parties and investments in ordinary shares.

2.19 Leased assets
Where assets are financed by leasing agreements that give rights approximately to ownership
(finance leases), the assets are treated as if they had been purchased outright. The amount
capitalised is the present value of the minimum lease payments payable over the term of the lease.
The corresponding leasing commitments are shown as amounts payable to the lessor. Depreciation
on the relevant assets is charged to the statement of comprehensive income over the shorter of
estimated useful economic life and the term of the lease.
Lease payments are analysed between capital and interest components so that the interest element
of the payment is charged to the statement of comprehensive income over the term of the lease
and is calculated so that it represents a constant proportion of the balance of capital repayments
outstanding. The capital part reduces the amounts payable to the lessor.
All other leases are treated as operating leases. Their annual rentals are charged to the statement
of comprehensive income on a straight-line basis over the term of the lease, or up to the first break
clause.

2.20 Accrued income
The accrued income amounts are discounted on initial recognition with the difference between the
present value of the accrued income and the absolute accrued income to be collected over time
being recorded against turnover. Following initial recognition interest receivable is recognised and
recorded to increase the accrued income amounts to the absolute accrued income balance which is
collectable over time.

2.21 Holiday pay accrual
A liability is recognised to the extent of any unused holiday pay entitlement which has accrued at
the statement of financial position date and carried forward to future periods. This is measured at
the undiscounted salary cost of the future holiday entitlement so accrued at the statement of
financial position date.

2.22 Cashback liability
A liability is recognised for cashback offered to customers at the date of connection, that the
company is contractually obliged to repay over a period of time. The liability is recognised in full
within other creditors and is settled on a monthly basis, with flashbacks being released when they
lapse.
The cashback liability is discounted on initial recognition with the difference between the present
value of the cashbacks and the absolute cashbacks to be paid over time being recorded against
cost of sales. Following initial recognition interest payable is recognised and is recorded to increase
the cashback amounts to the absolute cashback balance which is payable over time.
