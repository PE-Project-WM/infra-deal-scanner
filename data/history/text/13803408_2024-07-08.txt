Strategic Report

For the year ended 31 December 2023 ;

The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2023.

Principal activities

The company was incorporated on 15 December 2021. The principal activity of the company is that of a

holding company for the group.

The company acquired LMGH Limited and its subsidiaries which including trading company Line Management

Group Limited in the prior year on 1 April 2022. Therefore, the results presented in these financial statements

for the current period are for 12 months, whilst the comparatives are for 9 months of trading.

The principal activities of the group are the provision of services for the design, deployment, integration and

support of in-building ‘smart’ technologies including; network connectivity, both wired and wireless, electronic

security, audio visual systems, loT (environmental, energy and occupancy) sensor networks and building

management software.

Fair review of the group business

The group continues in its position of leadership within the UK smart building technology solutions market.

The group achieved turnover of £29m (2022: £25m) in the trading period. There was a reduction in Combined

Services turnover as a direct result of our strategic decision to transition from traditional, site-based cabling

services to higher margin multi-technology maintenance contracts and ‘on-demand’ services, which

encouragingly demonstrated significant growth. We forecast the growth of these new services to continue

strongly over the next 3 to 5 years. Consequently, gross profit margins improved to 29% (2022: 26%),

supported by a strong performance from key accounts.

As a result of investments in additional headcount in Operational Technology (BMS) and Audio Visual
solutions, to fuel our growth in new ‘smart’ services, average administrative expenses spend per month

increased to support this, achieving earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) of

£1.7m (2022: £1.5m), and a pre-tax profit of £1.19m (2022: £1.13m).

Business prospects for the group remain strong. Steadily increasing work in progress, coupled with a solid

pipeline of new business and genuinely exciting future opportunities, in the form of smart building enablement,

has been driven by the Corporate Real Estate (CRE) market's desire to attain the smart building certifications

(SmartScore, WELL, NABERs) that landlords and their tenants need to attract staff back to the office. This

unprecedented demand gives the directors confidence that the business will continue to build upon its post-

pandemic recovery at pace.

The group is well placed to respond to both the challenges and opportunities that continue to arise as a result

of the sea change affecting the CRE market and has already undertaken significant steps in adapting its

product and service offerings in response to present and future client needs.

Global services

Since 2010, the group has been the UK and European representative of LGAP, a global alliance of like-

minded service providers with combined annual sales exceeding $400m in 2022. With over 38 offices

worldwide, LGAP’s members support major corporate clients throughout North America, South America, Asia

Pacific, Europe, South Africa, Hong Kong, India, China, Australia, New Zealand and Japan. LGAP continues

to be a valuable partnership for the group, leading to opportunities in data centre builds, enterprise office fit

outs and IT integration projects.

The Brexit transition period and eventual exit of the UK from the European Union continues to make it difficult

to both pursue and fulfil opportunities within the EU; therefore, the group continues to explore opportunities to

establish one or more entities within the EU and will continue to foster and develop close partnerships with our

LGAP counterparts, enabling us to fully support our global clients in the region.

Quality and management systems :
The group places emphasis on quality and excellence and is accredited to ISO 9001:2015 (Quality
Management System — QMS), ISO 14001:2015 (Environmental Quality Management System - EQMS) and
ISO 27001:2013 (Information Security Management System - ISMS). :
The standards are embedded into our fundamental business processes and policies. The combination of
these three internationally recognised standards ensure that our clients receive the best possible quality of
service with minima! impact to the environment, whilst having the confidence that their information assets are
secured to the highest standard.
Research and development
The group's principal research and development (R&D) focus continues to be on developing our own Smart
Building Enablement Broker, a form of software that enables the unstructured and siloed data held within
disparate building systems to be homogenized into a single, structured data source - available to a wide
variety of Smart Building applications - and automating the technology deployment and software configuration
and integration process. Smart building development, and ‘PropTech’ in particular, has been supercharged
following the pandemic. There is a need for the group to be able to fully demonstrate the value that smart
building technologies, and most importantly their integration, can bring to organisations wishing to transform
their workspaces and entice staff back to the office.
Our goal is to demonstrate our ability to deliver improved operational efficiency (energy usage, maintenance
and occupancy), enhanced safety, health and wellbeing of occupants and offer a user experience that is far
superior to that which staff would encounter working from home.
Emerging industry standards such as those championed by WiredScore/SmartScore and The WELL Building
Institute have established benchmarks against which our solutions can be measured. Consequently, there is a
requirement and an opportunity to be able to demonstrate and prove the performance of our smart building
solutions to the market, prior to deployment in the field.
We have created a Smart Building Multi-Technology Integration & Demonstration Lab at our HQ to
demonstrate our smart building ‘ecosystem’ to building landiords and owners and to act as a test bed for our
integration platform, ExchangelQ, a Smart Building Operating System or ‘BOS’. The platform enables smart
building services via apps that will subsequently provide improved outcomes for both landlords and tenants
and compelling experiences for their occupants.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors have responsibility for the identification, evaluation and management of risks within the group,
recognising the importance of identifying and actively monitoring our strategic, reputational, financial and
operational risks. The group Risk Register is regularly reviewed by the directors and updated with key
stakeholders in order to maintain an effective risk management framework.
The directors have considered the risks that are most significant to the business in terms of both operational
and financial impact, and these are set out as follows:
Market conditions
The group's services may be affected by a future economic downturn and reductions or delays in client
spending, particularly where ongoing uncertainty in the future use, requirements and purpose of corporate real
estate continues.
The group’s strategy to combat such adverse conditions is to maintain a balanced portfolio in terms of client
base and service offerings, limiting the exposure to any one market sector.
The need to repurpose corporate real estate in order to provide high quality collaboration and social space
(‘hotelification’), including improvements in connectivity, environmental monitoring (particularly indoor air
quality), access control and video conferencing all present the group with significant opportunities for growth.

Customer base

Our client base includes financial services, data centre, technology, media, transport, education, professional

services, flexible workspace operators, residential and hospitality. Over recent years, the Group has taken

measures to significantly broaden service offerings and actively reduce the reliance on any particular market

sector, with a broader range of services being delivered to a wider range of vertical markets, in addition to

continuing to expand our footprint outside of the UK into Europe and beyond through our LGAP partnership.

Competition

Whilst relationships, reputation, quality and capability are key elements of a client's decision to award

contracts, price remains the dominant driver in decision making. The group operates in highly competitive

sectors, particularly during the construction phase, which can at least initially, dilute gross profit margins.

The group remains focused on cost and efficiency in order to remain competitive during the construction

phase but undertakes a rigorous bid evaluation process in order to ensure it only competitively bids for work

where the gross margin is commensurate with risk, and a project is well matched to its post-construction

service model. :

Commercial risk management :

The group is subject to contractual risk by virtue of the services it provides and contracts it undertakes.

In order to limit future exposure to high-risk onerous commitments, all potential contracts are thoroughly

evaluated and risk assessed, ensuring that commercial risk is both fully quantified and commensurate with the

anticipated return before agreements are entered into. Robust measures and controls in this particular area of

risk management continue to have a significant positive impact on both profitability and future business

stability.

Supply chain

Given the ongoing uncertain economic conditions, general disruption and the possibility of insolvency within

the supply chain remain significant risks. In addition, prolonged delays in the procurement process and longer

standard lead times continue to remain a threat to the successful delivery of projects and other services. The

group builds and maintains strong supply chain relationships, carrying out thorough due diligence, whilst
: * working to reduce overreliance on any one supplier or manufacturer.

Financial risk management

The group’s operations expose it to financial risks that include credit risk, foreign exchange risk and the risk

associated with the impact of materials and labour inflation on long-term, fixed price contracts. Given the size

of the group, the directors have not delegated the responsibility of monitoring financial risk management to a

sub-committee of the board. The group's finance, commercial and procurement departments implement the

policies set by the board.

Liquidity risk management

The objective of the group in managing liquidity risk is to ensure that it can meet its financial obligations as

and when they fall due, striving to ensure that suppliers are paid in accordance with agreed terms. This is

achieved by tightly managing cashflows, particularly at project level.

The group expects to meet its financial obligations through operating cash flows and existing financing

arrangements.

\ The threat of another pandemic, caused by Covid-19 or otherwise, will remain a key risk on our Corporate

Our Covid-19 response plan led to the implementation of a range of measures, some of which have resulted

Future business prospects
We have witnessed significant market change over the last three years; the pandemic has created challenges
but also brought opportunities, in many ways accelerating plans for repurposing and streamlining our service
offerings. Changes forced by the pandemic, such as hybrid working, improvements in occupant health and
wellbeing, Government legislation on energy efficiency, and enhancing the overail user experience of the
workplace, will continue to accelerate the adoption of smart building technology.
The increase in demand for smarter buildings will strengthen as the market matures and the definition of what
actually constitutes a ‘smart building’ is standardised by independent organisations such as WiredScore and
the International WELL Building Institute. These outcome-based certification programmes, independently
targeted at both landlords and tenants, require smart technologies to be integrated, providing building
managers with a ‘single pane of glass’ and users with innovative new applications that personalise their .
interaction with the building and other occupants. This will create an opportunity for the group to develop its
software offering (both as a reseller and developer), in addition to providing a high-value Master Systems .
Integration (MSI) service. ~
Technological change is also reducing demand for traditional, site-based, discrete IT and FM support services.
It is now a mobile, wireless world and fixed support models, designed for the last century, add diminishing
value. As a whole, the Support and Maintenance industry relies on the inefficient use of siloed technical
resources, often resulting in expensive repeat visits by specialist technicians to solve simple problems
complicated by an increasingly sophisticated and integrated built environment. Consequently, there is a
cultural shift toward integrated, centralised, remotely managed, ‘pay as you go’ service and support models. In
direct response to this shift in demand, the group continues to re-engineer our workplace support business
that has experienced the worst effects of the pandemic, to create a new, integrated, multi-technology, on-
demand support service.

From 2030, the Government’s minimum energy efficiency standards (MEES) regulations will make it unlawful
for jandlords to let a property with an EPC rating below B. Over 80% of CRE stock in London currently fails to
meet this rating, thus requiring London's landlords to upgrade over 15,000,000 sqft of office annually between -
now and 2030 to meet the: target. The planned launch of our loT sensor platform (SenselQ) in 2023 will be
pivotal in providing the group with access to new Corporate Real Estate and Facilities Management markets,
playing a key role in solving the most critical challenge facing landlords over the next five years. With its
wireless, cloud-based architecture, SenselQ and our building operating system, ExchangelQ, can be rapidly
retrofitted within existing buildings to monitor, analyse and control energy consumption, thereby providing
landlords with an affordable, effective solution to the MEES conundrum.

: From the forecast growth of SenselQ and ExchangelQ, coupled with the migration towards cloud-based smart
building technology seen within our Design and Build workstream, we can begin to see a future where there is
a blurring between the CAPEX and OPEX phases of a building's lifecycle. The potential to provide clients with
‘X as a Service’ (XaaS) solutions will have a profound effect on how landlords and property developers
procure and manage their property portfolios, and consequently, the construction industry itself. By paying for
their in-building technology on a monthly or quarterly basis for a fixed fee per sqm, not only will landlords be
able to align their investment with rental income from tenants (‘pay as you lease’), but also protect themselves
from technological obsolescence (a critical risk as PropTech expands) as equipment is maintained and
continually refreshed as part of the XaaS agreement.

The group’s successful transition from a project and manpower-based business to an integrated smart
building service provider fundamentally depends on our people, who increasingly need to be highly trained
and prepared for the cultural shift that we anticipate over coming years. Prioritising learning and development
whilst rewarding ambition within an inclusive, supportive and collaborative environment will allow us to set
new standards for efficiency, sustainability and occupant experience within the built environment.
As ever, we aim to grow our business responsibly, putting ethical, sustainable and transparent business
practices at the heart of all we do, whilst delivering best-in class, smart building technology, connectivity and
services to meet the constantly evolving needs of our clients.

1.7 Intangible fixed assets - goodwill
Goodwill represents the excess of the cost of acquisition of a business over the fair value of net assets
acquired. It is initially recognised as an asset at cost and is subsequently measured at cost less
accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is considered to have a finite
useful life and is amortised on a straight line basis over its expected life, which is 10 years.

For the purposes of impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the cash-generating units expected to
benefit from the acquisition. Cash-generating units to which goodwill has been allocated are tested for
impairment at least annually, or more frequently when there is an indication that the unit may be
impaired. If the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit is less than the carrying amount of the

: unit, the impairment loss is allocated first to reduce the carrying amount of any goodwill allocated to the
unit and then to the other assets of the unit pro-rata on the basis of the carrying amount of each asset in
the unit.

1.16 Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and deferred tax.
Current tax
The tax currently payable is based on taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from net profit as
reported in the profit and loss account because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable
or deductible in other years and it further excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The
group's liability for current tax is calculated using tax rates that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the reporting end date.
Deferred tax
Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all timing differences and deferred tax assets are
recognised to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax
liabilities or other future taxable profits. Such assets and liabilities are not recognised if the timing
difference arises from goodwill or from the initial recognition of other assets and liabilities in a transaction
that affects neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting end date and reduced to the
: extent that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the
asset to be recovered. Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period
when the liability is settled or the asset is realised. Deferred tax is charged or credited in the profit and
loss account, except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to equity, in which case the
deferred tax is also dealt with in equity. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset if, and only if, there is
a legally enforceable right to offset current tax assets and liabilities and the deferred tax assets and
liabilities relate to taxes levied by the same tax authority.

2 Judgements and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In the application of the group's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements,

Key sources of estimation uncertainty
The estimates and assumptions which have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the
carrying amount of assets and liabilities are as follows.
Useful economic lives of intangible fixed assets including goodwill
The annual amortisation charge for intangible assets is sensitive to changes in the estimated lives and
residual values of the assets. The useful economic lives and residual values are re-assessed annually.
- Goodwill impairment reviews are also performed annually. These reviews require an estimation of the

calculation requires the entity to estimate the future cash flows expected to arise for the cash generating

Useful economic lives of tangible fixed assets

equipment and note 1.9 for the useful economic lives for each class of asset.

This involves a level of judgement and therefore differences may arise between actual and estimated
result.
