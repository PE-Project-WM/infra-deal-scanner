STRATEGIC REPORT 
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2024
- 1 -
The directors present their strategic report and financial statements in respect of NesscoInvsat Limited ("the company") for the year ended 31 March 2024. In the comparative period, the company extended its accounting period from December to March in order to align with that of its former ultimate parent, Viasat Inc. As such its comparative period is 15-months versus a 12-month current period. 
Principal activities
The principal activity of the company continued to be the provision of integrated communications systems for the upstream oil and gas industry.
On 18 December 2024, the ultimate parent company Viasat, Inc., announced that it has completed the divestiture of its Energy Services System Integration (SI) business to US-based private investment firm MAG Capital Partners. The company is part of the business included in the sale. Although practical details of the transaction are still being finalised at the time of authorising these financial statements, from the acquisition date onwards MAG Capital Partners is now the company’s controlling party.
Fair review of the business
The results of the company show a pre-tax loss of £1.7m (2023: £0.02m pre-tax loss) for the year and turnover of £19.1m (2023: £24.4m). Net assets at the balance sheet date were £4.6m (2023: £6.4m). The directors consider key performance indicators for the company to be turnover, gross profit, profit before tax and net assets.
Turnover decreased by 22% from the previous period, and gross profit thereon was reduced (from 17% to 10%). The main driver in this was cost of materials and a spike in contract labour cost noted during the current year. Naturally turnover has decreased during the current year because of the longer reporting prior period, but pro-rata turnover values are relatively consistent current year versus prior period. 
After the major downturn in the Oil & Gas market due to the Covid-19 pandemic, business has slowly recovered with major project awards and a record backlog (£77.9m at the balance sheet date).  The company has expanded its profile with some new contract wins in the renewables sector, providing integrated communications packages for several offshore wind farms in the south North Sea. 
Overall, the company’s financial performance has regressed year on year, both at gross margin and profit before tax levels. As well as the significantly increased cost of sales base in delivering project revenues, the changes to the Corporate Global Transfer pricing also resulted in increased administrative expenses taking a significant hit, with these being up around $100k per month in the year ended 31 March 2024. Contributing factors are the additional overhead pushed downwards by Viasat Inc. as well as the reporting entity having an increased share of the allocation due to its share of the global revenue.
Despite the challenging financial year, its balance sheet remained robust and the directors are confident in the company's future prospects, particularly with the record project backlog. 
Future developments
The oil and gas industry continues to operate in increasingly remote locations and requires additional communications to serve its operational, safety and crew welfare needs. NesscoInvsat Limited is well positioned to provide a premium managed communications service to current and new customers.
The company expects to maintain its customer base in 2025 & 2026 as the project backlog is fulfilled. The company will continue to bid for suitable project work worldwide, and as indicated above has had some success with services to offshore wind farms in the renewables sector. It would appear that the company is well placed for long-term success.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The company operates in a competitive market and manages this risk by endeavouring to provide good service and maintaining strong relationships with customers. The main risk to its business is considered to be the impact of lower oil and gas commodity prices on customer activity levels and investment decisions, albeit this has been recovering during and post year end. While difficult to mitigate this risk across and entire industry, the company does have the ability to operate across different geographic regions, thereby focusing business development activity on regions which are seeing higher levels of activity. The company’s financial risk management policies and objectives are discussed within the Directors’ Report on page 3.

Financial instruments
Financial risk management
The company’s activities expose it to certain financial risks including liquidity risk, market risk and credit risk. The company does not use derivatives for either financial risk management or speculative purposes.
Liquidity risk
In order to maintain liquidity and to ensure sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations and future developments, management monitor the timing of cash flows and align these with its strategic planning. Milestone receipts on projects are negotiated with customers in order to help with cash flow requirements. Further cash resources are available from the wider group if required, however the company’s primary source of finance is the operational cash generated from trading activities.
Market risk
The company’s activities expose it primarily to the financial risk of changes in foreign currency exchange risk, with some customer contracts being denominated in currencies other than GBP (primarily USD). Bank accounts are maintained in foreign currencies, which assists the company to monitor this exposure, through being able to receive and make payments in these currencies and thus operating natural hedging wherever possible.  
Credit risk
The company’s principal financial assets are bank balances, trade receivable and amounts due from fellow group undertakings. Credit risk is primarily attributable to trade receivable and is managed through maintaining good customer relationships and the monitoring of credit levels and settlement periods. The amounts presented within the balance sheet are net of allowances for doubtful receivables. The credit risk on liquid funds is considered limited because the counterparty is a bank with a recognised credit rating assigned by international credit rating agencies.

Turnover
Turnover is recognised at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable for goods and services provided in the normal course of business
, and

. 
The fair value of consideration takes into account trade discounts, settlement discounts and volume rebates.
Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised when the significant risks and rewards of ownership of the goods have passed to the buyer
 (usually on dispatch of the goods)
, the amount of revenue can be measured reliably, it is probable that the economic benefits associated with the transaction will flow to the entity and the costs incurred or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be measured reliably.
Revenue from rental assets is recognised according to the terms of the rental contract, but generally across the term of the contract.
Revenue from contracts is recognised by reference to the stage of completion when the stage of completion, costs incurred and costs to complete can be estimated reliably. The stage of completion is calculated by comparing costs incurred, mainly in relation to contractual hourly staff rates and materials, as a proportion of total costs.  Where the outcome cannot be estimated reliably, revenue is recognised only to the extent of the expenses recognised that are recoverable.
When the outcome of a contract can be assessed with reasonable certainty, profit is recognised as the difference between revenue and related costs. Any foreseeable loss is recognised immediately in the profit and loss account.
