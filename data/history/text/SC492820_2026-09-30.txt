The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Review of the business
The company designs, deploys and manages business connectivity solutions, combining satellite and mobile connectivity with equipment, network integration and technical support. Its services support customers across a range of business environments, including remote locations and dispersed operations.
The business model combines equipment sales and installation activity with recurring connectivity subscriptions and managed support services. The company seeks to differentiate its offering through technical expertise, integrated solutions and ongoing customer support. Satellite connectivity and associated services remain central to this offering, with customer retention and the development of recurring services important to long-term performance.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors consider the following to be the principal risks and uncertainties facing the company.
Dependence on key suppliers and network providers
The company relies on key third-party network and technology providers, particularly Starlink within its satellite connectivity offering. Changes in supplier pricing, commercial terms, availability or reseller arrangements could affect service delivery, revenue and margins. Close engagement with suppliers and assessment of changes against customer commitments and pricing are management priorities. Developing integration and managed-service capabilities can strengthen the customer offering but does not remove the underlying supplier dependency.
Competition and pricing pressure
Competition from other resellers, network providers selling directly and alternative technologies could place pressure on pricing, margins and customer retention. The company seeks to differentiate its offering through technical expertise, customer support and integrated connectivity solutions. Maintaining commercial discipline and developing recurring managed services remain priorities.
Operational capacity and service delivery
Growth increases demands on employees, systems and operational processes. An inability to recruit and retain appropriate skills, insufficient support capacity or service disruption could affect customer retention, profitability and reputation. The directors’ priorities include developing staff capability and operational systems, and aligning growth with the resources required to maintain service quality.
Cybersecurity and information security
The company depends on the availability and security of its systems and the protection of business and customer information. A cyber incident, unauthorised access or material systems failure could disrupt services and result in financial loss or reputational damage. Maintaining appropriate access controls, system security, staff awareness and continuity arrangements remains an operational priority.
Credit risk and liquidity
Equipment purchases, supplier commitments and the timing of customer receipts create working capital requirements. Delayed payments, defaults or a mismatch between receipts and payment obligations could adversely affect liquidity. Customer credit assessment, review of overdue balances and monitoring of cash requirements remain important priorities alongside planned expenditure and growth
opportunities.
Future trading and strategic priorities
The directors’ strategy is to deepen existing customer relationships, expand recurring connectivity and managed-service revenues, and pursue opportunities where the company’s technical expertise and service capability provide a competitive advantage.
Priorities include developing integrated connectivity solutions and service-management capabilities, alongside disciplined investment in people, systems and operational capacity. Growth opportunities will be assessed against their contribution to profitability, cash generation and customer service.
The directors believe that the company’s established customer relationships, technical expertise and strengthened financial position provide a sound foundation for further development. They remain mindful of economic uncertainty, competitive pressures and potential changes in supplier terms, and will balance growth with financial discipline and service quality.

Deferred tax
Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all timing differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits. Such assets and liabilities are not recognised if the timing difference arises from goodwill or from the initial recognition of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting end date and reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered. Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period when the liability is settled or the asset is realised. Deferred tax is charged or credited in the 
profit and loss account
, except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to equity, in which case the deferred tax is also dealt with in equity. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset when the 
company 
has a legally enforceable right to offset current tax assets and liabilities and the deferred tax assets and liabilities relate to taxes levied by the same tax authority.
