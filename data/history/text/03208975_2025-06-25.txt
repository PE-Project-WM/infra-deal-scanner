Strategic Report
The directors are pleased to present the strategic report for the business, for the year ended 315 January
2025.
Business Review & FY 2024/25 Performance
The principal activities of the business continue to be the provision of IT, Network & Security infrastructure,
products, platforms, and support services, predominantly into the Critical National Infrastructure (CN1)
verticals of Defence, Central Government, Blue Light, Utilities, and Renewables, along with a number of
private sector clients.
The Board of Directors are pleased with the Company's ability to navigate ongoing macroeconomic &
geopolitical challenges which are impacting UK industry, and to achieve another successful year of revenue
and profit growth in FY 2024/25.
During the year, the business continued its progress in establishing itself as a centre of excellence and
trusted supplier in the provision of services into Critical National Infrastructure (CNI) verticals, with notable
contract wins to support large scale projects in Utilities, Renewables & Defence.
A refinement of the business’ delivery structure in FY 2024/25, combined with continued investment into
its staff capabilities and build & testing facilities, has created a strengthened foundation to deliver best in
class services to these verticals.

Following a number of years of sustained growth, Vysiion once again achieved a year of record contract

the timing of these contract wins, the extent of this sales performance isn’t fully reflected in revenue in FY

The sustained year-on-year growth in Vysiion’s revenues slowed in FY 2024/25 in spite of the record sales
year. Whilst the underlying trade of the business was strong, there were two items that impacted revenue
in FY 2024/25 — firstly, the loss of a large software resell customer in March 2025, that took the decision to
in-source the licenses provided by Vysiton, reducing revenue by approximately £2.3m. Secondly, Vysiion
and its parent company Exponential-e, embarked on a programme of integration in certain areas of the
business, in order to rationalise costs, and which resulted in the novation of a number of customer
contracts from Vysiion to Exponential-e from 15 February 2025. These contract novations resulted in a
year-on-year reduction in Vysiion’s revenue of approximately £1.0m. Without these two items, the
business would otherwise have seen growth of approximately 11%.
Despite revenue growing only 1% year on year, the changing mix of that revenue, resulting from the loss
of the low-margin software resell customer, meant that Vysiion was able to strengthen its Gross Profit,
growing it by 6% from FY 2023/24, and resulting in a Gross Profit % of 59.7%.

Future Developments

The business continues to develop its position as a centre of excellence in, and key partner to CNI verticals,

with ongoing success of new contract wins continuing into the beginning of FY 2025/26, particularly in the

Energy, Utilities & Defence sectors.

These are high growth and investing verticals, recognised as underpinning the stability of the UK economy

and national security, where Vysiion can leverage its experience and expertise as both design authority,

and a delivery and support partner of “secure by design” platforms and services.

The drivers behind investment include the restructure of the UK energy system, the digital strategy for

defence, and mandated regulatory compliance as part of the NIS-2018 directive, referenced in the April

Cyber Security and Resilience Policy Statement.

The opportunity for Vysiion manifests itself in the form of net new project builds, the augmentation and

replacement of aging digital assets, and provision of technical and enduring support services.

Vysiion have successfully built an “edge to core” proposition, aligned with these demands, where the use

and availability of technology is increasingly relied upon for the safe operation of systems and mission

critical end to end processes.

A high value component of the proposition is the capability to support customers with the emerging
, challenge of regulatory compliance, through the validation of system & platform performance, and being

an intrinsic part of their ongoing cyber strategy.

Investment in the business continues, and during FY 2024/25 we opened and launched a new Group

warehouse and configuration facility in Chippenham, enhancing the Group’s supply chain capability. In

addition, Vysiion refurbished & reinvigorated its own build & test facility, resulting in a greatly enhanced

secure staging area & configuration lab in which to deliver CNI customer projects.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Company are as follows:

i. Geo-Political & Macroeconomic risks

There continues to be global geo-political and macroeconomic pressures impacting upon the UK

economy. Over the FY 2024/25 period, the UK economic performance continues to be weak, with GDP

hardly offering any growth. Whilst the inflationary environment has calmed compared to previous years,

the wider economic landscape has continued to be challenging, with the resulting uncertainty impacting

upon public finances and influencing investment decisions in the public & private sectors alike.

ii. Technology sector specific

The directors have experience operating in the fast-moving technology sector, where the pace of product

and service obsolescence is high. To mitigate this risk, the management team continually review the market

drivers that justify investment, assessing the size of current and emerging opportunities, to ensure they

have the skills, products, and services, to meet demand.

This analysis considers three topical challenges faced by all private and public sector organisations,

Cyber-security is an ever-present, and growing risk in-all industries. Vysiion’s pedigree has been built upon

The recent upsurge in generative Al and large language models has the potential to increase the pace of

iii. Staff Recruitment risks
We work hard to ensure the long term retention and development of staff, this includes offering
competitive remuneration, and ensuring a progressive, and engaging workplace, taking matters of staff
welfare seriously, and supporting staff with their learning and development needs.
During FY 2024/25, the business was restructured into three verticals with three technology practices. This
has had a lasting positive impact, increased levels of engagement and opportunity, ownership and
accountability. The objective being to provide a workplace that guides and encourages all employees, that
can pace and align with the fastest tracking members of staff and our customers.
At the beginning of FY2025/26, we have reinvigorated our staff culture programme, to deliver a number of
social & team-building events throughout the year, and invest in staff benefits and wellbeing initiatives.
The Group has also continued to invest in its Learning and Development capability, providing access to
additional technical & workplace training for all staff.

iv. Financial .

, The Company uses financial instruments including items such as trade debtors and trade creditors that :

of these risks and they are summarised as follows. :
Price Risk :
The directors manage risk on hardware sales by ensuring back to back quotation validity with our

For service contracts, the price risk is managed by matching the prices of inputs to sales contracts where
this is possible through the supply contracts.

Currency Risk
The Company continues to have limited exposure to foreign exchange risk, with a small number of USD
creditor transactions up to $0.34m per annum and EURO creditor transactions up to €0.18m.
Cash Flow interest rate Risk :
The Company finances its operations through a mixture of profits and bank financing. The bank finance is
: provided through an overdraft whereby the interest is fixed in advance of any usage.
Liquidity Risk
The Company seeks to manage financial risk by regularly planning and monitoring payment obligations, to
ensure sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable needs. Short term flexibility is achieved by an
overdraft facility of £0.75m and Parent Company borrowing.
Credit Risk
The Company’s principal financial assets are cash and trade debtors. The credit risk associated with cash is
limited as the counterparties have high credit ratings. The principal credit risk arises, therefore, from its
trade debtors.
Trade debtors are carefully managed by policies that govern the credit limits that are offered to customers
and regular monitoring of the amounts due. As the Company continues to grow, the distribution across
customers continues to widen.

1.14 Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the Company a legal or constructive
obligation that probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, and a reliable estimate
can be made of the amount of the obligation.
If the effect of the time value of money is material, provisions are discounted using a current pre-tax
. rate that reflects, when appropriate, the risks specific to the liability. When discounting is used, the
increase in the provision due to the passage of time is recognised as a finance cost
Provisions are charged as an expense to the Statement of Comprehensive Income in the year that the
Company becomes aware of the obligation, and are measured at the best estimate at the reporting
date of the expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account relevant risks and
uncertainties. When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the
Statement of Financial Position.

Critical Accounting Judgements

Revenue recognition :

Identification of performance obligations

As described in the accounting policy note, contracts with customers have been analysed into their

underlying performance obligations. The identification of distinct performance obligations is a matter

of judgement, in particular the assessment of whether a whole combined contract should be analysed

as a single performance obligation or an aggregation of many performance obligations. Typically,

when delivering managed services, whilst the Company’s commitment is to provide all services

contained within the combined contract, due to the fact that typically each individual service within a

contract commences at different points in time and the customer is able to consume benefit from

each individual service as it is delivered, management therefore determine that each individual service

is a distinct performance obligation.

Identification of Bespoke Contracts vs Portfolio Contracts

As described in the accounting policy note, management have applied the practical expedient in
. IFRS15.4 to a portfolio of contracts.

Management have had to make a critical judgement over which contracts fall within the portfolio of

‘standard’ contracts and which have sufficiently different characteristics to be considered not part of

this portfolio and requiring separate assessment. To make this assessment, Management assess the

total contract value, the transaction price allocated to the installation activities, and the number of

services promised within a contract.

Where bespoke contracts have been identified from applying the judgements above, these contracts

are reviewed on an individual contract basis, to determine the performance obligations, allocate the

installation revenue to the relevant services and recognise revenue as each performance obligation

has been satisfied.

Critical Accounting Estimates

Estimate of commencement dates of services for portfolio contracts

As described in the accounting policy note, for a portfolio list of contracts, management have

estimated the service delivery period and service commencement date of these services.

The weighted average period to setup a service (from contract signature to commencement date) has

been calculated using historical data.

This weighted average period is applied from the contract signature date, to determine the date at

which revenue recognition commences for the installation and setup fees charged to a customer at

the outset of the contract.

Management have used this approach for the portfolio contracts as it would be impracticable to

determine and apply the exact service commencement date of each service and allocate a specific

element of the upfront fees to each individual performance obligation. For the majority of portfolio

contracts, the managed services will be live and being used by the customer by the end of the average

implementation period. While individual services will go live at different dates, the timing difference

has been determined not to have a material impact on revenue. As explained above, a more detailed

approach is taken for bespoke contracts where the setup and range of services provided mean the

setup time is over a longer period of time. The Company reviews the weighted average on an annual

basis at each end of financial year period.
