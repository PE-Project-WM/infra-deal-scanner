Redwood Technologies Group Limited

Strategic report

The directors present their Strategic Report and the affairs of the Company for the year ended 31

December 2024.

This report covers the overall performance of Redwood Technologies Group Limited (“Group”), with key

activities of Redwood Technologies Limited and Content Guru Limited, the two largest trading entities,

highlighted.

PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES

Key Group activities continue to be the provision of cloud CX (Customer Experience) and CCaaS (Contact

Centre as a Service) solutions, which it delivers via its storm® CPaaS (Communications Platform as a

Service) environment. The four key product pillars within Group’s CX portfolio are as follows;

- omni-channel, which enable customers to manage any form of communication, such as video, social
media, voice, email;

- omni-data, which enables different systems of information such as CRM (customer relationship
management) and specialised systems of record in industries such as finance, logistics, retail, utilities and
public sector to be integrated;

- omni-customer, which enables information from people and loT (Internet of Things) devices (digital
customers) to be processed into tasks;

- omni-automation, which allows tasks to be automated using technology such as Al (artificial intelligence).
Al services are managed with the Group's brain® Al orchestration software, processing huge amounts of
data and automating work previously performed by humans.

In connection with the delivery and ongoing operation of storm and brain services, Group also provides

consultancy, development, programme management, testing, training, operational and support services.

Group’s key target customer base is large enterprises and public sector organisations who need mission-

critical, feature-rich, highly-scalable, highly-available, highly-flexible CX, CCaaS and Al solutions. Customers

include central and regional government bodies, financial services institutions (including insurance and
banking), retailers, travel organisations, utilities providers, CSPs (communications service providers) and Sis

(System Integrators). Services are run in tens of countries and supported from regional offices in the

Americas, Asia Pac and Europe.

STRATEGIC REVIEW

Group defines its strategy over three time periods. Short-term planning is considered in detail for the

coming 12 months; mid-term planning is made for a 3-5 year period; long-term planning is made for 15-20 .

years. The first company in Group, Redwood Technologies Ltd, was formed in 1993 with the long-term

objective of leveraging the emerging internet and open-standards technology. Content Guru was founded
in 2005 with the fong-term objective of leveraging the cloud. Over the past 3-4 years Group has put in
place the foundations for its third long-term plan, which is to leverage the growth opportunity in CX

(customer experience).

In pursuit of this long-term objective, over the past 3-4 years, Group significantly boosted its engineering

capabilities, with Group's headcount increasing by over 50% during that period. Group has invested heavily

in its four core CX pillars, with a particular focus on Al development. In spite of its significant investment in

R&D during this period, which has also seen low double-digit revenue growth, Group has continued to be

profitable.

As per its previous two long-term plans, Group's strategy to focus on CX looks to be a good decision.

Within the contact centre industry, customers are embracing Al services to replace lower-skilled, lower-

value jobs and in higher-skilled roles using Al to provide greater per capita productivity. This resulted in a

_” strong sales performance for 2024, with a very positive 2025 anticipated.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
Group continuously tracks risk through its risk-management registers and management strategy reviews.
Group's risk management does not eliminate risk; it identifies risk and devises strategies for mitigating and
managing.
Principal risks and uncertainties raised in last year's reports were:

- Uncertainty due to Brexit

- Discounting from CCaaS competitors unable to command value with a strong service offering,
impacting market prices and potentially impacting Group's profitability

- Foreign exchange fluctuations (e.g. euro, US dollar, Japanese yen vs British pound)

-  Business-as-usual technology risks such as security, data protection, maintaining reputational
standing, keeping up with advances in technology

- Cost inflation.

Covering each item briefly in turn:

- Group continues to monitor the long term risks of Brexit. It has and will continue to reduce its
exposure to UK markets by continuing its investments in mainland Europe, the Americas and
AsiaPac.

- Discounting from CCaaS competitors continues to be a risk. Most of Group's major competitors
have reduced their headcount as they pivot from high growth to profitability. Group has won a

CORPORATE AND SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY / ENVIRONMENTAL, SOCIAL AND GOVERNANCE (ESG)
Group aims to ensure that its colleagues work in a happy and secure environment. Group is committed to
helping tackle climate change, and as part of that process it is becoming a Net Zero Carbon Emissions
company, which it will achieve on or before 1st January 2050.
Groups pledge is to become a Carbon Net Zero business by 1st January 2050, which includes its supply chain.
Key aspects of achieving this goal are:

- Use of 100% renewable electricity and installation of solar panels on main UK building

- Decarbonising its platforms (such as storm®), data centres, buildings and travel

- Working with and encouraging suppliers to go Net Zero

- Working with key stakeholders to maximise Groups platform, data centre and operational efficiency

- Offering technical solutions that assist customers in cutting their carbon footprints e.g. remote —_-

agent working
- Ensuring decarbonisation is a priority and that any residual emissions are covered by high quality
offsets
Senior management from Group has maintained its involvement with a number of organisations to ensure it
has good general input regarding business or environmental matters. Group is a founder member and
organiser of Berkshire Community Foundation’s Business Philanthropy Club. Group’s directors are involved
in voluntary activities both in the community and business, including holding advisory or trustee positions
within the local lieutenancy, the local Community Foundation, local sporting institutions, King’s College
London and the CBI. At a local level it is involved with events such as organising the Pride of Bracknell
Awards, celebrating achievements in charitable and voluntary causes, education and health and sport. All
Group's entities in all regions are provided budget to support local voluntary causes.
Group has established an ESG Task Force involving all departments within the business and sets and
promotes activities such as its recycling schemes, energy consumption reduction plans and green transport.
The Group puts quality first and focuses on achieving the highest standards with compliance across multiple
standards such as ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO 20000-1, ISO 22301, ISO 27001, ISO 27701, TickIT+, Cyber
Essentials Plus, PCI DSS compliance and SOC 2 Type 2.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
In the short term (12 months), Group expects to see increased revenue growth and profitability compared
to 2024. In the mid term (3-5 years) Group will aim to continue its strong profitable organic growth in
recurring revenues and will expand its services infrastructure and teams across all territories. It will
investigate ‘inorganic’, i.e. acquisition, opportunities for accelerating growth.
In the long term (15-20 years) Group will continue to enhance its four key pillars of CX (customer
experience):

1. Omni-channel. Group's customers will adopt more digital technology to engage with end
customers, such as increased use of video and more social media channels in addition to traditional
voice services. Group will continue to build its support for digital channels and engage with
customers using their preferred medium.

2. Omni-data. Customer data continues to increase in value and is used to create better customer
experiences. Group will continue to enhance its storm® CKS® CDP (Customer Data Platform)
solution.

3. Omni-customer. Group will continue to build more connections to loT (Internet of Things) ‘digital
customers’ such as health devices, smart meters and tracking devices.

4. Omni-automation. Artificial Intelligence and Intelligent automation, including technologies such as
generative Al (e.g. ChatGPT) are being used to bring efficiencies through automation (e.g. customer
self-serve) to customers.

Over the next five years Group anticipates continued strong revenue growth as it maintains its position as a
leader in the CX and CCaa&S (Contact Centre as a Service) market and continues to consolidate its longer-
term fifteen-year plan.

FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT OBJECTIVES AND POLICIES

Group’s activities expose it to a number of financial risks including in relation to competition, technological

development, price, credit, foreign currency and liquidity risk. Group’s primary goal is to continue a policy of

sustainable and controlled growth allied to an acceptable level of profitability. Group believes these

objectives can best be met by securing long-term relationships with customers who recognise the high

value added nature of Group’s products and services. .

Competitive risk

Market demand for Group’s products continues to be strong, with services such as cloud contact centre
