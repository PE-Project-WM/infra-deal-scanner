NLIGHTEN UK LIMITED
GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE PERIOD ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2023
Introduction
The directors present their Strategic Report for the period ended 31 December 2023.
nLighten UK Limited (the “Company”) is the UK subsidiary of nLighten HQ BV, and was formed in July 2022 with the purpose of acting as the holding company of the future UK trading division of the nLighten data centre group. The Company was dormant throughout its first year following incorporation.
The financial period ending 31 December was a short period of six months from 1 July 2023 to bring the financial year of the Company into alignment with the wider nLighten Group of companies. In this period, the Company transitioned from a dormant entity with its first acquisition in September 2023. This strategic acquisition reinforces the nLighten Group’s ambition to establish itself as a leading European player in the Edge data centre market.
The acquisition was a 100% equity transaction of Edge Data Centres Holdings Limited, the holding company of the UK data centre operator Proximity Data Centres. Funding for the acquisition came from an equity investment from nLighten HQ BV. In line with our commitment to enhancing the quality and capacity of our infrastructure, further capital was deployed to upgrade and improve the existing data centre sites. These investments have been key in ensuring that our facilities remain at the forefront of technological advancement, efficiency, and connectivity, positioning us as a leader in providing Edge data centre services across Europe.
To further drive our growth and ensure excellence in customer service, we have made significant new hires across the organisation. These strategic additions to our team bring specialised skills and experience, enabling us to meet our targets and maintain a high standard of customer satisfaction as we expand our operations.
Since the Balance Sheet date, the Company has continued to look at growth opportunities in the UK and made its second UK acquisition in April 2024, acquiring a regional colocation data centre in Hoddesdon, just North of the M25 London orbital motorway. The Company intends to continue looking for acquisitions in the UK that fit with its growth plan.
Overall, the period has been one of significant progress and strategic consolidation, setting a strong foundation for continued growth and success in the coming years.

Business review
Revenue
Company
: As a holding company for the UK operations of nLighten, the Company’s revenue in the period consisted largely of management fees relating to services provided to the UK Group by the larger nLighten central functions based in the Netherlands. The management fees are charged to the UK subsidiary companies through Edge Data Centres Holdings Limited. As such, the Company had no external revenues in the period.

At a Group level, revenues in the four month period that constitutes the active Group period of performance were stable. Colocation revenues were the majority of the revenues reflecting the core services sold in the Group making up 55% of total revenues. Power charges for customer use of power at the Group’s data centres, running their IT equipment, accounted for 42% of total revenues. These charges were again stable during the period. This reflects two aspects. The first is the level of customer colocation activity, driving the level of use, and the second is the price of power, which the Proximity Group had fixed earlier in 2023 for a 12 month period, eliminating power price variations. Other revenues associated with connectivity and other associated services comprised the remaining 3% of total revenues.  
The new financial period in 2024 is expected to show a stable, but slightly lower revenue profile as the Group re-structures the Proximity Group and implements new sales processes, however, any variations should not be material due to the long-term nature of much of the Group’s revenues, being contracted generally in multi-year contracts. Power revenues in 2024 are expected to remain stable, based on customer usage. As an added effort to provide customers with predictable and stable power charges, the Group will be implementing a new power procurement process using active hedging together with advance purchasing of committed power usage.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Company
: As holding company of a data centre group in the UK, Risks unique to the Company relate to long term funding and interest costs. These are largely hedged through the main investing company, I Squared Capital, having a sustained and detailed investment plan for the Company under the management of the directors. 
Group
: Risks for the trading group are principally power cost volatility and the availability of plant and machinery with which to build out and develop the data centre portfolio. The Group is addressing this through implementing a long term detailed refresh and expansion plan which will see the first application during 2024. This comprises advance ordering of long lead-time elements of plant and a rolling programme of works over the coming years.

The Company intends to drive the revenue per FTE upwards over the coming years, although 2024 may see a decline due to re-structuring and hiring in new employees to get the Group resourced for growth. In addition, the Company intends to monitor the ratio of management to other roles in the Group to ensure that productivity remains a key performance indicator. Again, in the short term, the Company expects to see the management ratio increase over the 2023 ratio as new resources are brought on board to manage the re-structuring and future growth.
Within a typical data centre company, it would be normal to use the fill rate as a KPI for the success of the company. This measures the available space that is actively revenue generating. This is a very useful measure for a data centre that has a static capacity. Upon reflecting what measures and KPIs would be useful in determining performance, the Directors have decided that the fill rate is not currently a useful measure in this context. This is because the Company has embarked on a three year plan to progressively refresh and expand the Group’s portfolio of data centres creating materially more capacity. The creation of capacity will inevitably impact any fill rate calculations as the capacity needs to be made available prior to customers occupying the space. The Directors are aware that more KPIs need to be added and the Company is actively setting out KPIs for future performance measurement, including those related to customer fill rate and capacity expansion to plan.
Credit Risk
The Company has a strong financial base with its parent company and its principal investor and has established good credit ratings across all credit agencies. The UK portfolio has a client-base made up of established companies with good covenant strength.
Liquidity and Cash flo
w
In addition to the continuation of the long standing debt facility with the Intermediate Capital Group (ICG) which had originally funded the core of the UK portfolio, through Proximity Data Centres Limited, the Company’s UK subsidiaries finance operations through operationally generated cash as well as capital investments sourced from equity investments in the Company by its parent, nLighten HQ BV. Detailed cash flow forecasting coupled with good cash collection rates underpin the UK business. Overall, cash flow and liquidity are not perceived as a risk for the Company.
Summary
The Company has seen a significant milestone in its progression in the six-month period to end December 2023. Significant funding has meant that the Company can look forward with great confidence, with development plans secured. 
Being an integral part of the pan-European nLighten Group, funded by I Squared Capital, underpins and enables the Company’s growth plans for the coming years.

Economic impact of global events
UK businesses are currently facing many uncertainties such as the consequences of Brexit, Covid 19, environmental sustainability and geopolitical events such as the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Key sources of estimation uncertainty

(ii) Recoverability of debtors
