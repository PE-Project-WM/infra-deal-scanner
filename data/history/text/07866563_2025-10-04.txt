— Principal risks-and uncertainties
The Group’s senior leadership team, departmental heads and compliance team meet regularly to discuss and
analyse key risks to the Group and to agree monitoring and mitigation strategies.
Competitor risk
The principal risk factor for the Group is competition in the genuine presence assurance sector. The demand
for genuine presence assurance is growing rapidly and globally due to a number of factors including an increasing
shift from in-person to remote digital interactions in the context of increasing sophistication in cyber-attacks.
iProov must constantly enhance its technologies and products to remain ahead of the ever-evolving genuine
presence assurance threats. iProov believes it is well-placed amongst its competitors thanks to its iSOC active
threat monitoring and response capability, a capability the Group believes to be unique in the market and difficult
to replicate, enabling it to offer a premium product offering significantly ahead of its competitors.
Facial Biometric risk
The business of the Group is to assure the genuine presence of a remote individual, despite attempts by skilled
attackers to develop persistent methods of bypassing this assurance. The rate of change of such methods is
rapid, and there is a continuing risk that a method will be found which the Group's technology is unable to detect.
iProov mitigates this risk with a wide range of methods, including intensive R&D and its iSOC active threat
monitoring and response system.
Data Breach risk
The Group processes personal data and special category personal data and stores it for a limited period of time,
necessary to carry out its task of securing clients against fraud attempts. There is a risk that such data may be
accessed illegitimately for the purposes of corruption or disclosure. iProov mitigates this risk with a wide range
of technology and process methods, many of which have been independently audited and certified to the highest
security standards worldwide.
Financial risk
The Group's operations expose it to a variety of financial risks that include price risk, interest rate risk, inflation
tisk, foreign exchange risk and credit risk. The Group has developed strong internal processes, procedures and
controls such that financial risks can be efficiently identified, managed and mitigated. Material financial risks,
mitigations and policies are regularly discussed between the finance function and CEO and, where relevant, are
escalated to,the Board for consideration.
Intellectual Property risk
Through the development of its industry leading genuine presence assurance biometric authentication
technology, the Group has developed significant intellectual property and know-how over the past 10 years. As
a result, the Group is exposed to third parties who may wish to copy, steal or otherwise seek to benefit from its
intellectual property.
The Group mitigates such risk via a combination of initiatives, including submitting for patents, rigid
non-disclosure arrangements with third parties, employees and contractors and through the use of intellectual
property insurance.
The Group also has in place systems and processes to minimise the risk that its technological or product
developments infringe upon any third party’s intellectual property.
Foreign Exchange risk
While the majority of the Group’s expenses are denominated in Pounds Sterling, the Group is exposed to some
foreign exchange price risk in the normal course of business principally on revenues in U.S. dollars. Following
U.S. dollar customer receipts in 2024 and a U.S. dollar funding round in December 2021, the Group is also
exposed to foreign exchange risk due to the translation of U.S. dollar cash balances principally into Pounds
Sterling. While the Group currently relies on a natural hedge as its U.S. dollar expenses grow as a proportion of
total expenses and has not used financial instruments to date to hedge foreign exchange exposure, this position
is kept under constant review.

2.10 Share based payments
Where share options are awarded to employees, the fair value of the options at the date of grant is
charged to profit or loss over the vesting period. Non-market vesting conditions are considered by
adjusting the number of equity instruments expected to vest at each reporting date so that, ultimately,
the cumulative amount recognised over the vesting period is based on the number of options that
eventually vest. Market vesting conditions are factored into the fair value of the options granted. The
cumulative expense is not adjusted for failure to achieve a market vesting condition.
The fair value of the award also takes into account non-vesting conditions. These are either factors
beyond the control of either party (such as a target based on an index) or factors which are within the
control of one or other of the parties (such as the Group keeping the scheme open or the employee
maintaining any contributions required by the scheme).
Where the terms and conditions of options are modified before they vest, the increase in the fair value
of the options, measured immediately before and after the modification, is also charged to profit or
loss over the remaining vesting period.
Where equity instruments are granted to persons other than employees, profit or loss is charged with
fair value of goods and services received.
The group scheme, awarding shares in the company, being the parent entity, includes recipients who
are employees in certain subsidiaries. The transaction is treated as an equity-settled share-based
payment, however as the subsidiary is receiving services in consideration for the company’s equity
instruments, an expense is recognised in the subsidiaries income statement for the grant date fair
value of the share-based payment over the vesting period.
In these financial statements the benefit provided to the subsidiaries is accounted for as an increase
in the company’s investment in subsidiaries (see note 15) and this increase in investment is then
assessed for impairment, as per the group’s accounting policy (see note 2.14).

3. Judgements in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In preparing the financial statements, management is required to make estimates and assumptions which
affect reported income, expenses, assets, liabilities and disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities. Use
of available information and application of judgement are inherent in the formation of estimates, together
with experience and expectations of future events that are believed to be reasonable under the
circumstances. Actual results in the future could differ from such estimates.
The directors consider the following areas to involve considerable degree of estimation uncertainty:
Share-based payments (see note 23)
The group is unable to directly measure the fair value of employee services remunerated via share-based
payment schemes and so have used a suitable option pricing model to determine the fair values of this
award using the Black-Scholes option pricing model. The model required various inputs which require
significant areas of judgement around weighted average contractual life, expected volatility and expected
dividend growth rate. The inputs for weighted average contractual life and expected volatility are based on
industry benchmarks and expected dividend growth rate based on group's current strategy.
Revenues (see note 2.5)
Revenue arising from the Group's customer contracts is recognised once: (1) the customer has enjoyed
the use of the Group's authentication software, platform and service; and (2) the customer has obtained
the benefit of authentication transactions delivered in the Group's platform and as measured by the Group's
platform.
Research and development (see note 2.7)
Due to the uncertain and complex nature of projects addressed by the Group's research and development
team, it is typically not possible to determine whether a particular work stream will deliver probable future
economic benefits, the quantum of such benefits, if any, and their longevity. As a result, the Group
expenses research and development expenditure as incurred.
