STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2024

Business review .
The Directors present the strategic report of the Company for the year ended 31 March 2024. The Directors are
pleased with this year’s results, which have been achieved against a backdrop of a very competitive market and
continuing economic difficulties in the UK.
The principal activities of the Company are to provide cutting edge hosted telephony services to the Primary
Care market in the UK and support it's Corporate Customer. The Company's strategic objectives continue to
focus on growth in the hosted telephony market.
The Company continues to invest in staff, systems and product offering throughout the business, to fuel the
growth of the business through increased revenue from new customers in the Primary Care market where
Surgery Connect, the Company's primary product, is becoming established as the market leader. This market
will remain the key focus for the business in the near future, with improving patient access to their General
Practitioner being a high-profile issue, eliminating the 8am rush to obtain a GP appointment over the telephone is
seen as a fundamental step to be achieved before other workflows to improve integrate Primary Care can be
worked on.
The Company’s revenues are focussing on the NHS Primary Care market. New procurement processes were
introduced by the NHS in the year to regulate the purchase of “advanced cloud based telephony systems” such
-as Surgery Connect. GPs are now mandated in their contracts to buy only systems approved by the NHS.
The board of Directors have approved a multi-million capital investment programme in the Company's data
centres upon which the services are delivered and are delighted at the progress made in building a safe, resilient
and secure infrastructure whilst coping with a substantial increase in call volumes.
The Company is part of the Southern Communications Group (the Group), who have continued to grow with four
acquisitions in the year. The acquisitions have added: further hosted and reseller partners, and geographical
expansion in the UK.
The Company continues to support its corporate customer base and to provide specialist billing services. The
board of Directors are exploring the best means of growing its revenues in these markets in conjunction with
other Southern Communications Group companies.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risks and uncertairities facing the Company are the ongoing difficult economic environment and
increasing price sensitivity within the calls and lines market leading to an extremely competitive industry sector,
which may impact company margins. More recently UK wide price rises and the cost of living crisis present some
uncertainty. . : °
Despite these factors, the Company continues to be profitable and to expand.
The Company's revenues and operations are entirely UK based, and while the UK economy continues to face
uncertainty, it will almost certainly create opportunities for the Company, which has traditionally seen strong
trading during downturns in the UK economic environment.
The Company's revenues are now focussing on the NHS Primary Care market. New procurement processes
were introduced by the NHS in the year to regulate the purchase of “advanced cloud based telephony systems”
such as Surgery Connect. GPs are now mandated in their contracts to buy only systems approved by the NHS.
Failure to maintain the approved status of Surgery Connect would present a risk to the future growth and
recurring revenues of the Company. : ‘
The Directors have not identified any specific risks associated with the current economic situation for its
customer base, but will continue to carefully monitor the situation.
Business review of trading and results
: Revenue in the year increased by 33%, predominantly due to the growth in the Companies own hosted platform,
Surgery Connect being established as the market leader, as discussed in the business review.
Cost of sales have seen some growth year on year. Where the Company is procuring equipment and services
: from other suppliers, they have noticed the impact of rising costs across the industry.
Administrative expenses have increased by £3.8m year on year, and this is predominantly due to the extra
resource required to manage and deliver the new contracts signed during the year. This is a mixture of
‘ increased permanent staffing, temporary staff and engineering subcontractors. The Company continues to invest
in the development of its market leading technology and services and capitalised development costs of £1.2m
(2023: £0.9m). The ability to utilise group resources also helps to keep administrative expenses as low as
possible. These costs are tightly monitored and controlled.
The Company made a profit before tax of £5.0m (2023: £4.4m), £4.2m is represented by the increase in gross
profit in the year. The bridge between EBITDA of £7.0m (2023: £5.2m) to the profit before tax of £5.0m (2023:
£4.4m) is attributable to the increase in gross profit in the year, offset by administrative costs including increase
in staff costs of £1.8m. (Refer to note 6 and note 8) .
Cash increased year on year by £0.3m to £2.0m (2023: £1.7m), but the Company has seen significant growth in
both trade debtors (£6.8m increase) and deferred income in the year (£7.9m increase). This is a result of the
requirement from the NHS to sign and invoice GP surgeries before the end of March 2024. The roll out of
installation for these surgeries will take place in the year to 31 March 2025 and expectation is that both deferred
. income and trade debtor levels will drop in future reporting periods. This is exceptional activity that has taken
place around the reporting date.
Both tangible and intangible assets have grown in the year. Intangibles have grown as a result of capitalised
research and development of work completed towards the continued development of the Surgery Connect
platform. Tangible fixed assets have grown as a result of purchasing hardware to support the increased number
of customers being serviced by the Company.

Price risk, credit risk, liquidity risk and cash flow risk
The Company operates in a competitive industry and, therefore, faces some price risk. As a reseller, the
Company attempts to pass on any increased costs to its customers, although competition in the industry is
occasionally a barrier. This is mitigated by focusing on growing the Company in higher margin revenue streams.
The Company faces little financial risk in the form of bad debts, due to retaining ownership of the lines which it
rents to customers. The maximum credit risk, therefore, can be restricted to one month's income for the majority
of customers.
The business generates positive cash flow from its operations and maintains a healthy net current assets
position. Liquidity risk and cash flow risk are therefore not high.
Qualifying third party indemnity provisions ,
Throughout the year indemnity insurance was held for the Directors of the Company.

Going concern
X-On Health Limited is a subsidiary of Saint Topco Limited, which has prepared forecasts, projections and
expected cashflows for a period of at least 12 months from the approval of the financial statements to 31
December 2025.
The Group reported a positive EBITDA for the year of £38.7million (16 months to March 2023: £44.5million), with
charges for amortisation of £44.4m (16 months to March 2023: £53.0m) and interest payable of £49.6m (16
months to March 2023: £53.3m) contributing to a reported loss before taxation for the year of £61.0m (16
months to March 2023: £67.5m), with £12.2m (2023: £17.7m) net current liabilities.
As at 31 March 2024 the Group financing arrangements consisted of total committed senior loan facilities of
£261.2m, of which £43.5m for purchase of acquisitions remained undrawn and £57m of A preference shares
with no fixed redemption term. The Facility A1 of £29.8million is due to expire on the 22 February 2026 with the
facility A2 and acquisition facilities, due to expire on 22 August 2026. The PIK facilities are due to expire on 25th
February 2029. On 27th July 2023 an additional facility for £50.0 million was agreed for further purchase of
acquisitions, this facility is due to expire on 22 August 2026 and bears interest of 7.25% plus SONIA, 5.25% will
be payable bi-annually and the remainder cumulatively accrues at 2% quarterly. During the year £6.5million was
drawn down against this additional facility.
The Group's forecasts and projections, taking into account reasonably possible changes in trading performance
show that the Group will be able to operate in compliance with the covenant requirements and within the level of
its facilities to a period to 31 December 2025 and is still expecting to achieve an increase in EBITDA for the
period to 31 December 2025. In drawing a conclusion on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern, the
Directors have assessed the financial risks to the organisation and evaluated a number of scenarios including
reverse stress testing covenant compliance. The principal risks to going concern have been identified as the
impact on the wider economic environment and the Group from rising inflation in the’'UK which could impact
revenue and EBITDA growth which in turn could impact covenant compliance. ,
Financial covenants set by lenders are cashflow cover, super senior net leverage and senior net leverage which
continue to be met and are anticipated to be met for the period to 31 December 2025. Stress testing resulting in
a covenant breach would require an 18% drop in EBITDA for the period to 31 December 2025 which ‘is
considered implausible as it would require the business to stop making new sales, and for a high number of
customers to churn away.
Forecasts for the Group's future performance include continued compliance with these covenants. In a
constantly changing environment an agile approach has been taken, to facilitate our response to changes to
organisational risk in a timely and robust manner. This includes both regular and event prompted reviews of

- risks, regular cash review and management, and staff working in a flexible manner to support the ongoing
success of the Group.
At the time of approving the financial statements, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Group
has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. Thus, the Directors
continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the financial statements.

2.15 Impairment of fixed assets
Assets that are subject to depreciation or amortisation are assessed at each balance sheet date to
determine whether there is any indication that the assets are impaired. Where there is any indication
that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset (or cash-generating unit to which the
asset has been allocated) is tested for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised for the amount
by which the asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the
higher of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs to sell and value in use. For the purposes of
assessing impairment, assets are grouped at the lowest levels for which there are separately
identifiable cash flows (CGUs). Non-financial assets that have been previously impaired are reviewed
at each balance sheet date to assess whether there is any indication that the impairment losses
recognised in prior periods may no longer exist or may have decreased.

2.16 Valuation of investments
Investments in listed company shares are remeasured to market value at each balance sheet date.

amount is reduced to its selling price less costs to complete and sell. The impairment loss is
