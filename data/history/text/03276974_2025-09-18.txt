FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024
Introduction
The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2024. The Company's long-term
strategy focuses on enabling clients to modernize and scale their IT infrastructure through comprehensive Hybrid
IT services, with a strong emphasis on cloud migration, infrastructure consolidation, and managed services. The
strategy includes: supporting clients’ growth ambitions by delivering scalable, flexible, and resilient cloud solutions,
reducing clients’ total cost of ownership and operational costs, allowing reinvestment into innovation and improved
service delivery, enhancing security and resilience, enabling clients to focus on higher-value strategic projects,
acting as a strategic partner to align technology transformation with clients’ ambitious business strategies,
ensuring agility and readiness for future growth and market changes.
Principal activities
The Company's principal activity focuses on providing comprehensive Hybrid IT services and governance,
covering everything from cloud to mainframe environments. The company primarily operates within the United
Kingdom, supporting clients through digital transformation and ensuring robust, secure, and efficient IT operations.
Business review ;
Running IT operations well is complex and requires expertise and experience and Ensono is trusted by some of
the world's most successful companies given its record in delivering solutions customised to each client's IT
environments, allowing companies to focus on their core business. Clients can rely on Ensono to develop, deliver,
and maintain their ideal managed IT solution.
Ensono provides this efficiently and effectively, taking the pain of IT operations away from clients through
mainframe, infrastructure, and public cloud offerings. Ensono’s Consulting and Advisory practice provides
business transformation capabilities, ranging from technology innovation reviews to solution design and
transformational project delivery.
Total turnover in 2024 increased to £76.9m from £70.5m in 2023, an increase of 9% from the prior year due to
: public cloud.
Turnover from our public cloud business, which is primarily comprised of AWS and Azure environments, increased
by 24% to £46m. Turnover from our traditional infrastructure services delivered £18.2m, a 15% reduction year on
year. The mainframe product increased by 3%, delivering turnover of £12.4m.
Adjusted EBITDA of (£1.2m), which is the metric used by management to measure profitability, increased from
: (£1.9m) in 2023 primarily due to the increase in revenue.

The Company is supported by the strong financial position of its intermediate parent, Ensono Inc.. Ensono Inc. is
committed to growing the Company's turnover and profitability and believes the most successful way to achieve
this is by continuing to deliver excellent service to current clients and by onboarding new clients while having long-

: lasting relationships with key suppliers. Based on recent financial results, the Company has taken several actions
to improve its financial profile. The company’s parent undertaking, Ensono Inc. has provided confirmation that it
will provide financial support to the company, if required, for a period of twelve months from the date of approval
of the accounts.
In 2024, the Company entered a consultation process with employees and reduced the number of roles within the
company. The consultation was concluded during the year at a cost of £210,000 affecting a total of 9 employees.
The intention is to improve profitability in the future by having employee volumes and technical skills which are
aligned to the revenue generated by the Company. The change will result in a reduction in staff costs of
approximately £812,000 per annum.
In 2025, the Company entered a consultation process with employees and reduced the number of roles within
the company. The consultation is estimated to result in a reduction in staff costs of £2,296,000 per year affecting
a total of 33 employees.

combine mainframe and cloud together, driving growth and efficiencies across their organisations. Hybrid Cloud
heips organisations optimise their IT estates, to ensure they are running applications on the most appropriate
platforms for cost performance and enhanced customer experience.

Business review (continued)
Staff turnover measures attrition through the ordinary course of business, which increased by 2% to 12% in 2024.
The increase was primarily attributable to redundancies implemented as part of the company restructuring
programme
Excluding the impact of redundancies, underlying staff turnover remained stable at 10%, consistent with the prior
year. This underlying stability is a positive indicator of the business's ability to retain talent and reflects ongoing
efforts to foster employee engagement and satisfaction.
Business environment and strategy
Ensono’s market — the management of enterprise and internet facing application infrastructure (a subset of the
operations segment of the overall IT services market) — continues to mature and grow.
The trend from in-house to outsourcing is likely to be sustained in the mid to larger size enterprise market for many
years to come. Ensono’s strategy is to maintain its strong organic growth through working with strategic partners
to introduce new clients and to broaden the services delivered to existing clients.
Research and development
The Company continues to invest in extending its products and services whilst delivering continuous
improvements to those services. Continued emphasis was placed on the implementation of Ensono Envision
globally. The Company continues to invest in enhancements to Envision Advisor. Envision Advisor was developed
and introduced to provide a new level of service to clients and was enhanced with additional machine learning Al
capability in 2024 known as Ensono Predictive Engine that predicts potential service outages proactively so that they
can be prevented from ever occurring. It provides continuous and proactive recommendations to clients, to ensure
that clients’ public cloud and traditional infrastructure environments are optimised for cost, performance, and security
as they continually evolve. Additionally, the Ensono Cloud Platform, a scalable, demand-based IT infrastructure
continues to be developed and enhanced to provide a flexible, cost-efficient private cloud platform for clients.
Costs capitalised during the year for development of software amounted to £2.4m (2023: £1.8m).
Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of the business and the execution of the Company’s strategy are subject to a number of risks;
these risks are identified and mitigated where appropriate. The key business risks affecting the Company relate
to competition and evolving technologies requested by clients. To mitigate this risk Ensono continues to expand
its offerings and increase the value-add provided to clients and carefully monitors client satisfaction to help
understand clients’ expectations and whether their needs are being met.
Technological Environment
Operating within a technical industry presents risks due to advances in products and services. The success of the
Company depends on our ability to continue to deploy services and solutions that meet the technological
requirements of our clients. The Directors of the Company and the wider Ensono Group continue to encourage
investment in the development of our Envision intellectual property which provides broad visibility and deep insights
into client's infrastructure, from mainframe to cloud, allowing Ensono to proactively respond to adverse events in
client infrastructure and manage our client's IT estates in real-time.

For information on financial risks please refer to the Directors’ Report.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The Company uses a variety of financial instruments including cash, borrowings, equity investments and various
items, such as trade debtors and trade creditors, that arise directly from its operations. The main purpose of these
financial instruments is to provide working capital for the Company's operations.
The Directors are of the view that the main risks arising from the Company's financial instruments are interest rate
tisk, liquidity risk and credit risk. The Directors set and review policies for managing each of these risks and they
are summarised below. These policies have remained unchanged from previous years.
interest rate risk
The Company has no external interest-bearing loans. it finances its operations through intercompany loans which
have a variable rate of interest. The agreements for these intercompany allow for deferral of interest payments
without penalty.
Liquidity risk
The Company seeks to manage financial risk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable needs
and to invest cash assets safely and profitably. Cash flows are monitored on a weekly basis. The Company is
supported by its parent company Ensono Inc which has provided a letter of support to the Company confirming it
has significant liquid assets to support the Company if required. The Company is also entitled to payment on
demand of intercompany receivables due from the securitisation of trade debtors (note 18) of £14m (2023 - £10.8m)
at 31 December 2024.
: Credit risk

The principal risk arising from trade debtors is the non-payment of customer invoices by clients. The Company
minimises this risk through the securitisation of the trade debtors (note 18) which is on a non-recourse basis. The
securitised amount is adjusted for any credit notes subsequently issued by the Company. in this arrangement an
intercompany special purpose entity (“SPE”) purchases the Company's trade debtors and unbilled revenue at fair
market value discounted based on prime rates published in the money market section of The Wall Street Journal
in a non-recourse arrangement. The Company retains responsibility for collections from clients and in exchange
receives immediate entitlement to payment from the SPE for the cumulative trade debtors and unbilled revenue.

Methodologies
The company has taken guidance from the ‘UK Government Environmental Reporting Guidelines’ (March 2019)
and the ‘Greenhouse gas reporting: conversion factors 2024’ documents for calculating its carbon emissions.
Gas and electricity data from our data centres and offices have been obtained directly from our landlords. Diesel
consumption has been obtained for our company van fleet directly from the contracted vendor. Transport mileage
data has been compiled for our employee mileage from personal cars. This data was converted to CO2e emissions
using the appropriate emission conversion factors from the UK Government GHG Conversion document.
Energy Efficiency Action
During the year, the company has entered into a sustainable energy contract at the Farnborough Data Centre. This
supplies 100% renewable energy and includes full carbon offsetting through REDD+.
The company will continue to implement further measures to improve energy efficiency, such as consolidation of
the company’s data centre footprint, where appropriate, and encouraging teams to take advantage of a flexible
desk booking system with a hybrid office provider across the UK, minimising unnecessary commuting.
The company will also review the potential to convert its van fleet to electric vehicles at the end of the life of the
current van fleet.
Moving forward, the company will continue its commitment to sustainable practices and dedication to reducing its
carbon footprint through the new implementation of the ISO 14001 standard, with the aim of identifying areas of
improvement, measure the environmental impact and implement strategies to achieve our environmental
objectives.

2.14 Financial instruments

The Company has chosen to adopt Sections 11 and 12 of FRS 102 in respect of financial instruments.

The Company only enters into basic financial instrument transactions that result in the recognition of

Debt instruments (other than those wholly repayable or receivable within one year), including loans

flows and subsequently at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Debt instruments that

are payable or receivable within one year, typically trade debtors and creditors, are measured, initially

and subsequently, at the undiscounted amount of the cash or other consideration expected to be paid

or received. However, if the arrangements of a short-term instrument constitute a financing transaction,

like the payment of a trade debt deferred beyond normal business terms or in case of an out-right

short-term loan that is not at market rate, the financial asset or liability is measured, initially at the

present value of future cash flows discounted at a market rate of interest for a similar debt instrument

and subsequently at amortised cost. —

Financial assets that are measured at cost and amortised cost are assessed at the end of each

Statement of Financial Position period for objective evidence of impairment. If objective evidence of

: impairment is found, an impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income. /

For financial assets measured at amortised cost, the impairment loss is measured as the difference

between an asset's carrying amount and the present value of estimated cash flows discounted at the

asset's original effective interest rate. If a financial asset has a variable interest rate, the discount rate

for measuring any impairment loss is the current effective interest rate determined under the contract.

an approximation of the amount that the Company would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at

the Statement of Financial Position date.

Financial assets and liabilities are offset and the net amount reported in the Statement of Financial

to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

2.20 Impairment of non-financial assets
Assets that are subject to depreciation or amortisation are assessed at each Statement of Financial
Position date to determine whether there is any indication that the assets are impaired. Where there
is any indication that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset (or cash-generating
unit to which the asset has been allocated) is tested for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised
for the amount by which the asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. For the purpose
of assessing impairment, assets are grouped at the lowest levels for which they are separately
identifiable cash inflows which are largely independent of the cash inflows from other assets or a group
of assets (cash-generating units). Non-financial assets that suffer an impairment are reviewed for
possible reversal of the impairment at the end of each Statement of Financiat Position period.

2.21 Impairment of investments
At each Statement of Financial Position date, investments are assessed to determine whether there is
an indication that these assets may be impaired. If there is such an indication, the recoverable amount
of the asset is compared to its respective carrying amount. The calculation involves use of significant
estimates and assumptions which includes turnover and earnings multiples, growth rates and net
margins used to calculate projected future cash flows, risk-adjusted discount rate, future economic and
market conditions (see Note 21).

2.22 Impairment of intercompany assets
At each Statement of Financial Position date, intercompany receivable balances are assessed to
determine whether there is an indication that these assets may not be recoverable. If there is such an
indication, the recoverable amount of the asset is compared to its respective carrying amount. If the
carrying amount is greater than the recoverable amount, an impairment is recognised to reduce the
carrying amount to its recoverable amount (see Note 21).
