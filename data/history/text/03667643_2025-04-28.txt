Principal risks and uncertainties : .
The directors have fully considered all known risks and uncertainties which may affect the company in the future.
The primary risks are as follows:
1. Changes in the regulatory environment;
2. Impact of market prices on customer demand and solvency; and
3. The impact of macro-economic factors such as inflation and inflation on customer business failures.
The directors’ report sets out the directors’ consideration of the business risks and the impact on the Company's
operations. The company maintains working relationships with relevant industry regulators in order to anticipate
- and respond to changes and mitigate the impact upon group results. The commercial offerings of the company
across all services are also regularly reviewed to ensure that they remain competitive in the marketplace and
good value to customers. Whilst increases in wholesale prices may adversely impact customer volumetric
usage, there is no such effect on fixed charges, and in the event of a specific customer insolvency, utility
supplies switch to the landlord or new tenant, which reduces the exposure of the company.

. Going concern
The group secured a new Senior Facilities Agreement (SFA) on 13 July 2023, which provided additional liquidity
and headroom, however, the commercial environment has continued to be extremely challenging, with not only
sustained higher energy .prices, but also wider inflationary pressures and interest rates impacting upon the
default rate for the group’s small business customer base. The group also faced ongoing operational challenges,
particularly in relation to debt recovery and disconnection processes for non-paying customers. These issues
had an adverse impact upon the group's forecast liquidity.

: In response, the group has initiated a series of operational, commercial, and strategic actions to reduce the
exposure of the group and its:customers to future energy price volatility, improve credit control timelines and
minimise potential bad debt risks. :

On 30 April 2024, the group announced a new strategic partnership with a business process outsourcing partner,
who have since taken over responsibility for customer care and debt operations. This arrangement provides the
group with greater flexibility to react to current growth objectives and any future market fluctuations.
In September 2024, the board detailed further plans to stabilise the business, create a platform for sustainable
growth, remove exposure to potential volatility in wholesale markets, and improve the quality of earnings. In
parallel, actions are underway to improve the water portfolio by working with third parties to address issues
arising from vacant sites and non-paying customers, whilst focussing on maximizing revenue from telecoms and
payments products via initiatives to improve our offerings, pricing and management of debt exposure.
To accelerate the growth of the group’s insurance customer base, a new strategic partnership one of the UK’s
largest business insurance providers was also announced in September 2024. The group will leverage the
strength of the partner's technology, processes, insurance panel and customer service, delivered under the
group's Clear Business brand and underpinned by our marketing. -
The partnership with the outsourcing partner commenced in May 2024, with the transition of services now
. completed, and the insurance partnership started operating in November 2024. Whilst some early operational
challenges have been experienced, and there remains execution risk in embedding these new relationships, the
board is confident that the approach of working with long-term strategic partners will provide a stable base for .
future growth.
In relation to energy customers, on 17 December 2024, the group secured an agreement with an energy partner
which will fundamentally change the group's business model in this market. The new commercial arrangement is
intended to mitigate the risks faced by the business, specifically in relation to the management of non-paying
. customers, and removing volatility associated with exposure to wholesale energy markets, with the transition to
the new trading arrangements commencing in January 2025. As of March 20285, all ‘deemed’ customers have
: been transferred to the partner's licence and in-contract customers continue to transfer to the partner's licence in :
accordance with the business plan. The group is now able to focus on selling and marketing energy products
. : without the associated pricing and hedging volatility.
Additionally, the group secured a new Senior Facilities Agreement (SFA) on 22 January 2025, which extended
the term of the Group’s senior debt facilities, reset the covenants applied thereon, and waived any previous
defaults. : :
The board is confident that the group now has a strong platform for further turnaround and growth, carrying a
significantly reduced risk profile. The recent agreement to restate and amend the group's banking facilities,
including an extension in the maturity of those facilities and refreshed financial covenants, demonstrates that the
group continues to enjoy the support and confidence of its financial stakeholders.

The board does, however, recognise that challenges remain in the economic environment which may impact on
our customer base, and consequently on the group's own liquidity and profitability through customer collections
and the pace of growth. Whilst the directors have confidence in the programme of transformations being
undertaken, some of the key elements of that programme remain a work in progress or are in early-stage
: delivery. All change carries some execution risk, and it is necessary for the board to highlight this.
It is always possible that these strategic changes will not deliver the anticipated improvements in quality of
earnings or cashflows, which could adversely affect group EBITDA, cashflows and liquidity. In addition to the
potential impact of adverse changes in expectations modelled by management as part of the going concern
assessment, management considered a final severe but plausible downside scenario impacting the period to
April 2026, based upon the three most significant sensitivities. In this scenario, the potential benefits of the
insurance partnership, in terms of renewal of the existing customer base, new business and cross-selling to
existing customers would not be achieved, assumed growth rates for both new-business and cross-sell of
telecoms and payments products would not be achieved, and the group’s planned investment in marketing spend
would be delayed.
The result of this would be a negative impact upon EBITDA of £2.2m in the year to 30 April 2026. This would not
, lead to a breach of the group's liquidity covenant during the going concern period, but would, without preventative
measures, potentially lead to a breach of the group’s EBITDA covenant at certain quarterly test dates during this
period. In order to prevent this, the board is already undertaking mitigating actions, which may include requesting
covenant waivers from the group’s financial stakeholders.
As a severe shock impacting on the business as the transformation plan is embedded cannot be ruled out, it is

: necessary to disclose that there is a material uncertainty over the going concern of the company and group.

: , After due consideration of these circumstances, the directors have a reasonable expectation that the group and
company have adequate resources to continue in operational existence and serve and settle liabilities for a
period of at least 12 months from the date of approving these financial statements. Accordingly, the group and
company financial statements continue to be prepared on the going concern basis. Further details regarding the
adoption of the going concern basis can be found in note 2. - :

Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures in line with our
responsibilities, outlined above, to detect material misstatements in respect of irregularities, including fraud. The extent to which our
procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud, is detailed below. 7
Based on our understanding of the company and industry, we identified that the principal risks of non-compliance with laws and
regulations related to those set by the communications industry regulators, and we considered the extent to which non-compliance
might have a material effect on the financial statements. We also considered those laws and regulations that have a direct impact on
the financial statements such as the Companies Act 2006 and tax legislation. We evaluated management'’s incentives and opportunities
for fraudulent manipulation of the financial statements (including the risk of override of controls), and determined that the principal
tisks were related to posting inappropriate journal entries to manipulate results and management bias in accounting estimates. Audit

© Discussions with management including consideration of known or suspected instances of non-compliancewith laws and regulation

. There are inherent limitations in the audit procedures described above. We are less likely to become aware of instances of non-

Critical accounting judgements ‘
Revenue recognition . .
In certain circumstances, when a customer cancels a contract before the agreed end date, the group is
entitled to charge a termination fee. Despite the fact that the group is contractually entitled to apply these
fees, recoverability of this type of revenue has historically been lower than for other revenue streams.
The directors believe that, based on past performance and.collection patterns, they can reliably measure

: the probable economic benefits that will flow to the group from termination fees, and revenue is .
recognised to the extent that it is expected to be recovered; however, there is inevitably some variability in
the level of recoverability of termination fees, which is taken into account when assessing the initial fair
value. Historic data indicates that the variability in recoverability of termination fees within group entities is
, less than 20%. Should the assumptions on which termination fee revenue is calculated vary by more than

20% then there may be a material impact on reported revenues and profits.

, In circumstances where a customer has unpaid debt, there can be delays and operational and legal
obstacles during the disconnection process, whereby the group is still providing services to a customer
with poor payment history and outstanding debt. These customers are classified as a ‘bad’ customer for
debt recovery purposes.

Analysis of past recovery patterns indicate that once unpaid debt reaches certain milestones, the

. probability of recovery is less than 50%; for this cohort of ‘bad’ customers with live services, recovery of
revenue is not considered probable at the point that services are provided, and revenue is therefore not
recognised for these services, nor are the related unpaid invoices recognised in gross trade receivables. .
Revenue is only recognised in relation to this group of customers upon successful recovery, or where :
changes in recent payment patterns indicate that future recovery is probable, and the customer is no
longer classified as ‘bad’. Should the receipts from the current population of ‘bad’ customers vary by more
than 5% from historical patterns, then there may be a material impact on reported revenues, gross trade
receivables, and gross bad debt provisions.

Recoverability of trade receivables
: After initial recognition of revenue and associated accounts receivable, excluding amounts invoiced to.
‘pad’ customers where recovery is deemed improbable at the point that invoices are raised, trade
receivables are reviewed to estimate the level of provision required for irrecoverable debt. Provisions are
made against the portfolio of trade receivables based upon payment history and the classification of
: customer accounts, which is aligned to the management of customer accounts and debt collection
processes, for example, including factors such as whether the customer has live services, and the relative

age of debt.
