Our Business Model
The Company's principal activities is the delivery of large-scale solutions for high-stakes examination
processing, with a focus on electronic marking. The business provides a combination of electronic
marking software solutions (e-Marker®) and, in the UK, a bureau service for the secure scanning of
examination papers. The integration of the software and services elements is an important aspect of the
Company's operations in the UK.
Results
The profit for the financial year after taxation amounted to £2,863,000 (2022: £6,762,000 loss) on a
turnover of £23,136,000 (2022: £11,136,000). The loss in the prior year was mainly due to the
cancellation of the Summer 2021 GCSE and A level examinations because of the Covid 19 pandemic,
as well as £3,006,000 of development amortisation which was incurred. The profit in the current year is
due to Summer 2022 GCSE and A level examinations going ahead as normal and in line with pre Covid
19 levels.
The net liability position of the Company at 31 March 2023 was £4,746,000 (2022: £7,609,000).
Donations
During the year ended 31 March 2023 the Company made charitable donations totalling £nil. in the
previous year the Company donated £nil to its parent company, AQA Education, under HMRC’s Gift Aid
scheme.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) (continued)

1 Total Revenue

Revenue performance tracks the level of activity of the business. Monitoring revenue year on year

provides a means of measuring growth in the business.

Total revenue increased by 107% compared to the prior year due to returning to pre-pandemic levels to

deliver a full summer series. In the prior year, virtually no examination scripts were processed. Returning

to the full summer series has resulted in a significant increase in revenue in comparison. International
. markets have continued to prove challenging, particularly in Africa. The sales in respect of the PS1000

scanners have decreased due to the lack of spare scanning machines following the decision to cease

production of the PS1000.

2 International Education Revenue

International education revenue remains an area of strategic relevance for the Company. Overall

international revenue was down 76% versus last year which is driven by a reduction in sales of scanning

machines and associated equipment to the international market.

3 Research and Development expenditure (cash expenditure excluding amortisation)

The Company bases its business approach on delivering a high level of technical excellence and by

forging long-term relationships with its customers. Investment in software and hardware products to

maintain a competitive advantage is very important.

During the year the main product development activity related to continuation of developing and

investing in the latest e-Marker® examination marking product to offer a Whole Script Marking solution.

4 Operational return on sales

The gain/loss before taxation figure for the year has no adjustments for the purposes of generating the

operational return on sales. Operational return was 5.5% (2022: operational loss 60.7%). In the prior

year this included £3,006,000 of development amortisation.

The operational return on sales is based on profit/loss before taxation, adjusted for exceptional items,

expressed as a percentage of total revenue.

5 Employees‘ average length of service
Retaining employees and ensuring an appropriate mix of skills is maintained is particularly important to
AQA Milton Keynes as the work undertaken requires highly trained employees to deliver large complex
solutions to plan. The purpose of monitoring the average length of service of our employees is to check
that we are retaining the experience required. The average length of service calculation is based on the
permanent monthly workforce. In the past year this has decreased from 9.14 years to 7.21 years, this
was due to a significant increase in headcount required to deliver a full summer series back to pre-
pandemic levels. This cohort of new employees has brought down the average length of service.
In the financial year ended 31 March 2023 AQA Milton Keynes employed an average 129 (2022: 119)
permanent monthly paid employees.
6 Total energy consumed
The Board is conscious of its social responsibility to use energy more efficiently and is continually
seeking ways to reduce unnecessary consumption as well as investing in sustainable sourcing.
The number of premises from which the Company operates has been reduced due to future workplace
project increasing employee capacity and the Wymbush building being closed. As a result, this has
decreased the energy consumption levels from 0.78 Gwh to 0.51 Gwh.
Developments
The Board expects the Company to continue trading for the foreseeable future focusing the majority of
immediate investment on software product development, in order to strengthen its position in the UK
marketplace, whilst maintaining a market presence in International Education.
During the year AQA Education and the Company continued to became further integrated by an
increased sharing of service functions to provide Group-wide Finance, Human Resources, Procurement,
Communication and Facilities functions to support the wider AQA family.
AQA Milton Keynes also completed its investment into the future workplace project which involved
developing and refurbishing the scanning centre site to enhance the facilities for the business, staff and :
staff wellbeing.

Environmental Policy ,
The Company strives to improve its environmental performance through the implementation of
sustainable development and the pursuit of appropriate environmental activities.
Environmental impact is reviewed regularly in order to:

* meet all statutory obligations;

* pursue working practices which reduce the business’ environmental load; and

e re-cycle waste products and safely dispose of non-recyclable materials.
AQA Milton Keynes works with environmentally responsible organisations that support its approach.
Several different waste types are recycled including paper reel ends, compactor waste polythene,
shredded materials, cardboard and plastic cups/bottles.
Employees are encouraged to submit their ideas on how consumption of energy and other resources

At the end of life of products supplied, customers are expected to return them to AQA Milton Keynes for

2011/65/EU (RoHS Directive) ensuring its products comply, where required, with the restrictions on the
use of certain hazardous substances. This revised directive includes additional categories of Electrical
and Electronic Equipment (EEE) and European Conformity (CE) marking obligations.

2.7 Provisions
Provisions are recognised when the Company has a present obligation (legal or constructive) as a result
of a past event, it is probable that the Company will be required to settle that obligation and a reliable
estimate can be made to the amount of the obligation. The timing or the amount of the future expenditure
required to settle the obligation is uncertain.
The amount recognised as a provision is the best estimate of the consideration required to settle the
present obligation at the Statement of Financial Position date, taking into account the risks and
uncertainties surrounding the obligation. Where a provision is measured using the cash flows estimated
to settie the present obligation, its carrying amount is the present value of those cash flows.
2.8 Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of the current tax expense and deferred tax expense. Current tax
assets are recognised when tax paid exceeds the tax payable.
Current tax is based on taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from total comprehensive income
because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable or deductible in other periods. Current
tax assets and liabilities are measured using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the Statement of Financial Position date.
Deferred tax is calculated using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted
and that are expected to apply to the period when the asset is realised, or the liability is settled. Deferred
tax is not discounted. :
Deferred tax liabilities are recognised in respect of all timing differences that exist at the reporting date.
Timing differences are differences between taxable profits and total comprehensive income that arise
from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in different periods from their recognition
in the financial statements. Deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that
they will be recovered by the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
R&D tax credit claims are recognised following formal confirmation of acceptance by HMRC or where
previous precedence is established and are reflected in the cost of sales line in the Income Statement
in accordance with the Company being part of a large Group.
In respect of potential Gift Aid payments which it is envisaged will be paid within nine months of the year
end, in line with UK tax legislation, a deduction is taken against the Company's taxable profits thus
reducing the Company’s income tax liability. The income tax effect of the Gift Aid payments is thus
recognised at the reporting date. However, the deferred tax effects in relation to the Gift Aid payments
are not recognised.

which incorporate multiple products and services such as scanning machines, software, printed
forms and professional services it is necessary to consider the nature of the contract with the

to take place. The judgement is determining when performance conditions are satisfied for the
respective elements of the solution to become recognised as revenue.

given their obsolete components, together with the uncertainties of marketability and support.

3 Financial risk management
The Company's activities expose it to a variety of financial risks. The Board reviews and agrees policies
for managing each of these risks and they are summarised below:
(a) Currency risk
: The Company operates internationally and is subject to foreign exchange risk arising from
various currency exposures, primarily with respect to the US dollar and the Euro. The Company
. does not hedge any transactions, and foreign exchange differences on retranslation of foreign
assets and liabilities are recognised in the Income Statement.
(b) Interest rate risk
The Company finances its operations through an intercompany loan agreement with its ultimate
parent company, AQA Education. In the agreement the interest rate is fixed for a 12-month
period on 1 April each year and interest is payable on daily balances outstanding at a rate of
4.75% pa over the Royal Bank of Scotland base rate. The Company currently does not hedge.
(c) Credit risk
The Company has a small number of customers that account for a significant proportion of the
turnover. Having close working relationships with these customers, established over a long
term, helps to manage the risk exposure. Where appropriate, sales to overseas customers are
usually underwritten using letters of credit unless the customer makes a significant up-front
payment. A summary of all customers with indebtedness in excess of £100,000 or that reside
in the top 90% of debtors by value is prepared on a monthly basis for the Board to review.
(d) Liquidity risk
The Company takes a prudent approach to managing liquidity risk to ensure sufficient cash is
available to meet foreseeable needs and to finance safely the successful completion of large-
scale contracts, thereby minimising liquidity risk issues.
