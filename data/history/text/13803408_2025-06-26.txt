Strategic Report

For the year ended 31 December 2024

The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.

Principal activities :

The principal activities of the group are the provision of services for the design, deployment, integration and
support of in-building ‘smart’ technologies including; network connectivity, both wired and wireless, electronic
security, audio visual systems, loT (environmental, energy and occupancy) sensor networks and building
management software.

Fair review of the group business

The group proudly maintains its leading position within the UK smart building solutions market, demonstrating
resilience and adaptability in a transforming landscape. Our strategic focus remains firmly on capitalising on
the burgeoning demand for smart building enablement driven by the Corporate Real Estate (CRE) sector's
pursuit of vital building certifications (SmartScore, WELL, NABERs). This unprecedented market pull, fuelled
by the need for landlords and tenants to create attractive and efficient office spaces, underpins our confidence
in sustained business growth.

In the past year, we achieved a slight increase in overall turnover to £29.49m (2023: £29.31m), highlighting
the underlying strength of our market position. While turnover in our Managed Services segment saw a
planned reduction to £9.14m (2023: £10.52m) as we strategically shift towards higher-margin opportunities,
we experienced robust growth in our Frameworks contracts, particularly within the Airports sector, which
increased to £6.85m (2023: £5.55m). Our multi-technology design and build contracts also demonstrated
positive momentum, rising to £12.53m (2023: £12.03m). We note the potential for even greater expansion in
this area, which was temporarily moderated by the administration of ISG. Importantly, we have successfully
secured the continuation of all our live ISG contracts through other main contractors, ensuring their delivery in
2025.

Our strategic direction is purposefully geared towards a transition from traditional, site-based cabling services
to higher-value multi-technology digital service contracts and flexible ‘on-demand’ digital service offerings.
This strategic pivot is clearly reflected in our robust project pipeline and we anticipate strong, continued
growth in these key service areas over the next 3 to 5 years.

Gross Profit Margins experienced a temporary reduction to 26% (2023: 29%), primarily attributable to the
planned evolution within our traditional managed service contracts. Recognising the significant opportunities
within the burgeoning ‘smart’ services sector, we have strategically invested in expanding our operational
capabilities through additional headcount in Operational Technology (OT) which includes BMS and lighting
control, and Audio Visual solutions. This investment was necessary to fuel future growth and resulted in an
increase in expenses to £7.9m for the year (2023: £7.08m), leading to group Earnings Before Interest, Tax,
Depreciation and Amortisation (EBITDA) of £640k (2023: £1.75m) and a group pre-tax profit of £57k (2023:
£1.19m).

Looking ahead, our business prospects are continually improving and remain exceptionally strong. We are
witnessing a steady increase in work in progress, complemented by a solid pipeline of new business
opportunities. The genuine excitement surrounding smart building enablement and loT technologies within the
CRE market provides a powerful tailwind for our growth. The directors are highly confident that the group is
strategically positioned to capitalise on this unprecedented demand and continue its upward trajectory.

. The group has proactively undertaken significant steps to adapt its product and service offerings in direct
response to the evolving needs of our clients in the rapidly evolving CRE market. We are well-equipped to
navigate both the challenges and the considerable opportunities presented by this ongoing digital
transformation, ensuring our continued success and leadership in the UK smart building solutions market. :

Global services

Since 2010, the group has been the UK and European representative of LGAP, a global alliance of like-

minded service providers with combined annual sales exceeding $400m in 2023. With over 38 offices
worldwide, LGAP’s members support major corporate clients throughout North America, South America, Asia

Pacific, Europe, South Africa, Hong Kong, India, China, Australia, New Zealand and Japan. LGAP continues

to be a valuable partnership for the group, leading to opportunities in data centre builds, enterprise office fit

outs and IT integration projects.

- The Brexit transition period and eventual exit of the UK from the European Union continues to make it difficult
to both pursue and fulfil opportunities within the EU; therefore, the group continues to explore opportunities to
establish one or more entities within the EU and will continue to foster and develop close partnerships with our
LGAP partners, enabling us to fully support our global clients in the region.

Quality and management systems
The group places emphasis on quality and excellence and is accredited to ISO 9001:2015 (Quality
Management System — QMS), ISO 14001:2015 (Environmental Quality Management System - EQMS) and
ISO 27001:2022 (Information Security Management System - ISMS).
These standards are embedded into our fundamental business processes and policies. The combination of
these three internationally recognised standards ensure that our clients receive the best possible quality of
service with minimal impact to the environment, and provide them with the confidence that their information
assets are secured to the highest standard.
Research and development.
Our commitment to research and development (R&D) remains focused on advancing our proprietary digital
commissioning and smart building enablement software. This innovative solution is designed to revolutionise
the management of building data by seamlessly homogenising unstructured and siloed data from disparate
systems into a unified, structured source. Its intrinsic multi-protocol support and built-in MQTT broker unlock
its ability to natively support a wide array of smart building platform applications. Additionally, it automates
critical digital asset deployment, configuration, and integration—dramatically enhancing efficiency while
reducing complexity and risk.
The smart buildings and 'PropTech' market has experienced accelerated growth in the wake of the pandemic.
This surge underscores a critical need for organisations to demonstrably understand the tangible value that
integrated smart building can deliver. Our strategic focus is to empower our clients to transform their
workspaces and create energy and operationally efficient environments that also effectively encourage staff to
return to the office and optimise productivity.
Our core objective is to showcase our ability to deliver significant improvements in operational efficiency,
encompassing energy usage, maintenance and occupancy management. Crucially, we are also focused on
enhancing the safety, health, and wellbeing of occupants, while providing a user experience that surpasses
the allure of working from home.
The emergence of industry-leading standards, such as those championed by WiredScore and The
International WELL Building Institute, provide clear benchmarks against which the efficacy of our solutions can
be rigorously measured. This presents both a necessity and a significant opportunity for us to proactively
demonstrate and validate the performance of our smart building solutions to the market before their
deployment.

Principal risks and uncertainties :

The directors have responsibility for the identification, evaluation and management of risks within the group,
recognising the importance of identifying and actively monitoring our strategic, reputational, financial and
operational risks. The group Risk Register is regularly reviewed by the directors and updated with key
stakeholders in order to maintain an effective risk management framework.

The directors have considered the risks that are most significant to the business in terms of both operational
and financial impact, and these are set out as follows:

Market conditions

The group's services may be affected by a future economic downturn and reductions or delays in client
spending, particularly where ongoing uncertainty in the future use, requirements and purpose of corporate real
estate continues.

The group’s strategy to combat such adverse conditions is to maintain a balanced portfolio in terms of client
base and service offerings, limiting the exposure to any one market sector.

The need to repurpose corporate real estate in order to provide high quality collaboration and social space
(‘hotelification’), including improvements in connectivity,. environmental monitoring (particularly indoor air
quality), access control and video conferencing all present the group with significant opportunities for growth.
Customer base

Our client base includes; enterprise tenants, commercial landlords, data centre operators, flexible workspace
operators, residential and hospitality. Over recent years, the group has taken measures to significantly
broaden service offerings and actively reduce the reliance on any particular market sector, with a broader
range of services being delivered to a wider range of vertical markets. This is in addition to continuing to
expand our footprint outside of the UK into Europe and beyond, through our LGAP partnership.

Competition :

Whilst relationships, reputation, quality and capability are key elements of a client's decision to award
contracts, price remains a key driver in decision making. The group operates in highly competitive sectors,
particularly during the construction phase, which can at least initially, dilute gross profit margins.

- The group remains focused on cost and efficiency in order to remain competitive during the construction
phase but undertakes a rigorous bid evaluation process in order to ensure it only competitively bids for project
work where the gross margin is commensurate with risk, and is well matched to its post-construction support
services model.

Commercial risk management
The group is subject to contractual risk by virtue of the services it provides and contracts it undertakes.
In order to limit future exposure to high-risk onerous commitments, all potential contracts are thoroughly
evaluated and risk assessed, ensuring that commercial risk is both fully quantified and commensurate with the
anticipated return before agreements are entered into. Robust measures and controls in this particular area of
risk management continue to have a significant positive impact on both profitability and future business
stability.
Supply chain
Given the ongoing uncertain economic conditions, general disruption and the possibility of insolvency within
the supply chain remain significant risks. In addition, delays in the supply chain continue to remain a threat to
the successful delivery of projects and other services. The group builds and maintains strong supply chain
relationships. It conducts a thorough due diligence process, whilst working to reduce overreliance on any one
supplier or manufacturer.

Financial risk management

The group's operations expose it to financial risks that include credit risk, foreign exchange risk and the risk

associated with the impact of materials and labour inflation on long-term, fixed price contracts. Given the size

of the group, the directors have not delegated the responsibility of monitoring financial risk management to a

sub-committee of the board. The group’s finance, commercial and procurement departments implement the

. policies set by the board.

Liquidity risk management

The objective of the group in managing liquidity risk is to ensure that it can meet its financial obligations as

and when they fall due, striving to ensure that suppliers are paid in accordance with agreed terms. This is

achieved by tightly managing cashflows, particularly at project level.

The group expects to meet its financial obligations through operating cash flows and existing financing

arrangements.

Credit risk management

The group maintains policies that require appropriate credit verification procedures on all new clients and has

robust credit control procedures in place. Credit limits and debtor balances for existing clients are monitored

on an on-going basis and provision is made for doubtful debts where necessary. Digital tools and subscription

services are also used to constantly assess the performance and risk profiles of our key clients. Given the

current economic uncertainty, there are particular risks associated with construction contracts and the group

carefully evaluates all prospective clients before entering into any new-contract.

Foreign exchange currency exposure

The group is occasionally exposed to foreign exchange risk as a result of overseas operations and also by

way of unmitigated price fluctuations on the cost of raw materials and other goods.

For all overseas work, the group assesses the potential adverse impact on profit of fluctuations in foreign

exchange rates and currency ‘pairings, notably Sterling/Euro and Sterling/Dollar rates. Where transaction

exposure is considered to be significant, a forward currency contract is evaluated and entered into if deemed

beneficial.

Future business prospects :

The increasing demand for smarter digital buildings is poised to accelerate as the market matures and the

definition of a ‘smart building’ gains standardisation through reputable independent organisations like RIBA,

WiredScore, NABERS and the International WELL Building Institute. Outcome-focused certification programs,

targeting both landlords and tenants, necessitate the integration of digital technologies. This integration

provides building managers with a unified operational overview and empowers users with innovative
. applications that personalise their interaction with the building and its community. This market evolution

presents a significant opportunity for the group to expand its software and digital service offerings.

Simultaneously, technological advancements are reshaping the demand for traditional, site-dependent,

discrete IT and FM support services. In today's mobile and wireless environment, fixed support models

designed for previous eras offer diminishing returns. The conventional Support and Maintenance industry

often relies on the inefficient deployment of siloed technical resources, leading to costly repeat visits by

specialised technicians to resolve issues within increasingly sophisticated and integrated built environments.

Consequently, we are witnessing a fundamental cultural shift towards integrated, centralised, remotely

managed, and ‘pay as you go’ digital service and support models. In direct response to this evolving demand,

the group is strategically re-engineering our workplace support business, which was significantly impacted by

the pandemic, to establish a holistic, integrated, digital on-demand support service.

1.7 Intangible fixed assets - goodwill
Goodwill represents the excess of the cost of acquisition of a business over the fair value of net assets
acquired. It is initially recognised as an asset at cost and is subsequently measured at cost less
accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is considered to have a finite
useful life and is amortised on a straight line basis over its expected life, which is 10 years.
For the purposes of impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the cash-generating units expected to
benefit from the acquisition. Cash-generating units to which goodwill has been allocated are tested for
impairment at least annually, or more frequently when there is an indication that the unit may be
impaired. If the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit is less than the carrying amount of the
unit, the impairment loss is allocated first to reduce the carrying amount of any goodwill allocated to the
unit and then to the other assets of the unit pro-rata on the basis of the carrying amount of each asset in
the unit. /

1.16 Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and deferred tax.
Current tax
The tax currently payable is based on taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from net profit as
reported in the profit and loss account because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable
or deductible in other years and it further excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The
group's liability for current tax is calculated using tax rates that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the reporting end date.
Deferred tax
Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all timing differences and deferred tax assets are
recognised to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax
liabilities or other future taxable profits. Such assets and liabilities are not recognised if the timing
difference arises from goodwill or fram the initial recognition of other assets and liabilities in a transaction
that affects neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting end date and reduced to the
extent that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the
asset to be recovered. Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period
when the liability is settled or the asset is realised. Deferred tax is charged or credited in the profit and
loss account, except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to equity, in which case the
: deferred tax is also dealt with in equity. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset if, and only if, there is
a legally enforceable right to offset current tax assets and liabilities and the deferred tax assets and
liabilities relate to taxes levied by the same tax authority.
