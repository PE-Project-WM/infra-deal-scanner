Business review
Pioneer Business Systems Limited ("the Company’) is a wholly owned subsidiary of 4Com Technologies Limited
("the Group") and is responsible for the marketing and selling of telephone systems for the Group.
The Company's objective is to provide telecommunication systems to UK SMEs, by delivering high-performance ~
phone systems which focus upon usability and versatility; allowing our customers to remain connected, wherever
they are choosing to work,
Financial review
Turnover for the year ended 30 June 2023 was £49,447,000 (2022: £19,591 ,000). A gross margin of 50% (2022:
46%) shows a slight increase from the previous year.
The profit on ordinary activities after taxation for the year was £4,530,000 (2022: profit of £2,260,000). Net
assets as at the year end were £1,935,000 (2022: net liabilities of £2,505,000).
Other matters
The Company recognises its responsibilities with regards to environmental matters, including the impact of the
Company's business activities on the environment and where appropriate takes action to minimise any adverse .
impact.
Employees are viewed as a key asset of the business and as such the Company aims to offer industry leading
benefits to employee involvement.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risks and uncertainties and the management of these are discussed in the Directors’ Report.
Financial key performance indicators
The key performance indicator for the Company is deemed to be revenue and gross margin.

Going concern
At the year end, the Company's assets exceeded its liabilities by £1,935,000 (2022: liabilities exceeded assts by
£2,595,000) and had a net current asset position of £1,935,000 (2022: net current liabilities of £2,505,000).
Furthermore, the Company has significant intercompany balances with Group entities. The Company's ability to
settle its liabilities as they fall due is dependent on the financial support of the ultimate parent company. The
directors have received confirmation via a letter of support from 4Com Technologies Limited which confirms this
financial support for a period to 31 March 2025.
In continuing to adopt a going concern basis for preparing the financial statements for the year ended 30 June
2023, the directors have reviewed trading and cash forecasts prepared for the wider Group up to 31 March 2025,
being the going concern assessment period. This included considering the availability of liquidity headroom on the
revolving credit facility, and a number of uncertainties which are set out in the wider Group's principal risks, as
well as the wider Group's exposure to credit risk, interest rate risk, liquidity risk, currency risk and foreign
exchange risk.
The directors have also considered impacts on future trading and liquidity in the context of the current operating
and macro-economic environment, which includes movement on interest rates, rising energy prices and inflation,
all of which have been reviewed in our going concern assessment period to the 31 March 2025. We have
considered these impacts and conclude that we have taken sufficient steps to mitigate our exposure to the current
operating and macro-economic environment. The mitigation steps taken include forward purchases of inventory
so that sufficient inventory levels are held to protect against any future supply chain impacts.
We have also considered the current geopolitical environment, most notably the conflict in Ukraine which has
created potential supply problems, product shortages and general price rises particularly in relation to fuel, gas
and electricity. We conclude the conflict has had a minimal effect on the wider group’s forecast and liquidity .
position, due to the geographic location of the wider Group’s supply chain, being predominately Asia.
The Company and Group has performed a full going concern assessment for the year ended 30 June 2023.
During the year the business has continued its significant growth, both organic and through acquisitions
and has continued to grow revenues above previous year levels. Underlying market trends are driving
demand in the 4Com focused segment due to the accelerated shift to more flexible working solutions, as well
as longer term drivers such as the upcoming switch off of PSTN in 2025. The wider Group's business model
is to continue to offer unified communications to SMEs, creating long-lasting revenue streams from a growing
customer base, as well as securing an upgrade pathway to customers to move to new technologies when
launched. This model is supported by a panel of asset-based finance providers. In addition, the wider Group has
demonstrated significant revenue synergies through its acquisition model, completing a further 5
acquisitions during FY23, growing recurring revenue and delivering on associated cost synergies.
The major term lender has continued to support the wider Group during the year, providing a £10m acquisition
loan to purchase Reach Digital Telecoms, and a further £2m drawdown of existing facilities to fund post year end
acquisitions. Term foan facilities were extended to August 2026 during the prior year. The wider Group has
access to additional undrawn facilities of £9m of term loan, of which £5m can be utilised as a liquidity bridge and
once repaid is available for further discretionary projects. The wider Group is subject to covenant compliance in
relation to the senior debt facilities. The wider Group has met all covenants during the year ended 30 June 2023
and is expected to continue to meet the covenants until the going concern period. In addition the wider Group has
access to an undrawn £0.8m of trade loan facility and a £6m RCF facility which the directors are expecting to pay
back in 2024. The RCF facility was extended during FY23 by a further two years to August 2025. Our base case
scenario used in the going concern assessment does not rely on the use or renewal of the RCF facility.

Going concern (continued)
The wider Group closely monitors and manages its funding position, preparing regular forecasts with sensitivity
analysis, to ensure it has sufficient headroom to fund operations and continue to invest in discretionary R&D .
projects. To assess the wider Groups resilience to more adverse outcomes, its forecast was sensitised to reflect
scenarios based on the Group’s principal risks and the downside prospects of a potential UK recession. The
wider Group assessed the going concern by comparing a base case scenario (being the Group's forecast for the
relevant going concern period) to a downside scenario, and in the case of the downside scenario taking into
consideration relevant mitigation steps. Our downside scenario was performed reflecting a change in
assumptions in relation to significant changes in customer demand, leading to a reduction in selling price by 25%,
which may lead to lower than expected cashflows. However the downside scenario would expect to be mitigated
by headcount rationalisation.
The mitigation that would be applied in the downside scenario relate to pay cost and headcount which are within
the control of the wider Group to implement quickly in response to any downward trends should they be
necessary. While these additional mitigating actions could be implemented more quickly, they have only been
forecast within the going concern assessment period to 31 March 2025 for the purposes of the going concern
assessment. Under the downside scenario assessed, the wider Group would remain cash positive and compliant
with debt covenants throughout the whole of the going concern assessment period. The mitigation actions have
not been applied in the downside scenario as they are deemed not’required.
The directors have considered a reverse stress test scenario, being assumed cash flow forecasts during the
going concern assessment period that result in a breach of banking covenants. A 69% decline in Group
EBITDA was applied, which took into account changes in customer demands, before banking covenants were
breached (assuming no mitigating actions had been taken to offset this). We consider these scenarios to be
highly unlikely, in addition we have the mitigations applied being the same as referred to in the downside
scenario. These mitigations are fully within the control of the wider Group.
The directors are satisfied that the Company and wider Group has sufficient liquidity to meet its current
liabilities and working capital requirements through to 31 March 2025, being the going concern
assessment period. Accordingly, the directors conclude it to be appropriate that the company financial
statements are prepared on a going concern basis.
However, the Company and Group, is currently subject to an unsolicited, non-binding acquisition offer as a going
concern. If the transaction were to complete, this would result in a change of ownership and financing structure.
This would not be expected to lead to any significant changes in the Company's and Group's current operationat
structure or business model. Although the directors do not expect that any potential new transaction and financing
structure would have a detrimental effect on the Company and Group's ability to continue as a going concern, as
this potential transaction is yet to be formally agreed and as ultimate control for the operational decisions of the
Company and Group would rest with the new majority owners, this constitutes the existence of a material
uncertainty which may cast significant doubt over the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern. The
financial statements do not include the adjustments that would result if the Company could not continue as a
going concern.

General economic risk .
The Group continually assess the impact of macro-economic uncertainty, potentially leading to a downturn in the
UK economy and stress tests its forecasts, including impact from UK business confidence. The Group mitigates
this risk by continuously updating credit scores prior to approaching customers to ensure high acceptance levels
are maintained. The customer base is mainly SMEs and therefore there is no significant concentration of credit
risk, with any exposure spread across a large and diverse customer base with no concentration to any industry
sector. The Group has experience of trading through economic downturns delivering robust revenue trends, as
UC and telecoms are a critical part of business, irrespective of economic conditions.
Credit risk
The Group’s principal financial assets are bank balances and trade and other receivables. The Group's credit
risk is primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of
allowances for doubtful receivables. An allowance for impairment is made where there is an identified loss event
which, based on previous experience, is evidence of a reduction in the recoverability of the cash flows. The
Group has in place policies to ensure that sales of products are made to customers with an appropriate credit
history. Where this is not possible, appropriate credit limits are set in place after taking into account the
customers’ financial strength. The customer base is mainly SMEs and therefore there is no significant
concentration of credit risk, with any exposure spread across a large and diverse customer base. For system

. installation revenue third-party finance companies are used to purchase the equipment and provide operating
leases to customers. Funds are received on the day of the installation with the third-party funder bearing the
credit risk with the customer. The credit risk on liquid funds is limited because the counterparties are banks with
strong credit-ratings assigned by international credit-rating agencies.
Liquidity risk
In order to maintain liquidity to ensure that sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations and future
developments, the Group uses a Revolving Credit Facility (RCF) and a Term Loan Growth Fund. Compliance
with the covenants is monitored on a monthly basis as part of the treasury process.
Price risk
The Group is exposed to price risk due to normal inflationary increases in the purchase price of the goods and
services purchased in the UK. The Group has no exposure to equity securities price risk as it holds no listed or
other equity investments.
Interest rate cash flow risk
The Group has both interest-bearing assets and interest-bearing liabilities. Interest bearing assets include only
cash balances, which earn interest at variable rates. The Group’s working capital facility bears interest at a
variable rate. The Group continues to stress test effects of interest rate increase and serviceability of debt. In
relation to its other facilities, interest rates remain unhedged.
In addition to financial risks, the Group is exposed to a number of commercial risks which we seek to identify and
manage as they arise, as well as building contingency options into our plans and processes.

2.4 Turnover
Turnover comprises income recognised by the Company in respect of goods and services supplied

taxes. The following criteria must also be met before revenue is recognised:
Sale of goods
Turnover from the sale of goods is recognised when all of the following conditions are satisfied:
. the Company has transferred the significant risks and rewards of ownership to the buyer;
. the Company retains neither continuing managerial involvement to the degree usually
associated with ownership nor effective control over the goods sold;
. the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
. it is probable that the Company will receive the consideration due under the transaction; and
. the costs incurred or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be measured reliably.
Rendering of services
. Turnover from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are

conditions are satisfied:
. the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
. it is probable that the Company will receive the consideration due under the contract;
. the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured
reliably; and
. the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.
