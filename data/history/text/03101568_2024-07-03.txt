The directors present the strategic report and financial statements for the year ended 30 September 2023.

Fair review of the Business

Principal activity

The principal activity of the Company is the provision of connectivity and cloud services to both its residential and
business customers. The connectivity services provided include broadband, voice, ethernet, private and wide-area
networks, hosting and associated services.

Purpose strategy and business model

Zen’s ultimate purpose is to deliver happiness to our people, customers, partners and suppliers. We choose not to
be constrained by short-term financial targets but to strive for long-term goals aligned to our pursuit of happiness.
Our objective is to be the most trusted service provider in the market with a reputation for delivering exceptional
customer service via our people and a reputation for excellence in technology.

Full year revenues of £110m (2022 - £100m) show growth of 9% (2022 - 10%) once again above market rates. Zen
operates in both the UK consumer and business markets. Alongside topline growth, the business returned to an
underlying operating profit. For the year under review, demand for our services has remained strong across Zen's
four trading divisions, Consumer, Business, Enterprise and Partner.

Zen’s strategic focus remains on Ultrafast connectivity services, such as FTTP and a broad range of Cloud-based
networking and communications services.

* Ultrafast connectivity: Zen has achieved and exceeded its ambition to carry 80% of all FTTP services over
its own network and closed the year with 85% of FTTP services delivered across its own infrastructure.
Once again, the Board has invested heavily in infrastructure over the year.

* Cloud-based networking and communications: Continued growth in Cloud services is based on a strong
partnership with Amazon Web Services. Delivery against the strategy is progressing well and the business
continues to be recognised in the UK marketplace for the quality and resilience of the service it provides.

Zen is committed to deploying the highest quality technology across its network. The business has once again
invested considerably over the past 12 months, in excess of £3.5m, in the continued enhancement and capacity of
its core network, completed the upgrade of its estate of exchanges to be Ultrafast-ready and the unbundling of a
further 250 exchanges. The business now has a total footprint of 690 exchanges and this completes the recent
expansion programme. The completed estate has resulted in an 85% coverage of FTTP services nation-wide. The
programme was funded by existing cash balances. The other major area of capital expenditure was the upgrade of
the Data Centre power infrastructure, which contributes to the total balance of £5.7m of capital expenditure.

Financial overview
Revenue increased by 9% (2022: 10%). Growth came from broadband, voice, leased line and cloud services.
Growth is underpinned by the continuing commitment and focus on providing excellent customer service.
Gross profit percentage reduced in the year to 20.2% (2022: 21.1%). The reduction in gross profit percentage was
primarily a result of cost increases from the Company’s supply chain that it was unable to pass through to its
customer base due to its price promise guarantees. The impact of supply chain cost increases was partially offset
by the margin benefit resulting from migrating existing and placing new circuits on to the Company's own
infrastructure, following in year capital investment. The UK connectivity market is mature and increasingly
competitive with a growing number of Alternative Network providers (AltNets). The market continues to see
aggressive pricing by the AltNets. Zen’s success in growing core broadband circuits by 7% and Ethernet circuits by
9% vindicates its decision to focus on service quality to distinguish its brand in a crowded market. The strategy to
invest in its own network is borne out by the improvement in both gross profit and operating profit.
The results for the year are EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) of £3.9m (2022
£24.2m) and an operating profit of £0.4m (2022 profit of £20.6m). The numbers reported for 2022 included a receipt
of £24m from an entity that is not a related party. The improvement in underlying operating profit to £0.4m versus
an underlying operating loss of £3.4m in 2022, is a result of migrating existing circuits onto Zen's own network, tight
control of people costs and reduced marketing and administrative spend. Existing cash balances together with
EBITDA and good cash conversion enabled the business to invest £6.1m (2022: £6,0m) of capital into its core
business. The year-end cash balance is £3.5m (2022: £7.5m) and the Company remains debt free. The net asset -
position of the business has improved to £28.1m (2022 £27.7m). The investment property valuation is unchanged
at £5.9m and reflects management's best estimate of the element of the Rochdale head office rented to tenants;
the valuation is based on current market and capitalisation rates.
There has been growth in both trade debtors and trade creditors; debtor days have increased by 7 days to 26 days
(2022: 19 days) and creditor days have increased by 9 days to 39 days (2022: 30 days). Both debtor and creditor
days remain within agreed commercial terms, the increases are as a result of the timing of high value supplier/
customer invoices raised and received. There has been an increase in supplier rebates within accrued income: the
principal reason is a change in the rebate process operated by a major supplier. The increase in other creditors of
£1.6m is due to funding received in advance from a strategic supplier to support marketing spend planned for
FY24.
The Company continues to utilise a 5-year revolving credit facility. The total facility remains at £5m. The facility was
not drawn down at the year end.
Taking the above factors into account the directors are satisfied with the financial performance of the business
during the year.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Directors consider the following risks to be the most materiai or significant for the business. These issues do
not purport to be a complete list or an explanation of all the risks facing the Company. In particular, the Company's
performance may be affected by changes in the market and/or economic conditions and changes in legal,
regulatory or tax legislation. Additional risks and uncertainties, not presently known by the Company or that the
Company currently deems immaterial, may impact the business.
Market
Description
The main risk facing the business is competition within the market and ensuring that the Company's offerings keep
abreast of technological change. Competition within the UK market remains strong as established players look to
grow their customer and revenue base and new entrants look to gain a foothold in the market through price
competition. Legacy services will be withdrawn, Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) and Integrated
Services Digital Network (ISDN) services will be switched off by Openreach by December 2025 and accordingly
Wholesale Line Rental (WLR) based broadband services will no longer be supported.
Potential impact
Growing competition may dilute the addressable market and slow down the rate of growth. If the Company does not
at least keep pace with the evolving market in terms of product and service development, then its plans for revenue
growth may be negatively impacted. Existing customers using legacy services need to be migrated to data only
broadband services or risk being lost to competitors.
Mitigating actions
A clear and competitive pricing strategy is in place with ongoing monitoring of pricing position and market
proposition. Plans to move existing customers from legacy services to data-only or full fibre products are well
advanced. The impact of this strategy is reflective in these results, as the Company continues its transition from
legacy copper-based products to Ultrafast full fibre and data-only services.
Security
Description
The Company's business systems and networks provide customers with access to the internet, presenting a risk of
cyberattack.
Potential impact
A breach of security could have a significant impact on the Company's reputation, and in some cases also impact
its commercial position. Potential fines could also be enforced if the Company was found to be in breach of its
obligations relating to various regulations. Following the adoption of Hybrid working, the potential for a breach of
security controls is heightened as our people continue to split their tine between the home and office. At home,
data loss is more difficult to monitor and security awareness is less acute compared to working in an office
environment. .
Mitigating actions
This risk is mitigated through procedures designed to identify and neutralise such threats; this includes the constant
monitoring of unusual traffic patterns by sophisticated applications. Zen continues to adapt its governance structure
to ensure best practice is followed in the identification and management of information and cyber security risks.
This includes increased frequency and broadened scope of both routine and bespoke penetration testing:
continuous compliance checks; security simulations; integrated security behaviours training, which is mandatory for
all employees; and dedicated security roles to track how cyber threats are evolving and are best detected and
mitigated. 3 : .

Mitigating actions (continued)
Board visibility is provided via the internal risk governance process. The Company is represented in various
industry forums to ensure it is fully aware of new areas of risk, methods employed by malicious actors and best
practice in the identification and mitigation of risk. Zen’s core infrastructure and operating capability is certified
under ISO 27001 for security.
Service availability
Description
Reliable, high-quality voice, data and cloud services are critical to any customer and are the core components of
Zen's products and strategy. Therefore, maintaining very high levels of service availability is central to its credibility,
competitive positioning, and its financial performance.
Potential impact
If Zen’s products and services perform below the market's expectations, then this could have a direct impact on
product and revenue growth through reputational impact and could also result in increased operating costs.
Mitigating actions .
Zen operates a comprehensive operational governance framework to manage the availability and performance of
its services. This includes the design and architecture of the network for resilience, product platforms, capacity ~
planning, change management and security. Business continuity planning is a component of the governance
framework. This governance is subject to external audit via ISO 27001.
The core network is designed to be resilient. Should part of the network be interrupted, traffic is routed through
alternative paths. Underlying network equipment and circuits are subject to a continual upgrade programme,
whereby key equipment is replaced and upgraded before its end of life and core capacity is increased in line with
the forecast growth in traffic and the demands of new ultrafast technologies.
There is an Incident Management process that is rehearsed on a regular basis. This capability is available
24x7x365 and ensures the business can respond immediately to events that may impact the performance of the
services provided to customers.
The Company has an established communications process which is initiated during any major service incident. This
ensures that the Company maintains effective communication both internally and externally with customers and
suppliers.
Ability to continue to attract and retain key staff
Description
The Company depends on being able to recruit and retain people of the right calibre to maintain its reputation for
service. For instance, in order for the business to achieve its strategic priorities, it is dependent upon recruiting and
retaining highly skilled technical, development and operational people with experience of modern technologies and
design principles.
The roll out of FTTP networks and the rise of the AltNets have intensified the market demand for network and cloud
engineers and software developers; it is becoming increasingly important to differentiate the Company's business
and brand to continue to attract new talent and retain existing talent.
Potential impact
Loss of key individuals or an inability to recruit the required quantity or quality of people could have an impact on
the future growth of the business or the quality of services provided.

Environment and Climate Change
Description
The impact of Climate Change on the environment has both immediate and progressive effects on the risk profile of
the business.
Potential impact
The impact of climate change risks on Zen is assessed as low. Extreme weather risks are mitigated via existing
resilience plans. Zen’s energy costs are a small proportion of its costs and likely regulatory interventions are seen
as manageable. The progressive effects on certain industry sectors are not expected to have a material negative
effect given the diverse nature of Zen’s customer base. Zen considers that the climate crisis is causing significant
damage to the environment and will continue to respond appropriately in reducing its contribution to global climate
change.
Mitigating actions
Zen has a formal Environmental policy and is committed to achieving the highest standards of environmental
management throughout every area of its business. The Company recognises the impact of its activities and
operations on the environment and it continues to balance its business aims with the need to provide adequate
environmental protection. The directors aim not to damage the environment in the areas in which the Company
operates, to meet all regulatory and legislative requirements and to apply responsible standards of its own where
relevant laws and regulations do not exist. Zen is committed to reducing its contribution to global climate change.
The commitment includes measurement of its full carbon footprint including supply chain, maintenance of carbon
neutral status as well as continuing to offset emissions and work on carbon reduction schemes.
Zen has formally set a target to half scope 1 and 2 emissions by 2028, reduce scope 3 emissions (supply chain) by
50% by 2030 and to reduce total emissions by 90% by 2040. These science-based targets were approved by the
Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) in August 2022.

B Corporation
As a certified B Corp organisation, the Company has a duty to balance its decisions not just on profitability, but also
on people, customers, suppliers, the community and the environment. Since incorporation in 1995, Zen has
constantly striven towards a better way of doing business, one that puts people before profit and takes
sustainability seriously. The B Corp ethos, balancing purpose and profit is very close to Zen’s heart and
complements Zen's most fundamental long-term objectives of happy staff, happy customers and happy suppliers.
Certified B Corporations are businesses that meet the highest standards of verified social and environmental
performance, public transparency, and legal accountability to balance profit and purpose. B Corps are accelerating
a global culture shift to redefine success in business and build a more inclusive and sustainable economy. The
board of directors take their responsibilities under the B Corp regimen very seriously.
There are five B Corp Impact Assessment areas; governance, people, environment, customer and community. The
B Corp working group has a programme of work to measure progress against each category. This is monitored by

Energy Efficiency Actions
During the year to 30 September 2023, the Company implemented several energy initiatives which have reduced,
renewed, and rebalanced its operational energy consumption and carbon emissions. This resulted in a 10.8%
teduction in electricity used, and water saving initiatives achieved a similar reduction in water consumption. In terms
of renewables, the Company generated 187MWh of electricity through its on-site solar installation, accounting for
3.75% of total facility consumption. Additionally, the Company continues to purchase a 100% renewable electricity
tariff through Bryt Energy, utilising solar, wind or hydro power technology. Gas is purchased through British Gas,
with 10% from RGGO-backed energy from biomethane renewable energy and the remaining 90% covered by the
VER scheme, where carbon emissions are offset via sustainability projects around the world.
In addition to energy saving technologies implemented, ZEN Internet's solar PV began generating electricity at the
beginning of the 22-23 period, resulting in on site generation of 3.8% of the company's electricity.
New EC fans were fitted in office Air Handling Units, and chilled water cooling units replaced with hybrid free cooling
units in one of the Company's data centres. Finally, old data centre rotary UPS systems were de-commissioned and
teplaced with modern efficient static equivalents.
Scope 2 Energy relates to the purchase of electricity. Zen purchases electricity from 100% renewable sources,
resulting in zero CO2e Tonnes of emissions. Although year on year we have seen a reduction in the volume of
energy used, in 2023 we introduced electric vehicles and the energy consumed by these vehicles is not always
100% renewable, resulting in a small increase year on year of carbon emissions.
