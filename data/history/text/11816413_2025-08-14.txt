STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024

This strategic report provides an overview of the performance, position, and prospects of Expereo UK,

a subsidiary of Expereo International B.V. for the year ended 31 December 2024.

Principal activity

Expereo UK is the leading provider of managed network and cloud connectivity solutions for enterprise and

wholesale channel.

Business review

Our business model is to deliver reliable, secure, and flexible network and cloud connectivity solutions to our

customers, leveraging our global network of partners and suppliers, our proprietary software platform, and our

local expertise and support. Our solutions include:

. Managed internet access and SD-WAN: We provide high-speed, cost-efficient, and resilient internet
access and software-defined wide area network (SD-WAN) solutions that enable our customers to
connect their offices, data centers, and cloud applications across the globe,

. Managed cloud acceleration and optimization: We offer cloud acceleration and optimization solutions that
enhance the performance, security, and visibility of our customers’ cloud applications and services

° Managed security and compliance: We deliver security and compliance solutions that protect our
customers’ network and cloud infrastructure, data, and users from cyber threats and ensure compliance
with industry standards and regulations.

Our strategy is to grow our business by:

. Expanding our customer base and retention: We aim to attract and retain more customers from various
sectors and sizes, by offering competitive pricing, superior quality, and excellent customer service,

. Diversifying our product portfolio and innovation: We seek to diversify our product portfolio by introducing
new and innovative solutions that address the evolving needs and challenges of our customers,

. Enhancing our operational efficiency and agility: We strive to enhance our operational efficiency and agility
by optimizing our processes, systems, and resources, and by leveraging our global network of partners
and suppliers, our proprietary software platform, and our local expertise and support.

Our key achievements and challenges in 2024:

. We increased our revenue vs previous year by 27% mainly driven by our Enterprise sales,

. We improved our profitability vs previous years, mainly due to our operational efficiency and cost savings,
as well as our value-added services and cross-selling opportunities,

. We expanded our customer base vs previous years, including several large and prestigious accounts,
such as EIDP and Innospec.

However, we also faced some challenges in 2024, such as:

. The increased competition and price pressure from other network and cloud service providers, both local
and global, which required us to differentiate and innovate our offerings and value proposition,

e The emerging and evolving cyber threats and regulatory requirements, which required us to invest and
enhance our security and compliance capabilities and measures.

Outlook, principal risks and uncertainties

Our outlook for 2025 is positive and optimistic, as we expect to continue our growth and profitability, despite the

uncertainties and challenges in the market and environment. We anticipate to increase our revenue, and our

profit based on the following assumptions and projections:

e The demand for network and cloud connectivity solutions will remain strong and grow, as more
organizations adopt digital transformation and cloud migration strategies, and as more employees and
customers access and use online services and applications,

° The competition and price pressure from other network and cloud service providers will intensify, but we
will be able to differentiate and innovate our offerings and value proposition, and retain and attract more
customers.

. The cyber threats and regulatory requirements will increase, but we will be able to invest and enhance our
security and compliance capabilities and measures, and protect our network and cloud infrastructure,
data, and users.

We face various risks and opportunities that may affect our business performance, position, and prospects. We

have identified and assessed the following key risks and opportunities, and the actions we have taken or plan to

take to mitigate or exploit them.

Opportunity: New technologies and innovations: New technologies and innovations, may create new and

enhanced network and cloud connectivity solutions and services, and generate new and additional revenue

streams and customer segments for us.

Action: We have invested and allocated resources to research and development, and to partnerships and

collaborations with technology providers and innovators, to explore and experiment with new technologies and

innovations, and to launch and integrate them into our product portfolio and operations. We also monitor and
analyze the market trends and customer needs and feedback, to identify and evaluate new opportunities and

2.6 Revenue
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
company and the revenue.can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured at the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes.
The company generates revenue from contracts with customers that generally include two distinct
service components:
e Installation Services: An upfront installation fee is charged and revenue is recognised upon
completion of the installation, which is the point at which the performance obligation is satisfied.
« Provision of Equipment: The ongoing provision of equipment is recognised as revenue over the
contracted term, reflecting the continuous transfer of service to the customer over time.
Revenue from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are
provided, in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract, when all of the following
conditions are satisfied:
e The amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
e — Itis probable that the company will receive the consideration due under the contract;
« The stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured
reliably; and
« The costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.
2.7 Interest income
: Interest income is recognised in the Statement of income and retained earnings using the effective
interest method.
2.8 Finance costs
Finance costs are charged to the Statement of income and retained earnings over the term of the
debt using the effective interest method so that the amount charged is at a constant rate on the
carrying amount. Issue costs are initially recognised as a reduction in the proceeds of the associated
capital instrument.
2.9 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
ce The company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a
pension plan under which the company pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the company has no further payment obligations. :
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not paid
are shown in accruals as a liability in the Statement of financial position. The assets of the plan are
held separately from the company in independently administered funds.

2.10 Current and deferred taxation
The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in profit or loss
except that a charge attributable to an item of income and expense recognised as other
- comprehensive income or to an item recognised directly in equity is also recognised in other
comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date in the countries where the company operates
and generates income.
Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not
reversed by the reporting date, except that:
. The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits; and
e Any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
allowances have been met.
Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of
business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair values of
assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences between the fair
values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax. Deferred tax is determined
using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date.
2.11 Intangible assets
Goodwill
Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and
the acquirer's interest in the fair value of its identifiable assets and liabilities of the acquiree at the
date of acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost less accumulated
amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a straight-line basis to the
Statement of income and retained earnings over its useful economic life.
All intangible assets are considered to have a finite useful life. If a reliable estimate of the useful life
cannot be made, the useful life shall not exceed ten years.
2.12 Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of
operating in the manner intended by management.

3. Judgments in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
The preparation of the financial statements requires management to make judgements, estimates and
assumptions that affect the application of the accounting policies and the reported amounts of assets and
liabilities, revenue and expenses. Actual results may differ from these estimates. Estimates and
underlying assumptions are continually evaluated and are based on historical experience and other
factors, including expectations of future events that are reasonable under the circumstances. Revision to
the accounting estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimates are revised and in any future
periods affected.
Tangible Fixed Assets
The company depreciates its Tangible Fixed Assets on a straight-line basis over their estimated useful
lives, in accordance with the accounting standards policy. The useful lives and residual values are
reviewed at each reporting date and adjusted if appropriate. The company has applied judgement and
estimates to determine the useful lives and residual values based on the expected usage, physical wear
and tear, technological obsolescence, and market conditions. The estimated useful lives range are: 2.5
years straight line for fixtures and fittings; 3 years straight line for computer equipment, 5 years straight
line for computer hardware.
Revenue
The company recognises revenue from contracts with customers in accordance with the accounting
standards policy. The company applies judgement to identify the performance obligations in each contract,
allocate the transaction price to each performance obligation, and determine the timing and amount of
revenue recognition. The company has considered the nature and terms of the contracts, the delivery and
acceptance criteria, the payment terms, and the existence of any variable consideration. .
Accruals

there is a firm commitment to occur, regardless of the date of payment or collection, i.e. it is a right earned
but not collected.
