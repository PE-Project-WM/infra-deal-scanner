contribution for our customers, staff, suppliers and communities.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The business is exposed to a range of operational, financial and external risks. Effective management of these risks 
remains essential to safeguarding long-term stability, service quality and sustainable growth.  The board continues to 
monitor and review the key risks:
1. Operational Delivery Risk
Risk: Service delivery issues, project delays, skills shortages or supply chain disruption may impact client satisfaction.
Uncertainty: Complex project demands and variation in regional resource availability.
Mitigation: Strong project management, supplier partnerships, and proactive workforce planning.
2. Health, Safety & Compliance Risk
Risk: Operating in high-risk environments increases exposure to safety incidents or regulatory breaches.
Uncertainty: Evolving health and safety legislation.
Mitigation: Safety-first culture, training, audits and compliance monitoring.
3. Talent Attraction & Retention
Risk: Competition for skilled engineers and technical specialists may limit delivery capacity.
Uncertainty: Labour market pressures and wage inflation.
Mitigation: Apprenticeships, training programmes, retention initiatives and career pathways.
4. Economic & Market Conditions
Risk: Inflation, interest rates and fluctuating customer budgets may reduce demand or impact profitability.
Uncertainty: Volatility in the construction, property and all sectors we provide services for.
=== page 6 ===
Mitigation: Diversification, strong client relationships, and prudent financial oversight.
5. Supply Chain & Materials Cost Risk
Risk: Material shortages, price increases or import delays may disrupt projects and erode margins.
Uncertainty: Global supply instability and changing trade conditions.
Mitigation: Supplier diversification, forward purchasing and cost-management strategies.
6. Technological Change & Cybersecurity
Risk: Failure to adopt modern systems or safeguard digital infrastructure could reduce competitiveness and expose the 
business to cyber threats.
Uncertainty: Rapid tech development and evolving cyber-attack techniques.
Mitigation: Technology investment, cybersecurity protocols, regular system reviews and user training.
7. Regulatory & Environmental Compliance
Risk: Non-compliance with electrical, fire, building, and environmental regulations may result in penalties or contract 
loss.
Uncertainty: Tightening sustainability and decarbonisation regulations.
Mitigation: Monitoring regulatory changes and investing in greener technologies.
8. Reputational Risk
Risk: Service failures, safety incidents or poor customer engagement could damage our trusted reputation.
Uncertainty: Growing customer expectations and public scrutiny.
Mitigation: Robust quality assurance, transparent communication and continuous improvement.
10. Cash Management & Liquidity Risk
Risk: Pressures on cash flow due to rising costs, delayed client payments, or increased working-capital demands could 
constrain operations and investment.
Uncertainty: Economic instability, longer customer payment cycles, and fluctuations in project pipeline timing.
Mitigation: Strong credit control processes, regular financial reporting, cost discipline, and maintaining adequate 
liquidity reserves.
During these challenging trading conditions cashflow remains a key focus for the Group. Other than a small number of 
operating leases the Group does not utilise external sources of finance and does not anticipate a need to do so in the 
immediate future. This conservative financial approach ensures resilience and preserves independence.

HF Group benefits from a blend of long-standing Board members and newer appointees, who together guide the

embedding these principles into its culture, HF Group safeguards its reputation, protects stakeholders, and promotes

STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT
Our primary focus during the year continued to be the safety and wellbeing of our employees and stakeholders.
The development of our HF Connect platform in 2024 has provided a quick and easy way for employees to report safety 
observations. Increased reporting reflects greater engagement in accident prevention; during the year, 457 safety 
observations were submitted (2024: 413.)
All managers are required to carry out regular Safety, Health, Environmental and Quality (SHEQ) inspections during site 
visits to reinforce the importance of health and safety. The target is one SHEQ inspection per 500 hours worked. In 
2025, inspections exceeded this target, with one inspection carried out for every 278 hours worked (2024: 359) 
demonstrating strong leadership engagement in safety management.
Employees
Our employees are central to HF's success, and we recognise the importance of fostering an engaged, healthy, and 
motivated workforce. Talented people drive our growth, and we continue to invest significantly in their training and 
development. We currently have 66 apprentices (2024: 62 apprentices), representing 19% of our workforce.  During the 
year we introduced a program for training in partnership with the Association for Project Management along with 
line-manager training.  These initiatives underscore our commitment to long-term capability building within HF and the 
wider sector.
We continue to promote and support employee wellbeing by offering annual health checks and access to additional 
support where needed.  We successfully completed a Group-wide mental health training initiative and reinforced the 
importance of both physical and mental wellbeing through regular toolbox talks.  We now have 26 trained Mental Health 
First Aiders, representing 8% of our workforce.
HF's Social Committee plays an important role in strengthening and embedding our staff culture. Several events were 
organised throughout the year, many of which generated contributions to local charities and supported our broader 
community engagement.
To support a healthy organisational culture, we maintain an anonymous reporting process that is accessible to all 
employees throughout the year. In addition, we conduct an annual anonymous engagement survey.  Feedback from this 
year's survey led to the introduction of an employee forum and a long-service recognition scheme, both aimed at 
strengthening engagement and supporting continuous improvement across the organisation.
Clients
We adopt a collaborative approach with our clients to maximise value, efficiency and productivity across all projects. 
Consistent with our values, we are committed to delivering our services safely, to the highest quality standards, and 
within agreed timescales and budgets. Our focus is to ensure complete customer satisfaction and to build long-term, 
trusted relationships with those we serve.
Suppliers and subcontractors
HF is committed to building strong, collaborative relationships with our suppliers and subcontractors. We recognise the 
critical role our supply chain plays in delivering high-quality services and we strive to operate with fairness, 
transparency and reliability.
We endeavour to pay all suppliers and subcontractors on time and are committed to resolving any disputes promptly. 
Our standard payment terms range from 30 to 60 days end-of-month. During the financial year, our average time to pay 
invoices was 47 days, with 82% of all invoices paid within 60 days.
To ensure our subcontractors are fully aligned with and committed to our safety values we review our subcontractors 
SHEQ inspections per hours worked and safety observations reported.
Local community 
HF aims to give back to the community and support local schools, clubs and initiatives.  During the year we donated 
10% of our profits to charities, schools and clubs and are proud to participate in local initiatives.  We encourage and 
promote our employees participation in charitable work as we recognise the benefit for our local communities and the 
individuals participating.

CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY
Diversity
HF recognises the benefits of gender diversity in its workforce to maximise innovation, creativity and competitiveness. 
Females currently make up 15% of our workforce but we are proud to have female representation across a wide variety 
of roles including apprentices, electricians, business support, management and at board level.
We acknowledge that achieving a more balanced gender profile will take time, reflecting the wider industry challenge. In 
the meantime, we remain committed to fostering an inclusive and fair working environment and continue to:

- Remunerate employees doing comparable roles with comparable skills equally.
- Not discriminate based on their sex, race, pregnancy and maternity, marital or civil partnership status, gender 
reassignment, disability, religion or beliefs, age or sexual orientations.
- Acknowledge the importance of diversity within our workforce.

Tangible fixed assets

Tangible fixed assets held for the company's own use are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and 
accumulated impairment loss.
At each balance sheet date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible fixed assets to determine 
whether there is any indication that any items have suffered an impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the 
recoverable amount of an asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the impairment loss. Where it is 
not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of the asset, the company estimates the recoverable amount of 
the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Expenditure of £1,000 or more on individual tangible fixed assets is capitalised at cost. Expenditure on assets 
below this threshold is charged directly to the profit and loss account in the period it is incurred.
Stock and work in progress
Stocks are held at the lower of cost and net realisable value, being the estimated selling price less costs to sell. 
Cost is based on the cost of purchase on a first in, first out basis.
At each balance sheet date, stocks are assessed for impairment. If stocks are impaired the carrying value is 
reduced to its selling price less costs to sell. The impairment loss is recognised immediately in the profit and loss 
account.
Work in progress is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value. Costs include all direct expenditure.
Amounts receivable on contracts
Amounts recoverable on contracts, which are included in debtors, are stated at the net sales value of work done 
after provisions for contingencies and anticipated future losses on contracts less amounts received as progress 
payments on account. Excess progress payments on account are offset against trade debtors or amounts 
recoverable on contracts.
Financial instruments
The company only enters into basic financial instrument transactions that result in the recognition of financial 
assets and liabilities like trade debtors and creditors and loans to related parties.
Financial assets that are measured at cost are assessed at the end of each reporting period for evidence of 
impairment. If any evidence of impairment is found, an impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of 
Comprehensive Income.
Taxation
Taxation for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive 
Income, except to the extent that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in 
equity.
Current or deferred taxation assets and liabilities are not discounted.
Current tax is recognised at the amount of tax payable using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or 
substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.

Deferred taxation
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance 
sheet date.
Timing differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from 
those in which they are recognised in financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that 
have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the 
timing difference.
Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they 
will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
Foreign currencies
Foreign currency transactions are translated into the sterling using the exchange rate prevailing at the dates of the 
transactions. Foreign exchange rate gains and losses resulting from the settlement of those transactions and from 
the translation at year-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities are recognised in the statement of 
comprehensive income.
