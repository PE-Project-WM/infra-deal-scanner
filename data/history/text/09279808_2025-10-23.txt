Strategic Report
The directors of Evero Energy Group Limited (the “Company”) and its subsidiaries (the “Group”) present their
Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Review of the business
The Group’s principal activity was the management and operation of bioenergy power generation assets with a
focus on value creation via long-term hold and active management of the assets.
Results and performance
During the year, the Group completed a reorganisation to focus on assets generating renewable electricity using
domestically sourced waste wood as fuel and to exit from those assets using refuse-derived fuel (RDF). As part of
this strategy, the Group divested from Hull, Levenseat, as well as its RDF operations and maintenance contracts.
This strategic repositioning aligns with the Group’s strategy to develop Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and
Storage (BECCS) on waste wood assets, as reinforced by the UK Government’s selection of the Group as a Priority
Project for the HyNet Carbon Capture and Storage Cluster.
It is important to note that the results contained in these financial statements include the performance from all
assets held during 2024, including those sold or written down, and a reconciliation to the Waste Wood asset
focussed business is shown below.
Group revenue for the year ended 31 December 2024 was £82,878,000 (2023: £50,832,000). The increase was
driven by improved plant performance and reliability, together with the prior year acquisitions of Widnes and
Lisahally (see Note 16). Other income for the year was £7,632,000 (2023: £11,794,000).
Group administrative expenses were £59.914,000 (2023: £57,020,000), comprising primarily staff costs,
professional fees, project overheads, and depreciation.
The impairment charge of £92,046,000 (2023: £83,168,000) on property, plant and equipment relates to the
disposal of Hull. The impairment charge of £67,164,000 (2023: £25,251,000) on loans receivable relates to
Levenseat, an equity-accounted associate that entered administration after the balance sheet date (see Notes 18,
19, and 36).
The Group’s adjusted EBITDA for the year was a profit of £27,418,000 (2023: £24,763,000). This excludes
impairment charges, one-off costs associated with exiting from RDF O&M contracts, the EBITDA loss for Hull,
and other non-recurring items. A reconciliation from the adjusted EBITDA to the Group loss is set out below:

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Group’s main financial instruments are equity and loan notes provided to fund project companies and loan
notes issued to investors to raise capital to provide project companies’ funding.
The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Group are credit risk, liquidity risk, operational performance risk
and market risk.
Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk arising from the failure of a counterparty to pay its debts. Each new counterparty is analysed
individually for creditworthiness, before negotiating bespoke contracts and payments terms in conjunction with
each potential investment. The review includes external ratings, where available, and in some cases bank
references. Contracts for gate fees and power purchase agreements are predominantly with investment grade and
tier one counterparties.
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the Group will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they fall due. The
Company’s approach to managing liquidity is to ensure, as far as possible, that it will always have sufficient
liquidity to meet its liabilities when due. Liquidity risk is also that the Group does not meet requirements under
financing agreements and a long-term loan becomes repayable on demand. The Group continually monitors
historic and forecast covenant compliance and has not identified any non-compliance in the period.
Operational performance risk
Once built the bioenergy assets must be run efficiently in order to deliver the levels of profitability necessary to
support the loan and equity investment into the Group. The projects operate with an operations and maintenance
contract, two of which are performed by the Group and the others outsourced to third parties. The performance
and management of these contracts is key to the financial standing of the Group. Contracts are in place with the
Group and third parties to incentivise them to meet targets (as well as there being contractual penalties for not
meeting required standards). When assets move from the construction to the operational phase, the Group retains
a seat on the Board so that the performance of the assets can be actively managed.

Market risk
Market risk is the risk that changes in market prices, such as foreign exchange rates and interest rates, will affect
the company’s income. The objective of market risk management is to manage and control market risk exposures
within acceptable parameters, while optimising the return on risk. The Group previously entered into a cross-
currency swap to minimise exposure to exchange rate risk on the debt facility undertaken in US dollars which
was terminated on 4 March 2022 upon settlement of the debt facility. In addition, the Group operates in the
waste and biomass markets and so the pricing and quality of these feedstocks is a material factor in its
profitability. This has been mitigated to the extent practical through supply contracts and the recruitment of
industry specialists to manage this exposure.
Energy prices are subject to market forces. The project companies mitigate this risk as far as possible by being
accredited to the Government’s Renewable Obligation Certificate or Contract for Difference schemes. These
measures guarantee an energy price for the power that is generated. Power price fluctuations have also been
mitigated through power-price hedging of a fixed volume of output.
Regulatory risk
The energy industry is constantly changing; government policy, the push for a low carbon economy, technology
advances and consumer needs all affect our industry. There is a risk of adverse impacts from changes in law and
policy, including environmental, tax and similar legal and policy regimes, including changes in regulatory attitude
and behaviours as a result of shifts in public opinion.

: Our business model is in line with society’s needs for low carbon energy and sustainable waste disposal which
generally provides opportunities for the business, with potentially adverse changes actively managed and mitigated
to the extent this is possible.

Future developments
As a result of the determination to improve productivity, we have recently reorganised the Group to increase focus
on the Waste Wood and to divest away from RDF assets. Our medium and long term forecasts predict significant
improvement in utilisation and we will continue to invest to increase profitability.
Carbon capture is major feature of the Group’s strategy for growth.

_ Business Overview:
Evero Energy Group Limited, formerly known as Bioenergy Infrastructure Limited, is the holding company for
Evero Energy Holdings Limited, which specialises in converting waste into energy, playing a pivotal role in the
UK's transition to a circular economy and sustainable energy future. We own several energy recovery facilities
across the UK, using locally sourced waste to generate low-carbon electricity. Our operations currently divert
approximately 481,000 tonnes of waste wood from landfills annually, with a generating capacity of 65 MW,
powering c.148,000 homes.
Key Stakeholders:
1. Employees:

e Evero’s employees are fundamental to our success. We genuinely care, listen and actively encourage
inclusion and diversity. We value employees’ contributions and are committed to fostering a zero-harm,
inclusive and supportive work environment. .

e We continue to enhance employee engagement through our Quarterly Roadshows, now expanded to
include cross-divisional business updates. This broader scope ensures relevance across the organisation
and drives consistent engagement at both site and corporate levels. The inclusion of an open Q&A
segment has proven highly effective in capturing employee feedback at scale; particularly important given
the continued growth and complexity of the business. Agenda topics are employee-selected, contributing
to strong participation and reinforcing our commitment to two-way communication. Our directors
leverage engagement feedback cycles as a strategic tool to inform and support business decisions. In line
with our strategic priorities, we are also establishing a Diversity & Inclusion Network. This cross-
functional group will play a key role in shaping initiatives that support a more inclusive and high-
performing workplace, aligned with our operational excellence objectives. Our approach to social impact
is evolving through the work of a dedicated Charity Engagement Group, currently identifying
opportunities to broaden our community partnerships. This will allow us to deepen our corporate social
responsibility efforts and ensure alignment with our values and stakeholder expectations.

2. Customers:
e Evero is committed to providing reliable and sustainable energy solutions. Our operations convert waste
into renewable energy, ensuring the provision of low-carbon electricity to our customers. We strive to
- maintain high service standards and transparent communication channels to address customer needs and
expectations.
3. Suppliers and Partners:

. e Collaboration with our suppliers and partners is integral to our business model. In 2024, we continued
to advance our partnership with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries to implement carbon capture technology at
our Ince Bio Power facility. This project, known as InBECCS, aims to extend the life of the asset and
decarbonise the facility.

e We have also begun to advance development activity to implement carbon capture technology at our
Mersey Bioenergy facility. This follow-on project, known as MBECCS, similarly aims to extend the

Deferred tax
Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable on differences between the carrying amounts of assets
and liabilities in the Financial statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the computation of taxable profit,
and is accounted for using the balance sheet liability method. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for
all taxable temporary differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that taxable
profits will be available against which deductible temporary differences can be utilised.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each balance-sheet date and reduced to the extent that it
is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered.
Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period when the liability is settled or the
asset is realised based on tax laws and rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted at the balance -date.
The measurement of deferred tax liabilities and assets reflects the tax consequences that would follow from the
manner in which the Group expects, at the end of the reporting period, to recover or settle the carrying amount of
its assets and liabilities.
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset when there is a legally enforceable right to set off current tax assets
against current tax liabilities and when they relate to income taxes levied by the same taxation authority and the
Group intends to settle its current tax assets and liabilities on a net basis.

Goodwill
