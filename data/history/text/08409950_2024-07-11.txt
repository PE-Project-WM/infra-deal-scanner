The directors present their report with the audited Financial Statements of Ascensos Limited (the “Group” and/or
“Ascensos’) for the year ended 31 December 2023.
Introduction
Ascensos is one of Europe's leading independent providers of Customer Experience Management (CXM).
Our specialty is providing excellence in customer service and our leading-edge digital technology ensures the
utmost agility and security. Our owner-led team move fast to provide intelligent and future-proof solutions, bespoke
to every client. :
Business review

‘Our report inthe 2022 financial statements noted that the foundations had been laid to enable strong growth in 2023
and in subsequent years, with a sharp improvement in profitability expected during 2023/24.
As a result, year on year revenues grew by 2% to £64.1m, and we also saw a marked improvement in gross
margin versus 2022. :
We are pleased to report that performance in 2023 was very much in line with that plan, and we fully expect
significant improvements during 2024 driven by better efficiencies. Towards the end of 2023, the Group was
delivering the scale of results previously seen during 2021, when COVID driven work was at its peak in the BPO /
CXM industry. The quick turnaround to get back to these levels is down to the significant account wins achieved
during the year and we were pleased to welcome several new contracts with leading retail and consumer brands,
demonstrating the increasing range and variety of clients who place their trust in Ascensos. We will see the full
benefit of these new contracts in our 2024 results. :
Earlier in the year, we increased our capacity in Cape Town, South Africa through the opening of our second site
in the city. With the BPO sector in South Africa continuing to grow, this new site in Cape Town means Ascensos
is perfectly positioned to support job creation in the region. We are now focusing on growth and strategic initiatives
such as impact sourcing and our new Ascensos BPO Academy.
We also launched our new entity in Trinidad, with operations starting in Port of Spain in the middle of the year with
a rapid rate of growth now being delivered. Ascensos Trinidad adds to our expanding global footprint and will enable
us to serve our clients’ North American retail and ecommerce customers and enhance our 24/7 coverage for
customers in the UK and Europe.
Evaluating the impact of the recent advances in Al continues to be a key area of focus for Ascensos, and we
continue to review and research the impact that emerging technologies and Al may have on our industry.
The group was pleased to finalise a 5-year term loan facility with our bankers, HSBC, in June 2023 in support of our
continued expansion into new territories.
The increase in revenues and margin outweighed the additional cost of investment in our expansion during the
year and resulted in a marginal pretax loss of £8k (2022: £0.7m loss). We fully anticipate that the group will move
significantly forward into a pre-tax profit position in 2024.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Group's operations expose it to a variety of financial risks. The Group has exposure to the main risk areas of
liquidity risk and client credit exposure. To a lesser extent the Group is also exposed to interest rate risk and
foreign exchange. The Group has a tight monitoring system, which allows the directors to act to limit any effects
of adverse movements in any of the above risk factors.
i) Liquidity Risk
The board regularly reviews the Group's liquidity projections to ensure the Group has enough funds to run its
operation together with compliance with loan covenants.
With increasing sustainable profitability being delivered by the group following a period of investment in our
offshore operations and a refinancing exercise having taken place during 2023, this risk has reduced during the
year. .
ii) Client Credit Exposure :
The Group may offer credit terms to its clients which allow payment of the debt after the delivery of the
services. The Group is at risk to the extent that a client may be unable to pay the debt on the specified due
date. This risk is mitigated by strong on-going client relationships, an assessment of an individual client's
credit risk prior to commencing business with them and finally ongoing monitoring of credit rating of existing
clients via an established third-party service.
This risk in unchanged since the previous year and is deemed to be a low risk, with most clients having a
strong credit rating as assessed by an independent 3% party.
, iii) Interest Rate Cashflow Risk
. The Group has both interest-bearing assets and liabilities. The interest-bearing assets only include cash
balances. The Group borrows from its bankers using either overdrafts or term loans whose tenure depends
on the nature of the asset and management's view of the direction of interest rates.
This risk has been reduced since last year with interest rates likely to have peaked and may reduce towards
the end of 2024, whilst at the same time, borrowing is forecast to reduce.
iv) Foreign Exchange Transactional Currency Exposure
The Group is exposed to currency exchange risk due to a proportion of its payables, receivables and operating
expenses being denominated in non-sterling currencies.
The Group operates hedging instruments to reduce the impact of fluctuations in foreign currency exchange
rates by contracting on a forward basis to sell EUR and GBP to purchase local currency in Romania and South
Africa. Over the year these hedging instruments have largely been successful, mitigating the impact of
fluctuations in foreign currency. Details are referenced within note 21 of the financial statements.
The low level of risk is unchanged in the year, both through the instruments noted and given the currencies
used by the group are relatively stable.
v) Loss of a key client
The Group works to ensure that there is no reliance on one or two key clients alone, and this risk is further
: mitigated by strong ongoing client relationships. As the Group evolves, and the client base broadens out, this
risk continues to reduce with client revenues and profit attributable to the top 3 clients reducing as a
percentage of the overall group total when compared to the previous year.
vi) Operational Risks .
The Group operates a quarterly risk and information management review (RIM), an output of which is a
regularly maintained operational risk register. In addition, management review day-to-day operational
performance through dashboards specific to each client on a daily and weekly basis taking corrective action
as appropriate.
vil) Advances in Al technology
__ We continue to evaluate the impact of the recent advances in Al and emerging technologies may have on our
industry. This is an emerging area of long-term uncertainty both in terms of opportunity and risk.

Energy efficiency actions
We are pleased to note the significant reduction (20%) in energy use by the company when compared to the
previous year. We have achieved this by reviewing the optimal approach to heating our sites in Scotland. This
review resulted in a switch to a greater use of electricity which was more than offset by a substantial reduction the
use of gas.
Total gross emissions did rise because of the increase in business travel versus the previous year, and this being
a function of the global expansion of the group during the year as we further expanded in South Africa together
with the establishment of an operation in Trinidad. The previous year would have still contained an element of
COVID related curbs on business travel, and 2023 provides a realistic base level from which we will seek to further
improve performance in 2024 and beyond.
Energy efficiency considerations for 2024 and beyond
The actions highlighted in previous reports remain relevant and in place:-
e Work with a specialist IT lifecycle management partner who refurnish and reuse devices rather than
dispose of IT assets at the end of their useful life with Ascensos.
e Within the office environment, we operate with LED lighting as a standard, resulting in less power usage
than that of normal lighting.
e Our buildings operate with sensors meaning that we are not unnecessarily using electricity.
e We continually monitor the most efficient use of office space versus hybrid working resulting in reduced
operational space in use at various points with an ability to restore office operations at short notice.
e Endeavour to use where possible, local suppliers as key providers of services to new sites in South Africa
and Trinidad and Tobago to reduce the need for travel by support teams.

2.23 Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the Group a legal or constructive
obligation that probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, and a reliable estimate
can be made of the amount of the obligation.
Provisions are charged as an expense to profit or loss in the year that the Group becomes aware of ,
the obligation and are measured at the best estimate at the Statement of Financial Position date of the
expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account relevant risks and uncertainties.
When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the Statement of
Financial Position.

2.24 Onerous Leases
Where the unavoidable costs of a lease exceed the economic benefit expected to be received from it,
a provision for onerous leases is recognised for the present value of the obligations under the lease.
The onerous leases provision relates to two leased office spaces that have been vacated and
represents the best estimate of the expenditure to settle the obligation.
Onerous lease provisions are expected to be utilised throughout 2024 and early 2025.

2.25 Taxation
Current tax is recognised for the amount of income tax payable in respect of the taxable profit for the
current or past reporting periods using the tax rates and laws that that have been enacted or
substantively enacted by the reporting date.
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences at the reporting date, except as otherwise
indicated. Deferred tax assets are only recognised to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits. If and when all
conditions for retaining tax allowances for the cost of a fixed asset have been met, the deferred tax is
reversed.
Deferred tax is calculated using the tax rates and laws that that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the reporting date that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.
Deferred tax liabilities are presented within provisions for liabilities and deferred tax assets within
debtors.
