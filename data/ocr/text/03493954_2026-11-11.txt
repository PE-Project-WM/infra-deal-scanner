Directors’ report
The directors present their annual report on the affairs of the Company, together with the financial statements and
auditor’s report, for the year ended 31 March 2026.
Principal activities
The principal activity of the company NEC Telecom MODUS, Ltd (NECTML), during the year was to undertake
Research and Development (R&D) relating to key elements of the 5th generation mobile phone system (5G). This is
based on the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) and O-RAN (Open Radio Access Network Alliance).
NECTML also continued its role in standardisation for 5G mobile cellular products and in O-RAN mobile
communication systems, delivering software/firmware, specification documents and research reports to its customer.
The customer was NEC Corporation Group (NECC).
Business review
NECTML is a UK based Telecoms R&D company that provides development deliverables to its key stakeholder —
NECC (Mobile Access Department, Network Software Development Department). NECTML is one of the
Technology Centre of Excellence for R&D, supporting the requirements of NECC for both Japanese and overseas
markets.
Tumover for the year ended 31 March 2026 was £6.654m, as compared to £8.414m in 2025.

: The R&D work continued to be carried out by the two divisions - Radio Systems Division (RSD) and Mobile Network
Division (MND). They continue to propose a multi-year R&D plan to NECC in line with the direction of technological
evolution. RSD and MND provided consultation services for and R&D results to the customer - NECC. RSD and
MND contributed to the expansion of both NECTML and NECC’s businesses, by providing deliverables. RSD and
MND contributed to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) of the customer. These achievements were attained
by the continuous yearly review of the R&D plan with the customer, which was subsequently implemented by
NECTML. Now, NECC and NECTML have increased their win-win relationship creating benefits for each other.
RSD contributed to the advanced technology development which drove the evolution of cellular network systems with
much higher capacity, higher user data rate, lower latency and lower energy consumption than the current mobile
system. This generation of cellular network is publicly known as the Sth Generation (5G hereafter). RSD also
continued its role in standardisation for 5G mobile cellular products in 3GPP and O-RAN.

MND undertook technology studies and system analysis activities, which contributed to the realisation of the 5G

Radio Access Network system. MND also continued its role in monitoring O-RAN (Open Radio Access Network

Alliance) specification groups. MND also worked on the RIC (RAN Intelligent Controller) component of the O-RAN

system. The O-RAN RIC performs resource optimisation functions for the RAN (Radio Access Network). MND

worked on design and evaluation of radio network optimisation algorithms to form part of NECC’s RIC.

NEC Telecom MODUS Limited achieved its objective to deliver the research and development output to its customer

(NEC Corporation in Japan).

Future projects and our R&D focus for the future:

One of the new trends in current 5G systems is O-RAN (Open Radio Access Network Alliance). It is expected that

radio base stations and systems equipped with the O-RAN interface and O-RAN requirements will dominate the

mobile market. RSD and MND have been tracing this trend. RSD will promote the core technology development of

Layerl and Layer2 conforming to the O-RAN interface. Also, MND will promote Layer2 and Layer3 core technology

development to improve O-RAN system performance. RSD and MND will promote performance improvement and

quality assurance of deliverables through simulations.

Going concern

The company has considerable financial resources, which is further enhanced by the development contracts specified

in the Mid Term Research and Development Plan Agreement with its customer, to include a 3-year development plan

1.3 Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis which the directors consider to be appropriate
for the following reasons.
As described in the Directors’ Report, the company provides R&D activities in respect of 5G technologies to its parent
company. These developments are integral to NEC Corporation’s continuing investment in mobile communication
systems. The company’s revenue comprises a recharge, with an appropriate mark-up, to NEC Corporation of costs
incurred. As the company’s cost base is largely fixed in the short term and the company’s income is directly related
to its cost base under longstanding recharge arrangements agreed with another group company, the directors can
predict, with some certainty, the level of its income.
The directors have made enquiries with the management of NEC Corporation and are satisfied that they will continue
to obtain services from the Company. This is supported by a documented R&D Plan Agreement through to March
2029. The directors have no reason to believe this agreement will be terminated during the next 12 months.
The day-to-day working capital requirements of the Company are met by access to a cash pooling facility managed
by NEC Capital Limited whereby the cash balance of the Company is swept into an intercompany bank account at the
end of each day. At 30 September 2026 the company had net assets of approximately £9.22m, net current assets of
approximately £6.81m including £5.55m of cash within the NEC Capital Limited cash pool.
Consequently, the directors are confident that the company will have sufficient funds to continue to meet its
liabilities as they fall due for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the financial statements and therefore
have prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis.

1.10 Employee benefits
Defined contribution plans
A defined contribution plan is a post-employment benefit plan under which the company pays fixed contributions into
a separate entity and will have no legal or constructive obligation to pay further amounts. Obligations for contributions
to defined contribution pension plans are recognised as an expense in the profit and loss account in the periods during
which services are rendered by employees.
The Company also operates a stakeholder pension scheme with Aegon. The amount charged to the profit and loss
account represents the contribution payable to the scheme in respect of the accounting period.
Defined benefit plans
NEC Telecom MODUS Limited, in conjunction with other fellow subsidiary undertakings of NEC Corporation,
operates a defined benefit staff pension scheme called the NEC Staff Pension Scheme (UK), which requires
contributions to be made to a separately administered fund.
Pension scheme assets are measured using market values and liabilities are measured on an actuarial basis in
accordance with the projected unit method and discounted at a rate equivalent to the current rate of return on a high
quality corporate bond of equivalent currency and term to the scheme liabilities.
Any increase in the present value of the liabilities expected to arise from employee service in the period is charged
against operating profit and included as part of staff costs. The interest cost and the expected return on assets are
shown as a net amount of other finance costs or credits adjacent to interest. Actuarial gains and losses are recognised
immediately in the Statement of Changes in Equity.
When the benefits of a plan are changed, or when a plan is curtailed, the portion of the changed benefit related to past
service by employees, or the gain or loss on curtailment, is recognised immediately in profit or loss when the plan
amendment or curtailment occurs.
The calculation of the defined benefit obligations is performed by a qualified actuary using the projected unit credit
method. When the calculation results in a benefit to the Company, the recognised asset is limited to the present value

115 Leases
At the inception of a contract, the Company assesses whether a contract is, or contains, a lease. A contract is, or
contains, a lease if the contract conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in
exchange for consideration.
As a lessee
At commencement of a contract that contains a lease component, along with one or more other lease or non-lease
components, the Company accounts for each lease component separately from the non-lease components.
The Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability at the lease commencement date. The right-of-use
asset is initially measured at cost, which comprises the initial amount of the lease liability adjusted for any lease
payments made at or before the commencement date, plus any initial direct costs incurred.
The right-of-use asset is subsequently depreciated using the straight-line method from the commencement date to the
end of the lease term, unless the lease transfers ownership of the underlying asset to the Company by the end of the
lease term or the cost of the right-of-use asset reflects that the Company will exercise a purchase option. In that case
the right-of-use asset will be depreciated over the useful life of the underlying asset, which is determined on the same
basis as those of property and equipment.
The lease liability is initially measured at the present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the
commencement date, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, if that rate cannot be readily determined,
the Company's incremental borrowing rate.

The pension scheme operated by the group is the NEC Staff Pension Scheme which is located in the UK . The Group
Scheme is subject to a statutory funding objective, as set out in UK pension legislation. The Group Scheme trustees
need to obtain regular actuarial valuation to assess the scheme against this funding objective. The trustees and
sponsoring companies need to agree funding plans to improve the position of a scheme when it is below the acceptable
funding level.
The UK Pensions Regulator has extensive powers to protect the benefits of members, promote good administration
and reduce the risk of situations arising which may require compensation to be paid from the Pension Protection Fund.
These include imposing a schedule of contributions or the calculation of the technical provisions, where a trustee and
the companies fail to agree appropriate calculations.
The Group Scheme is governed by the Trustees. The Trustees of the Scheme comprises three Company Nominated
Trustees and three Member Nominated Trustees. The Trustees are responsible for the management, administration,
funding and investment strategy of the Scheme.
The Group Scheme is exposed to the following risks:
investment retums that are below expectations, leaving the Scheme with insufficient assets in future to pay all its
pension obligations;
- members and dependents live longer than expected, increasing the value of the pensions the Scheme have
to pay;
- inflation rates are higher than expected, causing amounts payable under index-linked pensions to be higher
than expected; and
- increased contributions are required to meet funding targets if lower interest rates increase the current value
of liabilities. :
The Group Scheme has a risk management system in place to adequately monitor these risks, amongst others by
having an adequate Investment Strategy in place and engaging and working together with reputable actuarial advisor,
ensuring adequate asset-liability matching.
At each reporting date, the investment manager reports on the consequences of the strategic investment policy. The
current investment policy is to hold broadly 55% in defensive assets comprising Buy & Maintain Corporate Bonds &
Liability Driven Investments (LDI) and 45% in growth assets comprising Global Equities, Absolute return, Multi
Asset Credit and Illiquid Credit. The Scheme is subject to direct and indirect currency risk because investments are
held in non-sterling currencies. No specific measures are taken to mitigate the currency risks. The Scheme has
exposure to interest rate risk via Absolute Return, Multi Asset Credit and Illiquid Credit. The interest rate risk they
introduce is expected to be low and taken by the investment manager as part of its investment process to add value.
The Trustees of the Group Scheme aim to hedge broadly 90% of the interest rate and inflation risks associated with
the Long Term Funding Target basis, though with flexibility to implement tactical views around this. The LDI’s, held
in pooled funds, hold aim to hedge a proportion of the Group Scheme’s interest rate and inflation risks.
