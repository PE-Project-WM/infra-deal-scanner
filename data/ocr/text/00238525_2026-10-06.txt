STRATEGIC REPORT

The directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025.

Principal activities and business review : ,

The principal activity of Cable & Wireless Limited is that of a holding company. The investments of the Company are
disclosed in note 18 to the financial statements.

Our Company is a wholly-owned subsidiary undertaking of C&W Communications, which is itself a wholly-owned
subsidiary of Liberty Latin America, an international provider of fixed, mobile and subsea telecommunications services that

’ provides (i) residential and B2B services in over 20 countries across Latin America, the Caribbean and Puerto Rico, (ii)
enterprise services in certain other countries in Latin America and the Caribbean and (iii) wholesale services over its subsea and
terrestrial fiber optic cable networks that connect over 30 markets in that region.

The directors do not use key performance indicators to assess the performance of the Company as its principal activity is
that of a holding company.

The Company received dividends of $92 million and dividends in specie of $9,890 million from C&W Senior Finance .
during 2025 and 2024, respectively.

The significant year-over-year movements in the balance sheet include an increase in total debtors of $18 million and an
increase in total creditors of $134 million. These movements primarily relate to (i) a CECL provision recovery and (ii) cash
management activities within the C&W Group resulting in the up-stream and down-stream of funds within the C&W Group
intercompany loan structure given the fact that C&W Communications and its subsidiaries operate as a credit group on the
whole.

The Company reported income (loss) before income taxes of ($24 million) and $566 million for the year ended 31
December 2025 and 2024, respectively. The decrease is largely due to a reduction in income from shares in group undertakings.

. Principal risks and uncertainties

Financial and operational risk management is undertaken as part of Liberty Latin America operations as a whole. The
Company’s operations expose it to a variety of operational and financial risks. These are considered in more detail in Liberty
Latin America’s financial statements, which are available at their website: www. lla.com.

Future Outlook

The directors review management policies in light of changing trading and market conditions. Further details of the future
outlook of the group are provided in Liberty Latin America’s financial statements, which are available at their website:
www.lla.com. :

Financial Instruments

Exposure to foreign currency risk, interest rate risk and credit risk arises in the normal course of operations for the
Company.

Foreign currency risk. The Company is not overly exposed to foreign currency risk as we do not generally enter into
transactions denominated in currencies other than our functional currency.

Interest rate risk. The Company’s exposure to interest rate risk is limited to those interest-bearing notes indexed to market
rates (receivable or payable) with other C&W Group entities. The Company does not hedge its interest rate risk.

Credit risk. The Company does not have trade-related activities, accordingly, credit risk is limited to the borrowing and
lending activities with other C&W Group entities. C&W Group operates as a single standalone credit pool and the
intercompany lending and borrowing arrangements within the C&W Group is managed by the C&W Group treasury function.

Provisions are liabilities of uncertain timing or amount. They are recognised when the Company has a present legal or

Retirement benefits

C&W Group operates pension schemes for its current and former U.K. employees for which the Company is the
sponsoring employer. These schemes include both defined benefit schemes, where retirement benefits are based on employees’
remuneration and length of service, and a defined contribution scheme, where retirement benefits reflect the accumulated value
of agreed contributions paid by, and in respect of, employees. Contributions to the defined benefit schemes are made in
accordance with the recommendations of independent actuaries who value the schemes.

Defined Benefit Schemes

The Company’s net obligation recognized in the balance sheet in respect of defined benefit pension plans represents the fair
value of plan assets less the present value of the defined benefit obligations. Defined benefit obligations for each plan are
calculated annually by independent qualified actuaries using the projected unit credit method and key actuarial assumptions at
the reporting date. Defined benefit assets are only recognized to the extent they are deemed recoverable.

Actuarial gains and losses arising from experience adjustments and changes in actuarial assumptions are recognized in full
in the period in which they arise through the statement of comprehensive income (loss) together with returns on plan assets
(excluding net interest taken to the profit and loss account); these re-measurements are not subsequently reclassified to profit
and loss.

Other movements in the net deficit or surplus are recognised in the profit and loss account and included within operating
costs. These comprise current and past service costs including those arising from settlements and curtailments, and net interest
amounts representing the change in the present value of plan obligations and plan assets resulting from the unwinding of the
discount.

The net interest element is determined by multiplying the net defined benefit liability by the discount rate, taking into
account any changes in the net defined benefit liability during the period as a result of contribution and benefit payments.

Remeasurement changes comprise actuarial gains and losses, the effect of the asset ceiling and the return on the net defined
benefit liability excluding amounts included in net interest. These are recognised immediately in other comprehensive income
in the period in which they occur and are not reclassified to profit and loss in subsequent periods.

The net defined benefit pension asset or liability in the balance sheet comprises the total for each plan of the present value
of the defined benefit obligation (using a discount rate based on high quality corporate bonds), less the fair value of plan assets
out of which the obligations are to be settled directly. Fair value is based on market price information, and in the case of quoted
securities is the published bid price. The value of a net pension benefit asset is limited to the amount that may be recovered
either through reduced contributions or agreed refunds from the scheme.

Foreign Currency Translation and Transactions

Transactions in foreign currencies are recorded at the rate ruling at the date of the transaction. Monetary assets and
liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are translated at the rate of exchange ruling at the balance sheet date. The resulting
exchange differences are taken to the income and loss account.

On 30 June 2026, C&W Limited and Cable & Wireless Pension Trustee Limited agreed to proceed with the winding up of
the CWSF, which is expected to be completed on an orderly basis over the wind-up period.
Regulatory Framework and Governance
The assets of the CWSF are held in trustee-administered funds governed by U.K. regulations, as is the nature of the
relationship between the C&W Group and the trustees. Responsibility for the governance of the CWSF, including investment
decisions and contribution schedules, lies with the board of trustees who must consult with the C&W Group on such matters.
The board of trustees must be composed of representatives of the C&W Group, plan participants and an independent trustee in
accordance with the CWSF’s governing documents. .
The weighted average duration of the total expected benefit payments from the CWSF is 10 years.
Asset-Liability Matching
As of 31 December 2025 and 2024, 100% of the CWSF’s defined benefit obligations are covered by buy-in bulk annuity
insurance policies, the payments from which match the corresponding obligations to employees.
Funding Arrangements
During 2025 and 2024, C&W Limited made no contributions to the CWSF as no further contributions are required based
on the most recent valuation of the plan.
Minimum Funding Requirement and Asset Ceiling
There are currently no agreed future deficit recovery payments to constitute a minimum funding requirement. Consistent
with the previous year end, an adjustment to the balance sheet in the CWSF to account for the minimum funding requirement
(calculated in accordance with IFRIC 14) is therefore not required.
There is a surplus in the CWSF at 31 December 2025. During 2025 and 2024, an asset ceiling adjustment of $7 million and
$14 million, respectively, has been applied to restrict the surplus to $nil in accordance with IFRIC 14.
Guarantee Minimum Pensions Inequalities
In October 2018, the High Court ruled in a case involving Lloyds Banking Group that inequalities in pension scheme
benefits arising from unequal Guarantee Minimum Pensions need to be removed. As a result, an allowance of $16 million was
included as a past service cost as at 31 October 2018. A further prior service cost of $3 million was included in 2020 in respect
of a subsequent High Court Judgment in the Lloyds Banking Group case on 20 November 2020.
Virgin Media Case
As a result of the June 2023 judgment in the Virgin Media vs NTL Pension Trustees II Limited case, the validity of any
amendments made in respect of the rules of a contracted-out pension scheme between 6 April 1997 and 5 April 2016 was put
into question. The judgment means that some historic amendments affecting s.9(2B) rights could be void if the necessary
actuarial confirmation under s.37 of the Pension Schemes Act 1993 was not obtained.

Risks

Our defined benefit pension plan can be significantly influenced by short-term market factors and other actuarial risks, the

most significant of which are detailed below.
+ Interest. A decrease in the discount rate will increase the defined benefit obligations;
+ Longevity. The cost to the company of the pensions promised to members is dependent upon the expected term of
these payments. To the extent that members live longer than expected this will increase the cost of these arrangements;
and
* Inflation rate risk. In the U.K., the pension promises are, in the main, linked to inflation, and higher inflation will lead
to higher liabilities.
The above risks have been mitigated for all of the CWSF’s population through the purchase of insurance policies, the
payments from which match the promises made to members.
