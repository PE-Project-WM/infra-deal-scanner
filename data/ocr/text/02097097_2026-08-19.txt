Introduction
The Directors present the Strategic Report for Britannic Technologies Limited for the year ended 31 March 2026.
The year has been one of continued investment, market change and strategic development for the business. Whilst
trading conditions have remained competitive, the Company has continued to focus on strengthening its innovation
capability, enhancing its core platforms and developing long-term customer and partner relationships.

. This report outlines the Company's performance during the year, its principal activities, key developments across
products and services and the strategic priorities that will support future growth in an evolving technology
landscape.

Principal activities
Britannic Technologies Limited is a systems integrator and technology innovator delivering solutions across unified
communications, contact centre platforms, digital transformation, process automation, artificial intelligence (Al),
network services, managed services, cybersecurity, systems integration and application development.
Al is an increasingly embedded part of the Company's offering, supporting automation, analytics, workflow
orchestration and decision-support capabilities across communications and customer engagement platforms.
The Company operates an independent, consultative, service-led model, working in partnership with customers to
design, implement and support integrated technology solutions aligned to operational and strategic objectives.
With over 40 years of experience, Britannic combines in-house engineering capability, Al-enabled innovation,
proprietary platform development (including NetX) and a broad ecosystem of technology partners to deliver scalable
and future-ready solutions.
Business Model
The Company generates revenue across a diversified portfolio of technology-led products and services, including:
« Unified Communications (UC) and Contact Centre (CC) solutions
+ NetX (SIP call routing) platform services and associated applications
+ Managed Services and recurring support contracts
- Professional Services including consultancy and systems integration .
« Hardware and infrastructure solutions
« Product development and innovation-led services
Approximately 63% of revenue is derived from recurring or contracted service-based income, providing strong
revenue visibility and long-term stability.
Value Proposition and Customer Outcomes
The Company's value proposition is centred on delivering secure, intelligent and integrated communications and
digital transformation solutions that improve operational performance and customer engagement.
Key customer outcomes include: 2
« Improved operational efficiency through automation and workflow optimisation :
« Enhanced customer experience through integrated communications platforms
+ Increased security, resilience and compliance in regulated environments
+ Reduced complexity through unified technology architectures
+ Greater agility through cloud, hybrid and sovereign deployment models
These outcomes are delivered through the combination of proprietary platforms, Al-enabled services, managed
services and partner technologies.

1.6 Investment property
Investment properties (including properties held under an operating lease) are initially measured at cost and
subsequently measured at fair value if available without undue cost or effort. Changes in fair value are
recognised in the profit or loss.
The Companies Act 2006 requires all properties to be depreciated. However this requirement conflicts with the
generally accepted accounting principle set out in FRS 102. The directors consider that, because investment
properties are not held for consumption, but for their investment potential, to depreciate them would not give a
true and fair view.
If this departure from the Companies Act 2006 had not been made in order to give a true and fair view, the
profit for the year would have been reduced by depreciation. However the amounts of depreciation cannot
reasonably be quantified, because depreciation is only one of many factors reflected in the annual valuation
and the amount relating to depreciation of the property cannot be separately identified.
Acquisitions and disposals of investment properties are recognised in the financial statements when the
company has a contractual obligation to purchase or sell the property. On the disposal of an investment
property any balance relating to the property held in the non-distributable reserve is transferred to realised
reserves.

1.7 Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting period end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible
assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any
such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the
impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount.of an individual asset,
the company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in use,
the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for which the
estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying
amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. An
impairment ioss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued
amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.
Recognised impairment losses are reversed if, and only if, the reasons for the impairment loss have ceased to
apply. Where an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating
unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but so that the increased carrying amount
does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined had no impairment loss been
recognised for the asset (or cash-generating unit) in prior years. A reversal of an impairment toss is
recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which i
case the reversal of the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation increase.
