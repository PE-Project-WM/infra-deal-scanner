Principal Activities and Introduction
The directors present their Strategic Report for Sabio Ltd (‘the Company”) for the year ended 30 September
2025. The Company and its subsidiaries, together with its intermediate holding company Seattle Holdco Limited
and its immediate holding company Seattle Bidco Limited are hereinafter referred to as “the Group”. The strategic
report, including financial information included, reflects the business of the wider group as a whole to better reflect

: the manner in which the business operates.

Sabio is a pan-European, Al-powered customer experience (CX) specialist and one of the largest independent
CX expert services organisations in Europe. The Group serves long-standing blue-chip enterprise customers
across the United Kingdom, Spain, France, Benelux, the Nordics, South Africa and the Middle East and is
supported by a strengthened balance sheet and a portfolio of Al, data, automation and cloud services that are
now the driver of new bookings.

FY25 was a year of deliberate transformation on two fronts: the repositioning of Sabio as an Al-first CX expert
services business, with Al, data analytics, and automation now the leading offerings; and the reset of Sabio's
capital structure through its partnership with CVC, providing the financial strength to support scalable growth.
The progress of that repositioning is visible in the quality and scale of Al mandates secured with enterprise clients,
rising productivity per employee, and a strengthened balance sheet that underpins investment at the pace
demanded by the market. The shape of the reported profit and loss account, as in any revenue mix-shift year,
lags the strategic progress already in process.

The Board monitors and manages performance through key metrics commonly used across the sector, including
organic revenue growth, margins, cash conversion and revenues and costs per head, to support disciplined long-
term value creation.

Strategic Positioning

Sabio occupies a position of strategic scarcity. It is the largest and leading independent European customer
experience business that is genuinely platform-agnostic and Al-first. There is no equivalent independent
competitor of comparable footprint and capability in Europe.

Sabio's value was highlighted in FY25 by the Group’s partnership with CVC, which removed historic balance
sheet constraints and by continued investment in the Group’s pan-European footprint. Together, these provide
the platform for sustained organic growth, expanding margins as the revenue mix transforms with improving cash
visibility as the new commercial model normalises.

The Board's strategic intent is to scale a higher margin growth engine built on Al, data, and cloud revenues,
ensuring Sabio remains at the forefront of all things customer experience in Europe. The Group is profitable,
cash-generative, and built on multi-year relationships with enterprise customers that consume increasingly
higher-value services over time.

Market Leadership and Positioning

Sabio’s market share in its chosen enterprise segments is material and growing, supported by a deep pan-
European delivery footprint and the depth of its enterprise relationships.

. " Sabio’s customer base is anchored in institutional grade enterprises, with 61% of its GP generated from
FTSE100, FTSE250 or large mutuals, public sector/ state owned organisations, creating a durable, balance
sheet backed foundation from which to scale.

The Group's evolution from CX integrator to Al-powered CX transformation partner is now visible across all
activities and is both tangible, and measurable. Artificial intelligence, automation, and data analytics are the
leading offerings on which new client engagements are now built and while the legacy integration business
remains important as a cash-generative foundation, the centre of gravity for new revenues, new bookings, and
new investment sits firmly in Al-led transformation.

Sabio is demonstrably pan-European. The Group has established a growing Al-led presence in Spain, France,
Benelux, and the Nordics, alongside its established UK home market. This geographic breadth is a defining
differentiator: it allows enterprise clients to deploy consistent customer experience programmes across borders
and creates a delivery footprint that few independent competitors can match.

Customer Quality, Retention and Longevity

The longevity and stickiness of Sabio’s customer base is one of the Group's most valuable characteristics. Sabio

rarely loses a strategic customer; instead, the services those customers consume evolve over time as their

customer experience strategy matures. Where reported revenue from an individual client moves in any given
period, this is overwhelmingly driven by mix shift, from legacy services into cloud, Al, data, and automation.

Clients typically engage Sabio under multi-year contracts, with a high proportion of recurring and repeating

revenue — not solely one-off professional services. This recurring base provides the visibility, predictability, and

quality of earnings that underpin the Group’s equity story. Despite the erosion of certain legacy revenues, the

Group has high visibility over customer intentions and forward demand.

Top 100 client retention in FY25 was 93%, reflecting the depth of these relationships. Sabio’s Net Promoter Score

(NPS), independently validated by OC&C, was last measured at +55 (Oct, 2024), placing the Group amongst the

leading category for client satisfaction in the professional services sector.

The strength of Sabio's capability is best illustrated through measurable outcomes delivered for existing clients:

- British Airways (United Kingdom) - An initial consultancy engagement has grown into a multi-year
programme that has now handled more than 8 million customer calls, with 24% of customers successfully
directed to faster, alternative resolution routes for lower-value enquiries. This has allowed BA's customer
service teams to concentrate their time and expertise on more complex interactions, improving the quality of
service where it matters most.

- Securitas Direct (Spain) - Implementation and management of Verint Speech Analytics enabled the
company to audit 80% of its calls in real time, achieving a customer satisfaction score of 9.1, a 50% reduction
in call transfers, and a 15% reduction in wait times.

- Transcom (Benelux) - Sabio delivered an Al-powered real-time translation solution capable of supporting
more than 100 languages, allowing Transcom to recruit agents on the basis of knowledge rather than
language skills and reducing the total cost of ownership of multilingual service by up to 65%.

- IMA Iberica (Spain) - Sabio completed Europe's largest deployment of Google Agent Assist, equipping 200
agents with Al-supported tools providing instant access to more than 40,000 insurance documents and
supporting around 45,000 monthly customer interactions.

- Kisio (France) - Sabio’s Case Management platform enabled Kisio to win three French public transport
contracts with a combined value of €35 million.

FY25 saw Sabio secure a series of significant new client relationships that reflect the quality and breadth of its

Al-first positioning across financial services, regulated utilities, and the public sector:

- NatWest Bank (United Kingdom) — Sabio was engaged by NatWest to deliver a high-profile Al and data
insights programme in partnership with Amazon, supporting one of the UK's largest contact centre estates
of approximately 8,000 agents. The engagement combined advanced workforce management
consultancy with Al-enabled analytics, reinforcing Sabio’s position as a trusted partner for enterprise-
scale Al and decision support in UK financial services.

- ABSA Bank (South Africa) - Sabio was selected by Amalgamated Banks of South Africa — the third
largest bank in the country, operating across ten sub-Saharan African countries with assets of R1.9 trillion
(approximately £83 billion) — to take over management and support of its 6,300-agent contact centre
estate under a two-plus-one-year contract with a potential total contract value of R90 million
(approximately £4 million), Sabio was evaluated as the standout choice against all regional competition
on the basis of its expert services capability, proprietary monitoring platform, and CX training programme.

- GRDF — Engie Group (France) — Sabio was awarded the contact centre cloud transformation contract
for GRDF, a major French energy infrastructure company and sister brand to Engie, one of Europe's
largest energy groups. Sabio is designing, implementing and managing a Genesys Cloud solution for
420 agents across voice and digital channels on an initial three-year managed service term, further
cementing its position as the leading independent CX transformation partner to large-scale regulated
utilities in France.

Al-First Repositioning and Acceleration Programme
The most important strategic shift inside Sabio is moving activity and revenue from legacy to future state
services. Legacy revenue streams, such as on-premise contact centre, break fix support, and older technology
contracts, remain cash generative and help fund this transition, with Sabio actively managing these accounts
throughout. Future state services, including cloud contact centre, Al, data analytics, automation and
consultancy, carrying greater scalability and high margins.
Al-driven services are a growing share of new bookings, with +60% of the sales pipeline being Al and Data
related. The reduction in legacy services including lower-margin technology pass-through revenue is a
structural choice which depresses reported top-line in the short term but will support structural gross margin
improvement over time. The Group understands the rate of migration in detail: the remaining legacy exposure,
the customers within it, and the associated margin uplift as each cohort migrates. By the end of FY27, Sabio
expects a materially higher proportion of its revenue, profit and cashflow to come from future-state activities
with over 90% of the Group’s services expected to be delivered using public cloud-based technology rather
than on-premise or private hosted infrastructure.
Sabio’s differentiation lies not in Al technology itself, but in the application of Al within real-world customer
operations. With over 25 years of domain expertise in delivering conversational CX automation, the Group is
uniquely placed to help enterprise clients design, deploy, and scale Al solutions that deliver measurable
outcomes. This is a key differentiation and what separates Sabio from platform vendors and hyperscalers.
Following Board approval in FY24 of a significant investment commitment, the Al acceleration programme
moved from strategy to active execution during FY25. The Sabio Al Business Unit was established, the global
commercial team was realigned around an Al-led proposition, and a comprehensive go-to-market strategy was
activated with full effect from January 2026. The Al-first proposition is built around three reinforcing pillars:
« Al-powered automation that delivers tangible return on investment for clients.
e Expert professional and managed services that de-risk Al adoption.
e« Adeeply integrated partner ecosystem that accelerates innovation.
Sabio's roadmap is firmly oriented towards agentic Al capable of executing multi-step workflows autonomously
in customer service environments supported by outcome-based commercial models that offer Sabio a share
in the value generated by Al automation.
Partner Ecosystem and Platform Agnosticism
Sabio is deliberately technology and platform-agnostic. The Group selects the best combination of partner
solutions and its own technology to optimise customer outcomes. This independence provides flexibility for
clients and allows Sabio to adapt as the technology landscape evolves.
Sabio has established and continues to deepen strategic partnerships with Al native platforms and
hyperscalers, including Google Cloud, AWS, ElevenLabs and Deepgram, that are powering the rapid growth
of its voice Al business. These relationships are central to Sabio's position at the intersection of enterprise
customer experience demand and next-generation Al capability, with voice Al now firmly established as a key
growth engine for the Group. This momentum is further strengthened by Sabio's long-standing relationships .
with established contact centre platform partners, notably Genesys and Verint, providing the enterprise-grade
foundations on which its Al capabilities are built and scaled.

; Sabio's people remain a core competitive asset. As Al commoditises software and undermines pure platform-
based capabilities, Sabio's deep, tenured team with combined sector, CX domain knowledge, Al, and data
analytics expertise, taken with the Group's proprietary accelerators, methodologies, and proven delivery
playbooks combine to form a defensible competitive position unrivalled in Europe.
Investment in reskilling and capability development is directly linked to future growth and margin quality. The
Winning Ways internal development programme has refocused the entire commercial team on Al-led solution
selling. Al engineering and data science recruitment has continued through the year.
Sabio’s strategic investment in South African delivery operations extends capacity for Al and cloud migration
work and offers a competitive cost base, enabling the Group to scale without proportionately increasing costs in
more expensive European markets. The savings made here are being reinvested into the Al acceleration

Core revenue per head increased by 7% to £161,000 (FY24: £150,000), a direct and measurable indicator that
the repositioning is working at the unit-economic level. Total direct staff moved to 672 in FY25 (FY24: 770),
reflecting both the divestment of the Salesforce practice and the optimisation of delivery capacity as legacy
service lines contract and higher-value Al engagements scale.

CVC Partnership and Balance Sheet Reset
On 22 July 2025, majority ownership of the Group was acquired by funds and accounts managed and advised .
by CVC, a leading global private markets manager with approximately €202 billion in assets under management :
at time of investment. The transaction was a watershed moment: it provided the financial strength, balance sheet
security, and strategic backing required to invest confidently in Sabio’s Al-first future.
As part of the transaction, the Group's capital structure was substantially modified. Total debt was reduced to
£69 million, with maturities extended to fall between 30 June 2030 and 30 December 2031. Annual interest
expense more than halved. On a pro-forma basis, the Group moved from a net liability position at FY24 to a :
materially strengthened net asset position post-transaction — a fundamental improvement in balance sheet
quality.
CVC's investment reflects shared conviction in the scale of the Al opportunity ahead. Their backing aligns the
Group with a long-term partner whose capabilities and networks support the next phase of international growth.
Financial Performance & KPIs
The FY25 financials reflect two concurrent forces: a structural transition in how enterprise clients consume
traditional contact centre services as cloud and Al platforms mature; and Sabio’s own deliberate shift towards

longer-duration, Al-enabled enterprise engagements. Both forces compress reported revenue and tighten
cashflows in the short term but create a more durable foundation for sustained growth. The reported profit and
loss account should be read in that context.
Revenue and Gross Profit
Group revenue for the year, excluding the discontinued Salesforce business, was £114.2 million (FY24: £126.5
million), a decline of 10% (6% excluding one-off technology revenue). The movement was anticipated and is
attributable to the continued migration of legacy managed services contracts towards cloud-based services,
and to the deliberate shift away from non-strategic activity. Gross profit margin was 36.4% (FY24:
approximately 37.5%), reflecting the expected impact of transition-period mechanics noted above rather than a
deterioration in underlying margin quality. The Board expects future margins to increase supported by the
increasing proportion of complex, higher-value work rather than volume resell. For a services-led business,
Sabio's gross margin profile is strong and structurally underpinned by the complexity, depth and long-duration
nature of customer engagements rather than the volume of low-margin technology resell.

EBITDA and Profitability
Adjusted EBITDA was £14.9 million (FY24: £20.0 million), with a margin of 13.1% (FY24: 15.8%). The margin
reduction reflects in large part the Group's investment in the Al acceleration programme, including headcount
additions in Al engineering and data science, investment in thought leadership and market positioning, and the
cost of the global commercial team realignment. Sabio is a fundamentally profitable and cash-generative
business; the Board regards this period of elevated investment as necessary and proportionate to the scale of
the Al opportunity. The Group’s operational and business services platform is robust and well-invested,
positioning Sabio to generate EBITDA growth through price, mix and cross-sell rather than through proportionate
increases in headcount.
Cash Flow and Cash Conversion
Cash flows from operating activities improved to £2.7 million (FY24: £0.25 million) due to significantly lower
finance costs following the substantial modification of the Group’s capital structure. Capital expenditure reduced
to £1.5 million (FY24: £1.9 million), reflecting the completion of major platform and infrastructure investments
made in prior years. On a free cash flow basis (cash generated from operations less capital expenditure), the
Group generated a £1.2 million inflow (FY24: £1.7 million outflow).
The Board uses cash conversion — defined as operating cash flow less capital expenditure, divided by adjusted
EBITDA — as a primary performance indicator. Cash conversion on this basis was 27.3% in FY25, below the
Board's long-term target. This is driven primarily by working capital and billing profile changes rather than any
deterioration in underlying profitability or customer payment behaviour. Historically, a significant proportion of
legacy managed services revenue was billed annually in advance, supporting strong in-year cash conversion. Al
and data analytics engagements are increasingly billed monthly in arrears, shifting cash receipts later relative to :
revenue recognition during the transition. Customer quality and payment behaviour remain strong; what has
changed is the timing of cash flows as contract structures evolve. Cash conversion is expected to improve as the
new invoicing pattern normalises and working capital is actively managed.
FY26 Outlook
The Board approaches FY26 with confidence grounded in the Group's contract base and relationships,
pipeline, partnerships, an Al-led commercial model fully activated from January 2026 and underpinned by the
strengthened balance sheet.
FY26 will be a further year of investment in the Al acceleration programme with margin recovery anticipated
from FY27 as Al revenue scales and operating leverage improves.
The strategic rationale is clear. Enterprise clients are going through the most significant technology shift in
customer experience in decades. The question is not whether to adopt Al, but how to do it well and at scale
within complex existing environments, while delivering clear return on investment and managing the risks of
change with a trusted partner.
This is where Sabio stands out. The Group combines deep knowledge of customer experience operations,
proven delivery capability, strong Al engineering expertise, and long standing client relationships. This is
strengthened by the CVC partnership and a reskilled, energised global team, positioning Sabio as a natural
partner for European enterprises.
The Board and Directors acknowledge the continued support of CVC through its provision of additional funding
and covenant relief, which enhances liquidity and provides the Group with the ongoing financial flexibility to take
advantage of the current market opportunity.7
The Board is confident that the investments being made today will generate significant and durable shareholder
: value in the years ahead.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties _
The Group's operations expose it to a variety of risks, including loss of key customers, loss of key suppliers,
business continuity, reliance on key information technology systems, and risks associated with the growth of the
business and the underlying economic environment. These risks are formally reviewed by the Board at least
annually, with appropriate processes in place to monitor and mitigate their effects.
Specific risk areas of heightened focus in FY25 include:
Al Adoption Risk
The pace of enterprise Al adoption may differ from current expectations. Sabio mitigates this through a diversified
client base, multiple Al use cases, and a portfolio of managed service relationships that provide near-term
revenue stability.
Talent Risk
Competition for Al engineering and data science talent is intense. Sabio mitigates through investment in reskilling,
the expanded South African talent pool, and partnerships with leading Al platform providers.
Partner Concentration
The Group's revenue base retains some concentration around key technology partners, particularly Genesys and
Verint. Platform agnosticism and diversification across Al-native partners reduce this concentration over time.
From FY25, revenue and cost in respect of Genesys cloud contact centre subscription licences are presented on
a net basis (see Basis of Revenue Presentation), aligning the reported revenue line with the underlying economic
substance of the Genesys flow.
Annuity Erosion
Erosion of high-margin legacy annuity contracts remains a risk into FY26. Sabio mitigates through proactive
account management, the embedding of Al within existing managed service contracts, and the pursuit of longer-
duration Al-enabled service agreements.
Customer Concentration
Sabio maintains a diversified enterprise customer portfolio and operates rigorous account governance to limit
dependency on any single client. .
Business Continuity and IT Systems
The Group maintains tested business continuity plans, resilient infrastructure, and information security controls
appropriate to the scale and nature of its operations.

Section 172(1) Statement
In accordance with section 172(1) of the Companies Act 2006, the Directors have acted in good faith in the way
they consider most likely to promote the success of the Company for the benefit of its members as a whole,

having regard to the matters set out in section 172(1)(a) to (f). The Directors engage with, and have regard to,
the interests of the Group’s key stakeholders, summarised below.
Shareholders — the Board engages with shareholders through regular reporting, strategic reviews, and dialogue
on long-term value creation. The CVC transaction in July 2025 was undertaken to strengthen the balance sheet,
extend debt maturities, and provide the capital required to execute the Al acceleration programme.
Employees — the Directors recognise that Sabio’s people are central to the Group’s ability to deliver for clients.

and engages employees through global town halls, leadership cascades, and structured feedback channels.
Customers — the Group maintains long-term, partnership-based relationships with its enterprise client base,
validated by sustained retention rates and an independently measured Net Promoter Score. Account governance,
executive sponsorship, and outcome-based delivery are central to how Sabio engages its customers.

due diligence proportionate to risk. Technology partnerships with Genesys, Verint, and Al-native platforms are
governed at executive level.

community engagement. Sabio maintains its net-zero commitment, validated by the Science Based Targets
initiative and the United Nations Global Compact.
Long-term consequences and reputation — the Board considers the long-term consequences of its decisions,
including the impact of the Al-first repositioning on future growth, the durability of client relationships, and the
Group’s standing as a trusted expert services partner in the European customer experience market.

Revenue recognition
A contract involves agreement between two or more parties that creates enforceable rights and
performance obligations. The Company recognises revenue when an enforceable performance
obligation is satisfied, and it is identifiable separately from other promises in the contract. A performance
obligation involves the transfer of control of distinct goods and services to the customer where a
customer can benefit from the good or service on its own or together with readily available resources in
the market.
The Company allocates a transaction price to each performance obligation on the basis of the stand-
alone selling prices of each distinct good or service promised in the contract. The Company uses an
estimated price when stand-alone prices are not observable. Discounts, rebates, and consideration that
are generated through the synergy of the contract as a whole should be allocated to one or more, but
not all performance obligations.
For performance obligations satisfied over time, the Company recognises revenue over time by
selecting an appropriate method for measuring the Company’s progress towards completion of that
performance obligation.
i) Annuity services in respect of service, support and maintenance income is typically invoiced in
advance, termed deferred income. Revenue is recorded on a straight-line basis over the relevant period;
in accordance with IFRS 15, a time-based method to measure progress is assessed to be appropriate
as the performance obligation is satisfied evenly over a period of time and/or the Company has an
active obligation to perform over a period of time. In a number of Annuity contracts, the Company has
a right to consideration from customers in an amount that corresponds directly with the value to the
customer of the Company’s performance completed to date; in particular in relation to call and usage
charges. In that instance the Company recognises revenue in relation to those call and usage charges
for the amount to which the entity has a right to invoice. .
ii) Professional services revenue is in respect of project service income from consultancy, installation
and training services on telephony and data systems. It is recognised when performance, provision or
delivery of the service has occurred, based on measured input hours, to the degree that the associated
hours are recoverable, and when the contractual right to consideration has been earned. If any of these
services are invoiced and paid for in advance of being delivered, the income is termed deferred income,
and then recorded as revenue when under this accounting policy the Company is entitled to recognise
the revenue.
iii) Technology revenue (revenue from the sale of software and hardware) is recognised when goods
are delivered, or invoiced and the Company has an unconditional right to consideration before it has
satisfied a performance obligation.
iv) The Company considers itself principal in all the above revenue categories as the majority of its
projects and services involve the implementation, maintenance and support of complex customer
experience IT solutions, comprising multiple technologies — both third party and proprietary — that often
require specific configuration for customers’ existing IT infrastructures and processes. The Company
manages all interactions with its customers and provides 1st and 2nd level support services to its
customers.
