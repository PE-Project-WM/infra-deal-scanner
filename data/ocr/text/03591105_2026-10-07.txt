Introduction
JSM Group Services Limited is an Independent Connection Provider (ICP), providing integrated utility
infrastructure solutions. Our combination of end-to-end technical expertise, decades of industry experience, and
innovative thinking make us a trusted partner for the implementation of next-generation connectivity in Power
and Digital Infrastructure. Our focus is on building for tomorrow by delivering every day for customers across the
UK and supporting Group activities in Europe and beyond.
Key markets
The Company operates in market sectors which are seen as critical infrastructure areas for the future and are
experiencing strong long-term growth: in particular, energy transition and decarbonisation through Grid re-
enforcement and renewables projects together with providing data centres with HV Power solutions and
installing digital infrastructure networks. Ambitious net zero targets are driving demand for sustainable,
distributed renewable energy and the electrification of heating and transport, placing increasing strain on an
under-invested electricity distribution network. Technology advancements, Al and big data are exponentially
increasing demand for data, processing capacity and improved connectivity which is driving significant
investment in infrastructure.
The Company operates at the top end of a fast moving area of the economy, supporting blue chip customers in
the installation of large scale data centres. Activity has been concentrated predominantly in the UK to date. The
Group has expanded its presence in Germany and other European markets through separate legal entities,
positioning the Group for future growth. Growth in renewable related infrastructure, including battery storage
facilities, has also contributed to activity levels during the year.

Business review
During the year ended 31 December 2025, the Company operated in a dynamic market environment influenced
by a number of external factors affecting the timing of major customer programmes across the UK data centre,
power and digital infrastructure sectors. While underlying demand for the Company's core capabilities remained
strong and long-term market fundamentals continued to strengthen, these factors impacted the phasing of
: project delivery during the year.

During the year, several key customers undertook a re-sequencing of project start dates and delivery timelines.
This reflected changes in customer priorities, evolving technical requirements and broader macroeconomic

_ conditions. Importantly, these changes did not represent a reduction in underlying demand, but rather a deferral
of activity into future periods. The company was also impacted by delays in the Renewables Market caused by
the government Connection Reform Policies. .
Notwithstanding these timing effects, the Company continued to see sustained demand for its services across
its core infrastructure markets. The Company's reputation for delivering high-quality projects safely, on schedule
and in technically complex environments supported strong and long-standing customer relationships.
Market-wide macroeconomic uncertainty also influenced customer investment decisions. In particular, changes
in US economic policy and tariff considerations were cited by a number of hyperscale customers as a key factor
in deferring certain UK and European data centre programmes.
As a result, revenue reduced by 16.8% to £151.6m (2024: £182.2m), primarily reflecting a 25.1% reduction in
Power division revenue to £92.9m. This was partly offset by continued growth in the Communications division,
where revenue increased to £50.7m (2024: £40.4m), and in Gas services, where revenue increased to £8.0m
(2024: £6.2m).
The Board approved the decision during the year, as part of a strategic review, to cease the Gas division
following completion of existing projects. The division represented approximately 5.2% of total revenue. The
decision reflects the Company's focus on its core activities, where it has greater scale, stronger margins and
more attractive growth opportunities, with the Gas division having comparatively lower returns and higher
working capital requirements.
Operating profit for. the year was £12.4m (2024: £22.8m), with gross margin of 21.4% (2024: 25.5%), reflecting
the impact of reduced Power division revenue and changes in project mix during the period. Administrative
expenses increased from £16.9m to £18.2m, in line with continued investment to support the growth and scale
of the business. The year also included £2.7 million of exceptional costs, primarily relating to restructuring,
international expansion and specific business transformation initiatives, including the implementation of new
systems, process redesign and changes to the Group’s operating and governance framework, undertaken to
support future growth.
Profit before tax of £12.4m (2024: £23.0m) reflects the rephasing of major customer programmes, particularly
within the Power division, together with changes in project mix during the year, rather than any weakening in
underlying demand. .
The Company's balance sheet position remains strong, with net assets of £49.1m (2024: £35.9m) and a positive
cash balance. At Group level, as presented in the consolidated financial statements of JSM Group Services
Holdings International Limited, net assets were £155.2m.
During the year, the Company received external industry recognition supporting its focus on safety and
innovation, including a RoSPA President's Award, marking ten consecutive Gold Awards, and awards for the
NICE® (Non-intrusive Cable Extraction) system at the Ground Engineering Awards and Eddie Awards.
The Company has continued its focus on Environmental, Social and Governance matters, as detailed later in
this report, and has maintained its strong health and safety performance.

Principal risks and uncertainties
During the year, JSM Group Services Limited continued to demonstrate resilience despite ongoing economic
and political uncertainties in the UK, Europe and other markets. These external factors remain key risks for the
year ahead. However, the strength of the Company's core markets, particularly the continued growth in data
centre infrastructure, together with a strong pipeline of secured contracts, provides a significant mitigating factor
against these risks.
Ongoing geopolitical tensions, including the war in Eastern Europe and instability in the Middle East, continue to
influence the wider economic environment. While the Company does not have direct operations in these
regions, these developments can have an indirect impact through increased volatility in material prices,
pressure on supply chains, rising labour and subcontract costs, and some uncertainty in customer investment
decisions, particularly within energy and data centre infrastructure.
Despite these challenges, the Company operates in markets with strong long term fundamentals, including
sustained demand for high voltage power infrastructure, energy transition projects, digital connectivity
expansion and hyperscale data centre development. A strong pipeline of secured and framework based work
provides significant mitigation against these broader risks.
; The Company continues to manage these risks through early procurement, maintaining a diverse and reliable :
supplier base, and using established commercial frameworks to manage cost and delivery risk. Management
: continues to monitor the situation closely. At present, these risks are not currently expected to have a material
impact on the Company’s ability to operate, but they remain an area of ongoing focus.
Financial risk management
The Company's operations expose it to a variety of financial business risks including foreign currency exchange
rates, credit risk and liquidity risk.
Foreign currency risk
The Company purchases certain materials from European suppliers in foreign currencies. While this gives rise
to an underlying exposure to exchange rate movements, such exposure is not considered to be significant. Any
material price changes resulting from currency fluctuations are generally mitigated through contractual price
adjustment mechanisms with customers.
Credit risk :
The Company's credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade debtors and amounts recoverable on contracts.
Credit risk is managed by monitoring the aggregate amount and duration of exposure to any one customer and
any credit limit in place. A significant proportion of receivables were current and not overdue at the year end.
Liquidity risk
The Company's liquidity risk is managed through careful financial planning on major contracts, including the
negotiation of appropriate payment terms with customers and suppliers. The Company's objective is to ensure
an overall positive or neutral cash flow on all projects. /
Interest rate risk
The Company is exposed, through the Group’s financing arrangements, to interest rate risk on borrowings.
While no hedging instruments are currently in place, the Company manages this risk through regular monitoring
of market interest rates and incorporating interest rate sensitivity analysis into financial planning and cash flow
forecasting. The Company also ensures sufficient headroom in its available facilities to absorb potential cost
increases resulting from rate fluctuations.

Outlook/Prospects
The Directors remain confident in the future prospects of the Company, supported by sustained demand across
its core infrastructure and utility markets and a strong reputation for delivering high-quality projects on schedule
and in technically complex environments. The Company benefits from long-standing customer relationships, a
high level of secured orders for the year ahead and a robust pipeline of opportunities beyond that.
Whilst macroeconomic uncertainty continues to influence the timing of certain customer programmes,
underlying demand remains strong and the Directors expect deferred projects to progress as market conditions
stabilise.
The will continue to invest in capital expenditure, systems and its workforce to support operational efficiency
: and delivery capability. Investment in project controls, contract risk management and IT systems remains
central to the Company's operating model as it continues to scale.
The Company’s operations are primarily focused in the UK, with international activities and overseas expansion
undertaken by other entities within the Group.
Growth to date has been predominantly UK-based; however, the Group continues to see increasing demand ©
from customers with international requirements.. Opportunities are assessed at Group level, with current plans
for expansion across European and Middle Eastern markets being delivered through other Group entities,
supported by strong relationships with existing customers.
Subsequent to the year end, a Group entity secured a contract in Germany, representing an important milestone
in the development of the Group's European operations. The Group has established legal entities in the
Republic of Ireland, Germany, Sweden and the Middle East to support its international expansion strategy. In
addition, the Group is in the process of establishing a design hub and shared services centre in India to
enhance engineering, design and delivery capabilities across the Group.
These initiatives are expected to strengthen the Group’s operational capacity and support future growth while
, maintaining a disciplined and focused approach to international expansion. As an established business with
strong supplier relationships and access to skilled labour markets, the Company is well positioned to scale its
_ operations while maintaining a clear strategic focus.

Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis, which assumes that the Company will
: continue in operational existence for at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements.
During the year, the Company operated in a period of macro economic uncertainty influenced by inflationary
pressures, customer driven programme phasing, and the macro-economic effects of global geopolitical
tensions, including the conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East. These factors contributed to delays in
the timing of certain major customer programmes. However, they did not materially reduce underlying demand
across the Company's core markets. The Company continues to operate with a strong forward order book and
long term framework agreements, which provide a high degree of revenue visibility for 2026 and beyond.
For the year ended 31 December 2025, the Company generated revenue of £151.6m and EBITDA of £17.7m,
demonstrating continued profitability and cash generation despite timing impacts on programme delivery. The
directors expect trading conditions to strengthen as deferred customer programmes progress during 2026.
The Company continues to operate within a well funded capital structure. The Group’s term loan includes a
financial covenant based on a net debt leverage multiple. The latest forecasts reviewed by the directors,
including sensitised downside scenarios, show that the Group expects to continue to comply comfortably with its
covenant requirements throughout the forecast period and to meet its liabilities as they fall due.
Working capital requirements increased during 2025, reflecting customer programme phasing. These
requirements are expected to normalise as activity levels increase. The Company maintains strong relationships
with both clients and banking partners, supporting favourable payment practices and access to committed
funding facilities to meet working capital needs.
At Company level, the balance sheet shows a positive net asset position at the year end, and the Company
continues to generate positive operating cashflows. Based on the Company's financial position, forecast
performance, liquidity headroom and the Company's continued access to funding, the directors have a
reasonable expectation that the Company will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. .
Accordingly, the directors consider it appropriate to prepare the financial statements on the going concern basis.
