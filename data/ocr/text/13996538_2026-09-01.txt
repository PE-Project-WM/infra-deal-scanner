The Directors present their Strategic Report on the Company for the year ended 31 December 2024.

. Principal activity
SymphonyAl NetReveal UK Limited (hereafter “the Company’) is a private limited company organised under the laws
of the United Kingdom and incorporated in the United Kingdom.
The principal activity of the Company is the provision of enterprise investigation and intelligence software solutions
that enable financial institutions to combat financial crime. The Company is a wholly owned subsidiary of SymphonyAl
NetReveal BidCo Limited.
Business review
SymphonyAl NetReveal UK is a leading provider of enterprise investigation and intelligence software solutions that
enable financial institutions to combat financial crime.

£0.4m for
the year ended December 31, 2024, as compared to the same period in 2023.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Given the rapid growth of the Al SaaS industry, competitive pressure both domestically in the UK and internationally
is a significant risk for the Company. To manage this risk, the Company focuses on staying ahead of the fast-paced
SaaS environment by continually upscaling its operations, innovating, and regularly updating its product offerings to
meet the evolving needs of customers. This proactive approach ensures that the Company remains competitive and
can effectively respond to market dynamics.
The Company actively manages other key business risks, including data privacy and security and compliance risks.
Robust controls and risk management frameworks are in place to mitigate these risks, ensuring that they remain
within manageable limits for the Company. These include, but are not limited to, the implementation of data
encryption, the use of access controls and multi-factor authentication and regular review of the laws and regulations.
As a result, the impact of these risks on the Company is effectively minimised.

Business overview : ,
SymphonyAl NetReveal is a leading provider of enterprise investigation and intelligence software solutions that
enable financial institutions to combat financial crime. The Company's solutions help financial institutions detect,
analyse, investigate and action, on an enterprise basis, areas of financial crime such as money laundering, debit/credit
card fraud, identity theft, market abuse, terrorist financing, and the illegal transfer of funds to named individuals and
organisations. The Company’s suite is deployed globally in the retail banking, insurance, and investment banking .
sectors and monitors millions of transactions on a daily basis. The technology platform of the Company is at the core
of its financial crime prevention solution offerings. The platform provides highly flexible, enterprise-grade data, ML
and Al pipelines that analyse customer and entity behaviours to enable the detection, investigation, and reporting of
financial crime. It surfaces alerts and insights to operational users through a single-pane-of-glass case management :
solution that consolidates risk signals across the enterprise and orchestrates complex agent workflows based on
encoded operating procedures. The combined approach of big data analytics and Al-native investigations enables
financial services organisations to not just meet their regulatory obligations but also build up in-depth knowledge on
the risk profile of their business and take action to prevent crime and reduce their operational losses.

Allocation of the transaction price
The transaction price is determined based on the consideration to which the Company expects to be entitled
in exchange for transferring goods and services to the customer. Consideration may vary due to discounts,

and included in the transaction price if, in the Company's judgement, it is probable that there will not be a
significant future reversal of cumulative revenue under the contract.
If the contract contains a single performance obligation, the entire transaction price is allocated to the single
performance obligation. Contracts that contain multiple performance obligations require an allocation of the
transaction price to each performance obligation based on its standalone selling price (‘SSP’) in relation to
the total fair value of all performance obligations in the arrangement. Some of the Company’s performance .
obligations, such as support, and professional services, have observable inputs that are used to determine
the SSP of those distinct performance obligations. Where SSP is not directly observable, the Company
determines the SSP using information that may include market conditions and other observable inputs. When
license and software support are sold with other services, such as professional services, the Company will
use the residual method to determine the license and software support bundle because the information to.
determine SSP for our license and software support bundle is highly variable or uncertain and observable
stand-alone selling prices exist for the other services promised in the contract. When the only performance
obligations sold are license and software support, the Company allocates the transaction price first to the
software support using the determined SSP based on observable standalone sales and then allocates the

4. Market Risk — Foreign Currency Risk
The Company's functional and presentational currency is sterling. Revenue is generated from customers
across the United Kingdom, Europe and the rest of the world, and a proportion of operating costs are
, denominated in currencies other than sterling, principally US dollars and euros. The Company is therefore
exposed to movements in exchange rates. : .
During the year ended 31 December 2024, exchange differences of £2,074,249 (2023: £251,815) were
recognised in the income statement, reflecting the increased volume and geographic spread of the Company's
international operations. This exposure is mainly on account of Intercompany receivables and payables with
the group companies. .
The Company does not currently use formal hedging instruments; management monitors currency exposures
and manages the timing of receipts and payments where practicable to mitigate the impact of rate movements.
