Strategic report
The directors present their strategic report on Sonovate Limited (‘the Company’) for the year ended 31 December
2025. The Company’s accounting reference date is 29 December. These accounts and the comparison figures cover
the year ended 31 December in order to be consistent and as is permissible under the Companies Act 2006 s392.
The Company is an innovative financial services technology company that predominantly supports recruitment
agencies and HR Technology businesses in the contingent labour market. The Group provides technology solutions
and highly valued financial products to its customers.
Review of the business
The Company continues to invest considerable resources into the development of software, including AI, which
enables its customers, including recruitment agencies and other providers of contractors and contingent workers, to
leverage the back-office workflows provided by Sonovate, including processing contracts, invoices, payments, and
the provision of working capital funding with the objective of becoming the supplier of choice for such services. The
Company provides significant liquidity into UK small businesses and is an enabler of UK small business growth.
Its technology allows it to offer its services via APIs in a “Lending as a Service” type product. During the year, the
Company continued to enhance the new platform to enable projected growth and to provide existing and new
customers with exceptional solutions and experience.
The audited financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025 are set out on pages 15 to 30. The Company
continues to evolve and grow its business in the UK and selected international markets. The Operating loss for the
financial year to 31 December 2025 was £6,276,409 (2024: loss of £5,675, 881).
At the balance sheet date, the Company had net current liabilities of £26,995,415 (2024: net current liabilities
£21,537,997) and total net liabilities of £20,291,159 (2024: net liabilities £14, 121,342).
Over the course of the year ended 31 December 2025, the Company processed and funded £1,318,918,658 of invoices
(2024: £1,258,84 1,922) an increase of 4.8% (2024: 5.6%) over the previous year. Cumulatively to 31 December 2025
the Company has provided over £7bn of funding to businesses.
Funding and liquidity
The company is reliant on the funding structure of the entire Group headed by Sonovate International Holdings
Limited (“the Group”). In June 2022, the Group made a significant investment to expand the sources of funding by
refinancing existing facilities with a private securitisation facility and setting up a “bankruptcy-remote” Irish special
purpose vehicle (“SPV”), Sonovate Trade Receivables Designated Activity Company. This is a single purpose entity
established for the securitisation facility and is considered “bankruptcy-remote” as its sole purpose is to administer
the securitisation of the company’s receivables and is prohibited from incurring other debt or obligations. Therefore,
the Company now securitises certain receivables assets via the sale of these assets to the SPV, which in turn issues
asset-backed securities to investors. Also in 2022, the Group agreed a loan syndication for the senior loan facility. An
additional loan provider was required to support the company’s growing volumes.
As at 31 December 2025, as a result of the securitisation and the loan syndication the Group is funded by the following
facilities:
e Senior facility £200m (£100m each) - the Irish SPV has issued class A senior debt notes to BNP Paribas and
Lloyds Bank.
e Mezzanine facility £40m — the Irish SPV issued class B mezzanine notes to M&G Investments
e =6Arevolving £30m loan facility agreement between Sonovate International Holdings Limited and BCI Capital
Limited which is used to fund the vehicte’s subordinated notes
Additional equity of £15m was raised from existing investors in 2025.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Company is subject to principal risks and uncertainties as detailed below:
Continued access to funding and liquidity
In order to purchase invoices, mainly supporting the liquidity of UK small business, the Company requires access to
a committed funding line either through equity or debt capital. This is managed by the group and as detailed above
the Senior Financing Facility, the private securitisation facility of £150m of senior notes held by a large European
Bank, a £40m mezzanine notes facility issued to a UK asset manager which are both now held in a special purpose
vehicle ‘Sonovate Trade Receivables Designated Activity Company’, and the £30m revolving loan facility were all
extended subsequent to the year end.
Credit risk
The Company is exposed to the risk that debtors do not settle debts in a complete and/or timely manner. The Company
has in place credit risk models to ensure that lending levels are appropriate for the credit risk profile of the customer
and debtor. The vast majority of funding is credit insured which significantly mitigates the risk of non-collection.
This risk is monitored closely in terms of the delinquency of the invoices funded and the proportion of debt that is
uninsured. The Company has recorded a bad debt charge in the year ended 31 December 2025 of £1,230,367 (2024:
£1, 363,077) against debtors that are unlikely to be recoverable; for debts that have not been written off at 31 December
2025 attempts are continuing to recover the sums due. ,
Financial Crime risk
The Company is exposed to a number of financial crime risks, with the most significant exposure to fraud risk. The
most likely fraud risk to the Company is the financing of fake invoices. The Company mitigates this risk through a
proprietary software which uses a combination of public and non-public data sources to assess the potential risk for
fraudulent invoices on a continuous basis.
Operational risk
Operational Risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems or from
external events. As a result of these risks, the Company may have a negative impact to reputation with its stakeholders,
face fines or damages and loss of future business. The Company mitigates this risk through regular internal review
processes and the maintenance of an internal risk register.
Competition
The provision of finance into the temporary worker sector is a competitive marketplace. While the Company remains
significantly ahead in terms of its technology, the entrance of multiple and / or well backed competitors with
significant financial resources and a focus on technology could result in more competitive pricing and / or a reduced
rate of market share capture. The market is monitored regularly.
Key performance indicators
The directors manage the Company’s operations holistically and monitor the ongoing performance of the business
through several measures including:

* preparation of and monitoring of weekly and monthly management information including detailed
management accounts and variance analysis;

¢ regular review of the portfolio performance including delinquencies, debtor days, debt turn and credit quality
including credit insurance;

e monitoring of new business volumes, organic growth of existing customers, net revenue retention, client
churn, cost of customer acquisition, cost to service our customers and the lifetime value of our customers;
and

e gathering and responding to customer feedback.

Going concern (continued)
As a provider of financing to customers, the funding requirements of the business are a key management activity as it
is required to grow, and therefore be renewed, on a continual basis as the business scales. The Company and its
management are therefore focused on continuing to monitor, forecast and discuss with lenders and investors, both
existing and potential. Both the latest senior and junior debt facilities are aligned to the growth plans of the business
and have been agreed in accordance with the terms being more favourable compared to the previous facilities and
therefore, allowing the business to continue to scale.
The directors have prepared detailed cash flow forecasts for the Company, for a period of at least 12 months from the
date of approval of these financial statements, the going concern period, which indicate that, considering a severe but
plausible downside scenario including a reduction in volume of invoices funded by 30% for 12 months and cost
mitigations adopted, including headcount freezes, the Company will have sufficient funds to meet their obligations as
they fall due for that period.
All existing debt facilities have been renegotiated and extended until February 2029 to ensure the business has the
right sized debt portfolio to facilitate the next stage of growth. On this basis, the Directors are confident based on the
ongoing dialogue that there is sufficient interest amongst lenders to roll the transaction over for another term as their
intention is to grow the receivables book with Sonovate in the coming years.
The financial statements are prepared on a going concem basis and do not include any adjustments that would result
from the basis of preparation being inappropriate.
Post balance sheet events
In February 2026 the Company successfully completed the extension of its private warehouse transaction, bringing in
a new Senior noteholder and maintaining the Class B Noteholder. In addition, the maturity of the Parent Company’s
on-balance sheet revolving facility of £30 million with BCI Capital was also extended to February 2029.
Financial risk management
The Company’s activities expose it to a variety of financial risks: credit risk, liquidity risk and market risk (including
interest rate risk and currency risk).
Interest rate risk
The Company’s exposure to interest rate risk is minimal. While the Company’s financing is linked to underlying :
interest rate benchmarks, it has the ability to reprice and adjust its income based on a number of factors including
movement in underlying interest rates.
Currency risk
The Company transacts in USD, EUR, CHF, CAD, DKK, SEK, NOK and AUD although the vast majority of business
is in GBP. In cases where the Group funds customers in GBP, USD or EUR (Matched Currencies), the SPV is funded
in matching currencies which mitigates any potential currency risk. For the Non-matched Currencies, the Group draws
against the SPV’s GBP note and deals with the currency exposure by reserving a generous amount in cash to mitigate
this risk. However, with the debt tum standing at 40 days, the likely deviation in currency pairs in that term is small.

. 110 Taxation
Tax on the profit or loss for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the profit and loss
account except to the extent that it relates to items recognised directly in equity or other comprehensive income, in
which case it is recognised directly in equity or other comprehensive income.

Current tax is the expected tax payable or receivable on the taxable income or loss for the year, using tax rates enacted
or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date, and any adjustment to tax payable in respect of previous years.
Deferred tax is provided on timing differences which arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments
in periods different from those in which they are recognised in the financial statements. The following timing differences
are not provided for: differences between accumulated depreciation and tax allowances for the cost of a fixed asset ifand
when all conditions for retaining the tax allowances have been met. Deferred tax is not recognised on permanent
differences arising because certain types of income or expense are non-taxable or are disallowable for tax or because
certain tax charges or allowances are greater or smaller than the corresponding income or expense.

Deferred tax is measured at the tax rate that is expected to apply to the reversal of the related difference, using tax rates

enacted or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date. Deferred tax balances are not discounted.

Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that is it probable that they will

be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
