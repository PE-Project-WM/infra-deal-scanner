The directors present the strategic report for the period ended 2 January 2026.
Fair review of the business
The principal activity of the company is providing solutions for military and government organisations in the field of
encrypted tactical radio communications (CS) and precision approach radar (SAS), through the development,
manufacture, supply and field service support of a range of products.
Key performance indicators
In alignment with the budgetary process, the company establishes key performance indicators (KPIs) for critical
areas including accounts receivable and accounts payable, aimed at optimizing the management of free cash flow.
These KPIs are monitored monthly, with strategic interventions implemented as necessary to ensure financial
stability and operational efficiency. The company prioritizes prompt cash collections, underscoring its strategy to
maintain liquidity. Furthermore, the company upholds a policy of ethical treatment towards its suppliers, ensuring
payments are made promptly and not unduly delayed. This principled approach has enabled the company to
consistently meet the free cash flow targets delineated in the Annual Operating Plan and cash flow forecast for
future years.
For the period ended 2 January 2026, the Company reported turnover of £33.2m, representing a decrease of
£9.5m compared to the prior year (2024: £42.7m). This reduction is primarily attributable to the phasing of key
programmes and lower activity levels on certain contracts during the year (Bowman Support Contract Spares
orders, Leonardo Contract delays and European Repairs). Despite turnover decreasing by 22.3% to £33.2m (2024:
£42.7m), the Company delivered a strong operating profit of £7.9m (2024: £8.4m), representing a reduction of only
6.6% year-on-year. The resilience in operating profit was driven by a significant improvement in gross margin,
which increased from 31% to 39.3%, reflecting a favourable contract mix and continued focus on higher-value
activities. This stronger margin performance largely offset the impact of lower revenues and increased operating
costs. As a result, operating margin improved to 23.7% (2024: 19.7%), demonstrating the Company's ability to
maintain strong profitability despite reduced sales volumes. Profit after tax decreased to £8.0m (2024: £8.9m), with
the reduction arising from group relief. The Company's underlying profitability remained strong, supported by non-
operating factors. Overall, the Company has demonstrated resilience in maintaining robust profitability and
enhancing margin performance despite lower turnover, positioning it well for future growth as programme activity
evolves. Dividends paid out in 2025 were £0m (2024: £12.6m).

Principal risks and uncertainties
The company continued to experience challenging market conditions in Europe, including supply chain delays and
increases in material costs, whilst maintaining high-quality products and services for existing customers. The
impact of rising material costs is mitigated through pricing actions designed to offset cost increases and protect
margins. Supply chain risks are managed through proactive production and material planning to ensure availability
of components and minimise customer lead times. These risks are further mitigated through strong customer
relationships, high levels of customer care, and comprehensive after-sales support.
The defence industry is highly regulated, and the company has demonstrated compliance with UK and US
legislation in this area. Of note is the ongoing extensive training programs on the US International Traffic in Arms
Regulations (ITAR) and Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) legislative requirements. Increased pressure in terms
of compliance with ever increasing environmental and health and safety regulations is a risk managed principally by
the presence of specialised in-house advisers who work to ensure best practice. Our turnover is heavily dependent
on government defence expenditure and, in particular, funding allocated by the UK Ministry of Defence (MOD).
While the current Integrated Review and Defence Command Paper outline sustained investment in critical
communications and battlefield capabilities, there remains inherent uncertainty over the medium to long-term
budget position due to:
+ Macroeconomic pressures: Rising inflation, higher interest rates, and competing demands on public
finances could result in future defence budget reallocations or delays in programme funding.
« Policy changes: Changes in government or defence priorities may impact planned investments in tactical
communications programmes.
+ Foreign exchange volatility: As a significant proportion of components are sourced internationally,
fluctuations in GBP/USD rates may impact project margins unless fully hedged.
We have, however, multiyear commitments from the MOD which secure turnover for the next couple of years. The
MOD national defence budget increases create further opportunities for supply of regeneration and modemization
of equipment and services that the company can offer to the MOD.
In order to be successful, we must attract and retain key employees, and failure to do so could seriously harm the
brand. The ability to attract, develop, and retain skilled professionals is key to our success. The highly competitive
nature of the defence industry, along with the specialised skills required, presents a risk in terms of resource
availability and talent retention, especially in critical roles within engineering and project management.
The company could be negatively impacted by a security breach, through cyberattack, cyber intrusion or otherwise,
or other significant disruption of our 1T network and related systems. As a communications provider, and
particularly as a government contractor, we face heightened risk of security breaches or disruption from threats to
gain unauthorised access. Protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our data is critical to
maintaining trust with our clients and regulatory bodies. Investments in cybersecurity infrastructure and ongoing risk
assessments are essential to mitigate this risk. Although significant effort is made to maintain the security and
integrity of our IT systems, it is virtually impossible to entirely mitigate this risk.

Principal risk and uncertainties (continued)
In the period ended 2 January 2026, the company experienced stable trading conditions, with business volumes
returning to normal across both UK and European customer bases. Our commercial arrangements remain
primarily with UK-based customers; whilst European operations have continued but at a decreased rate.
Turnover with the rest of the world comprises of intercompany recharges and transfer pricing arrangements;

While global supply chain challenges persist in specific sectors, the company continues to actively monitor these
risks and has introduced mitigation strategies, including supplier diversification and forward planning on key
components.
Given the size and structure of the business, the directors have not delegated responsibility for monitoring
financial risk management to a subcommittee of the board. Instead, financial oversight is driven by the Finance
team, under the guidance of the directors, and aligned with policies set collaboratively with the corporate
management team of the parent company. These policies are enforced through robust internal reporting
processes, ensuring that finance managers maintain strong oversight of financial risks, including liquidity, credit,
and foreign exchange exposures.
The ongoing geopolitical events relating to the Ukraine and Middle Eastern conflicts do not present a risk to the
company at present.
Cash flow and liquidity risks .
The generation of cash is one of the company's key financial metrics and is closely monitored both locally, and by
our US parent company. The cash flow income from our commercial activities is sufficient to meet the going
concern of the company, and excess cash balances are routinely repatriated to our US parent in order to meet
corporate objectives.
Credit risk
The generation of cash is closely monitored both locally, and by our US parent company. The cash flow income
from our commercial activities is sufficient to meet the going concern of the company, and excess cash balances
are repatriated to our US parent on a ad hoc basis in order to meet corporate objectives.
Price and foreign exchange risk
The company does experience exposure to price risk including inflation and the impact of foreign exchange which
can affect the cost of performing fixed price contracts. The company manages this on a case by case basis. During
the period ended 2 January 2026, we continued working with the MOD to contract with them in split currency to
lower the foreign exchange risk on both parties.
Interest rate risk
The company has intercompany loans both receivable and payable, with interest rates applied at arm’s length, and
these are managed by the treasury team at parent company level. Therefore, the risk to the company is considered
to be low.

Decision making
The Company comprises of two distinct business units which report into different segments within L3Harris
Technologies Inc; Tactical Communications into the Communication Systems segment and Radar support into
the Space and Intelligence segment. Each segment is responsible for developing their own strategy, which is
communicated to employees via a strategic roadmap which outlines the current year priorities.
On an annual basis, the Company produces a strategic growth plan (SGP) which identifies key growth pursuits
across all segments and assesses the resourcing required to execute. The SGP determines a series of annual
financial targets which are measured and reviewed on a quarterly basis by the directors and senior leadership
team. A full assessment of all business risks and opportunities is undertaken as part of this review which considers
the business, our employees, stakeholders and the environment in order to focus on the impact of the decision
making.
All decision making is made in adherence to L3Harris Technologies corporate policy, and the procedures detailed
in L3H CS UK's Quality Management System (QMS).
Employees
The focus of the directors and senior leadership team is focused on the health and wellbeing of its employees. The
company maintains health and safety measures so that staff working on site can do so safely. A large proportion of
the staff population is now back to a normal routine (hybrid working) with productivity remaining high.
Employee engagement remains a priority for the company, and all managers have been tasked with developing
action pians which address any areas for improvement identified by their respective teams during the previous
employee engagement survey. The directors and senior leadership team regularly host employee all hands
meetings to communicate company performance, raise awareness and understanding of strategic direction and
provide any other relevant updates. In addition, a monthly operations newsletter is distributed to all staff
electronically. The company continues to give particular consideration to the mental health of its workforce by
hosting a Mental Health Wellness Week, which promoted positive messages about mental health and made a wide
range of resources and activities available to employees.
Fostering business relationships with suppliers, customers and other
The directors recognise the importance of building and maintaining strong, long-term relationships with customers
and suppliers as essential to the company's success. In 2025, key supply chain risks were reviewed and mitigated
through collaborative planning and ongoing relationship management.
The company continues to work closely with UK government and defense bodies to ensure high-quality service
delivery and regulatory compliance. The company strives to maintain close relationships with its customers,
suppliers and industry partners in order to stay agile in a fast-paced market.
The company considers themselves a collaborative organisation and have documented their approach with a view
to achieving formal certification to ISO 44001, Collaborative business relationship management systems. This will
enable us to leverage existing relationships with our customers who are already certified to ISO 44001.
The company's procurement and supply chain function work with its approved suppliers and their supply chains
to provide goods and services that meet its customers’ requirements. The company's supplier relationships are
often long-term due to the length of the product life cycles and our senior leaders, including Quality
Management, actively engaging with key suppliers in order to develop and maintain deeper relationships with
their counterparts. This is a key activity which helps us to proactively identify long lead time issues and supplies
at risk of obsolescence.
The community and the environment
The company regards its responsibility towards corporate sustainability as a growing priority, and commits to
contributing to L3Harris Technologies corporate 2026 targets of:

+ 60% reduction of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions over a baseline year of 2021

+ 40% Sourcing of L3Harris' total electricity from renewable sources

+ 20% reduction of water use over a baseline year of 2021; and

+ 10% reduction in solid waste from landfill over a baseline year of 2021.
