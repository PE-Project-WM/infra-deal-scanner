, Strategic report
Introduction
The directors present the Company Strategic Report for Toob Limited for the year ended 31 December 2025.
The Company has successfully invested in rolling out a full fibre broadband to 300,000 premises in the south of
England.
During 2025 the Company completed its transition to a consolidated in-house build capability, facilitating the infill of
network gaps as the opportunity arises and maintenance of its existing network. The Company has expanded its full
fibre broadband services under its ISP agreement with CityFibre scaling to become a national provider in the UK.
Network commercialisation remains the key focus for the Company as is evidenced by the significant growth in
customer connections both on its own network and that of its wholesale partner CityFibre.
The combination of these dynamics, supported by capital from funds managed by the Amber Infrastructure Group and
funds managed by Ares Management’s Infrastructure Debt Strategy, has resulted in positive EBITDA being achieved,
as planned, in the fourth quarter of 2025.
Looking forward to 2026 the Company will continue to leverage its network investment by acquiring customersto 8
drive toward being cashflow positive whilst expanding its national footprint on the CityFibre network.
Principal Activities
Toob Limited designs, builds, owns, sells and operates its own full fibre network and resells a third-party full fibre
network for the benefit of consumers and as appropriate, small businesses.
Business Review
Toob has a clear strategy and business plan to scale the Company as a full fibre network provider.
It has a very experienced and seasoned management team who have diligently managed the deployment of the Toob
Limited network in its chosen geographies and who continue to successfully commercialise the network investment
by acquiring and retaining connected customers.
The Company is supported by a mixture of equity and debt of up to £264. 15M to deliver its plan to create a sustainable
business focusing on the south of England and to take advantage of market consolidation if opportunities arise.
In its seventh full year of operation, 2025 has seen the Company concentrate on extending its customer acquisition
methodologies across its wider footprint. The Company has successfully leveraged its brand across the south of
England, adding incremental customers, business synergies and margin and has launched a nation-wide expansion
over the national CityFibre network.
The financial statements reflect the significant increase in revenues from the growth in connected customers as costs
and capital investment from the build programme reduce, partially offset by the necessary scaling of sales, marketing
and customer services capabilities to support the growing customer base.

Principal risks and uncertainties .
The directors identify, assess and manage principal risks and uncertainties that might directly affect the Company’s
strategy and operations. Risks generally arise from either external factors driven by the market in which the Company
operates or from internal factors driven by the operational aspects of the business.
Market
Changes in government policy can adversely affect the Company. Toob Limited works with industry groups such as
ISPA and INCA to engage with the government and Ofcom on key topics and consultations to ensure that the interests
of Toob Limited are properly and clearly represented.
The Company carefully and diligently reviews all new areas selected for potential build to understand the current and
possible future competitive landscape to identify circumstances where its economic prospects might be impacted.
Digital Disruption and Emerging Technology
Generative Al is a specific subfield of Al that focuses on creating new content and therefore represents both an :
opportunity and a risk for the Company. AI processes large amounts of data which gives rise to both privacy and cyber
security issues where AI has the potential to drive network attacks that exploit possible vulnerabilities.
Deepfake content and algorithms have the potential to create bias that influences opinion and decision making.
Generative AI tools have the potential to pose ethical, legal, and existential conflicts. The application of generative
AI tools in the workplace is therefore being evaluated by the government and the NCSC to assess its longer-term
impact.
The Company has a Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) policy advising employees on acceptable and prohibited uses
of Al.
Data and Cyber Security
Toob Limited as an infrastructure provider, is exposed to the ever-increasing risk of cyber-attacks. The Company is
continually enhancing its physical defences through application of latest patch updates, regular reviews and testing of
its perimeter defences through penetration testing supported by continuous network monitoring.
The Company maintains its Cyber Essentials and Cyber Essentials Plus qualifications together with renewal of its
1S022301 and ISO27001 accreditations.
The Company’s physical defences are supported by logical defences vested in robust business processes and training
to ensure employees understand and can respond to business continuity, data protection and security breaches and
phishing attacks.
Competition
Toob Limited competes for customers with other ISPs who either own or resell the wholesale networks of Openreach,
Virgin Media and CityFibre or compete as a standalone AltNet. The Company acquires customers from competitors
who either do not have access to a full fibre product or are unable to provide a suitably differentiated full fibre
proposition that is able to compete with the Toob Limited proposition. The Toob Limited network has very limited
overbuild with other AitNets which significantly reduces any competitive tensions in those geographies. The Company
continually reviews its products and services to ensure its offers meet the needs of its customers and remain attractive
to customers considering an alternative to their current provider.

Operations
Toob Limited maintains a practice of dual sourcing wherever possible for its key materials and equipment and ensures
that minimum inventory levels can mitigate potential supply chain risks. Service interruptions are a risk to brand and
reputation so the Company architects its network to be resilient at appropriate levels of consolidation in its core
network and its Points of Presence and responds promptly to minimise the effect of outages on its customers.
Outsourced Construction
The majority of new network deployment is now delivered by internal resources however where necessary Toob
Limited awards NEC (New Engineering Contract) contracts to construction partners to deliver any necessary civils
works. Toob Limited relies on their partners’ industry expertise and capability to deliver in line with agreed contractual
terms and within the UK legal and regulatory framework.
Climate
- Toob Limited recognises and actively manages the risks that climate change presents to the Company, including the
increased likelihood of extreme weather events such as floods, storms, and heatwaves.
Future Developments
The directors see no material change in the nature of the Company’s activities but expect to extend the service offering
with complimentary products to address the needs of different customer segments where appropriate and enter
wholesale agreements that will allow third party channels to resell the Toob Limited network under various retail
brands.
Financial Risk Management
The company is exposed to a variety of financial risks including credit risk, liquidity risk and interest rate risk. The
company adopts a risk management approach to review and manage these risks.
Liquidity
Cashflow is managed by ensuring there are sufficient funds available to support continuing operations and plans for
future growth. Toob Limited maintains a detailed rolling cashflow forecast to ensure there are adequate funds available
to meet commitments as they fall due. The Company has put in place appropriate equity and debt facilities that
underpin its funding requirements.
Interest
Recent funding rounds have increased the leverage of Toob Limited which exposes the Company to greater cost risk
from any increases in interest rates. The Company has put in place an interest rate cap to mitigate the exposure.
Credit risk
Exposure to credit risk is monitored on an ongoing basis. As most customers pay in advance on a monthly basis the
Company has the ability to withdraw services should a customer become overdue.

Financial key performance indicators
Financial performance is measured against a detailed annual budget and forecast. The Company finalises its
performance metrics on a monthly basis for the senior management and directors to monitor and manage the operations
of the business.
Operating loss for 2025 of £17,828k (2024: £23,509) has decreased year on year by £5,681k (2024: £4,022k), driven

future lease payment within ROUA under IFRS 16 of £713k (2024: £816k). This capital expenditure reflects the
ongoing investment in the reduced network deployment programme across the Company’s selected geographies and
connecting new customers to the Toob Limited network.

Going Concern
Notwithstanding the net liabilities of £207,237k as at 31 December 2025 (2024: £150,605k) , a loss for the year then
ended of £56,632k (2024: £52,825k) and operating cash outflows for the year of £7,395k (2024: £10,380k), the
financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis which the directors consider to be appropriate for
the following reasons.
As at 31 December 2025 the business had £964k cash available and as at 31 March 2026 £1,973k.
The directors have prepared cash flow forecasts for a period of 12 months from the date of approval of these financial
statements (‘base case model’) which indicate that, taking account of reasonably possible downsides, the Company
will have sufficient funds, through the availability of debt funding from funds managed by Ares Management’s
Infrastructure Debt Strategy entered into on 16 May 2023 as disclosed in note 13 that sets out the repayment terms on
the loan and further equity from Funds managed by the Amber Infrastructure Group to supplement its initial
investment. On 22nd January 2026 an extension to the existing loan was agreed for an additional facility of up to
£9.75m detailed in note 13.
The debt facility is subject to five covenant requirements; total net debt to equity ratio, net debt per premise passed,
net debt per premise connected, total premises passed and average revenue per customer. Cashflow forecasts under
the base case model produce debt and revenues to satisfy the covenants for at least the next 12 months.
Furthermore, the directors have carefully reviewed the prospects of the Company and its future cash flows for potential
adverse economic impacts. The directors have prepared a severe but plausible downside model (‘downside model’)
using the following assumptions: reduced customer take up versus base case, and sustained price competition forcing
pricing to be held flat for an extended period.
This downside model forecasts that the cash available to the business from its facilities will also satisfy the covenant
requirements for at least the next 12 months. In making this assessment management have considered what mitigating
action would be required should a forecasted breach be present. This includes utilising the additional facility available
from National Digital Infrastructure Fund I PF LP.
In modelling the above, the Company has not included additional cost saving measures that are in the control of the
directors which could be taken to further reduce costs or cash outflows such as reduction in capital expenditure and
payroll costs.
Consequently, the directors are confident that the Company will have sufficient funds to continue to meet its liabilities
as they fall due for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the financial statements and therefore have prepared
the financial statements on a going concern basis. Accordingly, these financial statements do not include any
adjustments to the carrying amount or classification of assets and liabilities that would result if the Company was
unable to continue as a going concern.

1.10 Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and any deferred tax.
The current tax charge is based on the taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from net profit as reported in
the Statement of Financia! Position because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable or deductible in
other years and it further excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The Company’s liability for current tax
is calculated using tax rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end date.
Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable on differences between the carrying amounts of assets
. and liabilities in the financial statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the computation of taxable profit
and is accounted for using the liability method. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all taxable
temporary differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that taxable profits will
be available against which deductible temporary differences can be utilised. Such assets and liabilities are not
recognised if the temporary difference arises from goodwill or from the initial recognition (other than in a business
- _ combination) of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects neither the taxable profit nor the accounting wee
Deferred tax liabilities are recognised for taxable temporary differences arising on investments in subsidiaries, except
where the Company is able to control the reversal of the temporary difference and it is probable that the temporary
difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future.
Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period when the liability is settled, or the
asset realised. Deferred tax is charged or credited to the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income,
except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to other comprehensive income, in which case the deferred
tax is also dealt with in other comprehensive income.
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset when there is a legally enforceable right to set off current tax assets against
current tax liabilities and when they relate to income taxes levied by the same taxation authority and the Company
intends to settle on a net basis.
