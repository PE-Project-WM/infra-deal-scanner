Introduction

The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025.

The principal activity of Veolia Energy & Utility Services UK Limited ("the Company") is the provision of energy,

utilities and technical facilities management services to a wide range of clients. The Company's services include

Contract Energy Management ("CEM") and the design, installation, maintenance, operation and financing of

energy services plant and Combined Heat and Power ("CHP") facilities.

The Company is registered and domiciled in the United Kingdom.

The Company is part of the Veolia Group, ("the Group") which is defined as all companies under the control of

the ultimate parent company, Veolia Environnement S.A., headquartered in Paris. The Company is also a

member of the "UK&I group", a division of the Group, based in the UK and Ireland and involved in the provision

of waste, water and energy services, and directly or indirectly under the ownership of Veolia UK Limited ("VUK").
: Business review

Revenue in 2025 decreased to £129,754k (2024: £138,431k). Whilst operational contract activity remained

stable, the Company was impacted by the timing of works across its design and build projects.

Adjusted EBITDA also decreased to £7,647k in the year (2024: £12,774k). This fall was largely driven by

updates to parental management fees profile along with the impact from the aforementioned lower reduction in

revenue.

Energy UK Limited.

Section 172(1) statement
The Directors have ensured compliance with their duties under s.172(1) of the Companies Act 2006 in relation to
the Company and the various stakeholders of both the Company and its subsidiary. These stakeholders include
the employees, main customers, suppliers, local communities and relevant regulatory authorities.
As a leader in ecological transformation, the Group designs and delivers meaningful environmental solutions,
with and for its stakeholders. The Group’s strategic programme, GreenUp 2024-2027 focuses on accelerating
the deployment of affordable, replicable solutions that depollute, decarbonise and regenerate resources. The
UK&l group tracks and reports annual progress on its sustainability objectives through regular sustainability
reporting. Its Sustainability Strategy sets out commitments to achieve net zero emissions by 2050, while also
advancing wider goals across social responsibility, governance practices, and environmental protection. The
Directors have ensured that the strategy, values and policies of the UK&l group have been adopted by the
Company, along with the Group's purpose of ecological transformation. In further support of the Streamlined
Energy & Carbon reporting within the Directors Report, the Financial Statements of the main UK holding
company, Veolia UK Limited, include within its Strategic Report, a Non-Financial and Sustainability Information
Statement, providing further information on the governance framework for climate-related risk and opportunities,
climate scenarios, metrics and targets along with the aforementioned net zero commitments.
The nature of the UK&l group’s activities means that many of the services it provides are essential to everyday
life. Driving positive economic, environmental and social outcomes remains integral to the operations of the
business, working alongside the public and private sectors as well as local communities. This involves delivering
social value in the communities within which it operates, and through the commitment to achieving net zero,
driving circularity and supporting nature recovery.
Engagement with the stakeholders of the Company and its subsidiary has informed the way in which the
Directors have discharged their duties and addressed the principal risks and uncertainties detailed below. Where
individual Directors are not directly involved in the processes described below, regular feedback and discussions
are held in the relevant management teams.
The Directors recognise that the Company's and its subsidiary's long-term success is predicated on the
commitment of the employees. Through the UK&l group, employees are provided with relevant training using
both in-house and external providers. Employees are given access to UK&I group information via various
channels, including the intranet, webinars, Veolia TV, team meetings and focus groups. Feedback from the
annual employee ‘Voice of Resourcers’ survey is disaggregated by business line and function and reviewed by
the UK&l Executive Committee. Information is benchmarked internally as well as against peer companies. Action
plans to address key themes are formalised and monitored throughout the year. The Company also engages
with trade union representatives on site and via regular Joint Trade Union Forum meetings.
The Company and its subsidiary have regular engagement with their main customers, including through
meetings and the provision of financial, operational and environmental reports, as required. For the wider UK&l
group, customer relationships are managed as follows:
e By regular, often monthly meetings, together with quarterly or annual review processes where service
obligations are assessed against contract targets along with the provision of technical advice as required;
e Via the Veolia Customer Hub supported, where necessary, by dedicated account managers;
e Through feedback from Customers who are approached every year as part of the Net Promoter Score
survey to provide insight into their experiences on the Company's services.

The UK&! group’s Procurement Team is responsible for sourcing providers of business-critical goods and
services as well as managing the associated supply chain risk. Category Managers oversee the categories

Principal risks and uncertainties ;
The Directors consider the principal risks and uncertainties to which the Company is exposed are intrinsic to the
business it operates and its ability to provide the relevant contracted services.
The following highlights some of the Company's principal risks, but is not intended to be an extensive analysis of
all risks affecting the business. Some risks may be unknown to the Company and other risks, currently regarded
as immaterial, could turn out to be material. All of them have a potential to impact the Company's business,
revenue, profits, assets, liquidity and capital resources. The Company manages the majority of these risks by
utilising the resources and processes developed and operated within the UK&l group as a whole.
Contractual risk
The Company's business, along with that of its subsidiary, is based on the performance of contracts put in place
with its customers. Therefore, the business may be adversely affected by a failure to perform in accordance with
the terms of the relevant contract. The UK&I group has a formal project authorisation and review procedure
which aims to ensure legal, operational, technical and commercial risks are properly considered before the initial
exchange of contracts, or significant contract alterations, with its customers.
Risks associated with meeting the terms of a contract are mitigated through ongoing day-to-day management of
the relevant operations, including tracking performance against budget and targets identified in the contract.
Performance is reviewed monthly by operational management and members of the Executive Committee.
Contracts identified to be at risk are subject to specific initiatives to improve performance.

Business continuity
The Company, along with its subsidiary, maintains a business continuity plan for each area of its operations so,
should the unexpected happen, there is a predetermined plan in place to allow the business to recover and to
continue servicing its customers with as little disruption as possible.
The UK&l group is accredited with ISO 22301 (Business Continuity Management) and is benefitting from the
programme of enhancements to the business continuity management system, which commenced in 2024 and
are now fully embedded. These enhancements include sharing of site infrastructure within the UK&l group, the
use of external suppliers who specialise in disaster recovery scenarios and leveraging other Group relationships.
IT continuity planning is closely aligned with the UK&l business continuity management system and forms a key
component of its overall resilience strategy. The UK&I group's continuity plans are linked to recovery point and
recovery time objectives and are regularly tested and subject to programmes for continuous service
improvement. These plans cover critical IT systems and infrastructure, ensuring that essential services can be
restored within defined timeframes in the event of a disruption.
The UK&l group recognises the critical importance of managing cyber risks effectively. Aligned with the NIST
Cybersecurity Framework's six functions — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover, and now, Govern — the
UK&l group conducts regular risk assessments across its digital estate. These assessments evaluate the
robustness of the UK&l group's cybersecurity controls, encompassing technical, operational, and managerial
aspects, prioritising confidentiality, integrity and availability in the face of potential cyber threats.
The UK&lI risk management approach considers a range of key areas, including:
e Vulnerability and patch management;
e Identity and access management;
e Network security (including perimeter defences and segmentation);
« Security monitoring, threat detection, and incident response;
e Data security and protection (including backup and recovery);
e = Third-party risk management and supply chain security; and
e Cybersecurity awareness training and education for staff. :
Key findings from these assessments inform the UK&l cybersecurity strategy and continuous improvement plan,
ensuring that appropriate and timely corrective actions are prioritised. This proactive approach strengthens the
UK&l group's overall cybersecurity posture and complements the Group's broader risk management framework.
Health and safety, quality and environment
The UK&l group adopts a proactive risk approach to health, safety and the wellbeing of its customers and
colleagues. Staff are trained in accordance with recognised trade body standards, giving confidence that advice
is both credible and current. This is guided by the suite of Veolia Minimum Requirements ("VMR") documents
that go beyond the recognised legislative expectations for keeping people safe, protecting the environment, as
well as providing high standards of service to customers. Role-specific training and activity instructions for
operational people are delivered as part of the VMR toolkits.

Financial risks :
It is the Company's objective to manage its financial risks so as to minimise the adverse effects of fluctuations in
the financial markets on its profits and cash flows. The Company utilises the resources of the UK&I group to
implement risk controls and loss mitigation plans to manage exposure to these risks. Policies for managing
these risks are summarised below:
° Credit risk
The Company and its subsidiary are exposed to counterparty risk in various areas of operating and treasury
activities.
The Company participates in Group-centralised treasury arrangements and therefore shares banking and
intercompany loan arrangements with the Group, VUK and other UK members of the UK&I group, which are
also provided with support from VUK. As set out in the Going Concern section (page 7), management has
. assessed the credit risk of the Group and considers that it is well placed to provide support and facilitate the
repayment of any debts as they fall due.
The Company has a large number of long-term and short-term contracts with well-established customers,
including local authorities. For this risk to result in significant financial loss to the Company, it would require a
number of customers to fail to honour their contractual obligations. Given the nature of the Company's
activities and its broad customer base, the likelihood of significant financial loss is considered low.
The UK&i group has a specific credit management team as well as procedures in place to monitor and
recover customer and intercompany debt. The carrying amount of trade and other debtors, and cash and
cash equivalents represent the Company's maximum exposure to credit risk.
* Interest risk
The Company lends funds via VUK, at floating rates of interest, priced according to a GBP index based on
Sterling Overnight Interbank Average Rates ("SONIA"). Changes in the Bank of England base rate may
therefore impact interest charges and income.
° Liquidity risk
The Company, along with other entities within the UK&I group, is party to cash pooling arrangements with
VUK, whereby each member deposits cash excesses and may borrow money in the form of short-term
advances. Amounts are swept on a daily basis and held on overnight deposit with the Group. At the year end
VUK was in a net surplus position. VUK has an additional overdraft facility with the Group which remains
unutilised. Cash forecasts are regularly prepared and submitted to the Group for review to ensure that the
liquidity profile of the UK&I group is actively monitored. :
The Group has confirmed that the UK&I group will continue to have access to these balances as required for
their activities. As outlined under Going Concern (page 8), the Directors of the Company have concluded
that, if required, the Group would be able to provide financial support to VUK, which in turn would be able to
provide financial support to the Company for the periods stipulated.

Net zero target

2. Reduce process emissions;
3. Capture and offset remaining GHG emissions;

6. Continue to develop low carbon solutions for customers.

The Company recognises right-of-use assets at the commencement date of the lease (i.e. the date
the underlying asset is available for use). The asset's initial valuation is based on the discounted
actual value of future rents paid in exchange of the right to use the asset over the lease term. The
lease term includes the non-cancellable period of the lease, together with periods covered by an
option to extend the lease when it is reasonably certain that the Company will exercise the option.
The rents are fixed or are considered fixed in substance and may include rents which fluctuate in line
with an index or rate. Right-of-use assets are subsequently measured at cost, less any accumulated
depreciation and impairment losses, and adjusted for any remeasurement of lease liabilities. The
cost of right-of-use assets includes the amount of lease liabilities recognised, initial direct costs
incurred, and lease payments made at or before the commencement date less any lease incentives
received. Right-of-use assets are depreciated over the shorter period of lease term and useful life of
the right-of-use asset. If a lease transfers ownership of the underlying asset or the cost of the right-of-
use asset reflects that there is an expectation to exercise a purchase option, the related right-of-use
asset is depreciated over the useful life of the underlying asset. The depreciation starts at the
commencement date of the lease.
The Company analyses each lease individually to determine its term and, in the absence of renewal
and/or early termination options that are reasonably certain to be exercised or not exercised, the
enforceable period is adopted. Where the lease obligation is associated with a customer contract,
which cannot be operated without the lease, the lease term is aligned with the customer contract end
date. If ownership of the leased asset transfers to the Group at the end of the lease term or the cost
reflects the exercise of a purchase option, depreciation is calculated using the estimated useful life of
the asset. :

2.15 Leased assets: the Company as lessor
Where assets leased to a third party give rights approximating to ownership (finance lease), the

A finance lease gives rise to two types of income: profit or loss equivalent to the profit or loss
resulting from outright sale of the asset being leased, at normal selling prices, reflecting any
applicable discounts, and finance income over the lease term.
