Directors' Report for the year ended 31 March 2025
The Directors present their report and the financial statements of Vodafone Ventures Limited (the Company’) for
the year ended 31 March 2025.
Incorporation
The Company was incorporated as a private company limited by shares on 8 June 2000 and registered in England
and Wales.
Principal activities
The principal activity of the Company is to hold investments in companies and other legal entities in the United
Kingdom and overseas, offering services or developing technologies, primarily for the mobile wireless industry and
to support Vodafone Group as a new generation connectivity and digital services provider.
Results and Dividends
The income statement is set out on page 11 of the financial statements. For the year ended 31 March 2025, there was
“* a profit after taxation of £158,223,000 (2024: loss after Taxation £23,372,000).
The operating profit for the year includes the management fee for the Company and the fair value adjustments on the Bee
investments held. AST Space Mobile Inc. (‘AST") is a quoted investment and hence it's fair market value (‘FMV’) is
determined by the quoted price on the Nasdaq stock exchange as at 31st March 2025. The FMV of AST has
increased in the year by £212,938,000 which includes FMV adjustments of investments of £131,486,000 and FMV
adjustments on the convertible securities of AST till the date of conversion of £81,452,000. The convertible
securities of AST were converted into Class A shares as on 10th March 2025. The other investments within the
Company's portfolio, VisionOSS Limited and Flybits Inc. has reduced by £600,000. Movements in investments are
also described in note 9.
The Directors do not recommend the payment of a dividend for the year ended 31 March 2025 (2024: £nil).

Principal risks and uncertainties
The financial risks the Directors consider relevant to the Company include currency risk, credit risk and liquidity
risk. The key risk facing the Company in relation to its investment activities is that existing investments may fall in
value. There is also a dependency on investee companies' performance, due to the limited control, in cases where we
are holding minority interests. The management is careful to monitor the trends within the industry and in preparing
the financial statements for the year, management has used assumptions in determining the fair values of its
investments which reflect the current market trends within these industries.
The Company follows the board approved policies of its parent Vodafone Group Plc, to manage these principal
financial risks. This includes the treasury function of the Vodafone Group, which provides a centralised treasury
service to the Company, and follows a framework of policies and guidelines authorised and reviewed annually by
the Vodafone Group Plc Board.
The Group's internal auditors review the internal control environment regularly. There has been no significant,
change during the financial year to the types of financial risks faced by the Company, or the Company's approach to
the management of those risks. Further details of the Group's risks and policies on financial risk management can be
found in the annual report and financial statements of Vodafone Group Plc for the year ended 31 March 2025.
The Company has also considered a number of specific risks that could impact the business over the going concern
period and have a noted relevant changes in the Groups principal risks. There are adverse changes in
macroeconomic conditions which has increased as a result of external factors, such as the conflicts in the Middle
East, the ongoing war in Ukraine and the uncertainty around global trade and tariff policies, with US and China
tensions resulting in a ban of high-risk vendors and a potential open conflict between China and Taiwan which could
impact product availability. These external factors could impact the future path of monetary and fiscal policies and
may affect economic activity. Linking also to the new emerging risk of adverse regulatory and policy environment
and regulatory measures and policies impacting the Group’s strategy, which could result in increased costs or create
a competitive disadvantage.
The Company continues to monitor its receivables’ position giving additional scrutiny of any balances within
countries affected by the Russian invasion of the Ukraine. There were no balances in either Russia, Ukraine or
Belarus. Russia and Belarus form part of the Group sanctions list. Any transactions or balances are monitored on a
half yearly basis through the Group’s certification process.
The Directors have considered all the above risks as part of the Company’s risk and going concern assessment. In
reaching its conclusion, the Directors also considered the findings of the work performed by the Group to support
the statement on the long-term viability of the Group. .
The business is set to continue to trade in the above business areas with no major changes in the outlook for the
Company’s specific principal business risks within the going concern period of 12 months from the date of signing
of the financial statements.

Explanation as to what extent the audit was considered capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud
Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures in
line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect irregularities, including fraud. The risk of not detecting a
material misstatement due to fraud is higher than the risk of not detecting one resulting from error, as fraud may

: involve deliberate concealment by, for example, forgery or intentional misrepresentations, or through collusion. The
extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud is detailed below. However,
the primary responsibility for the prevention and detection of fraud rests with both those charged with governance of
the entity and management.

Our approach was as follows:

+ We obtained an understanding of the legal and regulatory frameworks that are applicable to the company and
determined that the most significant are relate to the reporting framework (FRS101 and UK Companies Act
2006), the relevant tax compliance regulations in the United Kingdom and the General Data Protection
Regulation (GDPR).

« We understood how Vodafone Ventures Limited is complying with those frameworks by making inquiries of
management and those responsible for legal and compliance procedures to understand how the Company
maintains and communicates its policies and procedures in these areas, and to understand the culture and
whether there is a strong emphasis placed on fraud prevention, which may reduce opportunities for fraud to take
place as well as fraud deterrence. We corroborated our enquiries through our inspection of Board minutes,
review of any correspondence with relevant regulatory bodies, for which there were none, and made

_ consideration of the results of our audit procedures performed to either corroborate or provide contrary evidence

which was then followed up.

+ We assessed the susceptibility of the Company’s financial statements to material misstatement, including how
fraud might occur through internal team discussions and enquiry of management and those charged with
governance. Through these procedures, we identified a fraud risk relating to the valuation of investments due to
the significance and magnitude of the carrying value of the assets being assessed and the level of management
judgement required in the assumptions impacting the fair value assessment. We gained an understanding of the

, controls through a walkthrough of the process management has in place to estimate the fair value of investments.
We also performed a comparison, where applicable, of the multiples implied by management to the earnings
multiples of publicly-listed comparable companies and considered any contrary evidence identified in the course
of our procedures. We calculated a value range for the assets being assessed and concluded that management’s
valuations fal! within our estimated range.

+ Based on this understanding we designed our audit procedures to identify noncompliance with such laws and
regulations. Our procedures involved review of board minutes, testing of manual journals identified by specific
criteria, enquiries with management, those responsible for legal and compliance procedures and the company
secretary.

Deferred tax is recognised on temporary differences arising between the tax bases of assets and liabilities and their

2.7 Financial assets
Receivables
The Company classifies its financial assets in the category of receivables. Receivables are non-derivative financial

with the objective of collecting the contractual cash flows and therefore measures them subsequently at amortised
cost using the effective interest method.
The Company applies the IFRS 9 simplified approach to measuring expected credit losses, which uses a lifetime
expected loss allowance for all trade receivables and contract assets.
To measure the expected credit losses, trade receivables and contract assets are grouped based on shared credit risk
characteristics and the days past due. The contract assets relate to unbilled services and have substantially the same
risk characteristics as the trade receivables for the same types of contracts. The Company has therefore concluded
that the expected loss rates for trade receivables are a reasonable approximation of the loss rates for the contract
assets.

estimates are required to be made in respect of highly uncertain matters including management's expectations of the

performance, changes in technology and operational and financial cash flows and the marketability of the shares.

available, valuation reports from third party authors have been sourced and used to support the judgements over the
