at Business review ’
- : The Directors present the strategic report of the Company for the year ended 31 March 2025. The Directors are

. pleased with the Company's performance during the year, which has seen continued strong growth driven by
new business through customer growth.
The principal activities of the Company are to provide hosted telephony services to the Primary Care market in
the UK and provide its specialist billing services. The Company's strategic objectives continue to focus on
growing its product offering, Surgery Connect, to the Primary Care market.
This market will remain the key focus for the business, with improving patient access to their General Practitioner
(GP) and eliminating the 8am rush to obtain a GP appointment, a fundamental step to be achieved as the board
of Directors believe that there is a considerable scope to add to the product offering and increase the value of
each customer.
New procurement processes were introduced by the NHS in 2023 to regulate the purchase of “advanced cloud
based telephony systems” such as Surgery Connect within Primary Care. GPs are now mandated in their
contracts to only buy systems approved by the NHS. Almost all of the company's new business is now won
under these procurement processes. :
The Company continues to invest in staff, systems and product offering, to fuel the growth of the business and
the board of Directors have continued the capital investment programme in the Company's data centres in order
to ensure continual improvement in impoving the safety, resilience and security of the infrastructure and coping
with a substantial increase in call volumes.
The Company continued to provide specialist billing services during the year. The board of Directors however,
made the decision to divest this business post year end into a seperate legal entity within the Southern
Communications Group in order for the Company to focus on the Primary Care market.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Company are its reliance on a single market - the UK Primary
Care market where only approved providers are permitted. Loss of approval is therefore a fundamental risk to
the future growth and recurring revenues of the Company.
The Company continues to be profitable and to expand providing great financial resources to mitigate
operational risks for example by improving the safety, security and resilience of the data centres.
The Company's revenues and operations are entirely UK based, and are therefore subject to the fortunes of the -
UK economy and in particular, the levels of fundinf the government provides to the NHS Primary Care sector.
The Directors have not identified any specific risks associated with the current economic situation for its
customer base, but will continue to carefully monitor the situation.

Business review of trading and results

es Revenue in the year increased by 41%, predominantly due to the growth in the Companies own hosted platform,

Surgery Connect being established as the market leader, as discussed in the business review.

new customers. In addition, where the Company is procuring equipment and services from other suppliers, they
have noticed the impact of rising costs across the industry.
Administrative expenses have increased by £2.9m year on year, and this is predominantly due to the extra
resource required to manage and deliver the new contracts signed during the year. This is a mixture of increased
permanent staffing, temporary staff and engineering subcontractors. The Company continues to invest in its
market leading technology and services and capitalised development costs of £1.4m (2024: £1.2m). The ability
to utilise group resources also helps to keep administrative expenses as low as possible. These costs are tightly
monitored and controlled. -
The Company made a profit before tax of £8.7m (2024: £5.1m). The bridge between EBITDA of £11.2m (2024:
£7.0m) to the profit before tax of £8.7m (2024: £5.1m) is attributable to the increase in gross profit in the year,
offset by administrative costs including increase in staff costs of £0.3m. (Refer to note 6 and note 8)
Cash decreased year on year by £1.8m to £0.2m (2024: £2.0m) as a result of cash sweeps from the Group. The
Company has seen significant decline in trade debtors (£6.2m decrease) and a drop in deferred income in the
year (£3.6m decrease). This was due to exceptional activity that took place around the March 2024 reporting
date due to the requirement from the NHS to contract with and invoice new GP surgeries before the end of
March 2024. The roll out of installation for these surgeries took place in the year to 31 March 2025 and therefore
as expected, both deferred income and trade debtor levels have dropped by March 2025.
Both tangible and intangible assets have grown in the year. Intangibles have grown as a result of capitalised
research and development of work completed towards the continued development of the Surgery Connect
platform. Tangible fixed assets have grown as a result of purchasing hardware to support the increased number
of customers being serviced by the Company.

Price risk, credit risk, liquidity risk and cash flow risk .

: Going concern ,
sy X-On Health Limited is a subsidiary of Saint Topco Limited (‘the Group’), which has prepared forecasts, ~
projections and expected cashflows for a period of at least 12 months from the approval of the financial
statements to 31 December 2026.
: The Group reported a positive EBITDA for the year of £46.2m (2024: £38.7m), with charges for amortisation of
£50.4m (2024: £44.4m) and interest payable of £52.1m (2024: £49.6m) contributing to a reported loss before
* taxation for the year of £73.0m (2024: £61.0m), with £11.5m (2024: £12.2m) net current liabilities.
As at 31 March 2025 the Group financing arrangements consisted of total committed senior loan facilities of
£365.6m, of which £42.7m for purchase of acquisitions remained undrawn and £57m of A preference shares
with no fixed redemption term. The Facility A1 of £40.0m is due to expire on the 05 April 2027 with the facility A2
and acquisition facilities, due to expire on 22 August 2027. The PIK facilities are due to expire on 25 February
2029. On 7th March 2025 an additional facility for £30.0 million was agreed for further purchase of acquisitions,
. this facility is due to expire on 22 August 2027 and bears interest of 7% plus SONIA, 5% will be payable bi-
annually and the remainder cumulatively accrues at 2% quarterly. During the year ENil was drawn down against
"this additional facility. .
The Group's forecasts and projections, which incorporate reasonably possible changes in trading performance ~
including an average decrease against budget of 1.4% - indicate that it will continue to operate within its available
facilities and remain compliant with covenant requirements through to 31 December 2026. These projections
also reflect an expected increase in EBITDA over the same period. In assessing the Group's ability to continue
as a going concern, the Directors have evaluated a range of financial scenarios, including a plausible downside
case and reverse stress testing of covenant compliance. The principal risks to going concern have been
_ identified as adverse impacts from the wider economic environment, particularly rising inflation in the UK, which
could negatively affect revenue and EBITDA growth, thereby posing a risk to covenant compliance.
Financial covenants set by lenders are cashflow cover, super senior net leverage and senior net leverage which
continue to be met and are anticipated to be met for the period to 31 December 2026. Stress testing resulting in
a covenant breach would require an 11.44% drop in EBITDA for the period to 31 December 2026 which is
considered implausible as it would require the business to stop making new sales, and for a high number of
customers to churn away.
Forecasts for the Group's future performance include continued compliance with these covenants. In a
constantly changing environment an agile approach has been taken, to facilitate our response to changes to
organisational risk in a timely and robust manner. This includes both regular and event prompted reviews of
risks, regular cash review and management, and staff working in a flexible manner to support the ongoing
success of the Group.
At the time of approving the financial statements, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Group
has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. Thus, the Directors
continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the financial statements.

The Group reported a positive EBITDA for the year of £46.2m (2024: £38.7m), with charges for

facilities of £365.6m, of which £42.7m for purchase of acquisitions remained undrawn and £57m of A

cumulatively accrues at 2% quarterly. During the year £Nil was drawn down against this additional
facility.
The Group's forecasts and projections, which incorporate reasonably possible changes in trading
performance — including an average decrease against budget of 1.4% - indicate that it will continue to
operate within its available facilities and remain compliant with covenant requirements through to 31
December 2026. These projections also reflect an expected increase in EBITDA over the same
period. In assessing the Group's ability to continue as a going concern, the Directors have evaluated
a range of financial scenarios, including a plausible downside case and reverse stress testing of
covenant compliance. The principal risks to going concern have been identified as adverse impacts
from the wider economic environment, particularly rising inflation in the UK, which could negatively
affect revenue and EBITDA growth, thereby posing a risk to covenant compliance.
Financial covenants set by lenders are cashflow cover, super senior net leverage and senior net
leverage which continue to be met and are anticipated to be met for the period to 31 December 2026.
Stress testing resulting in a covenant breach would require an 15% drop in EBITDA for the period to
31 December 2026 which is considered implausible as it would require the business to stop making
new sales, and for a high number of customers to churn away.
Forecasts for the Group's future performance include continued compliance with these covenants. In
a constantly changing environment an agile approach has been taken, to facilitate our response to
: changes to organisational risk in a timely and robust manner. This includes both regular and event
prompted reviews of risks, regular cash review and management, and staff working in a flexible
manner to support the ongoing success of the Group.
At the time of approving the financial statements, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that
the Group has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future.
’ Thus, the Directors continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the financial
statements.

. 2.15 Impairment of fixed assets .
Assets that are subject to depreciation or amortisation are assessed at each balance sheet date to
determine whether there is any indication that the assets are impaired. Where there is any indication
that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset (or cash-generating unit to which the
asset has been allocated) is tested for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised for the amount .
by which the asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the
higher of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs to sell and value in use. For the purposes of
assessing impairment, assets are grouped at the lowest levels for which there are separately
identifiable cash flows (CGUs). Non-financial assets that have been previously impaired are reviewed
at each balance sheet date to assess whether there is any indication that the impairment losses
recognised in prior periods may no longer exist or may have decreased.
