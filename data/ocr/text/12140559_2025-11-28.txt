VOLT TECHNOLOGIES HOLDINGS LIMITED
STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024

Introduction :
We are pleased to present the strategic report for Volt Technologies Holdings Limited ("Volt") for the year ended
31 December 2024.
This report accompanies the audited financial statements and provides an overview of the group's strategy,
performance, and future prospects.
Business review

“Volt is a provider of open banking and real-time payments solutions, enabling seamless, secure, and efficient
financial data sharing between banks, third-party providers, and consumers.
Volt’s core product offering continues to be Payment Initiation Services (PIS) or “Pay by Bank” services, which
generated record revenues for the business during the year.
Sales of our cash management services, where we offer virtual IBAN accounts for customers together with
treasury management features including but not limited to, domestic and international pay-ins and payouts, have
also continued to grow.
Complementing this combined payments and accounts offering, the business also expanded this offering locally
in Australia through connectivity to the New Payments Platform (NPP). Merchants can now access all Volt’s
services through one Global API.
Another development for the company was a reduced reliance on third-party processors for PIS services in
Europe. Volt’s own connectivity to European banks now covers >95% of the market.
During 2024, the company’s loss after tax increased mainly driven by higher administrative expenses as Volt
invested in scaling its operations, technology and regulatory capabilities. While these investments temporarily
increase losses, they are expected to underpin sustainable revenue growth, with the company targeting a move
towards profitability over the coming years as operating leverage improves and non-core expenditure is reduced.
As well as continuing to invest for the future, across productisation and expansion, Volt in addition accelerated its
path to profitability, which will be achieved through future revenue growth and cutting expenditure which is non-
core to the business strategy or not achieving the required return on investment (ROI).

Principal risks and uncertainties
The group faces several risks and uncertainties, including:
1. Financial risks: Including liquidity and currency volatility.
2. Regulatory risk: Exposure to evolving financial regulations and compliance obligations.
3. Cybersecurity threats: Risks of data breaches and IT security incidents.
4. Market competition: The fast-evolving fintech space demands continual product differentiation.
5. Macroeconomic risks: Volatility in global economic conditions, impacting transaction volumes.
6. Technology risk: Potential failures within Volt’s own systems or with third-party vendors.
Mitigation strategies have been adopted, including enhanced security frameworks, compliance reviews, and
diversified financial planning.
Each of these risks are addressed in further detail below:
Financial risks
This incorporates both liquidity risk and currency risk and is mitigated by Volt by:
Preparing and monitoring regular cash flow forecasts to review liquidity requirements and preparing detailed
plans covering the next 12-24 months of trading. These plans are reviewed by the senior leadership team and
approved at board level.

- Regulatory risk
As Volt operates in a highly regulated industry and is currently regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority
(FCA), there are a large amount of reporting and statutory obligations Volt must adhere to.
Volt has dedicated resources in place to ensure continued and ongoing compliance with these regulatory
requirements. These include but are not limited to governance requirements such as Anti-money laundering
(AML) checks and regular risk and compliance committee meetings as well as in depth reviews and compliance
checks during onboarding of new customers.
Volt's compliance and risk teams ensure they stay up to date with any changes to this regulatory environment to
pre-empt anything that might cause disruption to Volt's ecosystem.
Cybersecurity threats
Cybersecurity risks and potential breaches or losses of confidential information or IT systems and security are
mitigated through robust security measures and protocols.
By leveraging a technology stack and building out an information security team headed by a chief information
security officer, Volt is able to operate in a secure and safe IT environment.
Market competition
Given the fast growth and highly competitive nature of the sector Volt operates in, it is imperative Volt stays
ahead of the market by continual product innovation and investment back into its own suite of products, offerings
and markets.
As such, Volt continues to invest heavily in its own technology as well as looking ahead to expand both
geographically and in its product offering as will be case now with the EMI license.

Qualifying third-party indemnity provisions
The directors have the benefit of an indemnity which is a qualifying third-party indemnity provision as defined by

Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. The directors and management team
have prepared projected cash flow and financial information in excess of 12 months from the date of their
approval of these financial statements.
Management have additionally considered two stress scenarios whereby the going concern basis was tested
under a 25% reduction in revenue versus forecast and a 50% reduction in revenue versus forecast.
The group continues to be loss making through the going concern assessment period. However, it has adequate
resources to continue in operational existence for the period of at least 12 months from the date of approval of
these financial statements, without immediate recourse to further financing. While the directors plan to seek
future funding to support the group's ongoing operations beyond this 12-month period, the current assessment
confirms sufficient resources for the going concern period. Even in the two above stress scenarios the group |
held adequate resources to continue in operation for at least 12 months from the date of approval of these
financial statements.

2.18 Share-based payments
Where share options are awarded to employees, the fair value of the options at the date of grant is
charged to the consolidated profit and loss over the vesting period. Non-market vesting conditions
are taken into account by adjusting the number of equity instruments expected to vest at each
balance sheet date so that, ultimately, the cumulative amount recognised over the vesting period is
based on the number of options that eventually vest. Market vesting conditions are factored into the
fair value of the options granted. The cumulative expense is not adjusted for failure to achieve a
market vesting condition.
The fair value of the award also takes into account non-vesting conditions. These are either factors
beyond the control of either party (such as a target based on an index) or factors which are within the
control of one or other of the parties (such as the group keeping the scheme open or the employee
maintaining any contributions required by the scheme).
Where the terms and conditions of options are modified before they vest, the increase in the fair
value of the options, measured immediately before and after the modification, is also charged to the
consolidated profit and loss account over the remaining vesting period.
Where equity instruments are granted to persons other than employees, the consolidated profit and
loss is charged with fair value of goods and services received.
