Strategic Report

For the year ended 31 December 2024

The directors present their strategic reporl on Gateway Global Communications Limited (the “company’).

Principal activities, business review and future development

The principal activities of the company are the provision of network-based telecommunications services to both extemal

customers and related group companies. The company serves a diverse customer base, including multinational

enterprises, mobile network operators, media companies, and other group entities, providing network connectivity,
+ mobility, security, voice, and media sofutions.

Revenue for the year ended 31 December 2024 decreased by 58% to £93.9 million (2023: £222.3 million), primarily

due to the transfer of wholesale voice customer contracts to olher group companies with effect from 1 Apnl 2024,

following a wider group reorganisation.

As part of this reorganisation, undertaken to streamline operations and align business activities by segment, the

company novated its wholesale voice customer contracts to other group entities on 1 Aprit 2024. Following this transfer,

the company ceased generating revenue from wholesale voice activities, resulting in a significant reduction in revenue

for the year. The reorganisation also included the transfer of relevant network assets and intangible assets, together

with certain employees under TUPE arrangements.

Gross profit increased to £16.2 million (2023: £6.9 million). largely due to the increased sharing of network costs to

group companies. Consequently, the company reported a net profit of £6,6 million (2023: net loss of £14.2 million).

further supported by lower staff costs and reduced operating expenses.

Looking ahead, the company is focused on strategic growth by leveraging the group’s global infrastructure and

technological expertise. Key priorities include expanding managed services and cloud solutions, enhancing

cybersecurity offerings, developing innovative on-demand connectivity and media services, and selectively entering

high-growth markets. The company also aims to strengthen customer relationships. optimise operational efficiency, and

maintain sustainable profitability, positioning itself as a trusted partner for enterprise digitat transformation giobally.

Key performance indicators (KPis}

Given the straightforward nature of the business, the directors are of the opinion that detailed analysis using additional

key performance indicators (KPIs) is not necessary for an understanding of the company's development, performance,

or position, .

Principat risks and uncertainties

The principat risk to the business would be a catastrophic failure of network connections. This risk has been reduced

through the implementation of dual site architecture, The directors are committed to make continued investment in the

business ta minimise this risk.

Competitive pressures in the telecommunications market remain a continuing tisk, which could result in price reductions

and margin pressures. The company manages this risk by leveraging the group's global infrastructure and technological

expertise to deliver innovative and differentiated services, expanding managed, cloud, and cybersecurity solutions, and

providing vatue-added offerings to its key clients. Strong customer relationships, a focus on service quality, and selective

expansion into high-growth markets further support the company's ability to mitigate competitive pressures.

Financial risk management

The management of the business and the execution of the company’s strategy are subject to a number of risks. The

key financial risks affecting the company are managed at the Group level. These risks include credit, liquidity, and

operational financial risks.

Geopolitical risks

The company monitors global geopolitical risks which may impact the supply chain, customer demand, or cross-border

operations. While the company does not have direct operations in conflict zones, itis exposed to secondary effects

such as increased logistics costs and global market volatility. The board continues to monitor the situation and adapt its

risk mitigation strategies accordingly.

Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities

At inception of a contract, the company assesses whether a contract is, or contains, a lease. A contract is, or contains,
a lease if the contract conveys the right to contro! the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for
consideration. To assess whether a contraci conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset, the company
assess whether:

* — the contract involves the use of an identified asset — this may be specified explicitly or implicitly, and should
be physically distinct or represent substantially all of the capacity of a physically distinct asset. If the supplier
has a substantive substitution right, then the asset is not identified;

« the company has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from the use of the asset
throughout the period of use: and

* the company has the right to direct the use of the asset. The company has the right when it has the decision-
making rights that are most relevant to changing how and for what purpose the asset is used.

Leases are initially recognised as a right-of-use asset and corresponding liability at the date of which the leased asset
is available for use by the company. Each lease payment is allocated between the liability and finance cost. The finance
cost is charged to the comprehensive income statement over the lease period so as to produce a constant periodic rate
of interest on the remaining balance of the liability for each period. The right-of-use asset is depreciated over the shorter
of the asset's useful life and the lease term on a straight-line basis.

Assets leased by the company and the corresponding liabilities are initially measured on a present value basis. Lease
tiabilities include the net present value of the following lease payments:

« — fixed payments (including in-substance fixed payments), less any lease incentives receivable,

* variable lease payments that are based on an index or a rate; and

* payments of penalties for terminating the lease, if the lease term reflects the company, as a lessee,
exercising an option to terminate the lease.

The lease payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease, if that rate can be determined, or the
