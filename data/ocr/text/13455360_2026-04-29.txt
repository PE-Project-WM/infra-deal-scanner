Introduction ,
The Directors present their strategic report on the Group for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Principal activities
During the financial year the principal activities of the Group were the provision of advanced solutions and services for
electricity, water and gas utilities.
Review of operations
CrescoNet Group is an advanced utility solutions and services company, enabling utilities to deliver on the promise of a more
sustainable future through a unique offering of next-generation smart metering, Distributed Energy Resources (‘DER’)
———— Tt egistration-and management_and-many-other integrated digitaltechnologies.
The Group operates in competitive markets that are continuing to look for sustainable smart energy and water solutions. Our
business strategy relies upon the following key elements:
e Innovating and investing in optimising and modernising the core smart metering processes and applications in liberalised
and unbundled utility markets through our mdeX product portfolio;
e Innovating and investing in DER registration and flexibility management services through our deX product portfolio; and
e Innovating and investing in design of intelligent measurement, communication and control end points.
During the year, the Group continued to grow in number of services and meters under management in Australia and New
Zealand.
On December 16, 2024, the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware approved the Order and Final Decree
closing the Chapter 11 (Subchapter V) case of Smart Earth Technologies, LLC (“SET”), which had originally filed—along with
one affiliated debtor—voluntary petitions for relief on November 14, 2023 (note 10).

Competitive risks

The Group operates in a competitive market. Innovation is constant and superior products that may be released to the market
: places pricing pressures on our product and causes our product to be repositioned by the market. We manage this risk through

maintaining product development teams that are highly experienced and remain abreast of latest technological advances and

implications for our current and future products.

Economic and political climate

The current economic and political climate the Group operates within has a number of uncertainties and external market forces

in play. The Group’s expansion, however, and investments align with one of the most important global energy transitions, a

transition to Net Zero. Government regulation and decisions relating to Net Zero transition may have an impact on the Group.

The management monitors changes through regular engagement with regulators and other relevant governmental bodies in

the markets the Group operates in. Our team has detailed knowledge of the market requirements and participates in working

groups and committees to help shape the future. .

a

Strategies and expansion plans

The Group's strategies and expansion plans may result in unforeseen costs or require significant management attention. With

extensive experience and partnerships worldwide, the Group is well-positioned to continue driving innovation and integrated

solutions for utilities, enabling economic growth and fostering a smarter, safer and more sustainable future.

Compliance risks

The Group's operations are covered by best practices as well as government or industry regulations. The Group has committed

resources, which support maintaining compliance currently and in the future. Policies and procedures are in place, mandatory

employee training is carried out and formal management meetings are held to discuss risk and compliance.

Environmental risks

Despite its asset light strategy, the Group is subject to environmental regulation in respect of its technological activities. The

relevant authorities are kept updated and, to the best of the Directors’ knowledge and belief, all responsibilities under the

regulations have been discharged and there have been no breaches of any environmental regulation.

Data security and data privacy

The Group is subject to data and privacy regulations, particularly General Data Protection Regulation (‘(GDPR’) and Privacy

Acts. Failure to comply with legal or regulatory requirements relating to data security or data privacy in the course of

the Group's business activities can result in reputational damage, fines or other adverse consequences, including criminal

penalties and consequential litigation, adverse impact on the Group's financial results or unfavourable effects on the Group's

ability to do business.

Exposure to price, credit, liquidity and cash flow risk :

With long term agreements in place for the majority of the Group’s revenues, the Group is not exposed to significant price risk.

With the majority of Group revenues coming from one major customer, Intellihub, the Group is exposed to concentration and

credit risk on the existing Intellinub business, if this counterparty would default on its contractual obligations, it would impact

the Group. This is a long-term contract with high dependency of the counterparty on the Group delivering its services hence

the risk is low.

People

The Group's ultimate success would not be possible without its high calibre of people. The Directors and staff continue to be

focused, loyal and dedicated to providing high levels of customer service.

Equal opportunity

The Group is committed to an active equal opportunities policy. It is the Group's policy to promote an environment free from

discrimination, harassment and victimisation, where everyone receives equal treatment regardless of gender, colour, ethnic

or national origin, disability, age, marital status, sexual orientation or religion. Employment practices are applied which are

fair, equitable and consistent with the skills and abilities of the employees and the needs of the Group.

Employee consultation

The Group places considerable value on the involvement of its employees and has a practice of keeping them informed on

matters affecting them as employees and on the various factors affecting the performance of the Group, which is achieved

through formal and informal meetings.

Going concern

The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. Forecast cash flows of the Group approved by
management show positive cash flows into the future.

. The Directors have, at the time of approving the financial statements, a reasonable expectation that the Group has adequate
resources to continue in operational existence for the period of at least 12 months. Management have undertaken an
assessment and forecasted cashflows, which contemplates the continuity of normal business activities and the realisation of
assets and discharge of liabilities in the normal course of business, and have concluded that the Group can pay its debt as
and when they fall due.

The Group generates a significant portion of its revenue from a single related party customer, which creates an economic
dependency risk. This risk is mitigated by a master services agreement entered for a ten-year contractual term, with an option
for the customer to extend for a further 5 years. The agreements are reviewed and renegotiated regularly for pricing and
scoping of services changes.
The Directors therefore continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the financial statement.

was approximately $32.3 million (approximately 81% of consolidated revenue).

Fair value measurement
When an asset or liability, financial or non-financial, is measured at fair value for recognition or disclosure purposes, the fair
value is based on the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between
market participants at the measurement date; and assumes that the transaction will take place either: in the principal market;
or in the absence of a principal market, in the most advantageous market. .
Fair value is measured using the assumptions that market participants would use when pricing the asset or liability, assuming
they act in their economic best interests. For non-financial assets, the fair value measurement is based on its highest and best
use. Valuation techniques that are appropriate in the circumstances and for which sufficient data are available to measure fair
value, are used, maximising the use of relevant observable inputs and minimising the use of unobservable inputs.

On the acquisition of a business, the Group assesses the financial assets acquired and liabilities assumed for appropriate

The difference between the acquisition-date fair value of assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any non-controlling interest

in the acquiree and the fair value of the consideration transferred and the fair value of any pre-existing investment in the

Business combinations are initially accounted for on a provisional basis. The acquirer retrospectively adjusts the provisional

amounts recognised and also recognises additional assets or liabilities during the measurement period, based on new

information obtained about the facts and circumstances that existed at the acquisition-date. The measurement period ends

on either the earlier of (i) 12 months from the date of the acquisition or (ii) when the acquirer receives all the information

possible to determine fair value.

For business combinations of entities under common control, the Group has elected as an accounting policy choice to apply

the pooling of interest method. Under the pooling of interest method, the assets and liabilities of the transferring party which

are transferred to the resulting entity are reflected at their previous carrying amounts at the control transfer date. No

adjustments are made to reflect fair values, or recognise any new assets or liabilities, at the date of the combination that would

otherwise be done under the acquisition method. The only adjustments made will be to align accounting policies at the Group

level.

No goodwill or bargain gain will be recognised as a result business combinations from entities under common control, unless

previously carried by the transferred business, and the difference in consideration transferred and the acquired net assets will

be reflected within equity as a merger reserve. The Group has also made an accounting policy election to retain all previous

equity balances, including any share-based payment reserve balances, and therefore the pre-combination equity reserves

and history of assets and liabilities of the transferred business will be carried over at the control transfer date and reflected

accordingly within the Group's consolidated financial statements.
