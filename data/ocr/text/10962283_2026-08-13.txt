~ making enquiries of management as to where they considered there was susceptibility to fraud, their knowledge of
actual, suspected and alleged fraud; and
- considering the internal controls in place to mitigate risks of fraud and non-compliance with laws and regulations.
To address the risk of fraud through management bias and override of controls, we:
- performed analytical procedures to identify any unusual or unexpected relationships;

detecting one resulting from error, as fraud may involve deliberate concealment by, for example, forgery or intentional
misrepresentations, or through collusion.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES
Basis of preparation
These financial statements have been prepared in accordance with UK-adopted international accounting
standards and with those parts of the Companies Act 2006 applicable to companies reporting under IFRS. The
financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention.
The financial statements are prepared in euros, which is the functional currency of the company. Monetary
amounts in these financial statements are rounded to the nearest €.
Revenue recognition
Revenue.is measured under the five step model applied to contracts with customers in accordance with IFRS 15.
This model comprises of the identification of the contract, the identification of the performance obligations of the
contract, the establishment of the price, the allocation of the price to the performance obligations of the contract
and the recognition criteria of the revenues when the entity satisfies each of the performance obligations.
The company recognises revenues through the utilisation of the percentage of completion criterion, valued as per
the input method (i.e the "Cost to Cost" method). This method is currently considered to best reflect the transfer
to the client of contro! of works under construction,
The five steps built around the revenue recognition model under IFRS 15 as stated below:
Step 1 - Identification of the cofitract with the customer:
In general, the contract is easily identifiable through the tender contract. In addition to the guidelines for the
contract's identification, the standard establishes the rules to be followed to aggregate two or more contracts
concluded simultaneously or almost simultaneously with the same client (or with the client's related parties).
Step 2 - Identification of the performance obligations envisaged by the contract:
Performance obligations are represented by the project as a whole, as provided for in the contract, since the
different services are characterised by high interdependence and integration are aimed at transferring the project
in its entirety.
Step 3 - Determine the transaction price:
The transaction price is the fee amount expected to receive in exchange for the facility's transfer to the client in
accordance with the contract. In order to establish this value, it is necessary to assess the different factors
including; variable payments, significant financial components, payments made to the client and non-monetary
payments.
Step 4 - Allocation of the price to the individual performance obligation:
The same considerations and conclusions reached in step 2 above apply to the allocation of the price to the
individual performance obligations.
Step 5 - Recognition of revenues on the satisfaction of the performance obligation:
_._- —______ __ In the context of Ellipse Projects (UK) Limited, revenues are recognised _progressively_as the performance 9)
obligation is satisfied, since the client controls the works covered by the contract at the point when it is built.

short-term, highly-liquid investments with original maturities of three months or less (as at their date of

In the presentation of the Statement of Cash Flows, cash and cash equivalents also include bank overdrafts. Any

Taxation

Financial instruments
The company considered that, in view of the characteristics of the sector in which it operates, the new methods
which replaced LAS 39 in January 2018 relating to estimates of financial assets’ losses in value has not led to. a
significant impact on current measurement processes.
The conclusion is derived from the fact that useful factors for qualification of credit risk used previously LAS 39,
such as client risk, country risk and evaluation of significant macroeconomic information, are considered to
already be representative of measurement method based on expected risk. In fact, credit risk is represented by the
exposure to potential losses deriving from the non-compliance of obligations by buyers, who are almost entirely
connected to sovereign states, government bodies, overseas state entities or public administrations.
Therefore credit risk is essentially linked to country risk. It should also be noted that exposure to credit risk on
the basis of past-due trade receivables is hardly significant since receivables, predominantly from the government
bodies and the company secures payment from customers through an approved export finance facilities which are
guaranteed by public export credit agencies. As a result, the company has limited exposure to credit risk.
Going concern
These financial statements are prepared on the going concern basis. The directors have a reasonable expectation
that the company will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. The validity of this assumption
depends on the continuing support of the company's directors and shareholders. If the company were unable to
continue in existence for the foreseeable future, adjustments would be necessary to reduce the balance sheet
values of assets to their recoverable amounts and to reclassify fixed assets as current assets.
