Principal activities and review of business

Company’s growth and success are largely dependent on the growth of other Samsung business operations in
these areas. In addition, the company offers IT application platform solution services, such as logistics, retail and
. mobiles. The company has branch operations in Germany, Greece, Latvia, Romania, Portugal and Dubai.

Future development :

The Company aims to continue its principal activity, servicing IT needs of related Samsung Companies based in

UK, European mainland, CIS (Commonwealth of Independent States), Middle East and Africa. The company

also focuses on expanding its strategic business and supporting key projects of affiliates in an effort to grow its

external business and revenue. The company will continue developing and specialising in automation and hyper-

automation in corporate business to help both internal and external stakeholders. .

CRM: Customer Relationship Management is an online tool integrating multiple systems to collect and analyse

large and complex data, whilst also delivering targeted marketing based on customer life cycles. This has

: demonstrated an improvement in business reach and sales, sustaining customer loyalty and capturing feedback
across multiple channels. The Company continues to develop and enhance its CRM capabilities, with ongoing
efforts focused on acquiring new customers and expanding its presence across both EU and Asia.

SRM: Supplier Relationship Management is an integrated online platform that aggregates supplier data,

evaluates performance, and orchestrates procurement across the entire supplier life cycle. By offering real-time

insights, automated workflows and collaborative tools, SRM enables companies to qualify top tier suppliers,
identify cost saving opportunities whilst simultaneously strengthening supplier loyalty through performance
scorecards and joint innovation initiatives. Samsung SDS Co. Ltd, the Company's parent now leads as the
number one provider for SRM in the Korean market and the group has continued to secure a solid foothold in the

United States and Europe. Ongoing enhancements, including Al driven analytics as well as comprehensive

sustainability reporting are further reinforcing partner relationships and creating new opportunities for growth

The directors believe that these continued developments in both Al and enterprise technology will deliver positive

results for internal and external users. This will enable employees and customers to improve productivity in an

efficient, sustainable manner. ;

Stakeholders Engagement & Section 172 statement
Section 172(1) of the Companies Act 2006 specifies that each director must act in the way they consider, in good
faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, and
in doing so have regard (amongst other matters) to:
(a) the likely consequences of any decision in the long term,
(b) the interests of the company’s employees,
(c) the need to foster the company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others,
(d) the impact of the company’s operations on the community and the environment,
(e) the desirability of the company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct, and :
(f) the need to act fairly as between members of the company.
The company is committed to fair and equal practise towards its all stakeholders and this report sets out how we
have applied and complied with s.172 (1) under Companies Act 2006.
The Company sees strong stakeholder relationships as a key part of both its management approach and
governance structure. Directors are central to this and are expected to meet their legal obligations while applying
their judgement, relevant skills, and care to meet long term company goals whilst in their role.
They are also expected to consider long-term impacts when making decisions, keep employee interests in mind,
and maintain good relationships with suppliers and customers. New directors receive appropriate training, and
support is available for existing directors as needed.
The Company recognises its wider responsibility to the community and the environment, and aims to maintain
high standards in how its operations.
Shareholders
Company directors represent the shareholders on the Directors and work in the best interests of the company to
help achieve long-term goals. The company is focused on adding new solutions, skills, and services to grow
revenue and improve performance, while maintaining strong internal controls and ethical standards that support
the company’s culture. Regular performance updates are shared with shareholders through the usual
communication channels. The board makes decisions that benefit both shareholders and other stakeholders,
carefully considering risks and uncertainties (see the Directors’ Report for further details). The board also

Financial Risk Management
Financial instruments .
The Company's operations expose it to a variety of financial risks, including the effect of changes in price risk. It
trades predominantly with other group companies and finances most of its computer equipment through direct
purchase. As a result, the exposure to liquidity and interest rate risks is considered low. The Company has in
place a risk management program that seeks to limit the adverse effects on the financial performance of the
Company by monitoring levels of debt associated finance costs. The Company does not use derivative financial
instruments to manage interest rate costs, and therefore, no hedge accounting is applied.
Market risk and Credit risk
Market risk arises from adverse commercial pressures, including intensified global or local competition, subdued
market demand, customer acquisition challenges, or supply chain constraints preventing the sufficient delivery of
products and services.
The Company's exposure to these risks are mitigated by its commercial structure. The substantial majority of
sales continue to be generated from other Samsung-affiliated entities under secured, fixed-term supply
contracts. Because these intra-group customers are well-established, financially resilient, and globally respected,
the credit risk is considered low.
Foreign exchange risks
The Company is exposed to foreign exchange risk arising from exchange rate volatility. While the Company aims
to protect its operating margin from adverse currency movements, it does not currently deploy derivative hedging
instruments, nor are there active plans to adopt a hedging framework. Consequently, the Company maintains an
unhedged exposure to foreign exchange fluctuations. To mitigate this balance sheet exposure, the Company
deliberately holds the substantial majority of its held-to-maturity financial assets in Sterling (GBP).

Price risk
The Company is exposed to price risk primarily through its long-term IT outsourcing agreements, under which
service fees are fixed for extended durations. This structure limits the Company’s ability to adjust pricing
dynamically in response to shifting inflationary or operational cost pressures. Conversely, the Company has no
exposure to equity securities price risk, as it does not hold listed shares or any other equity-based investments.
Liquidity risk
The Company prioritises the maintenance of robust liquidity reserves to mitigate the risk of failing to settle its
financial liabilities as they fall due. To manage this exposure, management conducts rigorous cash flow
: forecasting and continuously monitors projected capital balances to ensure operational demands are comfortably
met. Additionally, the Company dynamically evaluates and regulates its working capital requirements,
maintaining an optimal portfolio of cash and cash equivalents to preserve long-term operational resilience.

The Company recognises financial instruments when it becomes a party to the contractual

Financial assets and financial liabilities are initially measured at fair value.
Financial assets .
Classification
The company classifies its financial assets in the following categories: at fair value through profit or
loss, and measured at amortised cost. The classification depends on the purpose for which the
financial assets were acquired. Management determines the classification of its financial assets at
initial recognition.
(a) Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss
Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss are financial assets held for trading. A financial
asset is classified in this category if acquired principally for the purpose of selling in the short term.
Assets in this category are classified as current assets if expected to be settled within 12 months,
otherwise they are classified as non-current investments.
(b) Financial assets subsequently measured at amortised cost
These financial assets are originally recognised at fair value. The assets have a business model of
being held to collect contractual cashflows and are non-derivative financial assets with fixed or
, determinable payments that are not quoted in an active market. At the end of the reporting period

subsequent to initial recognition, these financial assets are carried at amortised cost using EIR less
provision for impairment. Interest calculated using EIR is recognised in the income statement.
Recognition and measurement :
Regular way purchases and sales of financial assets are recognised on the trade date — the date on
which the company commits to purchase or sell the asset. Financial assets carried at fair value
through profit or loss are initially recognised at fair value, and transaction costs are expensed in the
income statement. Financial assets are derecognised when the rights to receive cash flows have
expired or have been transferred and the company has transferred substantially all risks and rewards
of ownership. Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss are subsequently carried at fair
value. Financial assets at amortised cost are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the
effective interest method.
Gains or losses arising from changes in the fair value of the ‘financial assets at fair value through
profit or loss’ category are presented in the income statement within interest income or expenses in
the period in which they arise. :
