STRATEGIC REPORT

FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

Introduction

. The Directors present their Strategic Report of the Company for the year ended 31 December 2025.

Principal activity ,

Ipsum Power (Private Networks) Limited are a specialist provider of high and low voltage (HV & LV) electrical

services, is a wholly owned subsidiary of Ipsum Utilities Limited.

The Company trades as part of Ipsum Group Limited “Ipsum” and uses smart technology and solutions to

ensure that critical utility and infrastructure services are delivered safely and efficiently ensuring a great

experience for our customers and their consumers. ;

Business review

Ipsum is a provider of critical infrastructure services and our strategy is to operate across the water,

infrastructure and power sectors. Safety remains the top priority for the business and we continue to drive

improvements in this area.

Throughout FY25 Ipsum has continued to grow through both organic and inorganic activities. Existing

relationships have been strengthened, and this has been demonstrated through the development of new work

streams and existing contracts being extended. Growth has also come from a number of acquisitions which have
taken place during the year. These strategic acquisitions have enabled Ipsum to further its geographical reach

and enhance its offering to customers. .

Ipsum has a goal to be a market leader in specialist utility and infrastructure services throughout the UK. Such :

essential services encompass testing, inspection, maintenance, upgrade and renewal of infrastructure assets.

The markets that Ipsum operate in range from long term programmes with public power and water companies,

maintenance projects for a range of private companies to large multi million-pound projects.

To support the growth, Ipsum continues to invest in technology, equipment and people. The people brought in

have specialist skills and knowledge which strengthen the existing team in place. Ipsum is continuously

improving the standard of the equipment and utilising the latest technology to improve the level of service to
customers.

Our strategic goals are:

e To build a well balanced multi-utility platform focused on providing essential and recurring services to both
regulated and private network customers;

e To continue to develop our platform so that it is well placed for long-term growth, both organically and
through further M&A activity;

e To continue to develop and deliver smart, digital solutions which underpin superior business performance,
relationships with our customers and delivery of outstanding services to our customers;

« To continue to develop existing long-term customer relationships and utilise our increased capability, scale
and geographical coverage to facilitate further growth and provide an improved offering to our customers;
and

« To ensure our business is well managed and operates to the highest Environmental, Social and Governance
standards. , :

Future prospects ;
The future outlook for Ipsum is extremely positive. The business has a strong balance sheet and financial ~
headroom in place which will facilitate future growth by acquisition and by organic means.
We were also successful in securing a number of strategic business development targets within both our power
and water businesses which helped grow our orderbook, both in terms of secured work and the pipeline of
opportunities, which provides a solid platform for 2026 and beyond.
The Directors and management will continue to maintain appropriate levels of governance and control to ensure
we have a sustainable business. Our end markets are resilient, and have excellent long-term prospects from
which we are well positioned to benefit.
Reliance on supply chain
There is a risk that any disruption to the supply chain would impact the ability of the business to deliver services
to its customers. The business mitigates this risk by establishing preferred supplier relationships (which are
generally not exclusive) and always seeking to ensure that a balanced and stable supply chain is maintained,
which helps to deliver best value to customers.
Customer satisfaction
During the year we continued to build on our existing long-term customer relationships through new multi-year
contracts, frameworks and maintenance renewals where we provided specialist contributions, domain expertise
and insights whilst applying additional added value and engineering solutions to drive out unnecessary costs and
improve customer outcomes.
We monitor, discuss, share and report our customer findings monthly to ensure we are delivering against our
customer requirements and our financial expectations. .
Employee engagement
Effective relationships with our employees are critical to the continued success of our business, and as such, this
is an area that we prioritise, along with safety and service. We invest heavily in training to ensure that our
employees have the right skills to fulfil their roles safely and to help develop careers with Ipsum.
We continued to develop our apprentice and graduate programmes, with many of the people on the programme
moving into the second and third year of employment. We are now platinum members of the “5% Club”, which
acknowledges employers which have more than 10% of the workforce on earn and learn schemes.
We also continued to enhance our communications to our employees, providing information via a number of
different media, for example issuing quarterly business updates.
We continue to be committed to being an equal opportunities employer and ensuring all employees are treated
equally and fairly and without discrimination because of age, disability, gender reassignment, race, religion or
belief, sex or gender, sexual orientation, marriage and civil partnership or pregnancy and maternity during our
recruitment processes and the employee lifecycle. We continue to make use of an e-learning platform
highlighting the key risk areas of our business, such as anti-bribery and corruption and information security.

Risk management
Ipsum continues to position itself within strategically focused sectors and markets where cyclic infrastructure
spend on asset maintenance and replacements are driven by statutory and regulatory needs.
We have an established governance framework which maintains a sound system of risk management and
internal controls ensuring the alignment of strategy and risk across both operational and non-operational
departments that allows us to identify short, medium and long-term risks associated with the sectors in which we
operate.
Principal risks and uncertainties
During the year we have continued to develop, enhance and maintain our positive health, safety, quality and
environmental performance by identifying and managing out our risks across all operational and non-operationa!
activities.
The business faces the normal financial risks around cash and liquidity. During the year Ipsum had facilities in
place to ensure appropriate funding is available. Regular cashflow forecasts are prepared, which are reviewed
each month by the Board. Delegated authorities are in place for all key areas, ensuring that decisions/approvals
are made at the appropriate level.
Many of our risks relate to operational delivery and commercial activities, and as such these have been
managed, monitored and actioned by a range of Ipsum stakeholders with a vested interest in people, safety and
contract out-performance through weekly and monthly reviews and Power BI reporting.
Health, safety and environment
A key principle of our business is that we uphold the highest standards of safety and it is an ingrained part of our
culture. We achieve this through ensuring that all people delivering on our behalf, whether employed directly or in
our supply chain are appropriately trained and have adequate tools and protection to undertake their roles. All
people are empowered to stop work if they believe in good faith there is a safety concern.
We continued to invest in our safety function, which has helped build a strong foundation for a mature and
collaborative approach to increasing visual leadership and support, coaching of safety stewardship along with
people and supply chain engagement whilst enhancing early planning, insightful monitoring and further
embedding a positive culture model of Safety, Health, Environment and Quality (“SHEQ”) ownership and
accountability through operational and non-operational management teams.
Key Performance Indicators
The principal key performance indicators ("KPIs") which are monitored by the Directors include rate of growth :
both organically and through acquisition activity, profitability and cash flow derived from operating activities.
These KPIs are discussed at monthly business reviews and board meetings. Alongside financial KPls the
Directors also monitor a variety of metrics such as the following:
- Safety performance such as accidents, incidents and near misses
- Asset utilisation
- Order book and pipeline
- Acquisition targets :

2.4 Revenue
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Company and the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes. The following criteria must also be met before revenue is recognised:

Revenue

Revenue from the supply of services represents the value of services provided under project
contracts and maintenance contracts to the extent that there is a right to receive consideration which
is recorded at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable.

Revenue represents amounts invoiced excluding value added tax, except in respect of contracting
activities and maintenance contracts, as described below:

Project contracts

Revenue from a contract to provide asset based services is recognised in the period in which the
services are provided in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all of the
following conditions are satisfied: the amount of revenue can be measured reliably; it is probable that
the Company will receive the consideration due under the contract; the stage of completion of the
contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured reliably; and the costs incurred and the
costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.

Maintenance contracts

Revenue from the supply ‘of services represents the value of services provided under service
maintenance contracts to the extent that there is a right to receive consideration which is recorded at
the fair value of the consideration received or receivable.

2.5 Leasingcommitments —.

Rental income from operating leases is credited to the Statement of Comprehensive Income on a

straight-line basis over the lease term.

Amounts paid and payable as an incentive to sign an operating lease are recognised as a reduction

to income over the lease term on a straight-line basis, unless another systematic basis is

representative of the time pattern over which the lessor's benefit from the leased asset is diminished.
