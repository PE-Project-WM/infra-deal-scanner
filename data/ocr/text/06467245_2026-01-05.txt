The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2025.
Principal Activity and Review of the Business
The principal activity of the company is a marketing technology agency specialising in providing loyalty and reward solutions
across a number of highly competitive, including regulated, industry sectors. We help some of the world’s biggest brands to
“create positive emotions” for their customers and colleagues on a multilingual and international scale.
In addition to providing the technology infrastructure, we also provide the ideas, planning and delivery capabilities to ensure
we delight our client’s customers whilst delivering results for our clients. Our innovative technology platforms help large
organisations enhance customer engagement and retention through bespoke loyalty programs, employee reward schemes,
and customer incentive platforms.
MLP’s commitment to innovation and customer satisfaction has been instrumental in building a strong client base, with the
business continuing to develop bespoke technology solutions tailored to each client’s needs. We also offer consultancy
services to assist clients in designing and implementing successful loyalty strategies.
Our work during and post pandemic to invest in our technology platforms and long-term strategy, as opposed to short-term
profitability has successfully changed the direction of the business. The added value in our technology platforms is increasing
recurring revenues, contract tenure, and average first year spend. This move to larger strategic projects has inevitably
increased the conversion period from initial discussions to campaign delivery; whilst this tempers short-term profitability the
business has a strong pipeline of opportunities. .
Key Achievements in the Financial Year Ended 31 March 2025:

+ Expanded client base: Secured 13 strategic new clients across Banking, Automotive, Electrical, and Trade sectors,
contributing £0.85m gross margin. Average first-year margins were £66k, demonstrating consistent improvement
over prior years (‘24 £74k, ‘23 £36k, ‘22 £19k).

+ Recurring revenue growth: Recurring and reoccurring revenues rose to 68% of gross margin {up from 60% in FY24
and 35% in FY23), reinforcing the strength of our long-term revenue model.

» Deferred revenue uplift: Year-end deferred campaign revenue reached £0.9m, £0.55m ahead of the prior year,
providing visibility and stability into future performance.

« Platform enhancement: Delivered improved scalability and user experience through enhanced platform capabilities.

Financial Performance .

« Revenue growth: Revenues reached £45.2m, an 81% increase on the prior year, driven in part by increased reward
issuance as clients invest in customer engagement.

+ Gross profit: We are transitioning to a revenue share model on rewards issuance, which moderates the blended
gross profit %. In real terms gross profit had increased by 37% to £7.0m (PY £5.1m).

« EBITDA: Delivered £1.4m, £0.9m ahead of prior year, reflecting scale benefits.

« Return to profitability: Net income of £0.1m marks a significant milestone, reversing the prior year’s £0.3m loss,
even as we continue to invest in workforce and technology for future growth.

Market and Competitive Environment
The loyalty and rewards market continues to evolve rapidly, driven by rising demand for personalised, data-driven solutions.
Businesses are increasingly investing in technology to deepen customer relationships and strengthen brand loyalty, making
this a dynamic and highly competitive sector. Digital transformation has accelerated adoption of loyalty technologies, with
organisations seeking platforms that deliver measurable engagement and long-term value.
MLP operates within a diverse competitive landscape that includes both niche providers and global multinationals. Our
differentiation lies in a personalised approach, deep client insight, and the ability to deliver flexible, scalable solutions tailored
to each organisation’s needs. Unlike larger competitors, we combine agility with innovation—enabling us to respond quickly to
market shifts and client demands. This adaptability, coupled with our loyalty expertise, provides a clear competitive edge and
positions MLP as a trusted partner for businesses seeking sustainable customer engagement strategies. .

Strategy and Future Outtook
MLP’s strategic objectives over the next five years are designed to consolidate our leadership in loyalty and reward solutions
while driving sustainable growth.
¢ Accelerate Loyalty+ adoption: Capitalise on sector growth by prioritising Loyalty+ services to strengthen recurring
revenue streams.
+ Deepen client relationships: Maintain a laser focus on delivering measurable results and nurturing relationships
among key clients.
« Expand client base: Target the acquisition of 8-10 strategic new clients annually, who wish to invest in their
customers and share MLP’s ethos of “creating: positive emotions.”
__ * Diversify markets: Extend reach into new industry sectors and territories to broaden our footprint.
« Integrate technology: Embed our platforms within client systems, enabling smoother campaign delivery and
enhanced customer experiences.
+ Scale internationally: Target global clients and strategic partnerships to accelerate growth.
« Lead and innovate: Establish recognition as a worldwide loyalty leader, ahead of the curve in technology and
consumer trends.
« Strengthen brand visibility: Continue investment in B2B marketing to expand market presence and reinforce brand
equity. .
« Drive efficiencies: Continue improving operational performance through innovative technology, automation, and
disciplined fiscal management.
The business continues to perform strongly, with forecast gross margin growth exceeding 10% in the next financial year.
Ongoing investment in internal teams and infrastructure ensures service levels remain high and client expectations are
exceeded,
Our pivot to the Loyalty+ model is delivering financial maturity, with infrastructure investments improving operating cost-to-
profitability ratios—benefits that will become increasingly evident in subsequent years.
Investments in both technology and talent have strengthened client relationships, enabling greater penetration and cross-sell
opportunities across departments, brands, and territories. With operations now spanning 23 countries, serviced from the UK,
MLP is well positioned to capture international expansion opportunities with existing clients.
Financial forecasts demonstrate continued growth, and the Directors remain confident in the company’s resilience and
long-term viability as a going concern.
Key Performance Indicators

+ Employee Engagement: MLP gained Great Places to Work certification during the year with 84% of MLP employees

+ Operational Excellence: Continued monitoring of ISO27001 performance indicators, regularly reviewed by

Principal Risk and Uncertainties
The company’s operations expose it to a range of market, strategic, financial, and operational risks. The Board of Directors
retains overall responsibility for risk management, with the Information Governance Group (IGG)—a_ senior
cross-departmental team—meeting weekly to monitor and review risks. Policies set by the IGG are implemented across the
business to ensure consistent and proactive risk management.
Cybersecurity and IT Security
The incidence and sophistication of cyber threats continue to rise globally, posing significant risks to businesses that process
large volumes of data. As a technology services provider, MLP faces potential exposure to IT security breaches, malware, or
cyber-attacks that could compromise data, disrupt operations, and cause reputational or financial damage.
To mitigate these risks, the company has:

+ Invested heavily in systems and architecture (software and hardware) to strengthen resilience.

+ Achieved (SO 27001 accreditation, initially with external consultants and now maintained by internal expertise.

« Enhanced payment and fraud security measures to protect clients and end-users.

+ Implemented regular employee training, with annual IT security programmes for all staff and more frequent updates

for high-risk roles such as Finance.
+ Dedicated IT security personnel who monitor cybersecurity trends and continuously implement new processes,
systems, and technologies. -

+ Established disaster recovery plans to ensure incidents are managed efficiently and effectively.
The company remains vigilant and committed to ongoing investment in cybersecurity, ensuring it stays ahead of evolving
threats and maintains the trust of clients and stakeholders.
Concentrated Client Base
The company has proactively widened its scope of services and sector coverage to reduce reliance on individual clients or
industries. By continuing to execute our core growth strategy, we are diluting the financial impact of any potential client loss.
Diversification across sectors and geographies remains a priority, ensuring resilience and stability in revenue streams.
Macroeconomic Environment
The macroeconomic environment continues to shape the operating landscape, influencing customer demand, investment
priorities, and cost structures. Slower economic growth and elevated inflationary pressures may extend procurement cycles,
reduce discretionary spending, or delay large-scale digital transformation initiatives, Higher interest rates also affect access to
capital and increase financing costs for strategic projects or acquisitions.
While the risk of global economic downturn remains elevated, periods of economic uncertainty often accelerate demand for
solutions that offer added value to the end-user. We view this as an opportunity to support clients in enhancing the value they
deliver to their customers. Opportunities and risks are often sector-specific, reinforcing our commitment to diversifying
revenues across industries and geographies to mitigate exposure to any single revenue stream.

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and Environmental Impact
At MLP, we recognise that corporate social responsibility is integral to sustainable business success. Our CSR initiatives are
designed to create meaningful impact across communities, the environment, and the industries we serve:
« Community support: Contributing to local communities through charitable giving and active participation in social
initiatives.
« Industry engagement: Leveraging our team’s expertise to support the Marketing Industry Sprintathon, an annual
event raising funds for national cancer charities.
« Environmental responsibility: Minimising energy consumption across operations and working closely with our
supply chain to monitor and reduce environmental impact.
« Sustainable solutions: Embedding sustainability principles into our technology platforms to help clients achieve
responsible growth.
In February 2023, MLP implemented a carbon reduction plan aligned with our commitment to achieve Net Zero emissions by
2050 at the latest. To support this, we have subscribed to Zellar, enabling us to measure, track, and reduce our carbon
footprint with transparency and accountability.
Our CSR and environmental strategy reflects a long-term commitment to responsible growth, ensuring that we deliver value
not only to clients and stakeholders but also to society and the planet.
Financial Risk Management
Given the size of the company, the Board of Directors retains direct responsibility for monitoring financial risk management,
with policies implemented by the finance department. This ensures oversight remains close to the core of the business.
The company’s operations expose it to a range of financial risks, particularly in the current climate where even large blue-chip
organisations face uncertainty. Despite this, MLP benefits from a stable client base, with payments typically received within 45
days. Bad debt levels remain very low, though cashflow and debtor positions are monitored weekly to maintain vigilance.
MLP’s financial resilience is further supported by its limited reliance on third parties. The company avoids exposure to
vulnerable sectors—such as high-street gift card retailers—by adopting a just-in-time purchasing model. This approach
prevents over-commitment of funds and ensures purchases are only made from suppliers that pass ongoing due diligence
checks.
Through disciplined financial management, regular monitoring, and prudent procurement practices, the company maintains a
strong financial position and is well placed to navigate uncertainty in the wider economic environment.

1.4 Intangible fixed assets - goodwill
Goodwill represents the excess of the cost of acquisition of unincorporated businesses over the fair value of net assets
acquired. It is initially recognised as an asset at cost and is subsequently measured at cost less accumulated
amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is considered to have a finite useful life and is amortised on
a systematic basis over its expected life, which is 10 years.
For the purposes of impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the cash-generating units expected to benefit from the
acquisition. Cash-generating units to which goodwill has been allocated are tested for impairment at least annually, or
more frequently when there is an indication that the unit may be impaired. If the recoverable amount of the cash-
generating unit is less than the carrying amount of the unit, the impairment loss is allocated first to reduce the carrying
amount of any goodwill allocated to the unit and then to the other assets of the unit pro-rata on the basis of the
carrying amount of each asset in the unit.
