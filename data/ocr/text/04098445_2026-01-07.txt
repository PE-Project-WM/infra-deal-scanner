Principal activities
Intelsat Global Sales & Marketing Ltd. (the “Company”), is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Intelsat Ventures S.a.r.t.,
and is ultimately owned by SES S.A.. References to “Intelsat” are to the Intelsat group headed by Intelsat S.A.
Intelsat provides satellite communications services worldwide through a global communications network of 54
satellites and ground facilities related to the satellite operations and control, and teleport services.
Intelsat operates one of the largest, most advanced satellite fleets and connectivity infrastructures in the world. We
apply our expertise and global scale to reliably and seamlessly connect people, devices and networks in even the
most challenging and remote locations. We provide diversified communications services to the world’s leading
media companies, fixed and wireless telecommunications operators, data networking service providers for enterprise
and mobile applications in the air and on the seas, multinational corporations and internet service providers.
The Company serves as a key contracting party for many of the Intelsat customer contracts, which are generally
non-cancellable in nature with terms up to 15 years, providing visibility to future revenue and cash flows. These
contracts contribute significantly to Intelsat’s annual revenues and contracted backlog. The Company purchases the
satellite capacity that it requires from parent companies to deliver capacity and other service solutions to customers.
In addition, the Company continues to sell satellite capacity to Intelsat Brasil Ltda. These intercompany
arrangements are governed under the Intelsat Master Intercompany Services Agreement (“MISA”) and provide for
positive operating profit margins for the respective entities. In order to achieve these target profit margins, the
intercompany capacity purchases and sales are evaluated on a monthly basis and billed accordingly in lieu of fixed-
value, fixed-term purchase commitments.
The Company has a note payable with a direct subsidiary, upon which substantial interest charges accrue. The
interest payable on this note generally causes the Company to generate a loss before taxation. The subsidiary has the
, ability to recommend and pay dividends to the Company.
Business review
Turnover decreased by US$12.8 million, or 3.5%, for the year ended 31 December 2024 from the year ended 31
December 2023. The decrease in turnover is primarily attributable to non-renewals, price reductions, capacity
downgrades and the termination of services.
Operating profit increased by US$8.9 million, or 19.2%, for the year ended 31 December 2024 from the year ended
31 December 2023. The increase in operating profit was primarily attributable to lower administrative expenses,
primarily due to a US$10.7 million adjustment related to a decrease in the provision for credit losses. See Note {3 -
Debtors for further information.
The Company generated a loss after taxation of US$56.9 million for the year ended 31 December 2024 compared to
US$59.7 million for the year ended 31 December 2023. The decrease in loss was driven by the same drivers as the
operating profit increase as well as higher tax expense for 2024.

Key performance indicators
In addition to turnover, operating profit and loss on ordinary activities after taxation described above, the Company
also considers earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (“EBITDA”) and backlog at year end to
be a key performance indicator. EBITDA was calculated by taking the loss on ordinary activities after taxation and
adding back net interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. EBITDA is a measure commonly used in the fixed
satellite services (“FSS”) sector, and Intelsat presents EBITDA to enhance understanding of its operating
performance. Intelsat uses EBITDA as one criterion for evaluating its performance relative to that of its peers.
EBITDA increased by US$8.8 million in 2024 compared to 2023, reflecting the drivers of the increase in operating
profit discussed above. Contracted backlog at year end represents the Company’s expected future revenue under its
existing customer contracts, and includes both cancelable and non-cancelable contracts. The amount included in
backlog represents the full service charge for the duration of the contract and does not include termination fees. The
amount of termination fees, which is not included in the backlog amount, is generally calculated as a percentage of
the remaining backlog associated with the contract. In certain cases of breach for non-payment or customer financial
distress or bankruptcy, we may not be able to recover the full value of certain contracts or termination fees. A
significant percentage of the total backlog at year end relates to contracts that either are non-cancelable or have
substantial termination fees. For such backlog, we assess the contract term of our cancelable contracts as the full
stated term of the contract assuming each contract is not canceled since the termination penalty upon cancellation is :
substantive. Contracted backlog decreased by 21% from US$0.9 billion in 2023 to US$0.7 billion in 2024, primarily
: due to a continued decline in long-term contracts as most renewals are for shorter terms in-line with the competitive
landscape in the current environment.

Business risks
We face significant competition in the FSS sector in different regions around the world. We compete against other
satellite operators and against suppliers of ground-based communications capacity. The increasing availability of
satellite capacity and capacity from other forms of communications technology has historically created an excess
supply of telecommunications capacity in certain regions from time to time. We believe such an imbalance could
again occur in certain regions, particularly as we and other operators begin to introduce next generation high-
throughput satellite and software-defined satellite technology on our fleets. Additionally, there are new entrants that
have launched constellations in different orbits that compete with portions of our business. Increased competition in
the FSS sector could lower prices, which could reduce our operating margins and the cash available to fund our
operations and service our debt obligations. In addition, there has been a trend toward consolidation of major FSS
providers as customers increasingly demand more robust distribution platforms with network redundancies and
worldwide reach, and we expect to face increased competition as a result of this trend. As noted under “Events since
the balance sheet date”, SES S.A. and Intelsat announced an agreement for SES S.A. to acquire Intelsat and the deal
closed on 17 July 2025. Our direct competitors are likely to continue developing and launching satellites with
greater power and more transponders, which may create satellite capacity at lower costs. In order to compete
effectively, we invest in similar technology.
We also believe that there are many companies that are seeking ways to improve the ability of existing land-based
infrastructure, such as fiber optic cable, to transmit signals. Any significant improvement or increase in the amount
of land-based capacity, particularly with respect to the existing fiber optic cable infrastructure and point-to-point
applications, may cause our video and network services customers to shift their transmissions to land-based capacity
or make it more difficult for us to obtain new customers. If fiber optic cable networks or other ground-based high-
capacity transmission systems are available to service a particular point, that capacity, when available, is generally
less expensive than satellite capacity. As land-based telecommunications services expand, demand for some
satellite-based services may be reduced.
The FSS sector, as a whole, has experienced growth over the past few years. However, the future market for FSS
may not grow or may shrink and therefore the Company may not be able to attract new customers, retain existing
customers or implement its strategies to grow its business. In addition, pricing pressures may have an adverse impact
on FSS sector revenue.
The Company has several large customers and the loss of, or default by our larger customers could materially reduce
its revenue and materially adversely affect its business. Some customers have in the past defaulted and, although we
monitor our larger customers’ financial performance and seek deposits, guarantees and other methods of protection
against default where possible, our customers may in the future default on their obligations to us due to bankruptcy,
lack of liquidity, operational failure, devaluation of local currency or other reasons. Defaults by any of our larger
customers or by a group of smailer customers who, collectively, represent a significant portion of our revenue could
adversely affect our revenue, operating margins and cash flows. If our contracted backlog is reduced due to the
financial difficulties of our customers, our revenue, operating margins and cash flows would be further negatively
impacted.
The Company has significant intercompany debt and acts as guarantor to a substantial amount of indebtedness for
the Intelsat group, which may adversely affect its cash flow and ability to operate its business and make payments on
its indebtedness.

_ We may be subject to greater risks than other companies as a result of the international nature of our business
operations. We could be harmed financially and operationally by tariffs, taxes, government sanctions and regulatory
actions, and other trade barriers that may be imposed on our services, or by political and economic instability in the
countries in which we provide services, for instance in countries heavily reliant on revenues from natural resources.
If we ever need to pursue legal remedies against our customers or our business partners located outside of the United
Kingdom, it may be difficult for us to enforce our rights against them depending on their location.

Regulatory Risks
The Company is subject to regulatory and licensing requirements in each of the countries in which it provides
services, and its business is sensitive to regulatory changes in those countries.
The Company conducts business in or with customers or counterparties in multiple countries and jurisdictions. Tax
legislation or treaties may be amended, and the extent to which certain taxing jurisdictions may require the Company
to pay or make tax payments cannot be determined in advance. The Company could become subject to taxes in
excess of the taxation anticipated.

. We are subject to the UK Data Protection Act 2018 ("DPA 2018"), which is now the controlling data protection
regulation for the UK following the country’s departure from the European Union ("EU"). The DPA 2018 generally
implements the requirements and principles of EU General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR") for the UK. The
regulation of data privacy and security in the EU, the UK, and other jurisdictions continues to evolve.

Related company risks
The Company is subject to a number of benefits and risks by virtue of being a subsidiary within the Intelsat group of
companies. The principal examples of risks being:
+ Supplier risk - the Company is reliant on fellow subsidiary and parent companies for the provision of
capacity, people, resources and assets that the Company requires to deliver on its obligations; and
« — Liquidity risk - funds are held and managed by the Intelsat group’s corporate treasury function in order to
ensure that there is sufficient availability to meet the obligations of the Company as they fall due; and
* Capital risk - the first and normal source of capital is from the immediate parent company; in the event that
the Company requires additional capital, this may not be forthcoming from the Intelsat group.
The Company manages such risks by ensuring that the board of directors are recruited from senior executives within
the Intelsat group and that they are able to manage and influence the impact of these risks.
Through regular management reporting, risks are highlighted and monitored to identify potential business risk areas
and, wherever possible, to quantify and address the risk with a contingency/mitigation plan.
Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to the Company, or that are currently believed to be
immaterial, also may materially adversely affect the business, financial condition and/or results of operations in the
future.

. We occasionally enter into certain contracts in which the customer makes payments in advance of services to be
delivered, which may be years in the future. The reasons for the prepayments in these contracts vary, but generally
can be either for the customer’s benefit or for the Company’s benefit (ability to use the cash received from the
customer to pay for the construction of a satellite asset). The determination of whether contracts with a prepayment
provision contain a significant financing component requires judgment. The Company makes this determination
based on various factors, including the differences between the amount of promised consideration and cash selling
prices, the length of time between payment and the transfer of services and prevailing interest rates in the market.
While most satellite utilisation contracts contain multiple performance obligations for each transponder service on
different satellites, the service period for the different satellite utilisation performance obligations is generally the
same time period. In the event that the time period for multiple performance obligations is not the same, we allocate
the total transaction price to each performance obligation in an amount based on the estimated relative standalone
selling price of the promised good or service underlying such performance obligation. Judgment is required to
determine the standalone selling price for each distinct performance obligation. In order to estimate standalone
selling prices, we use an adjusted market assessment approach which involves an evaluation of the market and an
estimate of the price that our customers are willing to pay, or an expected cost plus a margin approach.
Our contracts generally contain multiple performance obligations. When a contract is separated into multiple
performance obligations, we allocate the total transaction price to each performance obligation in an amount based
on the estimated relative standalone selling price of the promised good or service underlying such performance
obligation. Judgment is required to determine the standalone selling price for each distinct performance obligation.
In order to estimate standalone selling prices, we use an adjusted market assessment approach which involves an
evaluation of the market and an estimate of the price that our customers are willing to pay, or an expected cost plus a
margin approach.
