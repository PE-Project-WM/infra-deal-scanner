2.4 Significant Accounting Policies
(a) Revenue recognition
Revenue of the Company primarily consists of market data services for which the Company has contracts with its
customers. Revenue is recognized when a customer obtains control of goods or service. A customer obtains control when
they have the ability to direct the use of and obtain the benefits from the goods or service. The core principle of IFRS 15
is that an entity recognizes revenue as it transfers the promised goods or services to customers in an amount that reflects
the consideration to which the entity expects to be entitled in exchange for those goods or services. Revenue is measured
at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable, taking into account contractually defined terms of
payment. The Company recognises revenues when specific criteria has been met for each of the Company’s activities as
described below:
Market data, technology, and infrastructure income
The Company earns market data, technology, and infrastructure income on fixed term contracts with its customers. These
contracts will have different types of services which are generally accounted for as performance obligations are met. The
Company does not provide warranties, returns or rebates and therefore does not result in variable consideration.
Transaction price is determined using pre-determined rates specified in the contracts. The market data, technology, and
infrastructure income is recognized when the services are performed over the time and right to receive income is
established.
Ancillary support services income
The Company charges separate fees for each connection of market data feed and this is identified as ancillary and support
services revenue. This fee is charged on a quarterly or annual basis depending on the contract terms with the customer.
Revenue is recognized once the performance obligations are met over time.
Outsourcing Income
The Company has entered into an outsourcing agreement with its parent entity, GTN Group Holding Limited. In
accordance with the agreement billing is done quarterly and revenue is recognized over time as the entity provides support
to the parent entity on market data feed sales, acquisition and support for market data feeds from exchanges and specialist
support services.

a) the rights to receive cash flows from the asset have expired; or .

approach. Under the simplified approach, impairment allowances are always measured at an amount equal to lifetime
ECL.
Lifetime ECL: These losses are the ECL that result from all possible default events over the expected life of a financial
instrument, if there is a significant increase in credit risk under simplified approach.
Measurement of ECL
ECL is a probability-weighted estimate of credit losses. It is measured as follows:

to receive);
¢ financial assets that are credit-impaired: as the difference between the gross carrying amount and the present

The Board of Directors has overall responsibility for the establishment and oversight of the Company’s risk

The risk management department of the Company oversees how management monitors compliance with the

Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk of financial toss to the Company if a counterparty to a receivable by the Company fails to meet
its contractual obligations and arises principally from the Company’s bank balances and trade and other receivables.
The Company limits its credit risk with regard to bank balances by dealing only with reputable banks and no material
ECL. on bank balances has been noted as at the reporting date.
The Company’s exposure to credit risk is influenced mainly by the individual characteristics of each counterparty.
The demographics of the Company’s counterparties including the default risk of the industry and country, in which
counterparty is located or operates has less of an influence on credit risk. The Company does not anticipate any
significant credit losses on its trade and other receivables and accordingly no material ECL has been recorded.
Exposure to credit risk

Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the Company will encounter difficulty in meeting obligations associated with -its
financial liabilities that are settled by delivery of cash or another financial asset. The Company’s approach to
managing liquidity is to ensure, as far as possible, that it will always have sufficient liquidity to meet its liabilities
when due, under both normal and stressed conditions, without incurring unacceptable losses or risking damage to the
Company’s reputation. The Company ensures that it has sufficient cash on demand to meet expected operational
expenses.
