The directors present the strategic report and financial statements for the year ended 30 September 2025.
Fair review of the Business
Principal activity
The principal activity of the Company is the provision of connectivity and cloud services to both its residential and
business customers. The connectivity services provided include broadband, voice, ethernet, private and wide-area
networks, hosting and associated services.
Purpose
Zen’s purpose is to do right for people and planet. Our purpose is underpinned by our most fundamental long-term
objectives which are in priority order to deliver happiness to our people, customers, partners and suppliers. These
sit alongside our values, launched earlier this year - A Better Way of Doing Business, Building Communities, Award-
Winning Services and Ambitious About our Future. We choose not to be constrained by short-term financial targets
but to strive for long-term goals aligned to our pursuit of happiness.
Founded on principles of quality service, integrity, and customer satisfaction, Zen operates in the competitive
telecommunications and broadband industry, specialising in delivering internet, hosting, and IT services to
residential and business customers. The Company remains committed to providing solutions that meet customer
needs while prioritising technological innovation, environmental sustainability, and customer service excellence.
Strategic objectives and business model
1. Be a greater place to work: we continue to build on our People First culture by fostering high levels of
: engagement and creating opportunities for personal and professional growth. This includes enhancing
leadership development, embracing innovative ways of working, and promoting health, wellbeing, and our
commitment to diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging. These efforts reflect our value of Building
Communities, ensuring every individual feels supported and empowered.

2. Positive impact on society and the environment: guided by our value A Better Way of Doing Business,
we remain committed to operating responsibly. We will advance our Net Zero goals, strengthen our B Corp
status, and actively support local communities, ensuring our success benefits both people and the planet.

3. Provide the best service in the UK: our ambition to deliver Award Winning Services drives us to maintain
high customer satisfaction levels by focusing on quality service, technical reliability, and responsive
customer support. Every interaction should reflect our dedication to excellence.

4. Digital transformation and innovation: we continue to leverage the capabilities presented by advances
in technology to improve our operating efficiency and customer service excellence.

5. Expand our network reach: we continue to increase our market share and geographical coverage
through strategic partnerships with full fibre Alternative Network providers.

6. Financially self-sufficient: continue to grow, trade profitably and invest for the long term.

Zen operates in both the UK consumer and business markets. For the year under review, demand for our services
has remained strong across Zen's three trading divisions, Consumer, Business and Partner.
Zen’s strategic focus remains on Ultrafast FTTP connectivity services, and a broad range of Cloud-based
networking and communications services.
¢ Ultrafast connectivity: Zen delivers 88% of its FTTP services over its own network infrastructure and
continues to invest to expand its network reach and the quality of service delivered. Geographic coverage

Financial overview

Full year revenues of £127m (2024 - £121m) show growth of 5% (2024 - 9%). Growth came from broadband, voice,

leased line and cloud services. Growth is underpinned by the continuing commitment and focus on providing .
. excellent customer service.

Zen’s Consumer broadband base grew by 15% and reflects the demand for full fibre services and the success of

Zen’s partnership with Alternative Network provider (AltNet), CityFibre. This impressive growth was partially offset

by a number of customer losses in the Partner and Business divisions where price competition is particularly

aggressive. Overall, the broadband base grew by 8% (2024 - 5%).

Gross profit percentage reduced in the year to 17.2% (2024 restated- 19.9%). The key reasons for the reduction in

gross profit percentage are firstly as a result of cost increases from the Company's supply chain that it was unable

to pass through to its customer base due to its price promise guarantees, secondly the move in sales mix from

higher margin copper based services to lower margin full fibre and finally lower year on year project activity in the

business division.

Comparative figures have been restated to reflect a revised presentation of staff costs, and their allocations

between cost of sales, distribution costs and administrative expenses. The reclassification resulted in a reduction in

cost of sales (and a corresponding increase in gross profit) of £1,285k, with no effect on EBITDA, operating profit or

equity.

The results for the year are EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) of £7.1m (2024

- £5.7m) and an operating profit of £3.14m (2024 - £1.9m). The improvement in EBITDA and operating profit is a

result of the profit on disposal of £4.8m made on the sale of IPv4 address blocks and income from ait-net

integration arrangements of £1.3m (2024 - £0.2m). This profit has allowed the company to invest in its technology

resources and spearhead the drive to maximise the opportunity presented by AltNet aggregation and the realisation

of operating efficiencies through the adoption of Al and customer self-serve applications. The investment accounts

for the increase in employees and accordingly employee costs. Profit before tax of £2.8m (2024 - £0.9m) includes a

loss resulting from a write down to the fair value of the investment property of £0.5m (2024 - £1.1m).

Existing cash balances together with EBITDA enabled the business to invest £2.1m (2024 - £2.9m) of capital into its

core business. Investment in intangible assets of £0.8m (2024 - £1.6m) is expenditure on third party and in-house

developed software. The year-end cash balance is £5.6m (2024 - £5.4m), the Company remains debt free. A 4-year

revolving credit facility of £5m was put in place during the year, the facility runs to the end of July 2028. The facility

has not been utilised during the year. The net asset position of the business has improved to £30.3m (2024 -

£28.4m). The investment property valuation of £6.7m (2024 - £6.0m) reflects management's best estimate of the

element of the Rochdale head office rented to tenants; the valuation is based on current market and capitalisation

rates.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The Company makes little use of financial instruments other than an operational bank account, bank and other :
loans and the use of hire purchase agreements when required so its exposure to price risk, credit risk, liquidity risk
and cash flow risk is not material for the assessment of the assets, liabilities, financial position and profit or loss of

The management's objectives are to:

* retain sufficient liquid funds to enable it to meet its day-to-day obligations as they fall due whilst maximising

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Directors consider the following risks to be the most material or significant for the business. These issues do
not purport to be a complete list or an explanation of all the risks facing the Company. In particular, the Company's
performance may be affected by changes in the market and/or economic conditions and changes in legal,
regulatory or tax requirement legislation. Additional risks and uncertainties, not presently known by the Company or
that the Company currently deems immaterial, may impact the business.
Market .
Description
The main risk facing the business is competition within the market and ensuring that the Company's offerings keep
abreast of technological change. Competition within the UK market remains strong as established players look to
grow their customer and revenue base and new entrants, the Alternative Network Providers (AltNets) look to gain a
foothold in the market through price competition. Legacy services will be withdrawn, Public Switched Telephone
Network (PSTN) and Integrated Services Digital Network (ISDN) services will be switched off by Openreach by
January 2027 and accordingly Wholesale Line Rental (WLR) based broadband services will no longer be
supported.
Potential impact
Growing competition may dilute the addressable market and slow down the rate of growth. If the Company does not
at least keep pace with the evolving market in terms of product and service development, then its plans for revenue
and margin growth may be negatively impacted. Existing customers using legacy services need to be migrated to
data only broadband services or risk being lost to competitors and/or their services becoming unsupported or in the
extreme case switched off by Openreach.
Mitigating actions
A clear and competitive pricing strategy is in place with ongoing monitoring of pricing position and market
proposition. Plans to move existing customers from legacy services to data only or full fibre products continues at
pace. The company continues to invest in targeted marketing focusing on its reputation for superior customer
service as the core differentiator to the competition. The company continues to expand the number of leading
Alternative Network providers it partners with in reselling their services to its consumer, business and partner
customer bases. The investment in technology resource has enabled the company to launch new services and
refresh existing service offerings.

Security
Description
The Company's business systems and networks provide customers and employees with access to the internet,
presenting a risk of cyberattack.
Potential impact
A breach of security could have a significant impact on the Company's reputation and in some cases also impact its
commercial position. Potential fines could also be enforced if the Company was found to be in breach of its
obligations relating to various regulations. Following the normalisation of Hybrid and remote working, the potential
for a breach of security controls is heightened as our people continue to split their time between remote locations
and office. Remote working can present more of a challenge to securing the organisation and its people, and this is
something Zen has focussed on over recent years. :
Mitigating actions :
This risk is mitigated through controls designed to identify and neutralise such threats; this includes the constant
monitoring of unusual traffic patterns by automated applications. Zen continues to adapt its governance structure to
ensure best practice is followed in the identification and management of information and cyber security threats. This _
includes increased frequency and broadened scope of both routine and bespoke vulnerability assessments;
continuous compliance checks; security and business continuity / disaster recovery simulations; integrated security
behaviours training, which is mandatory for all employees; and dedicated security roles to track how cyber threats
are evolving and are best detected and mitigated. Furthermore, Zen operates various technical security controls,
both commercial and proprietary, aimed at reducing its risk of exposure and incidents.
Board visibility is provided via the internal risk governance process, with security and governance represented at
both ExCo and Board-level. The Company is also represented in various industry forums to ensure it is fully aware
of new areas of risk, methods employed by malicious actors and best practice in the identification and mitigation of
tisk. Zen’s core infrastructure and operating capability is certified under [SO 27001 for security, with the additional
requirement to operate within the scope of the UK Telecoms Security Act, specific to its area of business. The
company has mandatory all-staff as well as role-based training on data protection and security best practices,
alongside a rolling refresher programme and training aimed at newly onboarded staff.
Service availability
Description
Reliable, high-quality voice, data and cloud services are critical to any customer and are the core components of
Zen’s products and strategy. Therefore, maintaining very high levels of service availability is central to its credibility,
competitive positioning, and its financial performance.
Potential impact
If Zen’s products and services perform below the market's expectations, then this could have a direct impact on
product and revenue growth through reputational impact and could also result in increased operating costs.
Mitigating actions
Zen operates a comprehensive operational governance framework to manage the availability and performance of
its services. This includes the design and architecture of the network for resilience, product platforms, capacity
planning, change management and security. Business continuity planning is a component of the governance
framework. This governance is subject to external audit via our ISO 27001.
The core network is designed to be resilient. Should part of the network be interrupted, traffic is routed through
alternative paths. Underlying network equipment and circuits are subject to a continual upgrade programme,
whereby key equipment is replaced and upgraded before its end of life and core capacity is increased in line with
the forecast growth in traffic and the demands of new ultrafast technologies.
There is an Incident Management process that is rehearsed on a regular basis. This capability is available
24x7x365 and ensures the business can respond immediately to events that may impact the performance of the
services provided to customers.

B Corporation
As a certified B Corp organisation, the Company has a duty to balance its decisions not just on profitability, but also
on people, customers, suppliers, the community and the environment. Since incorporation in 1995, Zen has
constantly striven towards a better way of doing business, one that puts people before profit and takes
sustainability seriously. The B Corp ethos, balancing purpose and profit is very close to Zen’s heart and
complements Zen's most fundamental long-term objectives of happy staff, happy customers and happy suppliers.
Certified B Corporations are businesses that meet the highest standards of verified social and environmental
performance, public transparency, and legal accountability to balance profit and purpose. B Corps are accelerating
a global culture shift to redefine success in business and build a more inclusive and sustainable economy. The
board of directors take their responsibilities under the B Corp regimen very seriously.
There are five B Corp Impact Assessment areas; governance, people, environment, customer and community. The
B Corp working group has a programme of work to measure progress against each category. This is monitored by
the Board via monthly reports from the Environmental Steering Group. The “what we have done” sections below
provide examples of the progress made in the year.

Company continues to purchase a 100% renewable electricity tariff through Bryt Energy, utilising solar, wind or
hydro power technology. Gas is purchased through Crown Gas and Power.
Our router refurbishment programme was scaled up during the year, resulting in a greater proportion of routers
being collected, refurbished and re-used. The refurbishment initiative also includes our ‘EveryRoom’ extender
devices which are designed to improve Wi-Fi quality in the home. We've seen 51% of routers returned to us
following customer cancellations and 13.4% of these have been refurbished and reused for other customers.
The Company travel policy has been reviewed and updated and now requires employees to take environmental :
considerations into account when travelling for work.

experience around emissions reductions and to work together to achieve our respective Net Zero targets. We
measure our carbon footprint on a quarterly basis, to drive improvements in data quality and granularity and to

1 Accounting policies
Company information
Zen Internet Limited is a private company limited by shares incorporated in England and Wales. The
registered office is Sandbrook House, Sandbrook Park, Sandbrook Way, Rochdale, Lancashire, OL11 1RY.
The company's principal activities and nature of its operations are disclosed in the Directors’ Report.

. Accounting convention .
These financial statements have been prepared in accordance with FRS 102 "The Financial Reporting
Standard applicable in the UK and Republic of Ireland” ("FRS 102") and the requirements of the Companies
Act 2006, including the provisions of the Large and Medium-sized Companies and Groups (Accounts and
Reports) Regulations 2008.
The financial statements are prepared in sterling, which is the functional currency of the company. Monetary
_ amounts in these financial statements are rounded to the nearest £000.
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention, modified to include the
investment properties at fair value. The principal accounting policies adopted are set out below. ;
Going concern
In order to take advantage of the opportunities presented by today's market, alongside enhancements to its
product portfolio and to realise the potential efficiency gains presented by artificial intelligence Zen has
invested heavily in its technology capability. This has led to the company entering a period of operating
losses, the operating losses have been supplemented by the sale of IPv4 addresses. The profits generated
from the sales have provided the working capital headroom required for the business to return to operating
profit and generate the level of EBITDA necessary to provide sufficient cash to manage the working capital
cycle and the capital investments required to run the day-to-day business operations.
A bank facility is in place to provide additional headroom should it be required to fund any short-term working
capital requirements or unforeseen capital expenditure. The Directors have assessed the impact of potential
material cash outflows on the latest projections, including the acquisition referred to in note 28 and the risk of
a significant trading shortfall in the next financial year, and remain confident this would not result in utilisation
of the facility.
The directors conclude that the company has adequate cash resources, access to a bank facility and that
financial projections indicate that future operational and capital commitments can be met with adequate
headroom.
On this basis, and at the time of approving the financial statements, the Directors have a reasonable
expectation that the company has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the
foreseeable future. Thus, the directors continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing
the financial statements.
Turnover
Turnover is recognised at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable for sale of internet services
and associated hardware to external customers in the ordinary nature of the business. The fair value of
consideration takes into account trade discounts, settlement discounts and volume rebates. Turnover is
shown net of Value Added Tax.
Connectivity, voice and hosting services are recognised over the period to which they relate. Deferred income
represents that portion of subscription fees paid by customers but relating to services provided after the
balance sheet date. Where amounts are invoiced in arrears, the appropriate amount is recognised within
prepayments and accrued income. :

Investment properties
Investment property, which is property held to earn rentals and/or for capital appreciation, is measured using
the fair value model and stated at its fair value as at the reporting end date. The surplus or deficit on
revaluation is recognised in the income statement.
Mixed use property is separated between Investment property and freehold property based on the floor area
usage. Shared or common areas are apportioned with reference to the floor area usage of the entire property.
The Companies Act 2006 requires ail properties to be depreciated. However, this requirement conflicts with
: the generally accepted accounting principle set out in FRS 102. The directors consider that, because
investment properties are not held for consumption, but for their investment potential, to depreciate them
would not give a true and fair view.
Impairment of fixed assets
: At each reporting period end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible
assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any
such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the
impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an individual asset,
the company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value fess costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in use,
the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for which the
. estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying
amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. An
impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued
amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.
Stocks
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell. Cost
comprises direct materials.
At each reporting date, an assessment is made for impairment. Any excess of the carrying amount of stocks
over its estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell is recognised as an impairment loss in profit or
loss. Reversals of impairment losses are also recognised in profit or loss.
Cash and cash equivalents .
Cash and cash equivalents are basic financial instruments and include cash in hand, deposits held at call with
banks.

Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when

Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include trade and other debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially
measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost
using the effective interest method.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of
impairment at each reporting end date.
Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that :
occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected.
If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present
value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss
is recognised in profit or loss.
If there is a decrease in the impairment Joss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was
recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not
exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or
are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of
ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of
the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the
assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.

Basic financial liabilities
Basic financial liabilities, including trade and other creditors, are initially recognised at transaction price.
, Debt instruments are subsequently carried at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method.
Derecognition of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities are derecognised when, and only when, the company's contractual obligations are
discharged, cancelled, or they expire.
Equity instruments :
Equity instruments issued by the company are recorded at the fair value of proceeds received, net of
transaction costs. Dividends payable on equity instruments are recognised as liabilities once they are no
longer at the discretion of the company.
Taxation .
The tax expense represents the sum of the current tax expense and deferred tax expense. Current tax assets
are recognised when tax paid exceeds the tax payable. ,
Current and deferred tax is charged or credited to profit or loss.
Current tax assets and current tax liabilities and deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities are offset, if
and only if, there is a legally enforceable right to set off the amounts and the entity intends either to settle on
the net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

Current tax is based on taxable profit for the year. Current tax assets and liabilities are measured using tax

Government grants
Grants are recognised at the fair value of the asset received or receivable when there is a reasonable
assurance that the grant conditions will be met.
Grants relating to expenditure on tangible fixed assets are credited to the profit and loss account at the same
tate as the depreciation on the assets to which the grants relate. The deferred element of grants is included in
creditors.
Grants received in respect of non-depreciable fixed assets have been deducted from the cost of fixed assets.
This is not in accordance with the Companies Act 2006 ("the Act"), which requires assets to be shown at their
purchase price with grants shown as deferred income. This departure from the Act is, in the opinion of the
directors, necessary to give a true and fair view as the assets do not have determinable finite lives and
therefore no basis exists on which to recognise grants and contributions as deferred income. The effect of this
departure is that the net book value of tangible fixed assets is £320k (2024: £330k) lower than it would have
otherwise been.
Foreign exchange
Monetary assets and liabilities in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rates of exchange ruling
at the balance sheet date. Non-monetary assets and liabilities and transactions in foreign currencies are
translated into sterling at the rate of exchange ruling at the date of the transaction. Exchange differences are
taken into account in arriving at the operating profit.

estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised where the revision affects only that
