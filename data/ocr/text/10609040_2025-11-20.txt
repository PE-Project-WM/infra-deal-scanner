The director presents his Strategic Report on the group for the year ended 31 March 2025.
: Principal activities .

Convergence Group design, build, install and fully manage and maintain public and private data networks for
large Enterprise and Corporate customers in the UK.
The vision of the group is to provide our customers with an ‘end-to-end’ transport network for all IP traffic with no
boundary and demarcation ambiguity. This ‘end-to-end’ ownership will cover all elements of the Local Area
Network (LAN) and Wide Area Network (WAN) including Network security.
This strategy is supported by the material shift to ‘cloud services’ and the need for efficient, cost effective,
reliable and flexible content and applications. :
Business review
Over Fiscal year 2024 — 2025, a degree of predictability and stability has returned to the group's trading. There
remains some uncertainty due to inflation, the war in Europe, the Global Political climate, but there is now a
degree of predictability to macroeconomic landscape in the medium to long term. The company’s income stream
remains predominantly annuity based, and across a relatively small number of high-quality customer contracts,
which naturally protects the group against such volatility.
The group is driven to provide excellent customer service and growth through a focused and standardised
product and service portfolio and increasing levels of standardisation, automation and self-service remain at the
heart of everything we do. Our healthcare and public sector customer success has continued, and reference-
ability in this market segment continues to strengthen. However, we have seen less significant amount of capital
spend and project spend in the year, as post pandemic spend has somewhat normalised and Public Sector
budgets have been tightened.
At the outset of the year, the business detuned any focus on selling hardware and renewed its focus on exiting
unprofitable customers. The core objective of the business was to grow Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) and
ensuring operational efficiency by optimising processes and hiring best in class talent. The impact of lower
hardware sales has been profound, a reduction of £9.8m revenue versus the prior year. This has had a direct
impact on EBITDAE (1), which closed at £8.3m (down from £9.0m in the prior year). Despite the headline decline
in revenue, ARR grew by 10% in year, the benefit of which, will be realised in 2026. This includes a material new ©
contract win with a large UK managed service provider. Recurring revenue as a portion of total revenue is up to
78%. Naturally, this revenue shift has improved gross margins (+4.5% to 41.6% and improved EBITDAE margins .
(+2.9% to 21.5%).
We expect revenues to increase as continued investment in our sales team, marketing and experience of |
bundling WAN and LAN products and services become imbedded in the culture and value proposition of the
business. Our CaaS (Connectivity as a Service) product and service is increasingly looking well-positioned and
commercially attractive given the continued need to deliver “more for less”.
Our CaaS product represents a material amount of time, effort and resources to run a business in an ‘As-Is’
state whilst building a new operating model that is scalable, sustainable and fit for the consumption changes that
have been occurring. It has been, and will continue to be, imperative that we understand and meet changing
connectivity requirements and meet user consumption needs. The economic pressure from inflation, skill
shortages, the commoditisation of connectivity and revenue and margin erosion will drive operating model
efficiencies that protect margin whilst supporting product and service reliability. Our investment in CaaS will
support our value proposition, competitiveness and allow manageable scale.

Future developments
Independent market analysis has identified that we currently have less than one percent of our target customer
base in the UK that we can currently service with our existing portfolio. We therefore believe we need to
continually invest in our products and services and as they continue to commoditise, we make consumption
simple, predictable and significantly more reliable and dependable. like the other core utilities. Maintaining this
level of focus, discipline and investment will drive organic growth as consumption demands change. Inevitably it
will also bring non-organic growth opportunities due to the efficient way we provide, deliver and service our
products and services and how inefficiently and unsustainable our competitors do.
Our intention is to remain disciplined and focused on providing a narrow set of products and services to a
customer base who have the right business, infrastructure, people and capability profile.
We must continue to evolve our product and service portfolio while increasingly integrating, and natively, security
as a service. Any development of products and services must be carefully identified, procured, planned and
executed to ensure our Operating model is not compromised, resulting in an increased cost to serve.

Principal risk and uncertainties
The connectivity market remains competitive, with largely homogenous products provided across the market.
The group mitigates the risk of losing customers to competitors by supplying best-in-industry engineering
solutions to technical problems, alongside first-class customer service.
Regulatory Risk - prices of products purchased by the group can be affected by regulatory bodies in the UK,
leading to an impact on gross profit. The group is able to mitigate the risk through its contractual ability to on
price increases, though in each case the group reviews the market and commercial sensitivities of passing on
additional charges. The group continues to monitor future regulation requirements.
Financial risk management and objectives
The group's principal financial instruments comprise of bank balances, trade debtors, and trade creditors. The
main purpose of these instruments is to finance the group's operations. The group activities mean that it is
exposed to a limited number of financial risks:
Credit Risk - the group's credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade debtors. This risk is managed by stringent
weekly checks and reporting processes to assess exposure on a customer-by-customer basis. The group
regularly monitors customer payment performance and can, if required, suspend services for non-payment. The
amounts presented in the Balance Sheet are net of allowances for doubtful debtors.
Liquidity Risk - the group's liquidity risk is managed by maintaining a suitable level of funds to support working
capital requirements. The group's cash balances are held in accounts where funds are immediately available.

Going concern
The group met its funding requirements during the year through a mixture of shareholders’ funds, cash at bank
and an RCF bank facility. On 5th June 2025 the group also secured two additional bank loans to fund this
acquisition. These new loans are repayable on Sth June 2028 and have financial covenant requirements. The |
RCF facility expired during the year and was paid in full. ;
The director has prepared detailed financial forecasts for the period ended 31 March 2027, being the going
concern period. The forecasts take into account reasonable possible sensitivities to trading performance, with
due consideration to the economic environment, to risks regarding the timing and extent of cash flows and the
impact on covenant compliance. More specifically, the director has tested various sensitivities regarding new
business sales, customer churn rates and pay inflation. The group does also have access to a secured £3m
overdraft facility should it be required. This overdraft has no expiry date and the bank may review the facility from
time to time and at least annually.
These forecasts show that the company has sufficient financial resources, to continue to operate for at least the
going concern period. As such, the director considers it appropriate to adopt the going concern basis in
preparing the financial statements.

Energy efficiency actions taken
Convergence has committed to measuring the carbon footprint of business activities year on year to gain an
understanding of pinch points and regularly make efficient and direct improvements to reduce these emissions.
We have appointed a sustainability partner to support with calculating baseline carbon footprint and reduction
recommendations. Within our last Financial Year, our Scope 1 emissions have slightly increased due to the
volume of business travel, however we expect to see a reduction in the coming year as we have now transitioned
our leased vehicles to hybrid vehicles.
Additionally, our Scope 2 emissions have reduced in comparison to prior years. This is due to a refinement in our
: teporting boundary: energy consumption on our leased premises is now reported under Scope 3 (upstream
. leased assets), as the landlord is responsible for procuring the energy supply. As such, we have limited ability to
directly influence these emissions. Moving forward, we will focus on engaging with our landlords and wider
supply chain to encourage the decarbonisation of these sources, as part of our broader Scope 3 reduction
strategy.

2.17 Impairment excluding stocks and deferred tax assets

Financial assets (including trade and other debtors)
A financial asset not carried at fair value through profit or loss is assessed at each reporting date to
determine whether there is objective evidence that it is impaired. A financial asset is impaired if
objective evidence indicates that a loss event has occurred after the initial recognition of the asset,
and that the loss event had a negative effect on the estimated future cash flows of that asset that can
be estimated reliably.
An impairment loss in respect of a financial asset measured at amortised cost is calculated as the
difference between its carrying amount and the present value of the estimated future cash flows
discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate. For financial instruments measured at cost
less impairment an impairment is calculated as the difference between its carrying amount and the
best estimate of the amount that the company would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the
reporting date. Interest on the impaired asset continues to be recognised through the unwinding of
the discount. Impairment losses are recognised in profit or loss. When a subsequent event causes
the amount of impairment loss to decrease, the decrease in impairment loss is reversed through
profit or loss.
Non-financial assets
The carrying amounts of the company’s non-financial assets, other than stocks, are reviewed at each
reporting date to determine whether there is any indication of impairment. If any such indication
exists, then the asset's recoverable amount is estimated. The recoverable amount of an asset or
cash-generating unit is the greater of its value in use and its fair value less costs to sell. In assessing
value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax
discount rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks
specific to the asset. For the purpose of impairment testing, assets that cannot be tested individually
are grouped together into the smallest group of assets that generates cash inflows from continuing
use that are largely independent of the cash inflows of other assets or groups of “cash-generating
unit"). The goodwill acquired in a business combination, for the purpose of impairment testing is
allocated to cash-generating units, or ("CGU") that are expected to benefit from the synergies of the
combination. For the purpose of goodwill impairment testing, if goodwill cannot be allocated to
individual CGUs or groups of CGLJs on a non-arbitrary basis, the impairment of goodwill is
determined using the recoverable amount of the acquired entity in its entirety, or if it has been
integrated then the entire entity into which it has been integrated.
An impairment loss is recognised if the carrying amount of an asset or its CGIJ exceeds its estimated
recoverable amount. Impairment losses are recognised in profit or loss. Impairment losses
recognised in respect of CGl-Js are allocated first to reduce the carrying amount of any goodwill
allocated to the units, and then to reduce the carrying amounts of the other assets in the unit (group
of units) on a pro rata basis.
An impairment loss is reversed if and only if the reasons for the impairment have ceased to apply.
Impairment losses recognised in prior periods are assessed at each reporting date for any indications
that the loss has decreased or no longer exists. An impairment loss is reversed only to the extent that
the asset's carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined,
net of depreciation or amortisation, if no impairment loss had been recognised.
