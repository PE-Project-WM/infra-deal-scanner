2.2 Going concern
The Company meets its day-to-day working capital requirements through its cash reserves, borrowings and reliance on group
support. The current economic conditions continue to create uncertainty particularly over the level of demand for the
Company's products. The Company's forecasts and projections, taking account of reasonably possible changes in trading
performance, show that the Company should be able to operate within the level of its current cash reserves and borrowings.
Management continuously assesses the conditions and potential effects on the Company's operations in order to ensure that it
will continue as a going concern. The decline in revenues in 2024 has put some pressure on the Company's profitability.
The company relies on support from the Group and has secured a letter of support from its Parent Company, Upstream Mobile
Commerce Limited. However, the Parent Company's ability to provide ongoing financial support to the Company is dependent
on the successful complction of a debt restructuring agreement of its ultimate parent company, the rollover of existing bank
facilities, and the achievement of projected cash flows. These factors involve significant judgment and uncertainty, and
. accordingly, a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the Company’s ability to continue as a going
concern.
Considering all the above factors, the Directors and the Group management has concluded that at the date of approval of these
financial statements, the going concern basis of accounting is appropriate. .

2.7 Taxation

The tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and deferred tax.

Current tax .

The tax currently payable is based on taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from net profit as reported in
‘ the income statement because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable or deductible in other years and it

further excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The company’s liability for current tax is calculated using tax

rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting end date.

Deferred tax

Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable on differences between the carrying amounts of assets and

liabilities in the financial statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the computation of taxable profit and is

accounted for using the balance shect liability methad. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognized for all taxable

temporary differences and deferred tax assets are recognized to the extent that it is probable that taxable profits will be

available against which deductible temporary differences can be utilized. Such assets and liabilities are not recognized if

the temporary difference arises trom goodwill or from the initial recognition of other assets and liabilities in a transaction

that affects neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.

The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at cach reporting end date and reduced to the extent that it

is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered.

Deferred tax is cafculated at the tax rates thal are expected to apply in the period when the liability is settled or the

asset is realized. Deferred tax is charged or credited in the income statement, except when it relates to items charged

or credited directly to equity, in which case the deferred tax is also dealt with in equity. Deferred tax assets and

liabilities are offset when the company has a legally enforceable right to offset current tax assets and liabilities and the

deferred tax assets and liabilities relate to taxes levied by the same tax authority.
