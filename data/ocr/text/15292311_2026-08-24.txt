Strategic report
Introduction
The directors present the Group Strategic Report for Toob Topco Limited for the year ended 31 December 2025.
The Group has successfully invested in rolling out a full fibre broadband to 300,000 premises in the south of England.
During 2025 the Group completed its transition to a consolidated in-house build capability, facilitating the infill of
network gaps as the opportunity arises and maintenance of its existing network. The Group has expanded its full fibre
broadband services under its ISP agreement with CityFibre scaling to become a national provider in the UK.
Network commercialisation remains the key focus for the Group as is evidenced by the significant growth in customer
connections both on its own network and that of its wholesale partner CityFibre.
The combination of these dynamics, supported by capital from funds managed by the Amber Infrastructure Group and
funds managed by Ares Management’s Infrastructure Debt Strategy, has resulted in positive EBITDA being achieved,
as planned, in the fourth quarter of 2025.
Looking forward to 2026 the Group will continue to leverage its network investment by acquiring customers to drive
toward being cashflow positive whilst expanding its national footprint on the CityFibre network.
Principal Activities
The Group designs, builds, owns, sells and operates its own full fibre network and resells a third-party full fibre
network for the benefit of consumers and as appropriate, small businesses.
Business Review
The Group has a clear strategy and business plan to scale the Group as a full fibre network provider.
It has a very experienced and seasoned management team who have diligently managed the deployment of the Toob
network in its chosen geographies and who continue to successfully commercialise the network investment by
acquiring and retaining connected customers.
The Group is supported by a mixture of equity and debt of up to £264.15M to deliver its plan to create a sustainable
business focusing on the south of England and to take advantage of market consolidation if opportunities arise.
In its seventh full year of operation, 2025 has seen the Group concentrate on extending its customer acquisition
methodologies across its wider footprint. The Group has successfully leveraged its brand across the south of England,
adding incremental customers, business synergies and margin and has launched a nation-wide expansion over the
national CityFibre network.
The financial statements reflect the significant increase in revenues from the growth in connected customers as costs
and capital investment from the build programme reduce, partially offset by the necessary scaling of sales, marketing
and customer services capabilities to support the growing customer base.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors identify, assess and manage principal risks and uncertainties that might directly affect the Group’s
strategy and operations. Risks generally arise from either external factors driven by the market in which the Group
operates or from internal factors driven by the operational aspects of the business.
Market
Changes in government policy can adversely affect the Group. The Group works with industry groups such as ISPA
and INCA to engage with the government and Ofcom on key topics and consultations to ensure that the interests of
the Group are properly and clearly represented. The Group carefully and diligently reviews all new areas selected for
potential build to understand the current and possible future competitive landscape to identify circumstances where
its economic prospects might be impacted.
Digital Disruption and Emerging Technology
Generative Al is a specific subfield of AI that focuses on creating new content and therefore represents both an .
opportunity and a risk for the Group. Al processes large amounts of data which gives rise to both privacy and cyber
security issues where AJ has the potential to drive network attacks that exploit possible vulnerabilities.
Deepfake content and algorithms have the potential to create bias that influences opinion and decision making.
Generative AI tools have the potential to pose ethical, legal, and existential conflicts. The application of generative
Al tools in the workplace is therefore being evaluated by the government and the NCSC to assess its longer-term
impact.
The Group has a Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) policy advising employees on acceptable and prohibited uses of
AL
Data and Cyber Security
The Group as an infrastructure provider, is exposed to the ever-increasing risk of cyber-attacks. The Group is
continually enhancing its physical defences through application of latest patch updates, regular reviews and testing of
its perimeter defences through penetration testing supported by continuous network monitoring.
The Group maintains its Cyber Essentials and Cyber Essentials Plus qualifications together with renewal of its
18022301 and ISO27001 accreditations.
The Group’s physical defences are supported by logical defences vested in robust business processes and training to
ensure employees understand and can respond to business continuity, data protection and security breaches and
phishing attacks.
Competition
The Group competes for customers with other ISPs who either own or resell the wholesale networks of Openreach,
Virgin Media and CityFibre or compete as a standalone AltNet. The Group acquires customers from competitors who
either do not have access to a full fibre product or are unable to provide a suitably differentiated full fibre proposition
that is able to compete with the Group proposition. The Toob network has very limited overbuild with other AltNets
which significantly reduces any competitive tensions in those geographies. The Group continually reviews its products
and services to ensure its offers meet the needs of its customers and remain attractive to customers considering an
alternative to their current provider.

Operations
The Group maintains a practice of dual sourcing wherever possible for its key materials and equipment and ensures
that minimum inventory levels can mitigate potential supply chain risks. Service interruptions are a risk to brand and
teputation so The Group architects its network to be resilient at appropriate levels of consolidation in its core network
and its Points of Presence and responds promptly to minimise the effect of outages on its customers.
Outsourced Construction
The majority of new network deployment is now delivered by internal resources however where necessary Toob
Topco Limited awards NEC (New Engineering Contract) contracts to construction partners to deliver any necessary
civils works. The Group relies on their partners’ industry expertise and capability to deliver in line with agreed
contractual terms and within the UK legal and regulatory framework.
Climate
Toob Topco Limited recognises and actively manages the risks that climate change presents to The Group, including
the increased likelihood of extreme weather events such as floods, storms, and heatwaves.
Future Developments
. The directors see no material change in the nature of the Group’s activities but expect to extend the service offering
with complimentary products to address the needs of different customer segments where appropriate and enter
wholesale agreements that will allow third party channels to resell the Toob network under various retail brands.
Financial Risk Management
The Group is exposed to a variety of financial risks including credit risk, liquidity risk and interest rate risk. The Group
adopts a risk management approach to review and manage these risks.
Liquidity
Cashflow is managed by ensuring there are sufficient funds available to support continuing operations and plans for
future growth. The Group maintains a detailed rolling cashflow forecast to ensure there are adequate funds available
to meet commitments as they fall due. The Group has put in place appropriate equity and debt facilities that underpin
its funding requirements.
Interest
Recent funding rounds have increased the leverage of Toob Topco Limited which exposes the Group to greater cost
risk from any increases in interest rates. The Group has put in place an interest rate cap to mitigate the exposure.
Credit risk
Exposure to credit risk is monitored on an ongoing basis. As most customers pay in advance on a monthly basis the
Group has the ability to withdraw services should a customer become overdue.

Going Concern
Notwithstanding the Group net liabilities of £207,552k (2024: £150,883k) as at 31 December 2025, a loss for the year
then ended of £56,669k (2025: £53,103k) and operating cash outflows for the year of £7,395k (2024:£7,362k), the
financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis which the directors consider to be appropriate for
the following reasons.
As at 31 December 2025 the business had £964k cash available and as at 31 March 2026 £1,973k.
The directors have prepared cash flow forecasts for a period of 12 months from the date of approval of these financial
statements (‘base case model’) which indicate that, taking account of reasonably possible downsides, the Group will
have sufficient funds, through the availability of debt funding from funds managed by Ares Management’s
Infrastructure Debt Strategy entered into on 16 May 2023 as disclosed in note 14 that sets out the repayment terms on
the loan and further equity from Funds managed by the Amber Infrastructure Group to supplement its initial
investment. On the 22™ January 2026 an extension to the existing loan was agreed for an additional facility of up to
£9.75m detailed in note 14.
The debt facility is subject to five covenant requirements; total net debt to equity ratio, net debt per premise passed,
net debt per premise connected, total premises passed and average revenue per customer. Cashflow forecasts under
the base case model produce debt and revenues to satisfy the covenants for at least the next 12 months.

. Furthermore, the directors have carefully reviewed the prospects of the Company and its future cash flows for potential
adverse economic impacts. The directors have prepared a severe but plausible downside model (‘downside model’)
using the following assumptions: reduced customer take up versus base case, and sustained price competition forcing
pricing to be held flat for an extended period.

This downside model forecasts that the cash available to the business from its facilities will also satisfy the covenant

requirements for at least the next 12 months. In making this assessment management have considered what mitigating

action would be required should a forecasted breach be present. This includes utilising the additional facility available

from National Digital Infrastructure Fund I PF LP.

In modelling the above, the Group has not included additional cost saving measures that are in the control! of the

directors which could be taken to further reduce costs or cash outflows such as reduction in capital expenditure and

payroll costs.

Consequently, the directors are confident that the Group and The Group will have sufficient funds to continue to meet

its liabilities as they fall due for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the financial statements and therefore

have prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis. Accordingly, these financial statements do not
7“ include any adjustments to the carrying amount or classification of assets and liabilities that would result if the Group

and the Group was unable to continue as a going concern.

1.12 Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and any deferred tax.
The current tax charge is based on the taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from net profit as reported in
the Statement of Financial Position because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable or deductible in
other years and it further excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The Group’s liability for current tax is
calculated using tax rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end date.
Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable on differences between the carrying amounts of assets
and liabilities in the financial statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the computation of taxable profit
and is accounted for using the liability method. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all taxable
temporary differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that taxable profits will
be available against which deductible temporary differences can be utilised. Such assets and liabilities are not
recognised if the temporary difference arises from goodwill or from the initial recognition (other than in a business
combination) of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects neither the taxable profit nor the accounting
profit.
Deferred tax liabilities are recognised for taxable temporary differences arising on investments in subsidiaries, except
where the Group is able to control the reversal of the temporary difference and it is probable that the temporary
difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future.
Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period when the liability is settled, or the
asset realised. Deferred tax is charged or credited to the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income,
except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to other comprehensive income, in which case the deferred
tax is also dealt with in other comprehensive income.
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset when there is a legally enforceable right to set off current tax assets against
current tax liabilities and when they relate to income taxes levied by the same taxation authority and the Group intends
to settle on a net basis.
