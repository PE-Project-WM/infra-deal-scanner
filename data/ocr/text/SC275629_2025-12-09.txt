REVIEW OF THE BUSINESS

The principal activity of the Company is the provision of managed cloud services from data centres owned and
operated by the Company.

Strategy and business model

The Company’s strategic growth plan is focused on two main activities:

1, Protect and Expand Our Core. The Company is committed to strengthening and growing its existing customer
base, leveraging its robust hybrid cloud infrastructure and associated services.

2. Broaden Our Solutions Portfolio. The Company aims to deliver to existing and new customers a fully
comprehensive secure private, public and hybrid cloud offering by continuing to evolve its product suite to
meet the digital needs of its customers.

The Company’s vision remains clear and consistent: to establish iomart as the UK’s leading provider of secure hybrid
cloud services to our market. The cloud landscape continues to expand rapidly, with customer needs becoming
increasingly complex as they seek to harness the latest technologies. The Company is well-positioned to meet this
demand, with deep expertise across private, public, and hybrid cloud infrastructures, cyber security expertise, and a
comprehensive suite of managed services that support our customers’ digital transformation agendas.

Business model .
The Company’s business model involves the provision of cloud and managed services, delivering computing power, -
storage, data protection and security capability customers’ need for the effective operation of their businesses. The
Company has invested in an estate of data centres, an extensive fibre network and for each customer the servers,
routers, firewalls and other assets that are necessary to create the IT infrastructure they require within a private cloud
environment. These resources, along with the associated staff, are shared across most of the Company’s revenue
streams. Customers pay for the provision of that infrastructure, with the potential to add third party technology and
provide various degrees of a managed services to wrap around their technology requirements.

Larger customers tend to have multi-year contracts for complex cloud solutions, which are invoiced and paid on a
monthly basis. Many of the Company’s smaller customers pay in advance for the provision of services which results
in a substantial sum of deferred revenue, which is then recognised over the period of the service provision. A
significant proportion of our revenue is therefore recurring and the combination of multi-year contracts and payment
in advance provides us with strong revenue visibility.

The Company has three core offerings, being: cloud managed services, self-managed infrastructure and non-recurring
revenue.

Operational Review :

Cloud managed services (recurring revenue)

: Cloud managed services revenue has increased by £2.4m (4%). The Company’s order bookings in this area of the
business grew well over the year, as both existing customers and prospects have responded positively to our broader
solution set and our re-invigorated focus on customer service. However, the run-rate entering FY25 was lower than
the previous year and as the year progressed, the Company also saw some customer losses and lower levels of renewals
from some existing customers which, in combination, negated the growth in order bookings.

Selfmanaged infrastructure (recurring revenue)
Self-managed infrastructure revenue decreased by £6.4m (21%). The Company has seen organic reductions within
this area for a number of years as dedicated servers, being the largest product in this area, is most susceptible to a
move to public cloud infrastructure as the customers have retained their own technical IT skills and an infrastructure
only service is more transactional. The last 12 months have seen an acceleration of customer churn especially from
the long tail of our customer base and also on a disproportionate basis from the smaller acquired brands of Memset
and Bytemark as the Company sought to consolidate platforms and service teams to more resilient core offerings.
Non-recurring revenue (non-recurring revenue)
Non-recurring revenue has increased by £2.3m (70%) and relates primarily to on premise product and licence reselling
plus consultancy projects. The revenue increase in the year is a result of improved order bookings as a result of restored
hardware refresh activity as the economic situation stabilizes.

Profit before tax
Profit before tax for the year was £0.5m (2024: £10.3m), a decrease of £9.8m, driving a profit before tax margin of
1% (2024: 12%). This was largely driven by the decline in the high margin revenue activity with the self-managed

long-term commitments made, these software costs are now capitalised and represent as intangible asset amortisation,
replacing the previous licence consumption cost of sale classification. Administrative costs also included an
exceptional non-recurring impairment charge on goodwill of £0.8m.
Market position
The past year has presented significant challenges as the Group, and therefore the Company, continues to reposition
iomart in higher-growth segments of the IT services sector. The Company is focused on transforming its market
position and its product offering as this is central to driving sustainable, organic revenue growth. The Company has
taken proactive steps to address margin pressures stemming from shifts in its revenue mix and also customer renewal
levels. While the benefits of product realignment and cost optimisation will take time to fully materialise, these actions
position the Company well for a return to revenue and profit growth in FY27 and beyond.
Sales Momentum
FY25 has been a pivotal year as the Company transitioned its portfolio, added significant capability, made operational
enhancements, and brought a sharper focus to our value proposition. Growth was led by Microsoft related solutions,
with demand for the Group's core offerings in managed private cloud and back-up & data protection services providing
a stable foundation.
Momentum in order bookings was tempered by accelerated customer churn from legacy self-managed infrastructure,
plus some private managed cloud customers. Specifically on the self-managed infrastructure revenue reductions,
through the Company’s reprioritisation of this customer base, the Company have lost business with a year-on-year
revenue impact of £1.6m from Bytemark and Memset combined customers. These businesses were acquired in 2020
and 2021, respectively. Rapidswitch, the largest of iomart’s sub brands in this area, while still seeing revenue declines,
was maintained at a consistent level. Although the dedicated server market remains under pressure, the Company
continues to explore opportunities to mitigate the rate of decline in this segment. This includes more dedicated sales
focus on the wider data centre services, which would include Rapidswitch offerings serviced primarily from our
Maidenhead site.
Enhanced Portfolio & Technology Partnerships
This year the Company has taken strides to enhance its end-to-end proposition and product portfolio. The Company’s
combined capabilities allow it to compete more effectively in the market with enhanced services, top-tier vendor
partnerships, greater scale and references across cloud managed services, modern work, data protection and cyber
security, cementing our position as a leading UK secure cloud provider.
FY25 also saw the deepening of our strategic alliances with Broadcom VMware (iomart has Pinnacle Partner status).
This partnership not only enhances the Company’s go-to-market capabilities but also enables joint innovation and
continued initiatives.
Operational Excellence & People
The Company will continue to invest in systems integration, cost efficiency, and automation to build a more scalable,
modern service organisation, whilst recognising that many of our legacy brands can, with renewed support and focus,
continue to generate good opportunities for iomart. The investment the Company has made in streamlining and
improving its operations has delivered measurable improvements in customer service around satisfaction scores, ‘right
first time’ deployment and service delivery turnaround times, benefitting customer retention and generating sales
opportunities. .

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The Directors who are responsible for the Company’s system of risk management and internal controls, have
established systems to ensure that an appropriate level of oversight and control is provided to manage principal risks
and uncertainties identified that could have a material impact on the Company’s performance. The controls are
designed to help the company meet its business objectives by appropriately managing, rather than eliminating, the
risks of failure to achieve business objectives, as any system can only provide reasonable, not absolute, assurance
against material misstatement or loss. A risk management framework is in place which sets out the ongoing processes
for the identification, assessment and management of risks, and for their ongoing monitoring and review. Effective
risk management is essential to enable us to deliver on the Company’s strategy and to achieve our operational
objectives.
Potential material risks and uncertainties remain similar to the prior year. These risks are as follows:
Staff
As with any service organisation iomart is dependent on the skill, experience and commitment of its employees and
especially a relatively small number of senior staff. The performance of the Company could be adversely affected if
the required staffing levels are not maintained or senior staff are not retained. The Company seeks to recruit and retain
suitably skilled and experienced staff by offering a challenging and rewarding work environment. This includes
competitive and innovative reward packages and a strong commitment to training and development. The Company
also has the ability to manage and recruit resource across multiple locations which creates, to some degree, flexibility
on where it recruits and deploys its resources.

Data centre operation
Any downtime experienced at the Company’s data centres would immediately have an impact on its ability to provide
customers with the level of service they demand. Should the Company be unable to provide the required level of
service this could have an adverse effect on the Company’s performance through the loss of customers and reputation.
The Company’s ongoing investment in preventative maintenance and lifecycle replacement programme ensures its
data centres continue to operate effectively. The Company also continually look at new innovations and technology
within the sector that can help to deliver operational efficiency and effectiveness in line with its [S050001 energy
management system, and its obligations within the Carbon Reduction Commitment (“CRC”) Energy Efficiency
Scheme.
Network
The Company provides an essential service to an extensive client base many of whom rely on the provision of that
service for their major internet presence. The service the Company provides to its customers is dependent on the
continued operation of the diverse fibre network which connects our data centre estate. Should the network fail, there
would be an adverse impact on customers and any diminution in the level of service could have serious consequences
for customer acquisition and retention. The Company has implemented a resilient network throughout its data centre
estate with no single points of failure to ensure the likelihood of network failure is minimised.
Data and Cyber Security
There has been a sharp rise in recent years in cyber and data related crime. The security of customer, commercial and
personal data presents both a reputational and financial risk to the Company. The Company continues to make
substantial investment in physical and data security systems and to promote a culture within the organisation which
embeds security across all of our operations. iomart continues to develop its security portfolio to equip its customers
with the means to counter the types of security threats that clients face. The Company continues to focus on enhancing
its internal process improvement, security awareness and training to ensure it provides solutions which customers can
rely on. The Company also carries specific insurance in relation to cyber related crime. The Company’s contracts and
associated schedules with customers make it clear where responsibilities lie in relation to the roles and responsibilities
of each party for the security of data and data protection in general.
Competition and product portfolio
iomart operates in a competitive and fluid marketplace and while the Directors believe the Company enjoys significant
strengths and advantages in competing for business, some of the competitors are significantly larger, allowing them
to offer similar services for lower prices than the Company would be prepared to match, or launching new product
offerings with significantly enhanced features. Consequently, these competitors could materially adversely impact
the scale of the Company’s revenues and its profitability. In response to this, the Company maintains a broad customer
base, with currently no single customer with more than 4% of the Group’s annual revenue. The Company also
mitigates the risk by establishing strong relationships with our customers, developing tailor-made and value-creating
solutions and delivering excellent service performance while being cost competitive in our day to day business. The
Company’s development team are continually working towards both enhancing, and augmenting, the services and
products iomart currently offers. The Company’s product board meets regularly to keep abreast of new technology
which could enhance the Company's service portfolio. ;
In the last two years the Company created a new executive position of Chief Portfolio Officer and has invested in its
product capabilities through the recruitment of product managers and built its product marketing team. Furthermore,
the Company have expanded its strategic partnerships team and established new governance to ensure it maximises
. the benefits, and pricing, available from key vendor alliances and that products stay relevant. This greater product led
approach ensures that the Company stays abreast of market developments and has a faster pace to its go to market
initiatives. .
Key suppliers .
The Company is dependent on certain key suppliers for the continued operation of its business, the most significant
of which are those for electricity, bandwidth and servers. Were any of these key suppliers to fail in their service
: provision to the Company this could have an adverse effect on the Company’s ability to provide services to its
_ customers. In all cases these suppliers are obtained from reputable organisations chosen after a thorough selection
process. After selection, the Company actively seeks to maintain strong relationships with the chosen suppliers. The
Group also seeks to maintain either several sources of supply or, in the case of electricity, alternative sources of power.

Inflationary pressure

Our largest cost base is our people cost which is managed centrally with annual salary awards and reviews of our

benefits packages to staff. Other significant costs, excluding electricity, relate to licence, data centre and connectivity

costs. Monthly reviews are undertaken of the cost base and we are in discussion with customers and suppliers where

we are seeing any inflationary impact. In addition, our pricing model is regularly reviewed to ensure that contracts

are priced adequately to cover inflation risk.

Our UK data centres are large consumers of electricity to power servers and provide cooling. While electricity costs

remain high, the energy market is stable. We have a proactive hedging strategy in place which is administered on our

behalf by energy consultants, Schneider Electric.

Growth management

The Company seeks to achieve high levels of growth through a combination of organic and acquisitive means. As a

consequence, the Company needs to continue to evolve as an organisation to meet the demands that such growth

places on its business operations. Failure to evolve in the necessary way could lead to deterioration in overa!] business

performance. As part of the Company’s annual strategy and budget review process, which is updated as necessary

throughout the year, the Company identifies the resource and organisational changes that are needed to support its

growth. In addition, an integration and migration plan is produced for each acquisition that is made to ensure the

acquired operation is appropriately integrated into the Group’s operations.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS

Our clear strategy for growth is designed to increase our customer portfolio in the higher growth areas of the expanding

cloud market. Unlike many of our peers we are doing this from a position of strength with our secure, resilient cloud

and network infrastructure, strong customer base, robust business model and financial position. The underlying drivers

for cloud computing, increasing complexity of the technical landscape and customers looking for a trusted and

experienced service partner gives the Directors confidence in the outlook for the long-term prospects for the Company.

The Company’s financial instruments comprise cash and liquid resources and leases together with various items such

meet foreseeable needs and to invest cash safely and profitably. As discussed in Note 22, the Group parent company

in compliance with all covenants under its original banking facility arrangements throughout the 31 March 2025

reporting period. In addition, Directors of the Group have prepared robust budgets and forecasts for the years ending

31 March 2026 and 31 March 2027 respectively. These budgets and forecasts take into account the financial covenant

requirements as well as possible downside scenarios. Even under the downside scenario, the Group is forecast to

maintain sufficient liquidity and comply with all financial covenants without requiring mitigating actions. As a result,

risk associated with this is deemed to be low.

Interest rate risk and currency risk

The Company is not materially exposed to movements in interest rates. The level of non-monetary and monetary

assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies in the Company are minimal, therefore, the company is not

materially exposed to currency movements.

Credit risk

Credit risk refers to the risk that a counterparty will default on its contractual obligations resulting in financial losses

to the Company. The Company provides standard credit terms (normally 30 days) to some of its customers which has

resulted in trade debtors of £9,477,868 (2024: £9,003,318) which are stated net of applicable provisions and which

represent the total amount exposed to credit risk (note 10). The Company manages trade receivable balances vigilantly

and takes prompt action on overdue accounts. The lease deposits of £111,211 (2024: £111,211) are held in escrow

accounts with the landlord’s main UK bankers. The Company’s cash at bank is held within clearing banks in the UK.

As a result of the aforementioned, the risk of exposure to credit is minimal in relation to trade debtors and cash at
