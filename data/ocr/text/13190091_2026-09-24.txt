Operating environment in Ethiopia
In the year under review, the Global Partnership for Ethiopia B.V. Group (GPE Group) operated in an evolving
landscape marked by economic fragility, constrained fiscal space and global disruptions affecting Ethiopia.
The operating environment remained challenging. driven by foreign exchange constraints, fuel price
adjustments and evolving regulatory obligations. Foreign currency availability remained constrained,
although inflationary pressures moderated, with headline inflation declining to 9.7% in February 2026 from
15.0% in February 2025. :
Fuel prices increased during the period, with an average year-on-year adjustment of approximately 29%.
Regional geopolitical developments, including the Iran crisis, continue to present risks through potential fuet
shortages and higher operating costs. Although Safaricom Telecommunications Ethiopia PLC has been
prioritised in fuel allocation, sustained price increases could adversely affect network operations, service
continuity, rollout activities, inflation and customer affordability. Management continues to monitor these
developments and implement mitigation measures to support operational resilience.
During the year, the Government introduced the Universal Access Fund under Regulation No. 585/2026,
requiring licensed telecommunications operators to contribute 1.5% of revenue towards expanding digital
infrastructure and connectivity in rural and underserved areas. The directive became effective in February
2026. The Government also introduced the Disaster Risk Management Levy Fund, an additional statutory
levy aimed at strengthening national preparedness, response and recovery for natural and man-made
disasters. .
Following the implementation of Ethio Telecom's Reference Infrastructure Sharing Offer (RISO) in January
2026, Safaricom Telecommunications Ethiopia PLC adopted the new framework by 31 March 2026. The
Group continued investing in the rollout of a robust and sustainable network and provides voice, data,
messaging and enterprise solutions to consumers, businesses and government institutions. Its purpose
remains to transform lives through a digital future for all Ethiopians.
These developments continue to influence the performance and valuation outlook of Safaricom
: Telecommunications Ethiopia PLC and, consequently, entities with investments in Ethiopia, including
: Vodafamily Ethiopia Holding Company. Persistently high operating costs, foreign exchange constraints and
additional regulatory levies may delay profitability and returns on investment. Management continues to
implement mitigation strategies focused on cost optimisation, operational resilience, network efficiency and
revenue growth.
Looking ahead, the operating environment is expected to remain dynamic as Ethiopia continues its
: economic reforms. While foreign currency constraints, inflation and high operating costs may persist, growth
in telecommunications and digital services is expected to support the business's long-term prospects, The
Directors will continue to monitor regional geopolitical developments and their potential impact on
operating costs. :

Classification
Financial assets that are held within a business model whose objective is to hold assets in order to collect
contractual cash flows, and for which the contractual terms of the financial asset give rise on specified
dates to cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest on the principal amount
outstanding, are classified and measured at amortised cost. :
Subsequent Measurement
Financial assets after initial recognition are measured at amortised cost. Gains and losses are
recognised as profit or loss when the asset is derecognised, modified or impaired.
Impairment :
The Company recognises a toss allowance for expected credit losses on receivables that are measured
at amortised cost. The loss allowance is measured at an amount equal to the lifetine expected credit
losses for trade receivables and for financial instruments for which: (a) the credit risk has increased
significantly since initial recognition; or (b) there is observable evidence of impairment (a credit-
impaired financial asset). If, at the reporting date, the credit risk on a financial asset other than a trade
receivable has not increased significantly since initial recognition, the loss allowance is measured for
that financial instrument at an amount equal to 12-month expected credit losses. All changes in the
loss allowance are recognised in the statement of comprehensive income as impairment gains or
losses.
Lifetime expected credit losses represent the expected credit losses that result from all possible default
events over the expected life of a financial instrument. 12-month expected credit losses represent the
portion of lifetime expected credit losses that result from default events on a financial asset that are
possible within 12 months after the reporting date.
Expected credit losses are measured in a way that reflects an unbiased and probability-weighted
amount determined by evaluating a range of possible outcomes, the time value of money, and
reasonable and supportable information that is available without undue cost or effort at the reporting

Impairment exists when the carrying value of an asset or cash generating unit exceeds its recoverable
amount, which is the higher of its fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use. The fair value less costs
of disposal calculation is based on available data from binding sales transactions, conducted at arm's
length, for similar assets or observable market prices less incremental costs of disposing of the asset.
The value in use calculation is based on a Discounting Cash Flow (DCF) model. The cash flows are derived
from the budget for the next 10 years and do not include restructuring activities that the Group is not yet
committed to or significant future investments that will enhance the performance of the assets of the CGU
being tested. The recoverable amount is sensitive to the discount rate used for the DCF model as well as the
expected future cash-inflows and the growth rate used for extrapolation purposes. These estimates are most
relevant to impairment assessment of the subsidiary.
