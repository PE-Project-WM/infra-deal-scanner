Strategic Report
for the Year Ended 31 December 2025
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
REVIEW OF BUSINESS
In view of the continuing challenges associated with the current economic climate the directors were pleased with the
results for the year and with the financial position at the year end.
The company recorded a 46.61% increase in turnover to £177,652,248 (2024 restated - £121,172,002) and produced a
gross profit margin of 22.40% (2024 restated - 21.67%). Pre-tax net profit increased to £14,838,697 (2024 - £5,496,199).
The profit for 2025 included dividends received from group companies totalling £16,755,611 and other income from
group totalling £1,200,582.
During the year the loan provided by Bechtle AG to fund the purchase of Qolcom Limited in 2024 was converted into a
capital reserve. ;
The company's continuing key objective is to maximize profits which is in line with the objectives of the Bechtle AG
group. The main strategies in achieving this objective are the maximization of sales and gross margins, supported by
effective cost management.
The financial statements have been prepared in accordance with Financial Reporting Standard 101 “Reduced Disclosure
Framework" following the company's first-time adoption of the standard in the previous year.
PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The company faces business risks and uncertainties associated with the competitive market place within which it trades
and those inherent in the current economic climate. In view of this the directors and the group constantly monitor existing
and potential new markets.
There is a general risk that customers will not pay for the products that they have purchased. This risk is managed by
company policies concerning the amount of credit offered to customers and then by regular monitoring of amounts
outstanding in order to minimise exposure to potential bad debts. Trade creditors liquidity risk is managed by ensuring
that there are sufficient funds available to meet the outstanding amounts when they become due.
Inflationary risks continue to have an impact on product prices. This risk is managed by adapting procurement activities
in order to obtain the most favourable terms possible.
SECTION 172(1) STATEMENT
This section serves as the section 172 statement and should be read in conjunction with the Strategic Report.
Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 requires directors to take into consideration the interests of stakeholders in their
decision making. The directors have discharged this duty as outlined below.
ENGAGEMENT WITH EMPLOYEES
The directors continue to recognise that the people employed are a key factor in the company's performance and as such
the aim is to ensure that they are successful both individually and as a team. The company has sought to ensure the health
and wellbeing of the workforce by adopting flexible working practices. The company also strives to provide employees
with a stimulating environment within which to work.
The directors continue to consult and work closely with employee representatives in order to improve the quality of
employee engagement and participation in the development of the business.
There are many ways in which the company engages with its employees which include both formal and informal meetings,
focus groups and appraisals.
The company's Human Resources manager continues to liaise well with staff and the directors.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES - continued

Income recognition

Turnover - This is measured at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable from the sale of goods,

excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales taxes.

Revenue is recognised when goods and services are delivered to customers such that the customer obtains control

of those goods and services.

Supplier income - The company receives income from suppliers in the form of volume and marketing rebates.

Volume rebates earned are based on purchase targets reached. The target levels are agreed with the suppliers and

the income is recognised when targets are reached and the supplier agreement has been received. The rebate

income is recorded against cost of sales to reflect the lower purchase cost for the goods on which the rebates have

been earned.

Marketing rebates are received from suppliers in support of marketing activities. The rebates are agreed with

suppliers for specific periods and products. Marketing rebates are included

Goodwill

Intangible assets which are considered to have an indefinite useful life are not subject to amortisation and are are

tested annually for impairment, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that they may

be impaired. This policy has been applied to goodwill and is in line with the treatment adopted by the group within

its consolidated financial statements.

Tangible fixed assets

Depreciation is provided at the following annual rates in order to write off each asset over its estimated useful life

or, if held under a finance lease, over the lease term, whichever is the shorter.

Short leasehold - 20% on cost and 10% on cost

Fixtures, fittings and equipment - 25% on cost, 20% on cost and 10% on cost

Motor vehicles - 25% on reducing balance

Computer equipment - 50% on cost and 33% on cost

Financial instruments

The company only enters into basic financial instruments that result in the recognition of financial assets and

liabilities like trade and other accounts receivable and payable.

Trade debtors, creditors and other accounts receivable and payable are recognised at transaction price. Amounts

receivable or payable within one year continue to be measured, after their initial recognition, at the undiscounted

amount of cash or other consideration expected to be received or paid.

Stocks

Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell.

Items within stock relate to goods supplied to customers but not yet invoiced as the company does not consider

the transaction to be complete and has not yet recognised it as a sale.

Taxation .

Current taxes are based on the results shown in the financial statements and are calculated according to local tax

rules, using tax rates enacted or substantially enacted by the balance sheet date.

Deferred tax is provided on the liability method and recognised in respect of all timing differences that have

originated but not reversed at the balance sheet date where transactions or events that result in an obligation to

pay more tax in the future or a right to pay less tax in the future have occurred at the balance sheet date.

Timing differences are temporary differences between profit as computed for taxation purposes and profit as stated

in the financial statements which arise because certain items of income and expenditure in the financial statements

are dealt with in different periods for taxation purposes. Deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that
. the directors consider that it is more likely than not there will be suitable taxable profits from which the future

reversal of the underlying timing differences can be deducted.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES - continued
Foreign currency translation
Assets and liabilities in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rates of exchange ruling at the balance
sheet date. Transactions in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rate of exchange ruling at the date
of transaction or at an average rate where this rate approximates the actual rate at the date of the transaction.
Exchange differences are taken into account in arriving at the operating result.
Leases
Leases other than those considered to be short term or relating to low value items are recognised as right-of -use
assets. The lease liability is initially recognised at the present value of the lease payments which have not yet been
made and subsequently measured under the amortised cost method. The initial cost of the right-of-use asset
comprises the amount of the initial measurement of the lease liability, any lease payments made prior to the lease
commencement date, initial direct costs and if relevant, the estimated costs of removing or dismantling the
underlying asset per the conditions of the contract.
Right-of-use assets are reviewed for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the
carrying amount may not be recoverable. Impairment losses are recognised in the profit and loss account.
The discount rate applied to lease payments is an incremental borrowing rate which the company considers to be
appropriate.
Lease payments are allocated between principle and finance cost.
Payments associated with short term leases or leases of low value assets are recognised on a straight line basis as
an expense in the profit and loss account.
Where ownership of the right-of-use asset transfers to the lessee at the end of the lease term, the right-of-use asset
is depreciated over the asset's remaining useful life. If ownership of the right-of-use asset does not transfer to the
lessee at the end of the lease term, depreciation is charged over the shorter of the useful life of the right-of-use
asset and the lease term.
Employee benefit costs :
The company operates a defined contribution pension scheme. Contributions payable to the company's pension
scheme are charged to the income statement in the period to which they relate.
Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are recognised when the company has a present legal or constructive obligation as a result of a past
event, it is probable that the company will be required to settle the amount of the obligation and the amount of the
obligation can be reliably estimated.
The amount recognised as a provision is the best estimate of the consideration required to settle the obligation at
the reporting date.
The company recognises the following provisions:
Warranty claims against the company in respect of potential problems with products sold - The provision is :
measured at the estimate for costs of rectifying any problems having given due consideration to current year sales
and historical claims data. The provision is made in line with the group policy.
Dilapidation costs in respect of leasehold property - The provision is measured at the estimate for costs of potential
future repairs.
Investments in subsidiaries :
Investments in equity shares which are not publicly traded and where fair value cannot be measured reliably are
valued at cost less impairment.
