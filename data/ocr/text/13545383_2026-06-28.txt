The directors present their Strategic Report on TNT Sports Broadcasting Limited (the “Company”, “we”, “us", or “our") for
the financial year ended 31 July 2025.
The directors, in preparing this Strategic Report, have complied with section 414C of the Companies Act 2006.
Principal activities
TNT Sports Broadcasting Limited runs a group of pay television sports channels in the United Kingdom and Ireland
known as TNT Sports ("TNT Sports") that presents exclusive live sport including the Premier League, UEFA Champions
League, UEFA Europa League, UEFA Europa Conference League, the NBA, Premiership Rugby, MotoGP, Cricket, UFC,
: Boxing and WWE. Discovery+ served as the streaming platform for TNT Sports in the United Kingdom during the
financial year; from March 2026, HBO Max became the new streaming platform.
TNT Sports is available across all major TV platforms globally, including BT TV, Sky and Virgin Media, offering a line-up
of up to four TV channels — TNT Sports 1, TNT Sports 2, TNT Sports 3, TNT Sports 4 — in high and standard definition,
plus up to six digital or red-button channels.
TNT Sports Ultimate offers fans selected coverage in up to 4K picture resolution with high dynamic range (HDR),
presenting images and colour in cinema-level quality and Dolby Atmos sound. TNT Sports Box Office brings both
subscribers and non-subscribers pay-per-view access to some of the most exciting events in the calendar.
TNTSports.co.uk brings fans the latest news, views, live updates and expert analysis as well as free-to-view video clips
from the sports TNT Sports presents live.
The Company is a joint venture and has three shareholders which include British Telecommunications PLC (BT PLC), BT
Ninety-Seven Limited (a subsidiary of BT PLC) and DN! Foreign Holdings Limited (DNIFHL).

The loss for the year is £597,297 ,000 (2024: £148,843,000). The equity position at the year end is £478,787,000 (2024:

Future prospects and developments
The United Kingdom sports broadcasting sector is a mature market driven by strong demand for live sports content,
widely distributed across digital and traditional platforms. The rise of streaming services offers further opportunities to
enhance viewer experiences. The strategy of the Company is to capitalise on this strong demand for live sport to deliver
growth whilst also developing further product improvements to drive customer value.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
The industry faces several risks, including competition from global streaming platforms that may bid for exclusive sports,
rights, driving up costs. Regulatory changes, particularly concerning broadcasting rights and advertising policies, could
impact revenue streams. Economic downturns and disruptions to major sporting events (e.g., due to pandemics or other
crises) could also affect viewership and profitability. These risks are formally reviewed by management and processes
put in place to monitor and mitigate them.
Economic conditions . t
The Company's business is significantly affected by prevailing economic conditions and by disruptions to financial
markets. The Company derives substantial revenues from advertisers, and these expenditures are sensitive to general
economic conditions and consumer buying patterns. Financial instability or a general decline in economic conditions
could adversely affect advertising rates and volume, resulting in a decrease in the Company’s revenues.
Changes in the distribution of content
Technology and business models in the Company's industry continue to evolve rapidly. Consumer behaviour related to
changes in content distribution and technological innovation affect the Company's economic model and viewership in
ways that are not entirely predictable.

Going concern
The directors of the Company have reviewed the Company's standing as a going concern in the preparation of the
financial statements.
It is noted that the Company generated a net loss during the year of £597,297,000 but was in a net current asset position
of £315,092,000 at year end. Net loss for the year ended 31 July 2025 is driven mainly by non-cash charges, that is
impairment of goodwill of £152,319,000, impairment of intangible assets of £463,381,000 and amortisation of intangible
assets of £234,765,000 (see notes 12 and 13).
As described in note 18, the Company has access to a revolving credit facility ("“RCF") up to a maximum of £200,000,000
which is provided by BT PLC. Interest is paid at a rate of SONIA plus 1% margin compounded daily in arrears. This
facility is in place for four years from the acquisition date. After this period, in line with JVA, both shareholders are
required to participate equally in providing liquidity to the Company, with each shareholder committing a £100,000,000
revolving credit facility. The Company has drawn £32,000 on the RCF as of year-end while £103,139,000 was drawn as
of 31 July 2024. RCF of £103,107,000 was repaid out of cash generated from operating activities during the year ended
31 July 2025.
The Company has cash pooling arrangements in place with BT PLC whereby various cash surpluses and overdrafts
(residing in the Company's various bank accounts) are pooled together to create a net surplus.
The cash pooling arrangement is structured such that cash, if any, generated by the Company is swept daily into a bank
account held by BT PLC. Interest is accrued on the aggregate amount of the surplus cash. The Company may from time
to time request repayment of cash surplus together with accrued interest thereon in whole or in part. No premiums or
penalties are charged by BT PLC to the Company for any repayments. Access to receive repayments is contractually
defined and is readily accessible. There is £19,213,000 of cash held with BT PLC in the cash pool at year end which is
presented in cash and cash equivalents on the Statement of Financial Position.
Taking into account the factors stipulated above, the directors have reviewed the Company's cash flow forecasts, based
on the latest revision of the business plan, for a period of 12 months from the date of signing of the financial statements.
Additionally, a severe but plausible downside scenario was calculated whereby projected cash flows were reduced. The
Company had sufficient funds available to operate the business in all reviewed scenarios.
Based upon the latest cash flow forecasts, the downside analysis, and current funds available to the Company to include
the revolving credit facility, the directors have concluded that the Company is able to operate for at least twelve months
from the date of signing the financial statements. For this reason, the directors have at the time of approving the financial
statements, a reasonable expectation that the Company has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for
the foreseeable future. Thus, they continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the financial
statements.

agreement and the market demand for the content provided by the Company.

use of it and obtain substantially all benefits.
Distribution revenue is a product of the number of subscribers and the cost per subscriber (CPS) or as a fixed fee
based on contractual minimums. The service is delivered and consumed continuously and therefore it is recognised
over time.

subsequently remeasured to their fair value at the end of each reporting period. The accounting for subsequent
changes in fair value depends on whether the derivative is designated as a hedging instrument and, if so, the nature
of the item being hedged. The Company designates certain derivatives as either:
« hedges of the fair value of recognised assets or liabilities or a firm commitment (fair value hedges),
e hedges of a particular risk associated with the cash flows of recognised assets and liabilities and highly
: probable forecast transactions (cash flow hedges).
At inception of the hedge relationship, the Company documents the economic relationship between hedging
instruments and hedged items, including whether changes in the cash flows of the hedging instruments are expected
to offset changes in the cash flows of hedged items. The Company documents its risk management objective and
strategy for undertaking its hedge transactions. The fair values of derivative financial instruments designated in
hedge relationships and movements in the hedging reserve in shareholders’ equity are disclosed in note 19 .
Cash flow hedges that qualify for hedge accounting
The effective portion of changes in the fair value of derivatives that are designated and qualify as cash flow hedges is
recognised in the cash flow hedge reserve within equity and reclassified into the Statement of Financial Position in
the same line item in which the hedged item is recorded.

r. Preference shares
Preference shares are separated into liability and equity components based on the terms of the contract.
On issuance of the preference shares, the fair value of the liability component is classified as a financial fiability at fair
value.
When the initial carrying amount of a compound financial instrument is allocated to its equity and liability
components, the equity component is assigned the residual amount after deducting from the fair value of the
instrument as a whole the amount separately determined for the liability component. The sum of the carrying
amounts assigned to the liability and equity components on initial recognition is always equal to the fair value that
would be ascribed to the instrument as a whole. No gain or loss arises from initially recognising the components of
the instrument separately.
Transaction costs (if any) are apportioned between the liability and equity components of the preference shares.

Impairment of goodwill
Goodwill is tested for impairment annually at year end. The impairment of goodwill is determined by assessing the
recoverable amount of CGU to which the goodwill relates. The CGU is defined as the "smallest identifiable group of
assets that generate cash inflows that are largely independent of the cash inflows from other assets or groups of assets".
Consistent with prior year, Management determined that the cash generating unit is the Company and it is made up of
the prepaid sports rights, intangible assets (the minimum guarantee intangible asset and the sports rights intangible
asset) and goodwill.
Management made estimates to determine the recoverable amount of the CGU considering both value in use and fair
value less costs of disposal. Both valuations were prepared using a discounted cash flow methodology. Based on the
valuation analysis performed, value in use exceeded fair value less costs of disposal and therefore represents the
recoverable amount for impairment testing purposes.
Following the assessment, impairments of £152,319,000 and £463,381,000 were booked against goodwill and intangible
assets respectively.
The key assumptions used to determine the recoverable amount for the CGU, including a sensitivity analysis, are
disclosed and further explained in note 12.
