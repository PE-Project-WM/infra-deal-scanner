Group Strategic Report
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
Introduction
This strategic report for Iris Infra Master Holdco Limited has been prepared for the entire business, and references to Iris,
The Group or The Company reflect the whole business.
Strategic overview and outlook
The Group is a fast-growing Full Fibre network operator, providing FTTP broadband in underserved market towns and
small urban areas. The Group has built extensive infrastructure, delivering high-quality service to a growing Customer
base, leading to long-term returns. The Group focuses on value, reliability, and Customer satisfaction, positioning itself
as a strong challenger brand. The Group ceased large scale network construction at the end of 2024 and growth wilt now
arise from adding connections rather than increasing the footprint.
As of the end of 2025, the business operated in 90 towns and has expanded its service-ready properties to 602,000
Customer uptake has accelerated, reaching 86,000 at the end of 2025. The Group is also exploring strategic options to
enhance shareholder vatue by leveraging its established infrastructure.
In January 2025 Zzoomm announced a merger with the Iris Infra group of companies including Full Fibre Limited and
BeFibre Limited, which completed in March 2025 following regulatory approval. The combined Group has over 602,000
properties Ready For Service as well as 86,000 customers and is ranked as the fifth largest Alt-Net in the UK by footprint.
The newly created Group Holding / parent Group, Altnet Partners Holdco Limited, is owned by Iris Infra Holdco Limited &
ZGL Investment Limited, and the Group continues to be backed by funds managed by Oaktree Capital Management &
Basalt Infrastructure Partners III.
As part of the transaction, the loan of £100m from ING, Hamburg Commerciai Bank and Kommunalkredit Austria was
extended by one year to September 2027.
Strategic Report
Outlook
The newly enlarged group, including FullFibre, Be Fipre and Zzoomm allows for a significantly more efficient operating
model for the business achieved through increased economies of scale that will be realised through 2026 and beyond.
The business’ rate of customer acquisition continues to be rapid with the growing subscription revenue stream. The
demand for Full Fipre-to-the-Premises across the market towns served by the Group remains strong and revenues
continues to grow in the face of increasingly intense competition.
A number of towns have significant penetration levels, with customer take-up of over 40% in some older towns. The
average take-up across the entire network is over 14.3% at the end of the year.
The capital raising market remains extremely tight in the UK and the cost of capital available remains too high to
generate equity returns through construction of a new network. Accordingly, the focus remains on commercialising the
network towards profitability.
This revised focus has no impact on the operation of the existing network or the ability to deliver service to new or
existing customers. The group continues to invest in commercialisation activities in marketing, sales, customer service
and field service functions supporting the continued rapid customer growth whilst maintaining the service quality for our
customers which was recorded as a TrustPilot score of 4.5 at the end of the year.

Markets
Approximately 31 million properties exist in the UK, with 24 million having access to Full Fibre-to-the-Premises
broadband. Demand for high-speed, reliable internet continues to grow due to the increasing reliance on online
entertainment, communication, and multiple connected devices. As a result of ongoing investment, Fibre-to-the-
Premises availability in the UK has reached 76%. The consumer market has remained challenging with continued cost of
living pressures and increasing competition.
Network and infrastructure
The group builds and operates its own Full Fibre-to-the-Premises network, supporting up to 150 devices per connection
with symmetrical speeds ranging from 200Mbps to 2Gbps. Residential and most business services use XGS-PON
technology, capable of delivering up to 8.5Gbps to each property. For enterprises, the group provides high-capacity
dedicated services over 10Gbps or 100Gpps bearers. The network is built using a mix of underground ducts, utility poles,
and BT Openreach infrastructure (“PIA”}.
Construction
The group has ceased building new network coverage.

Investment in capital expenditure, predominately the cost of connecting customers, remains the most significant cost

Principat Risks and Uncertainties
General
The principal risks and uncertainties are summarised below. These do not necessarily comprise all of those that are
potentially faced by the Group and are not intended to be presented in any assumed order of priority. The Directors
believe that, in particular, readers of this report should be aware of these risks and uncertainties, and that the Directors
take reasonable steps to mitigate and minimise the impact of the risks on the Group. However, some of these risks
cannot be eliminated entirely without incurring costs that the Board considers to be excessive. If any of these risks and
uncertainties, together with possible additional risks and uncertainties of which the Directors are currently unaware or
which they consider not to be material in relation to the Group’s business, actually occur, the Group’s business,
financial position or operating results could be materially and adversely affected.
Our relatively small size and age in comparison to competitors may be considered negatively by prospective customers
who may wish to remain with an inferior system but an established brand name.
Our competitors may be able to offer products bundled with internet access such as on-demand, television style
services or fixed/mobile telecoms that are more attractive to the consumer.
A key risk to any Group in the Fibre-to-the-Premises industry will stem from any overbuild that could arise from
competitors. The Group has assessed overbuild risk based on consideration of Fibre-to-the-Premises operator build
rates in the UX, likely investment strategies and known movements of market ptayers, and likely broadband penetration
levels. We conclude that the risk of overbuild remains in line with our planned penetration and revenue generation
targets.
Funding
The business activity of the Group requires significant expenditure to fund investment in customer connections and
commercialisation and marketing. If the Group is not able to raise the financing required for the planned expenditures,
then the Group will have to reduce its planned expenditures or reduce the scale of its operations.
inflation
Throughout 2025, the ongoing cost of living crisis has continued to place financial pressure on customers, resulting in
heightened cost consciousness and more price-sensitive purchasing behaviour. This has introduced greater
competition for customers within the Broadband supplier base and greater salary and supply side cost pressure.
Financial Risk Management Objectives and Policies
Liquidity Risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the Group wilt not be able to meet its short-term financial obligation as they fall due. As part
of its overall prudent liquidity risk management, the Group actively manages its operating cash flows and does not
commit to expenditure that it does not have immediate access to settle.
Cashflow Risk
Cashflow risk is that the fair value or cash flows of financial instruments will fluctuate as a result of market forces outside
the control of the group, including interest rate risk arising from the effect of changes in market interest rates on cash at
bank, overdrafts, investments in debt instruments, borrowings and obligations under finance leases.

Going Concern

in assessing whether the going concern basis of accounting remains appropriate, the directors have considered the
Group's financial position, its forecast cash flows and its access to funding for a period of at least 12 months from the
date of approval of these financial statements (the “going concern assessment period").

The Group is currently dependent upon continued external funding to maintain and extend the network, to connect
customers, and to fund ongoing- operational costs, capital investment and loan interest. The Group is expected to
remain dependent on such funding until the number of customers connected to the network increases to a level at which
the resulting revenue is sufficient to cover the Group's operating costs, capital expenditure and financing costs. This
funding has, to date, been provided principally in the form of equity investment.

In September 2021, the Group entered into a debt facility with a syndicate of three banks (ING, Hamburg Commercial
Bank and Kommunalkredit) providing access to borrowings of up to £100m. At 31 December 2025, the Group had drawn
down the full £100m available under this facility (2024: £100m). The facility is due to be repaid or refinanced in
September 2027.

In March 2025, the Group completed a business combination between the Zzoomm Group Limited and the Iris Infra
Limited group of companies, resulting ina new parent company, Altnet Partners Holdco (UK) Limited, which is owned by
funds managed by Oaktree Capital Management and Basait Infrastructure Partners ("the Investors"). As at 31 December
2025, the Investors had invested a total of £440m of equity in the Group, in addition to the Group's £100m bank debt
facility.

The Group remains dependent on the provision of additional funding by the Investors throughout the going concern
assessment period. The Investors have provided- written confirmation of their intention to provide continued financial
support to the Group for at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements. The directors have a
reasonable expectation that this support will be provided, on the basis that the Investors have to date invested £440m of
equity inthe Group and have consistently provided the required financing in line with board~approved business plans,
and that they maintain close oversight of the Group through their representation on the Board of Directors.

The directors note that whilst the Group's investors have demonstrated support and committed to provide funding until
30 June 2027, this support does not extend until the date of the refinance of the toan. As aresult, the Group is dependent
on securing additional funding from new or existing investors, or alternative financing arrangements, beyond the
committed support period. There can be no assurance that such funding will be obtained on acceptable terms, or at all.
The directors further note that the existing £100m debt facility falls due for repayment or refinancing in September 2027.
These circumstances indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the Group's and the
Company's ability to continue as a going concern and, therefore, the Group and the Company may be unable to realise
their assets and discharge their liabilities in the normal course of business.

Having considered the Group's forecasts, the written confirmation of continued support from the Investors and the
Investors’ track record of providing funding to date, the directors have a reasonable expectation that the Group and the
Company have access to adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the going concern assessment
period. Accordingly, the directors continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing these financial
statements. The financial statements do not include any adjustments that would result if the going concern basis of
preparation were no longer appropriate.
