The directors present their strategic report together with the audited financial statements of IDT Retail Europe

Limited (the “company") for the year ended 31 July 2025.

Business review: Principal activities and future prospects

The company's principal activity continues to be the provision of International Telecommunications Services.

During the year the company offered Retail Communication Services to be used by the end user in the United

States only.

Effective 1 August 2021, BOSS Revolution Europe business was licensed to Interdirect Tel Limited (IDT's

subsidiary registered in Ireland). BOSS Revolution is a prepaid international long-distance calling service

marketed to foreign-born and under-banked consumers in the United States.

BOSS Revolution Calling is offered through our digital channels - the BOSS Revolution Calling app (for iOS and

Android) and website. BOSS Revolution allows users to place international long-distance calls at affordable rates

from the BOSS Revolution app or by calling an access number. Regardless of how the call originates, our

customers must first establish and top-up a prepaid BOSS Revolution account that is linked to their phone.

The company's results show total profit this year of £1.5m compared to last year's profit of £1.1m, after foreign

exchange losses of £0.6m in 2025 and foreign exchange gains of £0.1m in 2024. Shareholders’ funds show a
. positive position of £7.9m compared to last year’s of £6.4m. The number of employees decreased from 4 to 3

during the year.

The company continues to develop its prepaid product offerings by introducing complementary products such as

international and domestic airtime top-up, to offer its product suite in multiple markets, and through increasing

the sales channels for its products, focused on Boss sales B2C. The Boss telecoms product will continue to be

sold directly to the end user in the US only. Effective 1 August 2021, BOSS Revolution Calling'’s margin

contribution has benefited from a gradual rotation to digital channels with higher per-unit margins. These trends

and cost saving efforts have, in recent quarters, more than offset the revenue and margin contribution pressures

of the paid minute voice communications market.

BOSS Revolution Calling is subject to fierce competition, and we do not expect to grow revenues and/or margins

without a successful strategy and sound execution. While virtually any company offering communication services

is a competitor, we face particularly strong competition from Tier 1 mobile network operators who offer flat-rate

international calling plans, other PIN-less prepaid voice offerings, prepaid calling card providers, mobile virtual

network operators, and VoIP and other “over the top”, or OTT, service providers. We also compete with large

state-owned or state-sanctioned telephone companies.

Going concern

The company has recorded an operating profit of £2.3m in current year (2024 - £1.5m) and has net assets of

£7.9m as at 31 July 2025 (2024 - £6.4m). The company is in a profit position and the directors have taken steps

to satisfy themselves as to the viability of the company such that the preparation of these accounts on the going

concern basis is appropriate.

Although the company has no external debt and there is no need for any additional cash injection from IDT

Telecom Inc., the company’s parent company, the directors recognise that the company is reliant on the

continuing support of IDT Telecom Inc. in respect to them not recalling the £3.6m held as current liability as

disclosed in note 11. After making enquiries, having considered the letter of support received from IDT Telecom

Inc., and having considered the company's medium-term plans, the directors have concluded that they have a

reasonable expectation that the company has adequate resources to continue operations for the foreseeable

future. The going concern basis for the accounts has therefore continued to be adopted.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Business risk
Our product offerings are highly sensitive to declining prices, which may adversely affect our revenues and
profitability.
BOSS Revolution is subject to fierce competition. While virtually any company offering communication services
is a competitor, we face particularly strong competition from Tier 1 mobile network operators who offer flat-rate
international calling plans, other PIN-less prepaid voice offerings, prepaid calling card providers, mobile virtual
network operators, and VoIP and other "over the top”, or OTT, service providers. The continued growth of OTT
calling and messaging services such as WhatsApp, Messenger, FaceTime, and others have adversely affected
the sales of BOSS Revolution and our other prepaid calling services. We expect the popularity of these IP-based
services—many of which offer free voice and/or video communications—to continue to increase, which will
increase substitution for, and pricing pressure on, our BOSS Revolution. However, free services typically require
both the caller and recipient to have a broadband connection. BOSS Revolution utilizes telephone networks to
enable voice communications even when neither party has broadband connectivity.

Section 172 statement

This section describes how the directors have had regard to the matters set out in section 172(1) (a) to (f)
Companies Act 2006 in exercising their duty to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its
members as a whole. In doing so the directors of a company must have regard (amongst other matters) to:

a. The likely consequences of any decision in the long term;

b. The interests of the company's employees;

Cc. The need to foster the company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;

d. The impact of the company’s operations on the community and the environment;

e. The desirability of the company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct; and

f. The need to act fairly as between members of the company.

The following summarises how the company's Board fulfils its duties under Section 172:

During the year, the Board considered information from across the organisation to understand the interests of
our stakeholders. The directors delegate authority for day-to-day management of the company to executives and
then engage management in setting, approving, and overseeing the execution of the strategy.

The Board remains mindful that its strategic decisions can have long term implications for the business and its
stakeholders, and these implications are carefully assessed. The directors recognise that the decisions they
make today will affect the long-term success of IDT Retail Europe.

The Board prides itself on its long history of honesty and integrity in its business dealings. It recognises the
importance of operating a robust corporate governance framework.

The company has long term shareholders of IDT who are Board members and active working directors. This
close involvement assists greatly in ensuring that their interests are not only aligned but also addressed in an
effective manner. :

Our workforce is our most valuable asset. We aim to be a responsible employer in our approach to the pay and
benefits of employees. Flexible schedules are offered which help balance work and family life for mothers and
fathers who request it providing equal opportunities. Based on knowledge gained through the pandemic, IDT
provide flexibility on how and where employees work. Home working or hybrid working including two to three
days a week working from the office (depending on the role) and the support for home office equipment.
Remote/hybrid working leverages the opportunity to reduce long-term emissions generated through employee
travel.

The Board takes active steps to ensure that the suggestion, views, and interests of staff members are gathered
and considered in its decision making by maintaining regular communication, attendance, and meetings with
branch staff. Employees are kept informed of performance and strategy through regular presentations and
updates from members of the Board followed by Q&A sessions. Feedback from employees is actively
encouraged. Professional and personal development of employees is viewed as fundamental to the continued
success of the company.

As members of the Board work within the business this ensures regular dialogue with customers, suppliers, and
other stakeholders. The Board recognises that as a telecom company it is crucial to offer competitive rates as
well as high-quality connections. Building strong relationships with suppliers enables the company to obtain the
best value, service, and quality. Our commercial and operations teams work hard to develop deeper and more
strategic relationships with key suppliers. Customers are central to the business, and we aim to deliver a
consistently high performance in an efficient and continuously improving way to meet the customer's needs. For
many of our customers, particularly the major carriers, we engage in buy-sell relationships, terminating their
customer's traffic in exchange for terminating our traffic with them. Account management and commercial teams
hold meetings with customers and providers to obtain feedback from them. Those teams regularly performance
reviews which are discussed with the directors to take the best decision for the benefit of the company and its
stakeholders.

2.3 Going concern
The company has recorded an operating profit of £2.3m in current year (2024 - £1.5m) and has net
assets of £7.9m as at 31 July 2025 (2024 - £6.4m). The company is in a profit position and the
directors have taken steps to satisfy themselves as to the viability of the company such that the
preparation of these accounts on the going concern basis is appropriate.
Although the company has no external.debt and there is no need for any additional cash injection
from IDT Telecom Inc., the company’s parent company, the directors recognise that the company is
reliant on the continuing support of IDT Telecom Inc. in respect to them not recalling the £3.6m held
as current liability as disclosed in note 11. After making enquiries, having considered the letter of
support received from IDT Telecom Inc, and having considered the company's medium-term plans,
the directors have concluded that they have a reasonable expectation that the company has
adequate resources to continue operations for the foreseeable future. The going concern basis for
the accounts has therefore continued to be adopted.
Our employees transitioned to work from home during financial year 2020 and, to a large degree,
continue to work-from-home. In 2025, employees are allowed the option of home-working, or hybrid-
working including two to three days a week working from the office (depending on the role) and the
support for home office equipment.
We continue to implement strong physical and cyber-security measures to ensure our systems
remain functional to both serve our operational needs with a remote workforce and to provide
uninterrupted service to our customers.
As of the date of the approval of these financial statements, the management believes that we
continue to have sufficient liquidity and capital resources for the foreseeable future. Over the longer
term, sustained levels of high unemployment along with declining economic activity and less
favourable foreign exchange market conditions could impact us by dampening demand for our retail
offerings but management continue to monitor these risks and consider they have currently been
sufficiently mitigated.

2.4 Turnover .
Turnover represents the revenue recognised in respect of prepaid telephone services exclusive of
value-added tax and also net of discounts and distributor commissions.
BOSS Revolution Calling are sold direct-to-consumers and it is offered on a pay-as-you-go basis or
in unlimited plans. The customer prepays for service in both cases, which results in a contract liability
(deferred revenue). The contract term for pay-as-you- go plans is minute-to-minute services and the
company recognises revenue at the point in time when minutes are utilised. For unlimited plans, the
company recognised revenue based on the services provided over time for an agreed term.
Unlimited plans include fixed consideration over the term.
Deferred revenue is recognised in the balance sheet based on unused minutes on activated pins
(see note 2.12).

2.11 Current and deferred taxation
The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Statement
of comprehensive income except that a charge attributable to an item of income and expense
recognised as other comprehensive income or to an item recognised directly in equity is also
recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been -
enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date in the countries where the company
operates and generates income.
Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not
reversed by the balance sheet date, except that:
. the recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will be

recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits; and
° any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
allowances have been met. .

Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of
business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair values of
assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences between the fair
values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax. Deferred tax is determined
using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.

2.12 Deferred revenue
Deferred revenue for future usage of top-up payments is recognised as a liability on the balance
sheet. The deferred revenue is released to the Statement of comprehensive income upon usage by
the end users or an expiry of unused balances of end users and then recorded as revenue.

2.13 Provisions
Provisions are recognised when the company has a present obligation (legal or constructive) as a
result of a past event, it is probable that the company will be required to settle the obligation, and a
reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation.
The amount recognised as a provision is the best estimate of the consideration required to settle the
present obligation at the end of the reporting period, taking into account the risks and uncertainties
surrounding the obligation.
