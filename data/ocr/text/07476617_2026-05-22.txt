Strategic Report
Introduction
Gigaclear Limited ("Gigaclear") has been designing, building, and operating new gigabit, full fibre networks since 2011
during which time it has firmly established itself as the largest rural, full fibre alternative network (altnet) in the UK.
With its headquarters in Abingdon, Oxfordshire the company employs c.600 people, serving more than 160,000 customers
across 26 counties. Gigaclear's vision is to be the best rural fibre provider in England, using its network to generate economic
and social value for the communities and customers it serves.
Business Review ;
The capital market in the altnet sectors has proven to be tightening and challenging in 2024 through 2025 and the market
has reached a strategic pivot point, shifting from large build volumes to an increased emphasis on revenue generated by
connecting customers.
To combat the challenges ahead, Gigaclear has continued its attention to delivering to the most rural communities and
slowing down its capital expenditure investment in network infrastructure in 2025 and beyond. Consequently, Gigaclear has
downsized its build team to match the reduced build volumes saving significant costs. These actions have helped to preserve
cash and provide sufficient liquidity to raise the super senior facility in November 2025.
Gigaclear, as one of the key leaders in the altnet market, seeks to stay ahead of the market changes and has been focusing
on customers experience in 2025. To drive this, Gigaclear overhauled its onboarding journey by seizing the opportunity of
One Touch Switch, to provide a smooth onboarding experience to all our new customers. Reengineered our repair and
maintenance approaches to provide excellent customer service in life which supports our recontract process and growing
revenues from existing customers while managing our costs. These changes have delivered strong Trustpilot scores.
Gigaclear's culture of continuous improvement and investments in key systems has enhanced customer experience to alll
our customers, the results being proved by customer additions increasing by 23%.
Despite the headwinds in the capital market of altnet sector, Gigaclear entered into productive dialogue with its lenders,
who remained supportive of the business throughout the process, to look for a longer-term solution to the Company's liquidity.
In this regard, a recapitalisation transaction has been agreed with the lenders, which culminated in the entry into the Lock-
up Agreement (“LUA") in November 2025. The LUA provides a dual-track framework to allow for M&A processes or lender-
led transactions.
As part of the dialogue with Gigaclear’s lenders, Gigaclear secured a £80,000,000 Super Senior Facility, with a further
£20,000,000 of uncommitted funding available under the Super Senior Facility in December 2025. This enabled Gigaclear to
secure adequate funding requirements and expected to be fully funded in the first quarter of 2027.
In January 2026, the lenders agreed not to pursue an M&A process at this stage and to proceed with an agreed consensual
lender led transaction. In April 2026, the recapitalisation of Gigaclear has been completed and was unanimously supported
by our existing lenders and major shareholders. As a result, ownership of Gigaclear has now transferred to a consortium of
experienced institutions, including the National Wealth Fund, ABN AMRO and NatWest. This involves a substantial
deleveraging and reduces the debt service requirement (note 32).
Financial Review
The year ended 31 December 2025 saw Gigaclear's revenues increase from £47.2m to £64.9m, an increase of 37%. This was
driven by the continued growth in customer numbers joining our network throughout the year.
The loss for the year, excluding fair value change of interest rate swaps, narrowed to £152.0m from £242.6m primarily due to
no impairment of property, plant and equipment in 2025 (2024: £110.2m) {refer to note 14 for details). This was offset by higher
finance costs contributed by an increase in net debt, more deprecation from the expanding network investment,
improvement in revenue from increased customer numbers together with better network cost efficiency. No impairment of
property, plant and equipment is considered necessary in 2025 as there is no material change in assumptions in the
assessment, e.g. ARPU, no. of customers, etc.
Gigaclear continues to draw on its debt facilities to fund its operations and grow its network. Total drawn debt at year-end
was £974.6m (2024: £912.9m). After including transaction costs capitalised, accrued interest and subsequent remeasurement
of debt by effective interest rate method against the total drawn debt, the bank borrowings give rise to £1,024.9m (2024:
£914.2m). This increased the net debt from £870.2m to £997.9m. The increase primarily funded the addition of property, plant
and equipment, mainly the broadband network, of £72.46m and operating costs in 2025.

Engaging with Suppliers
Gigaclear manages a significant amount of procurement across all parts of its business and seeks to maintain high standards
in its dealings with suppliers. The professional procurement function supports Gigaclear's engagement with its supply chain
and seeks to drive efficiency, cost reduction and reliability as well as offering suppliers a consistent and well managed service
with which to do business. 2025 has seen a reduction in the volume of network build when compared to the previous
year. Those suppliers engaged in new network build have been required to ramp down delivery levels, with management
seeking to achieve this in a collaborative way which supports the supply chain in managing its forward planning. In contrast,
suppliers engaged in customer installations have been required to maintain performance in volume terms, to support the
continuous increase in customer numbers. Improvements in efficiency have been a key requirement through the year to drive
improvements in the time between a customer placing an order for service and being installed and activated.
Engaging with Employees
Gigaclear headcount remained stable with an increase of 7 employees to 616 people at the end of 2025. During the year
the teams delivering the new network build were organised to ensure that provide clear focus on completing our Project
Gigabit contracts, as well as continuing to build our ‘ultra rural’ commercial builds. At the same time there was investment
and growth in the teams who support getting customers live on our network. Our people goal for 2025 to increase
collaboration and teamworking across the company for the benefit of our customers as well as the satisfaction of our
employees. 76% of employees responding to our annual engagement survey replied positively to the statement that we work
together to proudly make things easy for our customers.
Key workstreams across our people in 2025 included the introduction of a 12 month leadership development programme for
our senior leaders which has been very positively met, an engaging calendar of events focusing on health and wellbeing
across all our employees, some great end of year celebrations in recognition of our milestone achievements in 2025 and we
finished the year by successfully applying for certification by the Top Employers Institute.
Engaging with Customers and Communities :
Gigaclear realised its ambitious plans to increase its revenue and penetration in 2025, and finished the year with over 164,000
customers. This 23% growth in customers resulted from strong ongoing sales and marketing execution of ‘rural champion’ retail
brand as well as a step up in installation performance.
Improvements in the customer installation journey were borne out of clear feedback from customers giving a renewed focus
for both internal and external resources on achieving successful and efficient installations. In addition the business launched
One Touch Switching as part of an industry wide initiative to make it easier for customers to switch between providers.
2025 also saw significant improvements in the service provided to existing customers. Customer service has extended its
opening hours, invested in dedicated teams for technical support and business customers, and focused on ‘first contact
resolution’ to get it right first time for customers. Customers are kept informed with a customer magazine and have the
opportunity to give feedback through regular customer surveys. Gigaclear's retail loyalty team has a successful record of
customer retention and overall, Gigaclear's scoring on Trustpilot reached an all-time high of 4.7 during 2025 and its customer
Net Promoter Score improved to a healthy +35.
Environment Social and Governance
The safety of workers, employees and members of the public we interact with is a priority in all Gigaclear's activities and we
take our responsibilities to the protection of the environment and the wider community seriously. Gigaclear has policies on
reducing the impact of its activities on the environment and is developing a long term carbon reduction plan and; it also sets
out comprehensive rules and requirements for its supply chain to follow enforced by regular and rigorous audits and
inspections. 2025 saw significant progress in establishing a methodology for determining its ‘built? network carbon emissions
for its Scope 3 reporting and significant progress in recycling or reuse of 100% of its waste across all Gigaclear activities.
Gigaclear also seeks to have a positive social and economic impact on the communities we operate in and serve, both in
our mission to be the best rural fibre provider in England but also through providing employment and development
opportunities in the community we operate in through the provision of work experience placements, supporting careers
events in local schools, supporting 17 apprenticeship opportunities across 6 different pathways as well as initiatives within our
rural Communities such as sponsorship programmes for rural sports teams, community engagement events and free
connections for local community buildings.

2 Going concern
The ability of the Company to continue as a going concern is dependent on the ability of the Company to generate
sufficient cash flows, and have the necessary funds, to meet the requirements of the Company's operations to settle
its liabilities as and when they fall due. To assess the Company's status as a going concern, the Directors assessed the
Company's ability to fund its business for 12 months from the signing of these accounts. As part of this process, the
Directors considered the Company's key factors relevant to the Company's viability as a going concern and prepared
acash flow forecast for the review period.
The Company's primary operational focus has been in providing broadband services to existing customers,
onboarding new customers and investing in its network infrastructure. These rely on its ability to raise its funds through
debt and equity to satisfy its cash requirements. As such, the Company would require additional funding before it
becomes cash generative.
To provide a longer-term solution to the Company's liquidity position, the Company entered into productive dialogue
with its lenders, who were supportive of the business throughout the process, in relation to a recapitalisation transaction,
which culminated in the entry into the Lock-up Agreement ("LUA") being executed on 5 November 2025, between
the Company and all of the tenders.
As part of the LUA framework, lenders and the Company entered into a Super Senior Facility Agreement ("SSFA”) on 2
December 2025, which provided an £80,000,000 facility to the business, with a further £20,000,000 of uncommitted
funding available under the facility. This has been implemented by an Amendment and Restatement Deed ("ARD")
on 2 December 2025 to the existing debt facility signed on December 2023, establishing the SSFA and amending
relevant financial covenants.
In mid-January 2026, the lenders determined that the M&A processes would not be pursued and instead a lender-led
solution would be implemented to facilitate its transition to lender ownership, and the SSFA remains available.
On 8 April 2026, the recapitatisation of Gigaclear was completed and was unanimously supported by our existing
lenders and shareholders. As a result, ownership of Gigaclear has now transferred to a consortium of experienced
institutional investors, including the National Wealth Fund, ABN AMRO and NatWest. This involves a substantial
deleveraging and reduces the debt service requirement to the principal amount of £575m which will transform the net
current liabilities position into net asset position.
Management have produced detailed cash flow forecasts, which project that with the £80,000,000 available under
the previously agreed SSFA, the terms of which remain unchanged from the recapitalisation, the Company has
adequate cashflow up to the first quarter of 2027 and by then the business will be cash generative.
The key financial covenant under the amended facilities is the minimum liquidity requirement. This requires the
Company to maintain not less than £10,000,000 of cash and available facilities under the SSFA at the applicable test
date. The finance documents also include interest cover ratio, leverage ratio and “premises connected” financial
covenants, but these ratios will not be tested until 30 June 2028. With that in mind, sensitivity analysis has been
performed on ARPU change by 10%. The results demonstrate it would fall below the £10,000,000 minimum liquidity
threshold required during the going concern period. This risk could be mitigated via liaising with the lenders (who are
also the shareholders) to further drawdown the £20,000,000 of uncommitted funding potentially available under the
SSFA or to agree a covenant relief or deferral event or even further funding, and as such management recognises this

. creates an inherent material uncertainty.

The Company makes use of a simplified approach in accounting for trade and other receivables and records the loss

the potential for default at any point during the life of the financial instrument. In calculating expected losses, the
Company uses its historical experience, external indicators and forward-looking information to calculate the expected
credit losses using a provision matrix.

The Company assesses impairment of trade receivables on a collective basis (as they possess shared credit risk
characteristics) and are grouped based on days past due. Note 28 provides a detailed analysis of how the impairment
requirements of IFRS 9 are applied.

Leases
The Company considers whether a contract is, or contains a lease, at inception of the contract. A lease is defined as
a ‘contract, or part of a contract, which conveys the right to use an asset (the underlying asset) for a period of time
in exchange for consideration’. To apply this definition the Company assesses whether the contract meets three key
evaluations which are whether:
e the contract contains an identified asset, which is either explicitly identified in the contract or implicitly
specified by being identified at the time the asset is made available to the Company:
e the Company has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from use of the identified asset
throughout the period of use, considering its rights within the defined scope of the contract;

e the Company has the right to direct the use of the identified asset throughout the period of use.
Measurement and recognition of leases as a lessee
At lease commencement date, the Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability on the balance
sheet. The right-of-use asset is measured at cost, which is made up of the initial measurement of the lease liability, any
initial direct costs incurred by the Company, an estimate of any costs to dismantle and remove the asset at the end
of the lease, and any lease payments made in advance of the lease commencement date (net of any incentives
received).
The Company depreciates the right-of-use assets on a straight-line basis from the lease commencement date to the
earlier of the end of the useful life of the right-of-use asset or the end of the lease term. If a lease transfers ownership
of the underlying asset or the cost of the right-of-use asset reflects that the Company expects to exercise a purchase
option, the related right-of-use asset is depreciated over the useful life of the underlying asset. The Company also
assesses the right-of-use asset for impairment when such indicators exist.
At the commencement date, the Company measures the lease liability at the present value of the lease payments
unpaid at that date, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease if that rate is readily available or the
Company's incremental borrowing rate.
Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability are made up of fixed payments (including in
substance fixed), variable payments based on an index or rate, amounts expected to be payable under a residual
value guarantee and payments arising from options reasonably certain to be exercised.
After initial measurement, the liability will be reduced for payments made and increased for interest. It is remeasured
to reflect any reassessment or modification, or if there are changes in in-substance fixed payments.
When the lease liability is remeasured, the corresponding adjustment is reflected in the right-of-use asset, or profit and
loss if the right-of-use asset is already reduced to zero.
The Company has elected to account for short-term leases and leases of low-value assets using the practical
expedients. Instead of recognising a right-of-use asset and lease liability, the payments in relation to these are
recognised as an expense in profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

Revenue
Revenue consists of internet connectivity fees, customer connection fees, commission earned on the sale of VOIP

. services to customers by an external service provider and other income from the sale of stock and network build
carried out for customers who do not have an ongoing contract with the Company.
To determine whether to recognise revenue, the Company follows a 5-step process:
] Identifying the contract with a customer
2 Identifying the performance obligations
3 Determining the transaction price
4 Allocating the transaction price to the performance obligations
5 Recognising revenue when/as performance obligations(s) are satisfied.
Revenue is recognised either at a point in time or over time, when (or as) the Company satisfies performance
obligations by transferring the promised goods or services to its customers.
The Company recognises contract liabilities for consideration received in respect of unsatisfied performance
obligations and reports these amounts as other liabilities in the statement of financial position (see Note 24). Similarly,
if the Company satisfies a performance obligation before it receives the consideration, the Company recognises
either a contract asset or a receivable in its statement of financial position, depending on whether something other
than the passage of time is required before the consideration is due.
internet connectivity fees :
Revenue from the monthly fees charged to customers for providing access to the internet is recognised in line with the
time period for which the connection is provided. Fees are charged and payable monthly in advance and revenue
for dates beyond the current accounting period is reported in the statement of financial position as a contract liability.
Customer connection fees
Connection fees are one-off fees charged to customers at the outset of the contract to cover set up and installation
services. These fees are included in the client's first invoice and are payable together with the first monthly connectivity
fee. As such, the Company judges that connection services form a part of a bundle of services to deliver internet
connectivity services. The revenue from the connection fees is therefore recognised over the same time period as the
customer's internet connectivity services contract. Revenue which relates to the portion of the customer's contract
which is beyond the current accounting period is reported in the statement of financial position as a contract liability.
‘Customer contracts vary in length, the standard residential contract is 18 months, business contract is 24 months, and
wholesale customer contract is 12 months.
VOIP Commission
VOIP services are provided to the Company's customers by an external service provider. The Company receives
commission for each customer who subscribes to a VOIP service. The commission is received monthly in arrears and
is recognised in the period for which the commission is paid.
Other Income
Other income relates to sales of stock and network build activity carried out for customers who do not have a contract
with the Company for the provision of ongoing services. The revenue is recognised in the financial statements when
the stock has been transferred or the building work is complete.
