STRATEGIC REPORT FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024
Pursuant to the Companies Act 2006 (Strategic Report and Directors’ Report) Regulations 2013, Avanti HYLAS 2 Limited (the
"“Company") and the Directors present a Strategic Report.
Principal Activities
The Company is a part of the overall Avanti Group (the "Group", defined as Plate Newco 3 Limited and its subsidiaries) which
has a strategy of commercially exploiting its space assets, namely its spectrum rights, satellites, intellectual property and ground -
station assets. The Company owns and operates the HYLAS 4 satellite, and has a wholly owned subsidiary which owns and
: operates the HYLAS 2 satellite. Revenue is generated from the commercial exploitation of the Company's space and network
assets. These assets include its spectcum rights, satellites, intellectual property and ground station assets.
Financial Review
The 2023 financial year saw a significant improvement in the revenue profile of the Company. Revenue from legacy, low priced
: wholesale deals and from customers with poor payment performance was removed from the Company's network. This revenue
was replaced by new contracts signed in the second half of 2022 from cruise end users in particular and high quality
counterparties.
2024, the year of this Annual Report, was a more challenging year for the Company financially with significant competitive
disruption in the cruise market leading to a significant reduction in revenue and EBITDA. The Company's projects and plans to
rationalise and restructure its cost base to better reflect the strategy it has been pursuing since 2022 were further encouraged by
the disruption in 2024 and the Group's Flex-Geo offering coming to fruition. However, these initiatives had not yet taken full
effect and therefore continued to weigh on the Company's in-year performance in 2024.
The 2025 financial year (unaudited) has seen a strong improvement in financial performance. This has been driven by the full .
year impact of significant cost reductions that were largely delivered in the final quarter of 2024, growing revenues from
enterprise and backhaul customers in Western and Southern Africa and certain one-off BIU and enterprise revenues generated
by the Company. The early months of the 2026 financial year have seen continued commercial momentum and ongoing benefit
of the leaner cost base carried by the Company.
Financial Results :
The financial position and results for the year ended 31 December 2024 are shown on pages 9 and 10.
Revenue in the year decreased by $6.8m to $21.2m (2023: $28.0m). Cost of sales, staff costs, and operating expenses increased
by $6.3m to $48.7m. EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation) has improved from negative
$65.0m to negative $7.6m, driven by a $50.7m expected credit loss against intercompany receivables in the prior year.
The primary asset on the Comapny’s balance sheet is the HYLAS 4 satellite. Each year the Company considers the carrying value
of its assets and looks for indications of impairment. 2024 was a challenging trading year and the disruption in revenues seen in
the Company's cruise business impacted the HYLAS 4 satellite. This led to the impairment review, based on the facts and
circumstances as at 31 December 2024, resulting in an impairment charge of $52.0m. The Company made a loss before tax in
the year of $90.8m compared with a loss before tax of $92.3m in the previous year.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties

The Company faces a number of risks and uncertainties that may adversely affect its business, operations, liquidity, financial

position and future performance.

The principal business risks and uncertainties faced by the Company relate to the stability of the markets in which HYLAS 4 and

HYLAS 2 operate, as well as competition from similar operators in the industry. The Company also faces a number of ongoing

operational risks including credit and foreign exchange risk.

To the extent that these risks are under managements control, the directors aim to manage these risks and uncertainties going

forwards in order to maintain and improve on the current level of performance.

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of financial loss arising from a counterparty’s inability to repay or service debt in accordance with

contractual terms. Credit risk includes the direct risk of default and the risk of deterioration of creditworthiness. The Company
. . primarily generates its revenue by selling capacity on its satellite to other companies within the Group in order to service third

party customer contracts entered into by those entities. Those entities assess the credit quality of theic major customers before

trading commences, taking into account customers’ financial position, past experience and other factors.

Liquidity risk

The Company finances its business through operating cash flow. The Company is also dependent on Plate Newco 3 Limited,

through Plate Bidco 4 Limited, not seeking repayment of the amounts currently due and providing additional financial support

to the Group if necessary. At the date of approval of these financial statements amounts due to Plate Bidco 4 Limited amounted

to $308,651k. Plate Bidco 4 Limited has indicated that it does not intend to seek repayment of the amounts due at the balance

sheet date, for the forseeable future. See the Basis of Preparation in Note 1 for further details.

Global economy

The global economy remains fragile and it continues to be difficult to predict customer demand. The Group is susceptible to

decreased growth rates within high growth markets and/or continued economic and market downturn in developing markets.

The effects could lead to a decline in demand and deteriorating financial results, which in turn could result in the Group not

realising its financial targets.

Foreign exchange risk

The Company has foreign currency transactions and is exposed to foreign exchange risk arising from various currency

exposures, primarily with respect to the pound Sterling and the Euro. In order to mitigate the foreign currency risk, the Group

to which Avanti HYLAS 2 Limited belongs monitors the level at which natural hedges occur and continually reviews the need to

enter into forward contracts in order to mitigate any material forecast exposure. The Company's reported results of operations

and financial condition are affected by exchange rate fluctuations due to both transaction and translation risks.

Interest rate risk

The Company’s main source of loan finance is interest free intercompany loans from other entities in the Group. Cash and

deposits earn interest at fixed rates based on banks’ short-term treasury deposit rates. Short-term trade and other receivables are

interest free. As a result, interest rate risk is not currently a significant consideration.

Going concern
The Company is a member of Plate Newco 3 Limited Group (the Group) and is reliant on Plate Newco 3 Limited for funding and not seeking
repayment of the amounts due at year end. In reaching their assessment, the Directors have also considered the fact that in addition to the inter-
company liability that is repayable on demand, the Avanti business manages its cash flows at a Group level. Therefore, the Group, Plate Newco 3
Limited and each of the other subsidiary entities are reliant upon each other for funding being made available as required to be able to meet liabilities
as they fall due.
On 2 April 2024 the holders of the Super Senior Lacility (“SSL”) exercised a call option over the cquity shares in the Group. Ac the date of signing
these financial statements, the Company’s ultimate parent undertaking, Plate New ‘Topco Limited, was 100% owned and controlled by HPS
Investment Partners LLC (“TIPS”), the holder of the SSF. As a result, 100% of the Group’s secured financial debt is owned and controlled by TIPS.
Teis the Directors’ belicf that, given the holder of the SSI is also the sole equity owner of the Group, it is unlikely the SSI will be callcd in the short
to medium term. In the unlikely event the debt is called, the Group would have insufficient resources to repay the SSF at short notice, and would
need to immediately seek alternative sources of financing. In that unlikely scenario, there can be no certainty of alternative sources of financing being
found, and ths represents a material uncertainty over the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern.
In performing the going concern assessment, the Directors have prepared cash flow forecasts for the Group, covering a period to July 2027.
The Group currently meets its day to day working capital requirements from operating cash flow, and as at the date of signing these financial
statements has sufficient cash resources to meet its trading needs for the next 12 months should the business fail to substantially meet the Business
Plan.
At the date of approval of these financial statements, Plate Newco 3 Limited has provided a letter to the Company indicating that it will continue to

HYLAS 4 satellite impairment review
HYLAS 4 is a 32 GHz Ka-band High Throughput Satellite that came into operational service on 1 September 2018, and acquired by the Group in April
2022. .
Each year the Group considers the carrying value of its assets and looks for indications of impairment. HYLAS 4 is the largest satellite in the Avanti fleet.
2024 was a challenging trading year and the disruption in revenues experienced by the Group, particularly in its cruise business solely impacted the HYLAS
4 satellite. Therefore management have assessed this to be an indicator of impairment of the asset at 31 December 2024, and have performed a full
impairment assessment.
The review showed that an impairment of $56.8m was required as at 31 December 2024 to bring the carrying value of HYLAS 4 and associated ground
infrastructure, considered together as the CGU, to $109.4m.
The recoverable amount of each asset is based on the value in use, which is determined using cash Now projections derived from the most recent financial
budgets and forecasts approved by management covering the remaining useful life of the asset. The cash flows reflect management’s expectations of future
outcomes taking into account past experience, adjusted for anticipated growth from both existing and new business in line with our strategic plans for each
sector in which we operate. The cash flows also take into consideration our assessment of the potential impact of external economic factors.
Forecasts are driven by the following key assumptions:
1. Revenue - The discounted cash flow forecast assumes continuity of existing contracted revenue, and modest pipeline in the form of steerable beam sales,
and ramp up of revenues under existing customer contracts.
2. Satellite life - The discounted cash flow forecast is prepared over the estimated remaining useful economic life of the asset of 8.5 years
3. Discount rate - Management estimates discount rates using pre-tax rates that reflect the current market assessment of the time value of money and the
risks specific to the industry. The impairment is based upon a pre-tax weighted average cost of capital of 16.0%.
Sensitivity analysis was carried out by management over the assumptions made in the impairment model relating to revenue and the discount factor
applied. The sensitivities applied were based upon reasonably possible changes in the key assumptions and performed as a part of the impairment exercise
in order to provide insight into the sensitivity of the impairment charge to those changes.
> A 10% increase in forecast annual revenues would reduce the impairment by $15.3m. A 10% decrease in the forecast annual revenues would increase the
impairment by $15.3m.
> ‘the Group’s WACC was derived with reference to the Group’s incremental borrowing cost and cost of equity as assessed by the market. A 2% increase
in discount factor applied would increase the impairment by $7.4m. A 2% decrease in discount factor applied would decrease the impairment by $8.3m.
