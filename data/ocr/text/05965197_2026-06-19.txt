The directors present their strategic report on the Vodacom Business Africa Group Services Limited for the year ended 34 March 2025. .
Introduction and strategy
Vodacom Business Africa Group Services Limited ("VBAGS” or "the Company”) offers a range of connectivity services to multinational
corporations and large and medium-sized businesses operating in Africa. It is wholly owned by Vodacom (UK) Limited.
Vodacom Business Africa Group ("Group"), as well as Vodafone Carrier Services ("VCS") and Vodafone Group Enterprise ("VGE") within the
Vodafone Business, are focused on the launch of the billing hub.
The vision of the Group is to become the preferred Vodafone Group vehicle for delivering fixed enterprise services across Africa. The Company
has continued to focus on integrating and leveraging the assets across the continent. This will be undertaken along a horizontal axis where the
products and services will be homogenised along the access layer between Operational Companies (OpCo's). This will improve the user
experience, simplify pricing and reduce management complexity. In terms of a vertical axis, the products and services sets will be deepened to
further build and deliver value added services onto the unified access layer. This will entail cloud services, machine-to-machine (M2M) and
industry solutions including point of sale (POS) and education. The relationship between the Group, VCS and VGE will be simplified and enhanced
through an agreed governance structure to (a) enable VGE to lead large continental and multinational corporation (MNC) sales and (b) utilise the
capability of VCS to provide differentiated solutions in terms of capacity and price for international connectivity. VGE will lead the sales process,
the Group will develop and retain the customer facing relationship through delivery, while VCS will support both in terms of pricing and carrier
capacity management.
Operating & business review
Revenue for the year was USD 18.3 million (2024: USD 19.3 million). The loss for the year was USD 10.8 million largely due to regulatory costs
and the reversal of the deferred tax asset (2024: profit of USD 5.5 million). They represent the key financial indicators of the Company. Further
information on the activities and financial position of the Company are presented in the annual financial statements and notes thereto.
Key performance indicators
The purpose of VBAGS is to deliver fixed enterprise services across African markets. The key performance indicators for VBAGS in the current
year are Revenue of USD 18.3 million (2024: USD 19.3 million) and Loss for the year of USD 10.8 million (2024: Profit of USD 5.5 million). In the
2025 financial year, revenue decreased due to a reduction in activity levels compared to the prior period, and the net loss for the year was largely
driven by regulatory costs and the reversal of the deferred tax asset. Management's strategy to achieve the KPI's in the 2025 financial year was to
focus on increasing sales effectiveness and expansion. This was overseen by the Head of Sales. Each Sales team member had a target to
achieve incremental sales in specific routes to the various markets.
Furthermore, management will focus on new digital revenue streams like Internet of Things (‘loT'). To manage direct costs, a focused team has
actively monitored the core network for performance optimization, thus enabling VBAGS to avoid customer Service level agreements (‘SLA’) cost.
This has also enabled the Company to actively manage its cost base positively.
Principal risks & uncertainties
Possible key factors and uncertainties that could have a significant effect on the Company's financial performance include adverse changes in
macroeconomic conditions (reduced consumer spending, higher interest rates, adverse inflation or movements in foreign exchange rates) and
adverse market conditions (significant activity by competition, such as price wars and new market entrants). The business continued to operate
effectively and is recognised as an essential telecommunication service provider with a footprint in 47 countries across the globe and can leverage
‘on that advantage to get the best pricing for its offerings in the connectivity and digital space.

1.4 Impairment of non-financial assets

An impairment toss is recognised immediately in profit or loss if the recoverable amount of an asset is less than its carrying
amount. Recoverable amount is the higher of an asset's fair value less costs of disposal and value in use. In assessing value
in use, the estimated future cash flows from continuing use and ultimate disposal of the asset are discounted to their present
value using a pre tax discount rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific
to the asset for which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.

Assets that do not generate cash inflows largely independent of those from other assets are grouped at the lowest levels for
which there are separately identifiable cash flows, known as cash-generating units. If the recoverable amount of the cash-
generating unit is less than the carrying amount of the unit, the impairment loss is allocated pro rata on the basis of the
carrying amount of each asset in the unit.

Where an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the asset or cash-generating unit is increased to
the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, limited to the carrying amount that would have been determined had no
impairment loss been recognised for the asset or cash-generating unit in prior periods. A reversal of an impairment loss is
recognised immediately in the statement of comprehensive income.

The Company annually reviews the carrying amounts of its property, plant and equipment with finite useful lives in order to
determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any such indication exists,
the recoverable amounts of the assets are estimated in order to determine the extent, if any, of the impairment loss.

Deferred tax

Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable in the future arising from temporary differences between the
carrying amounts of assets and liabilities in the financial statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the

Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all taxable temporary differences and deferred tax assets are recognised

recognised if the temporary difference arises from the initial recognition of assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects
neither the taxable profit nor the accounting profit.

A deferred tax asset for the carry forward of unused tax losses and tax credits is only recognised to the extent that it is
probabie that future taxable profit will be available against which the unused tax losses and tax credits can be utilised.

The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting date and adjusted to reflect changes in the
probability that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered.

approach (Lifetime ECL). The ECL on trade receivables and contract assets are estimated using a provision matrix by reference
to past default experience of the debtor and an analysis of the debtor's current financial position, adjusted for factors that are
specific to the debtors, general economic conditions of the industry at the reporting date such as inflation and GDP growth. The
Company had recognised a loss allowance of 100% against all receivables where historical experience has indicated that these
receivables are generally not recoverable.
