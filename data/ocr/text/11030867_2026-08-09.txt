The Directors present their strategic report on euNetworks Holdings 2 Limited (the “Company’) and its
subsidiaries (the “Group") for the year ended 31 December 2025.
The Company is an investment holding entity for euNetworks Holdings Limited and its subsidiaries. .
The current reporting period is for the year ended 31 December 2025. The comparatives are for the year to 31
December 2024.
Review of the business
Principal activities
The principal activity of the Group is as a bandwidth infrastructure provider under the trading name “euNetworks”.
The Group owns and operates 18 fibre based metropolitan networks across Western Europe, with these networks
connected by euNetworks' tong hau) network. euNetworks is a market leader in data centre connectivity, directly +
connecting over 600 data centres in Europe. euNetworks is also a leading cloud connectivity provider. The Group
offers a portfolio of metropolitan and long-haul services including Dark Fibre, Wavelengths and Ethernet.
Wholesale, finance, content, media, data centre, hyperscale and enterprise customers benefit from euNetworks’
unique inventory of fibre and duct based assets that are tailored to fulfil their high bandwidth needs.

Going Concern
The Group meets its day to day working capital requirements through its revolving credit facility and ongoing

the financial statements.

our addressable market, both organically and inorganically through the acquisition of complementary businesses in
adjacent geographies.

Environmental matters (cont'd.)
In 2025, euNetworks’ total Scope 1, 2 and 3 greenhouse gas emissions were 51,006 tCO,e, 20% below the 2019
base year and 9% below 2024.
The 2025 reporting year represents a transition in euNetworks’ carbon accounting approach, with the GHG
inventory calculated fully in-house for the first time using a carbon management platform, replacing the previous
externally supported model. This transition has involved several methodology changes, moving from
predominantly spend-based, sector-level emission factors to a more granular approach using activity-based and
product-specific factors where supported by data quality.
The new methodology has been applied retrospectively to 2019, providing a restated 2019 baseline. The 2024
figures have not been recalculated and are provided for context only; comparisons with 2024 should therefore be
interpreted with caution, as differences in reported emissions reflect both underlying business activity and
methodological change.
Scope 1 direct emissions were 151 tCO;e in 2025. Emissions from fully electric leased company cars are now
accounted for under Upstream Leased Assets. As a result, Scope 1 is wholly attributable to refrigerants used to
top up air conditioning units in our colocation sites and/or ILAs, a category that was not systematically captured in
2019. A phased programme to replace the end-of-life cooling equipment is under way.
Scope 2 market-based electricity emissions are down 99% from the 2019 base year, from 9,315 tCOze to 104
1CO,¢. This reflects continued investment in renewable energy tariffs across euNetworks’ operating footprint.
Scope 3 accounts for 99.5% of total emissions. Purchased goods and services (PG&S) represent 60% and capital
goods 27% of the total footprint. These two categories are the primary focus of euNetworks’ suppiier engagement
and procurement-based decarbonisation strategy and are also the categories most significantly affected by the
methodology improvements made in 2025.
PG&S emissions of 30,740 tCOze are 36% higher than the 2019 base year (22,575 tCO;¢). This increase reflects
a combination of increased network construction and services activity and a significant methodology improvement.
In 2019, subsea marine fuel was classified under upstream transportation and distribution under the previous
methodology; the 2025 treatment under purchased goods and services is methodologically more appropriate
under the GHG Protocol. For remaining spend, a two-tier approach was applied: supplier-specific carbon
intensities derived from publicly reported GHG emissions and revenue data were used for key suppliers, with ihe
EXIOBASE 3 spend-based emission factor database applied to remaining suppliers.
Capital goods emissions of 13,878 tCO,e are 9% lower than the 2019 base year (15,298 tCO,e). This decrease is
primarily attributable to a significant improvement in emission factor accuracy rather than a change in the volume
or nature of equipment purchased. Under the previous methodology, network hardware was assigned a generic
telecommunications equipment emission factor. For 2025, product-specific, weight-based emission factors were
applied according to the dominant source of embodied carbon in each product type following a tiered classification
approached based on the nature and composition of the products. This tiered approach provides a more accurate
representation of the embodied carbon in euNetworks’ network investment.
Several Scope 3 categories showed significant reductions versus the 2019 base year:
+ Fuel and energy-related activities fell 81% from 2,173 to 410 tCQze, reflecting a methodology change: the 202
inventory applies Well-to-Tank emission factors aligned with the renewable energy tariffs used in Scope 2,
replacing the location-based factors applied in 2019.
+ Upstream transportation and distribution decreased 98% from 2,178 to 36 tCOze, principally because subse
cable project emissions are now reported under PG&S as described above, and distance calculations for
equipment shipping are now based on the supplier's country of production (where available) rather than generic
assumptions.
“Business travel emissions of 388 tCOze remain 59% below 2019 levels.
+ Employee commute and working from home emissions of 504 tCOze are 174% above 2019 levels (184 tCO2e
This increase reflects both headcount growth since 2019 and the introduction of a more comprehensive survey-
based methodology that captures a greater proportion of commuting and home-working activity than was
previously reported.
+ Upstream Leased Assets (primarily colocation data centres) decreased 55% from 10,695 to 4,779 tCO,e, drive
by increased renewable electricity sourcing by colocation partners and the application of Well-To-Tank emission
factors, as well as the replacement of residual mix emission factors with supplier energy source data where

+ Downstream teased asset emissions fell 59%, partly reflecting a methodology improvement thal eliminate

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The Group uses financial instruments such as cash, borrowings, receivables and payables in order to raise
finance for the Group's operations. The existence of these instruments exposes the Group to financial risks
which are detailed below:
Liquidity risk
The Company seeks to manage financial risk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable
needs and to invest cash assets safely and profitably. Short-term flexibility is achieved by cash balances
together with bank debt facilities and secured notes of €710m (2024: €710m), a capital expenditure facility
of €500m (2024: €500m) and a revolving credit facility of €50m (2024: €50m).
At the year ended 31 December 2025, the amount drawn on the debt was €710m (2024: €710m) on term
loans and €90m (2024: €40m) drawn on the available revolving credit and capital expenditure facitity of
€550m (2024: €550m).
Interest risk
The Group is exposed to interest rate fluctuations on its borrowings to the extent that the borrowings accrue
interest at a fixed margin above Euribor, with a floar on Euribor of nit. To mitigate this risk the Group has
entered into an interest rate hedge. At year end the amount drawn on the Group's debt facility totalled
€800m (2024: €750m) on the term loans and revolving credit facility. Of the total drawn amount, €410
million was subject to a floating interest rate, and €390 million of this floating rate portion was covered by an
interest rate swap which took effect from 30 June 2025 and runs until 30 November 2028.
Management do not consider either availability of future debt or probable interest rate movements to be a
significant risk to the business.
Credit risk
The principal credit risk for the Group arises from its trade receivables. In order to manage credit risk the
finance team set credit limits for customers based on a combination of payment history and third party
credit references. Credit limits are reviewed by the credit controllers on a regular basis in conjunction with
debt ageing and collection history.
Employee involvement
Information is provided to all employees regularly by means of on-going management communication
channels using written material, face-to-face and video conference meetings and regular postings to an
intranet. Employees are also made aware of their contribution through individual twice yearly performance
appraisals.
Disabled persons .
It is the policy of the Group to give full and fair consideration to applications for employment from disabled
persons, to continue wherever possible the employment of members of staff who may become disabled and
to ensure that suitable training, career development and promotion is afforded to such persons.

2.5 Income tax
Income tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and deferred tax.
Current income tax
The tax currently payable is based on taxable profit for the period. Taxable profit differs from profit as reported in the profit or
loss because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable or deductible in other years and it further excludes items
that are not taxable or tax deductible. The Group's liability for current tax is recognised at the amount expected to be paid or
recovered from the tax authorities and calculated using tax rates (and tax laws) that have been enacted or substantively
enacted in countries where the Company and subsidiaries operate by the end of the financial year.
Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised on all temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities in the financial
statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the computation of taxable profit, and are accounted for using the balance
sheet fiability method. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all taxable temporary differences and deferred tax
assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that taxable profits will be available against which deductible temporary
differences can be utilised. Such assets and liabilities are not recognised if the temporary difference arises from goodwill or
from the initial recognition (other than in a business combination) of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects
neither the taxable profit nor the accounting profit.
Deferred tax liabilities are recognised on taxable temporary differences arising on investments in subsidiaries except where the
Group is able to control the reversal of the temporary difference and it is probable that the temporary difference will not reverse
in the foreseeable future.
