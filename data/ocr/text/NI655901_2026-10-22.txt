Introduction
The directors have pleasure in presenting the strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2026.

Principal activity and strategy

Fibrus’ principal activity is the deployment of new full fibre broadband infrastructure to homes and businesses

across Northern Ireland and parts of the North of England.

Our strategy is to deploy our full fibre infrastructure to regional and rural areas that have been underserved by
. legacy networks. Fibrus has focused on regional towns and villages with lower fibre coverage. In addition, in both

Northern Ireland and Cumbria, Fibrus has won government funding to partly finance the deployment of network to

rural premises which would not otherwise be commercially viable, enabling our network to reach deeper into rural

areas,

We anticipate that the majority of homes and businesses in the areas that we operate will eventually have access to

other fibre networks in the future, so we focus on being first to get to market and proactively acquire the customers

based on better value and better service. ,

Operating review

Fibrus has established itself as one of the leading independent full fibre operators in the UK and Ireland due to a

track record of delivering on build and on customer take-up.

At a time when a number of its peers are struggling, Fibrus continues to deliver on its business plan, evidenced by

us reporting our first full year of EBITDA profitability.

Build programme delivery .

During the year we were awarded further UK government contract (“NI Gigabit”) to provide service to around

10,000 additional, hard to reach premises in Northern Ireland. This followed on from the delivery of the Stratum’

programme which brought full fibre connectivity to 81,148 homes across Northern Ireland. This large scale public

infrastructure project was delivered on time and on budget and has transformed access high speed connectivity in

tural areas and delivered significant economic benefits to the local economy. .

The addition of NI Gigabit will further boost Northern Irelands fibre coverage to 95%, helping to futureproof its

workforce by enabling access to remote and hybrid job opportunities across the UK and beyond.

In Cumbria we continued to deliver our expanded BDUK funded Gigabit on time and on budget. By the end of

the year we had delivered 32,400 premises, significantly expanding our rural footprint during the year, and already

ahead of the scope of the initial contract.

The remaining 20,000 premises will largely be delivered over the next 18 months. Once completed, alongside other

commercial expansion plans, 99% of homes and businesses in Cumbria will have access to next-generation

broadband.

Rapid customer take-up

Fibrus continued to deliver significant growth in connected customers, finishing the year on 146,700 customers

representing a penetration rate against premises ready for service of 31.1%.

Growth during the year of 33k customers was largely in line with the level of growth delivered in 2025 and the

company looks forward to delivering similar levels of growth next year.

Fibrus has delivered accelerated take-up across its footprint through its focus on product performance, best value

pricing and customer service.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of the business and the execution of the Group and Company’s strategy are subject to a number
of risks and uncertainties. The key risks and uncertainties affecting the Group and Company are considered to relate
: to competition with other broadband providers, customer take-up, churn and technological advance. The key risks,
and the activities in place to manage such risks, are monitored on a regular basis.
Economic risk
The Group has interest bearing liabilities with credit institutions and investors, and therefore movement in interest
rates or inflation may impact these liabilities. Interest is accounted for through the Consolidated statement of
comprehensive income.
Competition risk
The directors of the Group and Company manage competition risk through close attention to customer service
levels and providing an effective service.
Financial risk
: ; All key financial figures are monitored on an ongoing basis. The Group continues to have access to funding from :
investors to continue its investment in the roll out of its network. :
Credit risk
The Group is exposed to its customers defaulting on the payment of their debts. The Group mitigates this risk
through taking deposits on orders and practices robust credit control in collecting this debt.
Liquidity risk
The Group has obtained financing from its investors and lenders in order to meet its funding requirements. This
mitigates the risk to the Group.
Interest rate risk
The Group has interest bearing liabilities. Interest bearing liabilities relate to bank loans, which pay interest at
variable rates. The Group actively manages its interest rate risk by entering into interest rate swaps to help mitigate
interest rate rises.

Engagement with employees
During the period, the policy of providing employees with information about the Group has been continued
through internal media methods in which employees have also been encouraged to present their suggestions and
views on the Group performance. Regular meetings are held between local management and employees to allow a
free flow of information and ideas.
Engagement with suppliers
The Group recognises that the prompt settlement of amounts due to suppliers is fundamental to maintaining a
: resilient and sustainable supply chain, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). The Board is
committed to upholding high standards of payment practice across the Group through robust procurement, invoice
approval and payment processes, ensuring that suppliers are paid in accordance with agreed contractual terms. The
Group continually monitors its payment performance and seeks to promote fair, transparent and responsible
payment practices, thereby supporting the long-term sustainability of its suppliers and fostering strong commercial
relationships.
Disabled employees
The Group gives full consideration to applications from all disabled persons where the requirements of the job can
be adequately fulfilled by a disabled person. Where existing employees become disabled, it is the Group's policy
wherever practicable to provide continuing employment under normal terms and conditions and to provide training
and career development and promotion to disabled employees wherever appropriate.
Human resources
The Group's most important resource is its people; their knowledge and experience is crucial to meeting customer
requirements. Retention of key staff is critical and the Group have invested increasingly in employment training and
development and has introduced appropriate incentive and career progression arrangements.
