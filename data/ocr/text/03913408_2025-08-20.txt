Statement by the directors in performance of their statutory duties in accordance with Section 172 (1)

Companies Act 2006

This statement describes how the directors have had regard to the matters set out in Section 172 of the Companies

Act 2006 (the ‘Act’) in exercising their duty to promote the success of the Company for the benefit of its members as

a whole.

Section 172 of the Act requires a director of a company to act in the way he or she considers, in good faith, would

most likely promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole. In doing this, Section 172

requires a director to have regard amongst other matters to the:

a) Likely consequences of any decisions in the long-term. .

b) Interests of the company’s employees.

c) Need to foster the company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers, and others.

d) Impact of the company’s operations on the community and environment.

e) Desirability of the company maintaining a reputation for high standards on business conduct.

f) Need to act fairly between members of the company.

Examples of how the directors have had regard to the matters set out in Section 172 when discharging their duties

are set out below. Key stakeholder groups can be impacted in different ways by decisions which are taken by the

Board. The directors consider that the groups listed below are the Company's key stakeholders. They are identified

as those most likely to be affected by the principal decisions of the Board:

¢ Customers: Our business model is driven by customer retention and as such we put our customers at the
heart of everything we do, ensuring we deliver a business plan anchored in their needs. In a competitive
marketplace, we differentiate our brand by committing and investing in a local workforce who understands
the needs of our customers and remain focused on delivering a high-quality service. Understanding the needs
and challenges of our customers allows us to help them succeed.

SMBs are the backbone of the UK economy. Most of these are physical businesses first, such as
hairdressers, personal trainers and clothing shops, that have huge potential to grow and connect with new
customers by offering or marketing their services online. This makes having a digital presence a non-
negotiable for SMBs regardless of their location, product or mission.

A lack of digital skills among small teams and a market crowded with technology solutions can make it
daunting to invest in digitalisation. SMBs need technology solutions that are relevant to their specific needs
and are going to improve and simplify their operations, not complicate them. Whether it's to take dinner
reservations, haircut bookings, or sell or market products and services to new customers, SMBs all have
different reasons for bringing their company online. One thing that they all need is digital solutions that are
catered to their business. However, the challenge of choosing and managing tools requires heavy time
investment and digital capabilities that SMBs with small teams often lack. They often end up juggling several
tools and platforms that don't integrate and struggle with tech support that isn’t catered to the specifics of
their product or market.

As introducing new technology is at risk of slipping down the list of priorities for SMBs, there is a desperate
need for consolidated, simple solutions that are local to operations. In different markets across Europe,
founders have been building technology platforms and tools for SMBs to address this. As various digital
solutions emerge, SMBs need help navigating this fragmented market to select the tools suitable for their
operations that can work together seamlessly. Our full suite of digital products offers SMBs a one-stop shop
to establish and grow their business online. Made up of a vast ecosystem of European brands united in the
mission to simplify online business, we give SMBs:

o Acombination of products to fuel business growth, from helping customers establish their presence
online with domain hosting, site-builders and compliance, to facilitating success through lead
generation, online booking and eCommerce.

o Localised support that understands the diverse needs of SMBs and can easily communicate in local
languages to problem solve efficiently.

o A safe online home for their business with a strong focus on cybersecurity through continuous
investment and a dedicated information security team.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Risk is regularly reviewed at board level to ensure that risk management is being implemented and monitored
effectively. This includes monitoring commercial and general risk as well as financial risks.
Commercial and general risk
During the extended period of high inflation, the impact to COS from technology supply partners was significant due
to increase in energy and material prices. In the latter part of 2023, we also saw a reduction in availability of parts
and slowing of technology supply chains that could have impacted our ability to purchase the relevant hardware to
operate our business. These risks were reviewed and monitored to ensure supplier pricing and stock levels were
forecasted regularly to ensure business growth and expansion was, and is, acceptable. Where supplier pricing
increases were proportionally higher than the market, alternative suppliers were sourced and onboarded to ensure
we could minimise any pricing increases we had to pass onto our customers.
Financial risks
The company uses various financial instruments including cash, trade and group debtors and creditors and other
loans and overdrafts that arise directly from its operations. The main purpose of these financial instruments is to
raise finance for the company’s operations. The existence of these financial instruments exposes the company to
a number of financial risks, which are described in more detail below. The main risks arising from the company's
financial statements are interest rate risk and liquidity risk. The directors review and agree policies for managing
each of these risks and they are summarised below.
Liquidity risk
The company expects to generate positive cashflows for the foreseeable future and seeks to manage financial
tisk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable needs and to invest cash assets safely and
profitably.
Foreign exchange rate risk
The company seeks to manage its exposure to foreign exchange risk through netting of exposures wherever
possible.
