GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

The board of directors have pleasure in presenting their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activities and vision
Intercity Technology Limited is a leading Technology Services Provider, delivering IT managed services,
incorporating IT infrastructure, cloud services, security, hosting and IT professional services, mobility solutions,
and unified communications. With particular expertise in delivering award-winning levels of customer service to a
range of public and private sector clients by a team providing ‘eyes on' national coverage 24 hours a day, 365
days a year. Our vision is to be the best technology partner to work for and with, by providing an environment
that drives engagement and collaboration for our colleagues and helping our customers to make the right
technology decisions for their business to enable them to Do More.
Business review
2025 marked a landmark year for Intercity as the group celebrated its 40th anniversary and secured numerous

: accolades including Lloyds British Business Excellence Employer of the Year and the Business Desk West
Midlands Large Business of the Year awards. We continued to invest in our infrastructure, technology skills and
modern and fully integrated business systems to support further growth ambitions. The group has secured many
new logo business wins, including our largest ever deal, and we ended the year with a record level of sales
pipeline.
We are extremely proud to maintain Gold Investors in People, achieve 2-star Outstanding status with Best
Companies, retain our various ISO certifications and be ranked within the top 3 percent globally with EcoVadis in
relation to our sustainability performance. These positive outcomes reflect the incredible dedication and
professionalism of colleagues across the entire business.
Revenue increased by 4.5% to £52.6m (2024: £50.4m), reflecting continued commercial activity and new
business wins. However, gross margin declined from 39.8% to 36.1%, resulting in gross profit falling to £19.0m
(2024: £20.1m). EBITDA declined by 58% from £4.4m to £1.8m, and the group reported a loss before tax of
£3.4m (2024: loss of £0.2m) which is predominantly the result of one-off exceptional items or acquisition related
amortisation charges which are non-trading costs and distort underlying trading performance.
The geopolitical landscape has been particularly challenging, with ongoing tensions related to the conflict in
Ukraine, instability in the Middle East, the impact of US tariff policies, and the consequences of the October 2024
UK budget, all of which havc contributcd to reduced customer spend and delayed purchasing decisions across
the sector. Whilst some expected customer churn and contract erosion provided additional headwinds, customer
service has always been at the heart of the group’s growth and this year we ended with a Net Promoter Score
(NPS) of +90.
The group remains financially strong, with net assets of £6.3m (2024: £9.2m) and no external debt or
stakeholder influence. The reduction in net assets reflects the loss incurred during the period, which is largely
driven by acquisition amortisation costs that do not’impact the group's cash position. The board remains
confident in the group's long-term strategy and continues to invest in its people, technology and operating model
to strengthen capability, improve efficiency and support sustainable profitable growth.

Section 172 statement
Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 (the Act) imposes a duty on a director to act in a way that he or she
considers, in good faith, would be most likely to promote the long-term success of the company for the benefit of
its members ‘as a whole. In doing so, the directors have regard to the various matters including the interests of
stakeholders as well as various other matters. The Companies (Miscellaneous Reporting) Regulations 2018
require companies to report on how the board has fulfilled the requirements of Section 172(1), including how the
board has factored stakeholder considerations into its decision-making. :
The board is fully aware of and supports these requirements. We are pleased to describe below how the group
board engages with our stakeholders.
The group's key stakeholders have an important role to play in the successful operation of our business. Our
board are fully aware of, and take seriously, their responsibilities to those stakeholders under the Act.
We believe that it is appropriate to consider the potential impact on our stakeholders when considering the
group's strategy and in making our key decisions. Indeed, these responsibilities are rooted in our culture, values
and company purpose. ;
The board considers that, in its decisions and actions to date, it has acted in a way that would promote the
success of the group for the benefit of its members as a whole, while having regard to stakeholders and matters
set out in Section 172(1) (a—f) of the Act. /

, The board has identified the group's key stakeholders as our colleagues, customers, suppliers and vendors, the
environment and communities in which we operate. It receives updates on each of these and takes steps to
ensure that it remains well informed about them.

The group directors believe strongly in doing business in the right way, with all its decisions underpinned by the
impact they have on our five main stakeholder groups. We set out below where our board are discharging its
duties and the effect of this on its decision making.

High standards of business conduct
We have a Code of Conduct setting out the behaviours and values expected of all of our colleagues, which we
communicate to all colleagues and third parties. We have processes to update our board and management on :
the operation of our code and an independent whistleblowing service to enable colleagues and third parties to
anonymously raise concerns. There were no whistleblowing concerns raised during the period. Through its
oversight and monitoring role, the board requires all of our people to work to the highest standards of business
conduct. Our focus is to do what is right ahead of what is easy. This is supported through ongoing
communication and awareness of and training in, acceptable company conduct. Any reports of inappropriate

“behaviour are independently investigated, and action taken where necessary. Following the Wates Principles, we
have formally established an Audit and Risk Committee with clear terms of reference which is chaired by an
independent Non-Executive Advisor. We have developed the strategic risk register that is now under quarterly
review and standardised board reporting across all committees. An independent board effectiveness review is
also planned in the year ahead to further strengthen our governance posture.
Long-term decision-making ‘
The board has put in place a structured governance model, with scheduled board meetings and clear
documentation and authority levels to control its decision-making process. Our governance model supports the
group in ensuring that decisions are considered, documented and reported upon, and in alignment with our
strategic plans. Detailed integrated business planning includes setting of strategic business objectives and
financial budgets over the short, medium and long term (typically 1, 3 and 5 years). These objectives are
cascaded to colleagues and measured through our Plan-on-a-Page and review of actual performance against
budgets and forecasts. The board and individual directors operate within this structure, with the aim of promoting
the success of the group and delivering long-term shareholder value.
Customers
Providing outstanding service to our customers, across SME, Enterprise and Public Sector is pivotal to our
success. We measure this through NPS, which continues to be sector leading at +90 (2024: +93). We also
measure customer satisfaction (CSAT), and we are delighted our teams have achieved a CSAT of over 9.3/10
and 94% service level adherence across thousands of customer tickets. We continue to enhance our product
offering to support customer needs and are particularly proud of being recognised as one of a select number of
elite Microsoft partners that are members of the Microsoft Intelligent Security Association. We have also
successfully maintained all our external accreditations, including various ISO standards, Cyber Essentials Plus,
IT Health check and many more.
Suppliers and vendors
We have built an extensive network of relationships with our suppliers and vendors, which ensure we have
appropriate resilience, competitiveness and flexibility to fulfil our operations. The integrity and strength of our
supply chain is critical to our success and all suppliers are evaluated and regularly reviewed in accordance with
our guidelines on the environment, human rights, business ethics, cyber security and overall quality assurance. It
is the group's policy that all key supplier agreements are reviewed by the board and external professional
advisors as required and suppliers are onboarded in line with specified policies.

Colleague engagement
Our colleagues epitomise the personality and values of the business which are at the heart of everything we do.
Our commitment to supporting and developing our colleagues is underpinned by our Gold Investors In People
(liP) and 2 Star Outstanding Best Companies Accreditations. This is fantastic recognition of our world-class
dedication to workplace engagement which ranks us as the 28th best large-sized company to work for in the UK,
the 7th best company to work for in the West Midlands, the 29th best company to work for in the East of
England, the 63rd best company to work for in the North West and the second best telecoms company to work
for in the whole of the UK.
We always expect that our colleagues act with integrity and professionalism and in return the board considers
that our colleagues are rewarded fairly and incentivised to deliver the group strategy. Colleagues receive ongoing
communication through our annual all company conference, quarterly business updates, company news
SharePoint page, digital notice boards, newsletters, meetings with management and team briefings.
Performance reviews, which include monthly 1 to 1s and annual appraisals, are undertaken for all colleagues to
measure achievement of objectives which determines the necessary recognition and reward. This process has
helped to reduce voluntary colleague turnover by 26.8% and maintain our outstanding colleague engagement.
Disabled persons
The company is committed to making all roles available to the broadest range of potential employees, including
those with a disability. The company is committed to furthering its obligations under the Equality Act 2010 by
affording people with every opportunity to develop, by providing a working environment conducive for disabled
people with the relevant skills and qualifications. The company will make reasonable adjustments to assist
employees or prospective employees. The company will interview all disabled applicants, provided they meet the
selection criteria for the vacancy. If an existing employee suffers a disability whilst in our employment, the
company will make every effort to help them stay in work, by making reasonable adjustments. Initiatives will be
implemented which help employees to develop a greater awareness of disability issues. ,
Our community and the environment
Environmental Social Governance (ESG) is a strategic focus for the business and is something we are fully
committed to supporting. We are pleased to have secured a Gold sustainability rating from EcoVadis, the world's
largest and most trusted provider of business sustainability ratings. While our overall assessment score
remained consistent with the prior year, the relative benchmark moved from the top 1% to the top 3% of
companies completing the assessment. This continued recognition evidences our ongoing commitment to
reduce our impact on the planet, create social value for the communities we serve, and be a world-class place to
work.
Our underlying business ethos is to always do what is right for our customers, colleagues, partners, community
and environment. We actively encourage all our colleagues to give back to the community in whichever way they
feel the most comfortable. This ranges from the ability to participate in community volunteer work, fundraising
events, or matching donations that our colleagues have raised. We are particularly proud of supporting Ahead
Partnership to deliver work experience, develop employability skills and provide careers advice to >1,100
students in the West Midlands.
Our board and our senior management are acutely aware of the impact our business operations can have on the
environment and actively seek to minimise this where possible. We are committed to reducing our carbon
footprint and partner with environmental consultants to drive continuous improvement in our sustainability efforts.
We continue to be carbon neutral across scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions, power our HQ on renewable energy,

. provide a green car scheme, planted 400 trees and continued to support young people through our work with the
Ahead Partnership.
In 2025 we raised an incredible £25,552 for charity and we also made company donations of £44,000 to a
number of well deserving causes, including Kids Village, Acorns Children's Hospice, Macmillan, Birmingham
Children's Hospital, LoveBrum, Breast Cancer Now, Save the Children and the Royal British Legion.

Energy Efficiency
The group remains committed to reducing energy consumption and carbon emissions as part of our group
strategic objectives by aiming "to give real value to our community and environment”. This has been achieved

installing LED lighting, and delivering colleague training to promote energy efficiency at work. The output of this
work has resulted in a re-certification of [SO50001:2018 Energy Management System across our operational
facilities, including our Data Centres.
Our overall energy consumption has increased as a result of the acquisition of Centrality Limited and a full year's
usage from an additional office location now being included. We continue to promote the use of renewable
energy sources (REGO), to underpin our de-carbonisation initiatives. We have invested in electric vehicles
where possible, including the provision of an electric car scheme to colleagues, and are pleased to see an
overall reduction in our emissions from business travel.

The group's credit risk is primarily attrioutable to its trade debtors. Credit risk is managed by stringent credit
check procedures that assess exposure on each deal prior to accepting and processing any orders, the group

Balance Sheet are net of allowances for doubtful debtors.
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is managed by maintaining a suitable level of funds to support working capital requirements. All of
the group's cash balances are held in such a way that achieves a competitive rate of interest and the business
makes use.of any deposits and other investment facilities where funds are available.

financial instruments.
Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference
between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the
asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the
estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The
impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of
the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment floss then the
impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying
amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The
: impairment reversal is recognised in the profit or loss.
