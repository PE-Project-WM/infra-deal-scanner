BUSINESS REVIEW AND FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
The Company is jointly owned by Laing O'Rourke Delivery Limited, a company registered and domiciled in
England and Wales, and Bouygues Travaux Publics SAS, a company registered and domiciled in France.
The Company employs and pays operatives for the purpose of delivering the scope of works included
within the HK2201 Hinkley Point ‘C’ Main Civil Works package. The costs are reimbursed fortnightly by the
BYLOR JV, an unincorporated joint operation between Laing O'Rourke Delivery Limited and Bouygues
Travaux Publics SAS. The Company will continue to employ the labour to be used for the civil works of the
Hinkley Project; these are expected to continue until 2028.

Principal activities
The principal activities of the Company in the year under review were those of employing and paying
operatives for the purpose of delivering the scope of works included within the HK2201 Hinkley Point 'C' Main
Civil Works package.
Information in respect of the Business Review, Financial Risks, Principal Risks and Uncertainties, as well as the
future developments, are not discussed in the Directors’ Report because they are presented within the
Strategic Report in accordance with s414c(11) of the Companies Act 2006.
Review of business
The results for the year ended 31 December 2025 are set out in the Income Statement on page 14.
Revenue increased to £652,440,000 for the year ended 31 December 2025 (2024: £587,205,000). This increase
was accompanied by a rise in cost of sales to £642,958,000 (2024: £576,428,000), mainly due to the increase
in the average number of employees to 5,783 during the year (2024: 5,518). Profit for the financial year
increased to £791,000 (2024: £344,000). The Company’s total share capital remained unchanged at £2 as at
31 December 2025 (2024: £2).
Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis which assumes that the Company
will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. The Company is jointly owned 50:50 by Laing
O'Rourke Delivery Limited (a UK entity) and Bouygues Travaux Publics SAS (a French entity). Each are
members of groups of companies whose ultimate parents are Laing O'Rourke Corporation Limited (the “Laing
O'Rourke Group") and Bouygues SA (the “Bouygues Group") respectively. The directors have assessed
whether the going concern basis of preparation is appropriate by reference to the position of the entire
Laing O'Rourke Group and Bouygues Group (together “the Groups"), as the Company is reliant on financial
support from each group of companies.
The directors of the Company have carefully considered those factors likely to affect the Company's future
development, performance and financial position in relation to the ability of the Company to operate within
its current and foreseeable resources, financial and operational. Furthermore, no voluntary strike off of the
Company is expected within the going concern period.
Laing O'Rourke Corporation Limited and Bouygues Travaux Publics SAS have provided separate letters of
support to the Company on behalf of fhe Laing O'Rourke Group and the Bouygues Group. The directors
have concluded that it is appropriate for these financial statements to be prepared on a going concer
basis. The financial statements do not include the adjustments that would result if the Company were unable
to continue as a going concern.
The Directors have obtained letters of financial support from each of the Company's joint venture partners.
These letters confirm the partners’ will provide financial support to the Company, as necessary, to enable it to
meet its obligations as they fall due and fo continue operating for the foreseeable future. The Directors
consider these support arrangements to be a key factor in their assessment that the Company is able to
continue as a going concern.
Although a voluntary strike-off of the Company is expected following the completion of the relevant activities,
the Directors have assessed the Company's ability to continue as a going concern for at least twelve months
from the date of approval of these financial statements.

Employment policy
The Company continues to provide employees with relevant information and to seek their views on matters
of common concern through their representatives and through line managers. Through the BYLOR JV, which
incorporates inputs from the Company's directors, priority is given to ensuring that employees are aware of
significant matters affecting the Company's trading position and of any significant organisational changes.
The Company treats each application for employment, training and promotion on merit. Full and fair
consideration is given to both disabled and able-bodied applicants and employees. If existing employees
become disabled, every effort is made to find them appropriate work and training is provided if necessary.
Streamlined energy and carbon reporting
Whilst the Company is a large unquoted company and meets the requirement to deliver a report for
streamlined energy and carbon reporting, its principal activity is an employment bureau and it has no energy
consumption to report. The office, site and other associated energy consumption is incurred by the BYLOR
joint venture for delivering the main civil works package. The Company confirms that it is a low energy user
of less than 40MWh and has taken exemption from this reporting requirement.

Purpose and leadership
The Company was incorporated as a joint venture as an employment and payroll bureau for the Hinkley
Point “C" Main Civils package that is being constructed by the Hinkley integrated joint arrangement. Once
the package has reached completion and there is no longer a requirement for employees, the Company
will apply for a voluntary strike off. As both parties have a 50% share of voting rights it is logical for there to be
an equal representation from both parties as statutory directors.
As the workforce are fundamental to the success of the project this Company is not managed as an
individual entity but included within the management of the Hinkley integrated joint arrangement, whereby
the directors of the Company, discharging their responsibilities, are involved in the management of the
project in whole. :
Board composition
The Company board comprises of a statutory director from each of the shareholders' group of companies.
Whereas, the project board comprises of the afore mentioned directors, production directors, finance
director and the human resources director from the project, who have a wider remit to that of the board of
directors.
Director responsibilities
Each year one of the directors will sign the Directors’ Responsibilities Statement on behalf of the board
confirming that every director has confirmed that they have fulfilled their obligations as a director for the
. financial year in concern.
The project board's key management personnel will allocate tasks and responsibilities to individuals based
on their knowledge and ability. It will also be their responsibility to measure progress against these
objectives/tasks and to feedback when opportunities or risks occur. This will always be considered with the
highest regard to achieving the timely objective of constructing the package with safety at the forefront of
decision making.

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES
2.1 Accounting Policies
Basis of preparation of financial statements
The financial statements of the Company have been prepared in accordance and complies with UK-adopted
International Accounting Standards and with the requirements of the Companies Act 2006 as applicable to
_ companies reporting under those standards. The financial statements have been prepared under the historical
cost convention. They include comparatives as of and for the year ended 31 December 2024., .
The principal accounting policies applied in the preparation of these financial statements are set out below.
These policies have been consistently applied to all the years presented, unless otherwise stated. The financial
statements of the Company are presented in thousands of pounds which is the functional currency for the ©
° Company. :

Going concern .
The financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis as the Directors have a reasonable .
expectation that the Company will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. The Company
is jointly owned 50:50 by Laing O'Rourke Delivery Limited (a UK entity) and Bouygues Travaux Publics SAS (a
French entity}. Each are members of groups of companies whose ultimate parents are Laing O'Rourke
Corporation Limited (the "Laing O'Rourke Group") and Bouygues SA (the "Bouygues Group") respectively. The
directors have assessed and concluded that the going concern basis of preparation is appropriate by
reference to the position of the entire Laing O'Rourke Group and Bouygues Group (together "the Groups").
The Directors have obtained letters of financial support from each of the Company's joint venture partners.
These letters confirm the partners’ will provide financial support to the Company, as necessary, to enable it to
meet its obligations as they fall due and to continue operating for the foreseeable future. The Directors
consider these support arrangements to be a key factor in their assessment that the Company is able to
continue as a going concem
Although a voluntary strike-off of the Company is expected following the completion of the relevant activities,
the Directors have assessed the Company's ability to continue as a going concern for at least twelve months
from the date of approval of these financial statements.

New standards and interpretations not yet adopted

Trade and other receivables
: Trade receivables are initially recorded at fair value and subsequently measured at amortised cost as reduced
by allowances for expected credit losses and appropriate allowances for estimated irrecoverable amounts.
Subsequent recoveries of amounts previously written off are credited to the income statement line in which
the provision was originally recognised.
The transactions with the BYLOR JV are paid in less than 30 days and the Company operates solely alongside
one client being the BYLOR JV.
Cash and cash equivalents
Cash and cash equivalents includes cash at bank and in hand, deposits held at call with banks, other short-
term highly liquid investments with original maturities of three months or less and bank overdrafts.
Tax , .
Tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and deferred tax. The current tax expense is
based on the taxable profits for the year, after any adjustments in respect of prior years. Taxable profit differs
from net profit as reported in the income statement because it excludes items of income or expenses that are
taxable or deductible in other years and it also excludes items that are neither taxable or deductible. The
Company's liability for current tax is calculated using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or
* substantively enacted by the reporting date.

Creditors
Creditors are not interest bearing and are stated at cost.
Share capital : .
Ordinary shares are classified as equity. Incremental costs directly attributable to the issue of new ordinary
shares or options are shown in equity as a deduction, net of tax, from the proceeds.
Pension costs
The Company also operates a defined contribution pension scheme and contributions are invested within the
pension fund in the month following the month of deduction. The amount contributed by the Company is
charged to the income statement as the contributions fall due.
Revenue recognition
Revenue consists of labour and administration costs re-charged at cost to the BYLOR JV and is recognised at
the time that the labour services have been performed. It represents amounts receivable for services provided
in the normal course of business, net of discounts, VAT and other sales related taxes. The company operates
alongside one client and provides the employees labour for that client. It invoices the gross wage cost to the
BYLOR JV at cost. There is no revenue from the sale of goods or from rental of equipment.
Government grants
Grants from government are recognised at their fair value where there is reasonable assurance that the
government and the Company will comply with all attached conditions. Government grants relating to costs

: are deferred and recognised in the income statement over the period necessary to match them with the costs
that they are intended to compensate. Management choose to disclose grant income separately on the
Income Statement as Other income to aid understanding of the financial statements.
CITB apprenticeship income is received once the expenditure has been completed and is not dependent
upon any other condition to be satisfied.

Finance income/(expenses)
Finance income/(expenses) is recognised using the effective interest method. In calculating interest
income/(expenses), the effective interest rate is applied to the gross carrying amount of the asset, when the
asset is not impaired or to the amortised cost of the liability for interest expense. For financial assets that have
been impaired after initial recognition, interest income is calculated by applying the effective interest rate to
the amortised cost of the financial asset. If the asset is no longer impaired, the interest income calculation
reverts to the gross carrying amount.
Key judgements and estimation uncertainty
The preparation of financial statements under IFRS requires management to make estimates and assumptions .
that affect amounts recognised for assets and liabilities at the statement of financial position date and the
amounts of revenue and the expenses incurred during the reported year. Actual outcomes may therefore
differ from these estimates and assumptions. No estimates and assumptions have been deemed to represent
asignificant risk of impacting the carrying value of assets and liabilities of the Company within the next financial
year.
Climate change (judgement and estimate)
The directors acknowledge the interdependence of climate-related risks between the Company and the
BYLOR JV. Climate considerations primarily stem from BYLOR JV's activities, affecting assets related to the JV,
specifically, the IFRS 9 impairment lifetime expected credit losses as they relate to accrued income from the
‘JV. BYLOR JV's risk assessment does not specifically note climate-related factors but is expected to be
influenced by the Company's shareholders. The directors reviewed shareholders’ climate-related risks from
separate reports. As a result, detailed climate impact descriptions are deemed unnecessary. Climate
change considerations had no material impact on financial judgments, and it's not expected to significantly :
affect the Company's going concern assessment.

11. FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS
FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT
Financial risk management is an integral part of the way the Company is managed. In the course of its
business, the Group is exposed primarily to liquidity risk and capital risk management. The overall aim of the
Company's financial risk management policies is to minimise potential adverse effects on financial
performance and net assets.
The Company's treasury team manages the principal risks within policies and operating parameters
approved by the Board of Directors and purchases derivative financial instruments where appropriate.
Treasury is not a profit centre and does not enter into speculative transactions.
The Company's assets and liabilities are all sterling denominated, there is no risk of currency fluctuation.
LIQUIDITY RISK
Prudent liquidity risk management involves maintaining sufficient cash and available funding to meet
: liabilities as they fall due. The Company has procedures in place to minimise liquidity risk such as maintaining
sufficient cash and other highly liquid currency assets and by having an adequate amount of committed
credit facilities.
CAPITAL RISK MANAGEMENT :
The Company's objectives when managing capital are to safeguard the Group's ability to continue as a
going concern in order to provide returns for shareholders and benefits for other stakeholders and to maintain
an optimal capital structure to reduce the cost of capital. In order to maintain or adjust the capital structure,
the Company may adjust the amount of dividends paid to shareholders, return capital to shareholders, issue
new shares or sell assets to reduce debt.
