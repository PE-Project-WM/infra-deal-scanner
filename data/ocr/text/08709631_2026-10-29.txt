Strategic Report
Period from 1 October 2024 to 31 December 2025
The directors present their strategic report for the group for the 15-month period ended 31 December
2025.
Principal activities and business model
The group designs and builds telecommunications networks and, since the acquisition of Complete
Technology Group, also owns and operates fibre infrastructure.

« Network Planning Solutions Ltd (the company) provides network planning, design, survey and
engineering services to telecommunications operators and contractors across the UK, from offices in
Swanley, Kent.

* Complete Technology Group Limited and its subsidiaries - Complete Technology Solutions
Limited, Complete Technology Services Limited and Complete Fibre Delivery Limited - plan, build and
own fibre networks within Multi-Dwelling Units (MDU). Capacity is made available to operators, under
long-term rights-of-use agreements and under leases.

During the period the company changed its accounting reference date from 30 September to 31
December to align with the group. The results therefore cover 15 months, and the comparatives cover
12 months to 30 September 2024, so they are not entirely comparable.
Review of the business
The period was transformational. In October 2024 the company acquired 100% of Complete
Technology Group Limited for nominal consideration, adding a fibre build and network ownership
business to the group's established planning and engineering services.
Group turnover rose to £18.7m (12 months to September 2024: £10.9m), driven by higher services
volumes and the post-acquisition contribution of the CTG businesses, including the transfer of fibre
rights of use to operators. Gross margin eased to 29.3% (2024: 31.0%) as the mix shifted towards
build activity.
The group reported a profit before tax of £4.0m (2024: loss of £0.2m). This includes a non-cash gain
of £3.2m relating to the purchase of CTG and gains of £0.6m on the disposal of tangible assets.
Excluding the purchase gain, operating profit was £0.8m (2024: loss of £0.1m), reflecting the
turnaround from the prior year's loss.
The group invested in tangible fixed assets, mainly fibre network infrastructure in CTG, funded from
cash reserves. Net assets increased to £8.9m (2024: £5.3m). Dividends in the company of £0.3m
were paid (2024: £0.24m). .
Average headcount more than doubled to 55 (2024: 26), mainly through the acquisition of CTG and
growth in the company during the period as we built to support larger projects for our Tier 1 customers
alongside further investment into new technology opportunities such as Hollow Core Fibre (HCF).

Principal risks and uncertainties

«Customer concentration. A significant share of group income, particularly fibre build and
rights-of-use income, depends on Openreach and a small number of major operators and contractors.
The directors manage this through long-term framework agreements, close relationship management
and by developing new customers and services.

* Liquidity and funding of network build. Network build is capital-intensive and cash is
recovered over time. Following the period's investment, cash balances are lower. The directors
monitor cash weekly, phase build against confirmed demand, and manage working capital and
supplier terms closely. :

* Integration of CTG. The acquired business was loss-making before acquisition. Delivering the
expected returns depends on integrating operations, controlling build unit costs and maintaining build

- quality. Progress is reviewed monthly by the board through group management accounts.

* Contract delivery and margin. Fixed-rate and unit-rate work exposes the group to cost
overruns, materials price inflation and labour availability. The group manages this through project-level
reporting, standard unit costs and supplier management.

* People. Growth depends on recruiting and retaining skilled planners, engineers and field staff in ;
a competitive market. :

* Interest rate and credit risk. Borrowings are limited to finance leases and hire purchase. Credit
risk is low because customers are mainly large national operators; trade debtors are monitored
regularly.

Future developments
The group's year end is now aligned at 31 December. In 2026 the directors’ priorities are to continue
to invest into the HCF market both here in the UK and abroad, continue to win and complete larger
infrastructure projects for our Tier 1 partners, complete the integration of CTG and grow the fibre
network assets we hold and maximise its long-term income from operators.

Judgements and key sources of estimation uncertainty
The preparation of the financial statements requires management to make judgements,
estimates and assumptions that affect the amounts reported. These estimates and judgements
are continually reviewed and are based on experience and other factors, including expectations
of future events that are believed to be reasonable under the circumstances.
Significant judgements
The judgements (apart from those involving estimations) that management has made in the
process of applying the entity's accounting policies and that have the most significant effect on
the amounts recognised in the financial statements are as follows:
Significant judgement in relation to bargain purchase gain
Management exercised judgement in determining the appropriate accounting treatment for the
excess of the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired over the consideration transferred.
Having considered the commercial circumstances surrounding the acquisition and the nature of
the assets acquired, management concluded that the gain did not relate to specific identifiable
non-monetary assets but represented a genuine bargain purchase arising from the distressed
nature of the transaction. Accordingly, the gain was recognised immediately in profit or loss.
Long-term fibre infrastructure agreements
Within revenue and other operating income, the Group has recognised income arising from
long-term agreements granting telecommunications operators exclusive rights to use specified
fibre infrastructure for periods of up to 99 years.
Although the legal form of these arrangements is that of a lease, management has exercised
significant judgement in determining that the arrangements represent disposals in substance. In
reaching this conclusion, management considered the transfer of substantially all economic
benefits associated with the underlying fibre infrastructure, the length of the arrangements
relative to the expected economic life of the assets, the negligible residual value expected at the
end of the contractual term and the limited significance of any ongoing obligations retained by the
Group.
Management concluded that the customer obtains substantially all of the remaining economic
benefits associated with the relevant fibre infrastructure and that the Group retains no significant
residual economic interest. Accordingly, the related infrastructure assets have been
derecognised and the consideration received has been recognised as revenue upon transfer of
the economic benefits.
Allocation of carrying value to disposed fibre lines
In determining the gain or loss arising on disposal of fibre infrastructure rights, management is
required to allocate a proportion of the carrying value of installed network assets to the fibre line
transferred.
The total carrying value of a completed network connection includes amounts attributable to
shared infrastructure and supporting network components which continue to be owned and °
controlled by the Group following the disposal of an individual fibre line.

Revenue recognition
Turnover represents amounts receivable for goods and services provided in the ordinary course
of business, net of discounts and Value Added Tax. Revenue is recognised when the group has
fulfilled its obligations under the relevant contractual arrangement, the amount of revenue can be
measured reliably and it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the group.
Where services are provided over a period of time, revenue is recognised by reference to the
stage of completion of the services provided. Revenue from the sale or transfer of assets or
rights is recognised when the significant risks and rewards and economic benefits associated
with them have transferred to the customer. :
Income tax .
The taxation expense represents the aggregate amount of current and deferred tax recognised in
the reporting period. Tax is recognised in profit or loss, except to the extent that it relates to items
recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity. In this case, tax is recognised in
other comprehensive income or directly in equity, respectively.
Current tax is recognised on taxable profit for the current and past periods. Current tax is
measured at the amounts of tax expected to pay or recover using the tax rates and laws that
have been enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date.
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences at the reporting date. Unrelieved
tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that they
will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
Deferred tax is measured using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the reporting date that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.
Goodwill
Goodwill arises on business acquisitions and represents the excess of the cost of the acquisition
over the company's interest in the net amount of the identifiable assets, liabilities and contingent
liabilities of the acquired business.
Goodwill is measured at cost less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment
losses. It is amortised on a straight-line basis over its useful life. Where a reliable estimate of the
useful life of goodwill or intangible assets cannot be made, the life is presumed not to exceed ten
years.
Intangible assets
Intangible assets are initially recorded at cost, and are subsequently stated at cost less any
accumulated amortisation and impairment losses. Any intangible assets carried at revalued
amounts, are recorded at the fair value at the date of revaluation, as determined by reference to
an active market, less any subsequent accumulated amortisation and subsequent accumulated
impairment losses.

Impairment of fixed assets
A review for indicators of impairment is carried out at each reporting date, with the recoverable
amount being estimated where such indicators exist. Where the carrying value exceeds the
recoverable amount, the asset is impaired accordingly. Prior impairments are also reviewed for
possible reversal at each reporting date.
For the purposes of impairment testing, when it is not possible to estimate the recoverable
amount of an individual asset, an estimate is made of the recoverable amount of the
cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs. The cash-generating unit is the smallest .
identifiable group of assets that includes the asset and generates cash inflows that are largely
independent of the cash inflows from other assets or groups of assets.
For impairment testing of goodwill, the goodwill acquired in a business combination is, from the
acquisition date, allocated to each of the cash-generating units that are expected to benefit from
the synergies of the combination, irrespective of whether other assets or liabilities of the
company are assigned to those units.
Stocks
Stocks are measured at the lower of cost and estimated selling price less costs to complete and
sell. Cost includes all costs of purchase, costs of conversion and other costs incurred in bringing
the stock to its present location and condition.
Finance leases and hire purchase contracts
Assets held under finance leases and hire purchase contracts are recognised in the statement of
financial position as assets and liabilities at the lower of the fair value of the assets and the
present value of the minimum lease payments, which is determined at the inception of the lease
term. Any initial direct costs of the lease are added to the amount recognised as an asset.
Lease payments are apportioned between the finance charges and reduction of the outstanding
lease liability using the effective interest method. Finance charges are allocated to each period
so as to produce a constant rate of interest on the remaining balance of the liability.
Provisions
Provisions are recognised when the entity has an obligation at the reporting date as a result of a
past event, it is probable that the entity will be required to transfer economic benefits in
settlement and the amount of the obligation can be estimated reliably. Provisions are recognised
. as a liability in the statement of financial position and the amount of the provision as an expense.
