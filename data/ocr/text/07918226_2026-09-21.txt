Introduction

Kiteworks UK Ltd (the Company) is a wholly owned subsidiary of Accellion, Inc. (doing business as Kiteworks), a

company incorporated in Delaware, United States, in May 2001. Accellion, Inc. is headquartered in San Mateo,

California, with offices across North America, Asia, and Europe.

Kiteworks UK Ltd operates primarily in the United Kingdom and Europe, where it is responsible for sales

activities and customer support. The Company provides solutions as part of the Kiteworks enterprise content

firewall platform, which is designed to prevent data breaches and ensure compliance by mitigating third-party
cyber risks.

Key corporate events and acquisitions:

« December 2021: Kiteworks UK Ltd acquired Kiteworks Europe AG (previously known as Totemo AG), a
Switzerland-based software company offering solutions for the secure exchange of business information,
including email communication and file transfer through encryption, including mobile devices. Kiteworks
Europe AG has a wholly owned subsidiary in Sofia, Bulgaria, Kiteworks BG.

e November 2023: Kiteworks Europe AG acquired OwnCloud GmbH (OwnCloud), a Germany-based software
company that develops and provides open-source software for content collaboration, allowing teams to
easily share and work on files seamlessly regardless of device or location. ownCloud remains a wholly
owned subsidiary of Kiteworks Europe AG.

« November 2023: Kiteworks Europe AG acquired Maytech Holdings Limited, which wholly owned Maytech”
Communications Ltd (Maytech), a United Kingdom-based software company that provides simple and
secure data sharing solutions with features like automated workflows, large file handling, and secure API
integration.

¢ December 2023: Kiteworks Europe AG acquired Dracoon GmbH (Dracoon), a Germany-based software
company that provides secure file sharing and collaboration services with industry-specific solutions and
certifications. Dracoon enables efficient and compliant file sharing, along with features like encrypted email
and cloud drive integration.

e August 2024: Kiteworks Europe AG acquired 123 FormBuilder, a Romanian-based software company that
provides secure file sharing, forms and solutions for its clients. 123 FormBuilder remains a wholly owned
subsidiary of Kiteworks Europe AG.

Business review
Kiteworks is a cybersecurity company specialising in secure and compliant data exchanges for both public and
private sector organisations. Its control plane for secure data exchange unifies, tracks, controls, and secures
sensitive data movement across email, file sharing, managed file transfer (MFT), web forms, APIs, and Al

: agents.

: Mission & Platform: Kiteworks empowers organisations to ensure regulatory compliance and effectively manage
risk in every send, share, receive, and use of sensitive data. The platform controls, monitors, and protects every
data interaction between people, machines, and systems across user collaboration, automated workflows, and
enterprise Al environments -— including Al agent integrations secured through the Kiteworks Al Data Gateway
and MCP server.

Deployment & Compliance: The platform supports on-premises, private cloud, hybrid, and FedRAMP

deployments (Moderate Authorized since June 2017; FedRAMP High Ready status also achieved). It enables

organisations to achieve strict regulatory compliance across frameworks including NIST CSF, HIPAA, SOX,

GDPR, GLBA, FISMA, CMMC 2.0, and ITAR. It carries FIPS 140-3 validation and ISO 27001/17/18

certifications, with automated audit reporting and a unified audit log providing up-to-date data governance and

protection.

Users & Sectors: Kiteworks is trusted by ClOs, CISOs, and CDOs across more than 35,000 global enterprises

and government agencies, including organisations like NYC Health + Hospitals, KPMG, NHS, Purdue Pharma,

Morgan Lewis, and Tyler Technologies. Over 100 million end-users rely on Kiteworks for sensitive data security.

Unique Value: Kiteworks is committed to measurable security outcomes — ensuring visibility, compliance, and

control over data such as Pll, PHI, intellectual property, and controlled unclassified information (CUI). Its zero-

trust architecture, possessionless editing (SafeEDIT), and standards-based digital rights management (via the

OpenTDF Trusted Data Format) deliver data protection that travels with the data itself — beyond the perimeter.

The platform is designed for any organisation transmitting or managing sensitive data, providing a safe, simple

solution for secure data exchanges across all digital channels.

Principal risks and uncertainties

They key principal risk is deemed to be concentration of credit risk.

Financial instruments that potentially expose the Company to concentrations of credit risk consist principally of

cash and cash equivalents and accounts receivable.

The Company deposits cash and cash equivalents with multiple financial institutions, the balances of which

frequently exceed locally government insured limits.

Accounts receivable consist primarily of amounts due from customers in the course of normal business activities.

The Company generally does not require collateral or other security in support of accounts receivable.

The allowance is based on management's ongoing assessment of the economic health of its customers and its

history of credit losses. .

Misstatements can arise from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually or in the aggregate, they

could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of these financial

statements.

Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures

in line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect material misstatements in respect of irregularities,

including fraud. The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud is
detailed below:

We obtained an understanding of the legal and regulatory frameworks within which the group operates, focusing

on those laws and regulations that have a direct effect on the determination of material amounts and disclosures

2006 and Taxation legislation. We identified the greatest risk of material impact on the financial statements from

irregularities, including fraud, to be the override of controls by management and the recognition of revenue,

specifically in respect of the cut-off assertion.

2.5 Revenue
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Group and the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the
consideration received or ‘receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes. The following criteria must also be met before revenue is recognised:
The Group’s revenues are generally derived from one-year subscriptions to its file-sharing platform
services. Occasionally the Group will sell multi-year subscriptions. The Group’s multiple-deliverable
arrangements may also include annual subscriptions of software licenses and annual software
maintenance and support with one-year to three-year antivirus support on the server. The Group’s
arrangements generally do not include any provisions for cancellation, termination, or refunds that
would significantly impact recognised revenue.
Revenue is recognised upon transfer of control of promised products and services to customers in an
amount that reflects the consideration the Group expects to receive in exchange for those products
or services. There is no variable consideration within the Group’s contracts with customers that would
need estimation of the amount it expects to receive for the total transaction price if it is probable that
a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognised will not occur.
Subscription revenue — Subscription revenue is comprised of Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) fees for ©
the right to use the software for a limited period on a hosted public or private cloud or on premise.
Included in the subscription are frequent updates/upgrades to its platform, which require customers
to update the software license after a release is made. Without the ongoing updates for security, .
there is significantly diminished value to the product. The software license and the subsequent
updates are not distinct from each other and, therefore, are considered a single performance
obligation delivered to customers. The customer accesses and uses the software on an as-needed
basis for up to the number of designated users. The underlying arrangements typically include a
single fee for the service that is billed monthly, quarterly, annually, or as specified in the contract.
Subscription revenue represents distinct services that are substantially the same and have the same
pattern of transfer to the customer. Subscription revenue is recognised ratably over the term of the
arrangement.
Contract costs to obtain a contract — Incremental costs of obtaining a contract with a customer are
capitalised as an asset (deferred commissions) when the Group expects to recover those costs over
a period beyond one year. Such costs consist primarily of sales commissions and are amortised on a
straight-line basis over a benefit period, generally one to three years, The Group determined the
benefit period based on the nature of its customer relationships and other factors. Amortisation of
deferred commissions is recognised in sales and marketing expense in the consolidated statements
of operations. The Group applies a practical expedient to expense sales commissions when the
amortisation period would have been one year or less.
