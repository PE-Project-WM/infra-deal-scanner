Strategic report
The directors present their strategic report on the company for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Review of the business
Imagine Communications EMEA (UK) Ltd. (‘the company’) is part of a group of companies owned by Imagine
Communications Corp., a company incorporated in the United States of America. The company provides products
and services for media and entertainment, multi-channel video programming distributors and enterprise markets.
The company manages its operations with local presence via its own branches in Italy and Spain and other group
entities in certain other European countries.
The company segregates its business operations into two primary business units: 1) Ad Tech, and 2) Playout and
Networking Solutions.
Ad Tech products comprise of powerful, integrated media software that helps broadcast, cable and broadband
companies make faster, smarter business decisions to optimize inventory value and drive ad spend across
multichannel, multimarket operations. This segment also includes ad-tech oriented delivery products including
video packaging technology for end user devices, dynamic ad insertion, and video edge solutions for advertising
and content delivery.
Playout and Networking Solutions includes playout products comprised of highly scalable, software-based
solutions that speed channel deployment time and easily upgrade with no costly hardware replacement - ensuring
today's investment fits an operation's requirements well into the future. It also includes networking products that
simplify and streamline signal management and processing, overseeing the entire network from any location and
ensuring signal integrity with ease.
No significant change in nature of these activities occurred during the year.

KPlis are set out below:
The company recorded turnover of £41,007k, an increase of 4.2% over the prior reporting period (2023: £39,354k)
with operating profit for the financial year of £1,181k (2023: £1,253k). The increase in turnover was primarily a
combination of an increase of £7,181k of product turnover and a decrease of £6,446k of service turnover in 2024.

Financial risk management
The company's operations may expose it to a variety of financial and operational risks as mentioned below. The
policies set by the board of directors in conjunction with the corporate management team of the immediate or
ultimate parent company are implemented by the company's management. Stringent internal reporting ensures
that managers are able to maintain a vigilant watch over such financial risks.
Liquidity risk
The company has access to longer term funding from its parent company if required. The parent company’s
treasury department manages this risk, ensuring that sufficient funding and liquidity is available to meet the
. expected needs of the company.
Currency risk
A proportion of the company’s turnover is derived from overseas sales and intercompany sales and purchases,
and as such the company has currency exposures on transactions which arise from sales and purchases in
. currencies other than its functional currency. Potential exposures to foreign currency exchange rate movements
are managed by having multiple currency bank accounts, through monitoring cash flow forecasts and monthly
currency exposure reporting in all currencies in which the company trades.
Credit risk
The company is exposed to risk if a counterparty to a financial instrument fails to meet its contractual obligations.
Such a risk arises principally in relation to receivables due from customers and cash deposited with banks. The
company manages this situation through credit approvals, credit limits and monitoring procedures, which are
closely evaluated by specialist staff and management. Credit risk with respect to receivables is concentrated in the
broadcast, cable, satellite, and out-of-home networks sector. As far as it is both feasible and practical to do so,
cash is held centrally by the parent company's treasury department. The board is of the view that the risk is at an
acceptable level.
interest rate risk
The company has non-interest-bearing liabilities due to other group undertakings. As of the year end, the company
had no third-party loans.

Revenue recognition (continued)
Revenue from the sale of products and services is recognised when persuasive evidence of an arrangement
exists, the fee is fixed or determinable, collectability is reasonably assured, delivery of a product has occurred
and title has transferred or services have been rendered. If an arrangement requires the delivery or performance
of multiple deliverables or elements, the company evaluates whether individual deliverables represent separate
units of accounting. If they do, revenue will be allocated among the separate units of accounting based on relative
selling price and we will recognise revenue associated with each unit of accounting separately. If multiple
deliverables within an arrangement are not considered separate units of accounting, they are accounted for as a
combined unit of accounting and revenue is generally recognised over the performance period. In cases where
contractual obligations related to customer acceptance exist, revenue is not recognised until acceptance is
obtained.
Revenue from sale of multi-element arrangements
The company derives a portion of its revenue from term-based licensing of software bundled with multiple-year
post-contract support arrangements. For term-based licenses and related support, customer payments are
generally received, and revenue is recognised, on a monthly basis over the term of the agreement. In limited
circumstances, the company licenses software on a perpetual basis with bundled support. If vendor specific
objective evidence of fair value exists for the support arrangement, the support arrangement is deferred and
recognised ratably over the support period and the remaining arrangement consideration is recognised as product
license revenue upon delivery. In cases where vendor-specific objective evidence of fair value cannot be
established, the entire arrangement consideration is recognised ratably over the term of the support arrangement.
Revenue from sale of services
The company’s services revenue consists primarily from installation, product maintenance, and consulting. The
nature of the services is reviewed to determine which revenue recognition method best reflects the nature of
services performed. When a service transaction consists of a single act, revenue is recognised at the time the act
is performed or at the completion of the single service. When the amount of services to be performed in the last of
a series of acts is so significant in relation to the entire service transaction that performance is not deemed to have
occurred until the final act is completed, the service revenue is not recognised until the final act is completed. When
the provision of services extends beyond an accounting period with more than one performance act, revenue for
these multi-performance acts are recognised for each act expected to be performed, based on the ratio of the direct
costs incurred to the total estimated direct costs of the entire transaction or ratably over the period of performance.
Revenues from separately priced and optional extended warranty and product maintenance services are generally
deferred and recognised on a straight-line basis over the contractual service period.
The company records shipping and handling costs billed to customers as revenue with offsetting costs recorded
as cost of sales.
