Strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025
The Directors present their Strategic Report on Classic Cinemas Limited (the “Company”) for the year ended 31
December 2025.
Principal activities
The Company’s principal activity arises from booking fees charged to customers, recognised at the date of the film
showing from the Group’s UK cinema operations. The Company’s immediate parent is Cineworld Cinemas Limited
and the ultimate parent is Cineworld Parent Limited. Throughout the rest of this report, the “Group” is used to refer
to actions Classics Cinemas Limited has performed as part of the Cineworld Group headed by Cineworld Parent
Limited. Subject to the events noted in note 17, there are no changes foreseen to the company’s principal activities.
Financial Performance and Position
Revenue for the year is £9,664,795 (2024: £10,758,437) which has decreased from prior year due to lower volume
of the bookings made during the year.
The profit before and after taxation for the year ended 31 December 2025 was £25,821,825 (2024: £8,229,907). The
net profit during the year principally represents the movement on an expected credit loss provision recognised
against intercompany balances as a credit of £16,347,367 (see note 11) due to the improvement in the company’s
credit rating from B- to B. The probability of default used in the calculation decreased from 2.6% in the prior year to
0.28% in the current year to reflect the change in credit rating.
At 31 December 2025 the Company had net assets of £98,075,839 (2024: £72,254,014).
Business review
During 2025, the Group continued to benefit from the Restructuring Plans (“RPs”), which became effective on 1
October 2024. The RPs were designed to improve the sustainability and financial stability of the Group, primarily
through the restructuring of the UK lease portfolio and associated cost savings. The anticipated savings were realised
during 2025 and, together with improved trading performance, contributed to an improvement in the Group’s
financial performance.
The Group also continued to invest in its cinema estate and customer proposition during the year, supporting the
longer-term performance of the business.
During 2025, the Group undertook a review of its corporate structure with the objective of simplifying and
streamlining the legal entity structure and reducing the associated administrative burden. As part of this exercise, a
number of entities which were no longer required were struck off. These changes had no material impact on the
Group’s underlying operations or financial performance.
As the Company's principal activity is the generation of booking fee income from the Group's UK cinema operations,
its performance is dependent on cinema attendance and trading activity across the UK estate. Steady trading
performance during 2025, together with the benefits arising from the RPs, supported the Company's financial
performance during the year. .

Going concern
The Directors have prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis, which assumes that the Company
will have sufficient resources to meet its obligations as they fall due.
The Company is a subsidiary of, and is controlled by, Cineworld Parent Limited. The Company’s activities are
principally booking fees charged to customers and, where required, financial support from Cineworld Parent Limited.
As part of their going concern assessment, the Directors reviewed the Company’s forecasts, cash flow and liquidity
projections, together with the financial position and available liquidity of the wider Group. The forecasts reflect
: management's expectations regarding the film release slate, admissions, box office performance, average ticket
price and concession spend, as well as the expected cost base of the retained cinema estate. They also incorporate
the continuing benefits of the lease restructuring undertaken in 2024.
In assessing the appropriateness of the going concern basis, the Directors have considered the Company's forecast

Impairment of financial assets
The Company recognises a loss allowance for expected credit losses on investments in debt instruments that are
measured at amortised cost, trade debtors and contract assets. The amount of expected credit losses is updated at
each reporting date to reflect changes in credit risk since initial recognition of the respective financial instrument.
The Company always recognises lifetime expected credit losses (ECL) for trade debtors and contract assets. The
expected credit losses on these financial assets are estimated using a provision matrix based on the Company’s
historical credit loss experience, adjusted for factors that are specific to the debtors, general economic conditions
and an assessment of both the current as well as the forecast direction of conditions at the reporting date, including
time value of money where appropriate.
For all other financial instruments, the Company recognises lifetime ECL when there has been a significant increase
in credit risk since initial recognition.
Lifetime ECL represents the expected credit losses that will result from all possible default events over the expected
life of a financial instrument. In contrast, 12-month ECL represents the portion of lifetime ECL that is expected to
result from default events on a financial instrument that are possible within 12 months after the reporting date.
All amounts due from Group undertakings are repayable on demand and the nature of these receivables is
considered within the expected credit loss calculation.
The expected credit losses are calculated using the 3-stage general impairment model as follows:
e probability of default — the likelihood that the borrower would not be able to repay in the very short payment
period;
© loss given default — the loss that occurs if the borrower is unable to repay in that very short payment period;
and
® exposure at default - the outstanding balance at the reporting date.
The probability of default is based on an external assessment of the Group’s weighted corporate default rate
which is a function of the Group’s external credit rating.
Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of current and deferred income tax expense.
Tax on the profit or loss for the ‘year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the profit and loss
account except to the extent that it relates to items recognised directly in equity or other comprehensive income,
in which case it is recognised directly in equity or other comprehensive income.
Current tax is the expected tax payable or receivable on the taxable income or loss for the year, using tax rates
enacted or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date, and any adjustments to tax payable in respect of
previous years.
Deferred tax is provided on temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities for financial
reporting purposes and the amounts used for taxation purposes. The following temporary differences are not
provided for: the initial recognition of goodwill; the initial recognition of assets and liabilities that affect nether
accounting nor taxable profit other than in a business combination, and differences relating to investments in
subsidiaries to the extent that they will probably not reverse in the foreseeable future. The amount of deferred tax
provided is based on the expected manner of realisation or settlement of the carrying amount of assets and
liabilities, using rates enacted or.substantively enacted at the balance sheet date. A deferred tax asset is recognised
only to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profits will be available which the asset can be utilised.
