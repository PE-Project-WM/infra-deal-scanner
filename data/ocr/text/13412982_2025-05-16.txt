Strategic Report
Year ended 31 December 2024
The directors in preparing this strategic report have complied with s414C of the Companies Act 2006.
Principal activities ,
The principal activity of the Company continue to be development and operation of solar photovoltaic (PV) plants located in
the United Kingdom.
Review of the business
ASE UK Holdings (“ASE UK”) is primarily engaged in the development of potential solar and Battery Energy Storage System
(“BESS”) site from origination all the way through to approved permitting. The year for the industry has not been without its
challenges. Whilst the supply chain crunch as a consequence of the COVID lockdowns that led to the rise in engineering,
procurement and construction ("EPC") costs has abated significantly and the solar panel prices are at competitive lows,
interest rates continue to remain high. ASE UK Holdings evaluates each potential investment in a development asset via an
investment committee process and the high interest rates present a challenge when meeting hurdle rates required for asset
development. In addition, UK grid congestion and connection delays have pushed out the potential connection dates for
development projects. However, ASE UK has leveraged off both its fixed funding lines and the market intelligence and access
of its shareholders and its connections with the grid operators to cushion itself from such effects to the extent possible.
Despite the market challenges, ASE UK successfully acquired 1 projects (65MWp of solar and 18MWp of BESS) in early 2024
and was swiftly able to finance the construction of the same by closing a framework facility for up to £200 million of project
financing with NordLB.
ASE UK submitted 202MWp of planning applications in 2024. 123MWp has obtained approval and ASE is expecting the.
remainder to be successfully by H2 2025. In addition, the development team will file planning applications for an additional
400MWp in 2025. The lack of volatility and the relative political stability implies a cautiously optimistic outlook for the industry
as a whole.
Financial position .
The result of the Group shows a pre-tax loss of £4,364,469 (2023: profit £10,413,859), these costs mainly relate to recharge of
relevant management services from wider group service companies that are attributable to the Group.
ASE UK started generate revenue in 2024, will more projects expect to start selling energy in 2025, the position of the group is
strong. The Group has closed a £200 million project finance facility and its ultimate parent company ASE Holdings 1 B.V. has
raised over €400 million of funding to take all the projects in the United Kingdom, the Netherlands and Germany through to
operations.
Key performance indicators
Whilst the business is undergoing the development cycle there are limited key performance indicators. However, the main
indicators below will determine the performance of the company as it goes through its full cycle of development, construction
and operations. -
Development milestones :
The Group has an estimate of its development milestones, i.e., the expected dates where it hopes to receive certain consents.
The development milestones are tracked and if the actual date of receipt is later than anticipated, management must determine
if that was down to industry norms or specific performance.
EPC costs
The Group benefits from the framework agreements for both EPC and procurement that have been negotiated by ASE LP.
However, certain projects fall outside the framework agreements as they are bespoke, and the EPC costs associated with those
can vary during the construction of a project and between projects.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Development
The Group is in the business of developing net new assets to take through to construction and operation. The risk associated
with getting full consent to construct the solar PV farms can be broken down into the following activities:
a) Land
There are instances where the land leases may require changes (based on items that become clear during the construction
phase). Such changes could reduce the equity returns for the Group
b) Grid
The UK is heavily grid constrained. Like all other developers, the Group pays a deposit for its grid application and gets any
utilised portion of the deposit back if there is no grid availability for a particular project. In certain circumstances, grid will
be made available subject to modifications, with the costs to be borne by the applicants.
Credit risks
The Group aims to sell power to mid-market UK corporates either directly or via the grid. The direct sales leave the Group
exposed to the credit risk of its power off-takers. There is less risk associated with sales via the grid as a credit event for a
particular off-taker can be compensated by selling the power to another off-taker via the grid.
Price risks
To the extent the Group will sell its energy directly to the grid for the merchant spot price, it is exposed to power price
fluctuations. It will seek to hedge power prices with market counterparties like banks and energy companies. A separate credit
analysis is undertaken on the hedging counterparties.

Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and deferred tax.
The tax currently payable is based on taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from net profit as reported in the profit and
loss account because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable or deductible in other years and it further
excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The Company's liability for current tax is calculated using tax rates that
have been enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.
Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable on differences between the carrying amounts of assets and
liabilities in the financial statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the computation of taxable profit and is
accounted for using the balance sheet liability method. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all taxable temporary
differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that taxable profits will be available against
which deductible temporary differences can be utilised in the foreseeable future.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each balance sheet date and reduced to the extent that it is no longer
probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered. Deferred taxis calculated
at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period when the liability is settled, or the asset is realised based on tax laws
and rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date. Deferred tax is charged or credited in the
profit and loss account.
