FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

The directors present their Strategic Report together with the audited financial statements of Cognism Limited
(‘Cognism’” or the “Company”) together with its subsidiaries (the “Group”) for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal business activities

Cognism provides go-to-market sales intelligence to sales and marketing teams. The Cognism platform helps
customers identify the right organisations, reach relevant decision-makers and build a more predictable sales pipeline,
combining high-quality, compliant contact and company data with workflows that fit the way modem revenue teams
work, ‘

Europe is a particular area of strength for Cognism, where the Group has developed strong data coverage and
compliance capabilities. During 2025, the Group further sharpened its commercial focus on mid-market and enterprise
customers, where the Group believes these capabilities provide the greatest value and support deeper, longer-term
customer relationships.

The Group is headquartered in the United Kingdom and serves customers internationally, with operations across
Europe and North America.

Strategy

The Group's strategy is centred on three priorities which shaped its actions during 2025 and continue to guide its
development:

e Focus where Cognism is strongest. The Group is concentrating its go-to-market investment on mid-market
and enterprise customers, particularly in Europe, where Cognism believes its data quality, compliance
credentials and integrated workflows provide the strongest differentiation. The focus is on helping customers
achieve better outcomes from their sales and marketing activity and building relationships that endure over
the long term.

« Build one great platform for customers. Cognism is concentrating product and engineering investment
behind its core sales-intelligence platform. A simpler product portfolio enables the Group to focus resources
on the capabilities customers value most, accelerate innovation and deliver a more consistent and connected
customer experience.

e Grow sustainably. The Group aims to grow while improving operating efficiency and reducing cash
consumption. Sustainable growth enables continued investment in customers, people and the platform, while
building a stronger and more resilient business capable of creating long-term value for all stakeholders.

Key performance indicators
The Board monitors a combination of financial and operational measures to assess the Group’s progress against its
strategy and the value Cognism delivers to customers.
Financial measures include tumover, gross margin, operating profitability and cash flow. The Board also closely
monitors Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR), Gross Dollar Retention (GDR), churn and expansion within the existing
customer base. Together, these measures provide insight into the growth and durability of recurring revenues, the
strength of customer relationships and the effectiveness of the Group's commercial strategy.
Performance is considered as a whole, alongside customer, product and operational indicators, rather than through
any single measure in isolation.
Review of the business
2025 was a year of continued growth, greater focus and improved operating discipline. The Group grew revenue while
concentrating resources on the customers, products and capabilities where Cognism believes it can deliver the
greatest vaiue over the !ong term.

Growing with customers
Group turnover increased by 12.3% to $83.7 million (2024: $74.5 million), reflecting continued growth across the .
Group's principal markets and a sharper commercial focus on mid-market and enterprise customers.
Cognism’s focus remains on building durable customer relationships by delivering high-quality, compliant data that
helps sales and marketing teams work more effectively. During the year, the Group continued to concentrate its go-
to-market resources on the customers and use cases where its data quality, compliance capabilities and integrated
workflows provide the greatest value.
Investing in the customer proposition
Gross profit increased to $63.6 million (2024: $57.6 million), while gross margin was 76.0%, compared with 77.3% in
the prior year. The 1.3 percentage point movement principally reflected higher data-acquisition and hosting costs as
the Group continued to support the scale and quality of the platform.
Investment in the capabilities customers value remained a priority. Research and development costs expensed during
the year increased to $16.5 million from $14.9 million in 2024, as Cognism increasingly concentrated product and
engineering resources behind its core sales-intelligence platform.
The Group also took further steps to simplify its product portfolio during the year, including the disposal of Mailtastic
and the planned phasing out of Kaspr. This increased focus enables Cognism to concentrate investment on the core
platform and to create a more consistent and connected experience for customers.
Improving operating efficiency
Alongside continued investment in product, data and the customer experience, the Group took action during the year
to align its cost base and commercial capacity more closely with its strategic priorities. As a result, the operating loss
reduced by approximately 11% to $37.8 million, compared with $42.5 million in 2024. This improvement was achieved
while continuing to invest in product development, data quality and the capabilities that underpin the customer
proposition. :
The net loss for the year was $47.4 million (2024: $42.2 million). The result included significant non-cash charges,
principally a $15.1 million goodwill impairment and a $13.0 million fair-value loss on the Group's Convertible Notes.
The Group reported net liabilities of $38.4 million at 31 December 2025, compared with net assets of $5.1 million at
the end of 2024. The movement principally reflects the loss for the year, including the significant non-cash charges
described above. The Board continues to monitor the Group’s balance sheet and liquidity position closely alongside
its focus on improving operating performance and cash generation.
Cash performance improved materially during the year. Net cash used in operating activities reduced by
approximately one third to $18.7 million, compared with $27.9 million in 2024, while the Group ended the year with
cash and cash equivalents of $21.4 million, compared with $13.2 miltion at the end of 2024. This improved cash
performance provides a stronger foundation for continued investment in customers, the platform and the Group's
long-term development.
Future developments
Cognism’s priorities remain centred on delivering greater value to customers through a focused, high-quality sales-
intelligence ptatform.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Group operates in a dynamic technology and data market and is exposed to risks that could affect its ability to

deliver value to customers, execute its strategy and achieve its financial and operational objectives. The Board

regularly reviews the principal risks facing the Group and the actions taken to manage them.

Technology and innovation risk

Sales intelligence, artificial intelligence and data-processing technologies continue to develop rapidly, alongside

changing customer expectations. Failure to innovate effectively or to respond to these developments could reduce

the relevance and competitiveness of the Group's products, affecting customer acquisition and retention.

Cognism manages this risk through continued investment in product development, data infrastructure and Al-enabled

capabilities. Product and engineering resources are increasingly concentrated behind the core Cognism platform,

supported by regular development cycles and a scalable cloud-based architecture. This focus is intended to enable

the Group to respond more quickly to customer needs and changes in the market.

Market and competition risk .

The sales-intelligence market remains highly competitive, with established providers and newer technology

businesses competing on data quality and coverage, product functionality, price and integration with customer

workflows.

The Group seeks to differentiate itself through the quality and usefulness of its data, its compliance capabitities and

the integration of Cognism into the tools and processes customers already use. Its sharper focus on mid-market and

enterprise customers, particularly in Europe, is intended to concentrate resources on the segments where the Group

believes these capabilities provide the greatest customer value and differentiation.

Regulatory and legal risk

The Group's services depend on the lawful collection, processing and use of business information. Data-protection,

privacy and related technology regulation continues to develop across the markets in which the Group operates,

creating an increasingly complex compliance environment.

Changes in legislation, regulatory interpretation or enforcement practices could affect the Group's operations,
. increase compliance costs or restrict the way its products and data can be used.

Cognism manages this risk through dedicated legal, privacy and compliance processes, ongoing monitoring of

regulatory developments and the application of privacy-by-design principles within product development. Maintaining

customer trust in the compliant use of data remains an important part of the Group's proposition.

Cybersecurity and data privacy risk

Cyber-attacks, security vulnerabilities or failures in the Group's systems or those of its service providers could disrupt

services or result in the loss or unauthorised disclosure of confidential, proprietary or personal information. Such an

event could adversely affect customers, the Group's reputation and its financial performance.

The Group maintains a security and privacy programme supported by recognised standards, including ISO 27001

Protecting the availability and integrity of the platform and the data entrusted to Cagnism is fundamental to maintaining

Currency risk

The Group operates internationally and generates revenues and incurs costs in a number of currencies. It is therefore
exposed to movements in foreign-exchange rates, particularly between GBP, EUR and USD, which may affect
reported results and cash flows.

Credit risk

Credit risk principally arises from customer receivables and cash held with financial institutions. Failure by a customer
or financial counterparty to meet its obligations could result in financial loss to the Group. .

Customer credit risk is managed through credit assessment and approval processes, monitoring of outstanding
receivables and active collection and impairment procedures. Treasury exposure is managed through the use of
established financial institutions with appropriate credit quality.

Liquidity and funding risk

The Group continues to invest in its platform and growth while operating cash flow remains negative, and the Group
has moved into a net liability position. Insufficient liquidity, or a reduction in available financing, could constrain the
Group's ability to meet its obligations as they fall due or to sustain planned investment in customers, people and
product. Maintaining appropriate liquidity therefore remains an important area of focus for the Board.
Liquidity is managed through regular cash-flow forecasting, close monitoring of expenditure and performance against
plan, consideration of downside scenarios and the maintenance of appropriate cash and financing resources.
Operating cash performance improved materially during 2025. In September 2026, the Group agreed to an
amendment to its existing debt facility, providing commitments of up to $30.0 million with no financial maintenance
covenants. Availability under the amended facility is determined by an ARR-based borrowing-base mechanism, and
the Group's access to financing is therefore linked to the performance of its recurring revenue base. The Board
monitors this relationship, together with the maturity profile of the Group's financing arrangements, as part of its
regular review of liquidity. Further information regarding the Group's liquidity, financing arrangements and going-
concern assessment is provided in the Directors’ Report and notes 20 and 21 to the financial statements.

During 2025, the Board considered these matters in a number of significant decisions, including the Group's sharper

Business partners, conduct and wider stakeholders

Going concern

The Directors have considered the Group’s future liquidity and reviewed Board-approved cash-flow projections
covering the period to 31 December 2027. In making their assessment, the Directors considered the Group’s cash
resources, forecast trading and cash flows, net liability position and available financing arrangements. The Directors
also considered relevant downside sensitivities to the Group's forecasts.

In September 2026, the Company amended its existing revolving credit facility with its current lender. Under the
amended terms, the facility provides commitments of up to $30.0 mitlion, with availability determined by an ARR-
based borrowing-base mechanism. The amended facility has no financial maintenance covenants and matures on 30
June 2029. The $10.0 million balance drawn under the previous facility was rolled into the amended facility.

Having considered the Group's forecasts, sensitivities and available financing, the Directors have a reasonable
expectation that the Group has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future.
Accordingly, the Directors continue to adopt the going-concern basis of accounting in preparing the financial

continue as a going concern.

Revenue
Revenue is mainly generated from the delivery of Software as a Service (SaaS) to our customers. Revenue
recognition requires an agreement with a customer, which creates enforcement rights and obligations

revenue when (or as) a performance obligation is satisfied, which occurs when control of the services
underlying the relevant performance obligation is transferred to the customer.
The relevant performance obligation is access to the platform. Revenue recognition begins when the
performance obligation is satisfied and is apportioned straight line over the contracted term as the services
are rendered. Services are deemed to be delivered on a continuous basis throughout the course of the
contract.
Revenue is measured as the fair value of the consideration received or receivable and represents amounts
receivable for services provided in the normal course of business, net of discounts and sales-related taxes.
Intangible assets — goodwill
Goodwill represents the excess of the cost of a business combination over the fair value of the group’s share
of the net identifiable assets of the acquired subsidiary at the date of acquisition. Goodwill on acquisitions of
subsidiaries is included in intangible assets. Gains and losses on the disposal of an entity include the carrying
amount of goodwill relating to the entity sold. Goodwill is carried at cost less accumulated amortisation and
accumulated impairment losses. For the purposes of impairment testing, goodwill acquired in a business
combination is, from the acquisition date, allocated to each of the Group’s cash-generating units that are
expected to benefit from the combination, irrespective of whether other assets or liabilities of the acquiree
are to those units.
Goodwill amortisation is calculated by applying the straight-line method to its estimated useful life. Goodwill
is being amortised to ‘administrative expenses’, over a period of 10 years.

lowest levels for which there are separately identifiable cash flows. Where there is any indication that there
may be an impairment, the carrying value of the cash-generating unit (“CGU”) is tested for impairment.
Management estimates the fair value of its CGU using the income approach based upon a discounted cash
flow model. The income approach requires the use of many assumptions and estimates including future
revenues, expenses, capital expenditures, and working capital, as well as discount factors and income tax
rates. There are inherent uncertainties and management judgement required in these determinations. An
impairment loss is recognised for the amount by which the asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable
amount. The recoverable amount is the higher of a CGU’s fair value less costs to sell and value in use.
Provision for bad debts
The Group continually monitors its trade debtors to determine recoverability. Doubtful debts are assessed
regularly and are determined based on multiple criteria, including the age of the debt, payment history and
the debtor's financial condition. The Company applies significant judgement in assessing the recoverability
of debts and determining the appropriate level of provision.
Acquisitions
While Management uses its best estimates and assumptions as part of the purchase price allocation process
to accurately value assets acquired and liabilities assumed at the business acquisition date, the estimates
and assumptions are inherently uncertain and subject to refinement. As a result, during the purchase price
allocation period, which is generally one year from the business acquisition date, Management records
adjustments to the assets acquired and liabilities assumed, with the corresponding offset to goodwill. For
changes in the valuation of intangible assets between the preliminary and final purchase price allocation, the
related amortisation is adjusted in the period it occurs. Subsequent to the purchase price allocation period,
any adjustment to assets acquired or liabilities assumed is included in operating results in the period in which
the adjustment is determined.
Goodwill is tested for impairment at the cash generating unit (“CGU”) whenever an event occurs, or
circumstances change that would more likely than not reduce the fair value of a CGU below its carrying value.
