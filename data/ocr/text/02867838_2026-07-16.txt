Strategic Report for the Period from 1 March 2025 to 27 February 2026
The Directors present their Strategic Report for the period from 1 March 2025 to 27 February 2026.
Principal activities . .
The principal activities of the Company are the design and installation of electrical infrastructure projects
and facilities management and maintenance services to the UK Distribution Network Operator (DNO)
sector and to the wider utility and infrastructure industry.
Business review
The Company delivered robust year-on-year profit growth, alongside a successful year of work-winning,
and remains well positioned for further growth. The Company's core business is in good shape
supported by solid operational performance, customer relationships and cash generation with no
significant commercial issues. Additionally, the Company maintains a strong health and safety culture,
ensuring the wellbeing of its employees, customers,- supply chain partners and communities. The
decarbonisation of the UK economy underpins a range of tangible opportunities from privately funded
connection projects (e.g. for datacentres and gigafactories) and the Company's work with the electricity
distribution network operators (DNOs) is expected to grow given the regulatory push to further electrify
the UK.
The Company operates in the essential infrastructure services market with repeat income from essential
work through its framework arrangements with the DNOs. The latest regulatory investment cycle for the
DNOs that started in April 2023 has driven an increase in underlying run rates. There is also growing
demand for independent connections projects which resulted in the business securing its largest ever
connections project with GLP. This project involves the construction of a new substation and
: construction of cable routes through West Ham and Islington to connect the new data centre to the grid.
As a result, the Company achieved sales growth of 54% during the period with turnover in the period
increased to £262.0m (2025: £170.5m). The change in work mix has contributed to a reduction in gross
profit margin to 12.3% (2025: 15.7%), notably from the growth in independent connections work where
some caution is reflected in recognised margins until projects are well progressed. Combined with
robust control of overheads, this has resulted in the Company delivering a healthy and increased
operating profit of £11.4m (2025: £9.0m).
The business is closely aligned to the spend patterns of the DNO regulatory investment cycle and has
a substantial order book comprising large long-term framework contracts and shorter-term build /
refurbishment contracts. The Company has capabilities which closely align with the skills requirements
in the DNO and electricity regulatory environment and is continuing to invest in its business
development resources to maximise the opportunities which will become available during this DNO
regulatory cycle. During the 2025/26, the focus has been embedding the Company's new regional
delivery model to enable its strategy and future growth. This approach has strengthened customer
relationships, improved collaboration and integration across teams, and enhanced the agility of the
Company in responding to evolving customer needs. .
Whilst seeking to maximise these opportunities, the business also intends to expand its business to
markets outside of the DNOs including private connections and asset maintenance offerings. The future
outlook remains favourable as the business has a strong reputation in all its sectors and continues to
grow the pipelines of opportunities with the wider NG Bailey Group.
2025/26 was the first full year of our Journey to 2030 strategy, which lays out a clear roadmap for the
NG Bailey Group’s exciting future. This strategy sets a clear and effective framework for action and
decision making by carefully prioritising the projects and sectors we work in, focusing on recession-
resilient industries aligned to investment trends to ensure sustainable long-term growth. The strategy
builds on the heritage and strengths of NG Bailey, with a commitment to invest for the long-term to allow
us to capitalise on the significant opportunities in our marketplace.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Company’s principal risks and mitigations are as follows:

Health and safety

Heaith and safety continues to be of key importance for the Company, underpinning our abitity to deliver

projects, protect our people and maintain our reputation. Our ‘Safety First & Foremost’ principle is at

the heart of everything we do and has been developed to influence our employees, our customers and
. our supply chain, through leadership, to behave in a way that puts safety first. In order to achieve this,

there are comprehensive health and safety policies and procedures in place along with effective

leadership and organisational arrangements to operate, monitor and adapt these procedures and

ensure management accountability.

We use the MySafety system, an interactive system available to our teams and supply chain, to report

: and record safety, health and environmental metrics such as, ‘Don’t Walk By’ observations, inspections
and risk assessment method statements (RAMS). The focus is now on using this data more effectively
to identify safety trends, prioritise risks and inform targeted improvement activity. Opportunities to use
data and emerging technologies, including Al, are being explored to enhance risk insight and proactive
intervention. The behavioural Safety in Mind initiative continues to support and further develop the
health and safety culture. The next phase of the initiative introduces a targeted huddle culture, providing
short, focused safety conversations led by supervisors. This approach enables rapid sharing of learning
and alerts anc is particularly effective for our remote and dispersed workforces.

Market conditions

The Company's services may be affected by an economic downturn and reductions or delays in the
DNO regulatory investment cycle and government and private sector spending.

The external economic environment remains uncertain, shaped by a combination of geopolitical
instability, macroeconomic pressures and sector specific challenges. Ongoing conflicts and tensions,
including the US-—Iran situation, are contributing to volatility in global energy markets and supply chains,
while inflationary pressures, interest rates and labour availability remain key considerations for the UK
construction and infrastructure sector. These factors can result in customers delaying or cancelling
proposed and existing projects as well as presenting operational challenges around delivery and control
of costs. :

. The Company’s strategy is to focus on providing a quality service in its largely recession resilient sectors
to retain and win new frameworks and contracts. In particular, the UK government's spending plans on
infrastructure sectors and its targets for transitioning the UK to a low carbon economy should present
opportunities. The UK government recognises the importance of infrastructure to enable economic
growth and have publicly committed to accelerating the building of critical infrastructure for energy,
transport and technology. .

The Company continues to monitor the impact of the challenging market conditions and has developed
plans to respond to a range of scenarios. Underpinned by the wider NG Bailey Group’s strong balance
sheet, these plans consider market conditions, the availability of the workforce, changes in productivity
and the availability and price of materials.
A significant proportion of the Company's sales are protected against inflationary pressures by
contractual provisions through advanced purchases on secured jobs.

Competition
: The Company operates in highly competitive sectors. Whilst quality, capability, stability and reputation
are key parts of a customer's decision, price remains an important factor. The Company applies a
rigorous ‘bid no bid’ process to ensure it only tenders for and wins work where the margin is
commensurate with the risk. This rigour is increasingly important in the current environment with some
competitors accepting lower margins, inflation-related risks and other risks to ‘win’ turnover.
The Company continues to focus on cost and efficiency in order to remain competitive in the market.
Our continued investment in people, technology and training, supported by the NG Bailey Group's .
strong financial position, means the business is well placed to differentiate itself in a competitive
environment. We regularly undertake Group-wide Net Promoter Score (NPS) surveys with excellent
. feedback from our customers. Customer retention rates and levels of repeat business remain excellent
across the business.
Customers and supply chain
There is a high risk of insolvency within the construction industry as businesses struggle to deal with
inflationary cost pressures, supply chain challenges, high interest rates and customer demand
uncertainty given wider geopolitical pressures. The Company continues to appropriately manage its
cash flows through robust administration of its contracts and undertakes credit checks on customers
and the supply chain as a matter of routine.
Against a backdrop of high levels of insolvencies in our sector, we routinely monitor the Company’s
exposures in order to avoid over dependency on individual counterparties and carefully manage the
tisks of potential business failures from both the upstream (i.e. customer) and downstream (i.e. supply
chain) perspectives.
The Company builds supply chain resilience and strong relationships, working closely with suppliers
and subcontractors to achieve the highest quality standards for the best price whilst ensuring that it is
not over-reliant on any one supplier or subcontractor , supported by the implementation of a new supply
chain management system to enhance visibility, consistency and control across supplier onboarding, :
performance monitoring and risk management. The procurement and operational teams work hard to
agree prices with the supply chain early to reduce exposure to inflationary pressures and achieve
forecast targets. The Company is committed to paying its supply chain promptly as demonstrated by its
payment performance metrics. The percentage of invoices paid within 60 days is 100%.
Increasing levels of insolvencies and businesses under strain may present opportunities for low-risk
“ strategic acquisitions. We have the agility, funding and track record to deliver acquisitions quickly and
effectively as demonstrated by our acquisition of Schneider Electric’s substation engineering service
business in December 2019 and Fundamentals Limited’s HV services division in January 2021, adding
capability, geography and resilience to the Company.
Attracting and retaining talent
The Company recognises that attracting, retaining and developing people is key to ensuring it has the
right skills and capability to support the success and future growth of the business. Structural labour
and skills shortages in our industry have been exacerbated in recent years by the impacts of the
pandemic and Brexit. The Company has an excellent track record of retaining its employees and aims
to be a great place to work through market-benchmarked competitive remuneration, training and
development, a growing number of apprentice and graduate schemes and fostering an inclusive culture
through our ‘Fairness, Inclusion and Respect’ programme. We offer a competitive package of salary,
fixed and flexible benefits and employee discounts across the Group, which are reviewed annually to
ensure our people are fairly rewarded for their work.

System, data, cyber security and GDPR
The cyber security landscape continues to evolve with increasingly sophisticated threats being seen
through the combined use of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and the use of social
engineering techniques. Phishing and ransomware attacks continue to be the most common form of
cybercrime with an increase in supply chain attacks, which aim to breach company defences via its
suppliers.
The Company’s ability to enable safe, secure, and resilient business operations is dependent on its
systems being robust and secure and its data being protected. It is recognised that a loss of key systems
through an information security breach or attack could impact business operations and potentially lead
to a loss of confidential data, damaging the Company’s reputation.
We are continually developing and upgrading our IT infrastructure, software and cyber threat and
assessment capabilities. We have developed a comprehensive cyber security maturity model with
supporting preventative activities. Robust controls and procedures are in place to monitor the
performance of the systems, to identify and mitigate new and emerging cyber threats, and prevent
suspected cyber attacks to IT infrastructure (including a third party security monitoring service).
Response protocols are in place to support the Group’s response to threats or incidents with crisis
management exercises carried out periodically to test our plans and rehearse the Group’s response to,
and recovery from, a cyber attack.
Given that employees can be unwitting participants to data breaches, our digital safety awareness .
campaign continues to provide regular communications and mandatory online training to ensure that
employees are aware of the nature and potential consequences of cyber threats.
The NG Bailey Group holds Cyber Essentials Plus accreditation which demonstrates its commitment to
providing secure and robust ICT and information assurance.
