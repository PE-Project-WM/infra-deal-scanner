Strategic Report ——
For the Year Ended 31 December 2025
The director presents his Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the Company is the sale, installation and after sales servicing of wind energy converters , :
in Great Britain.
Business review
Management defined the turnover, operating profit and profit after tax as key performance indicators.
Turnover, operating profits and profits for the year were £53,883,796 (2024 (as restated) - £57,470,599),
£1,707,733 (2024 - £3,086,068) and £1,404,009 (2024 - £4,727,208) respectively. Turnover decreased
compared with 2024, primarily due to lower revenue from the supply and installation of wind turbines. This was
partly offset by an increase in maintenance and repairs revenue, which remained the Company's largest source
of turnover. Operating profit and profit for the year also decreased. During the year, management continued to
focus on the efficient delivery of maintenance services, turbine availability and timely response to customer call-
outs.
Principal risks and uncertainties ;
The principal risks currently existing for the Company are as outlined below.
Competitive risk
Technological and pricing pressures remain high in the onshore wind energy market. The ENERCON Group, as
consolidated by UEE Holding SE & CO. KG (the "ENERCON Group" or "ENERCON") continues to review global
market needs to develop new technology to reduce the levelised cost of energy.
ENERCON has developed product offerings that are specifically suitable to the UK market to allow the
Company to remain cost competitive. The Company is in a good position to continue to generate profits due to
its installation pipeline and its long-term maintenance contracts.
Political and legislative
The policy environment for onshore wind in Great Britain has become more supportive following the removal of
testrictive planning policies in England in July 2024. The Government's Clean Power 2030 Action Plan sets an
ambition of 27-29GW of onshore wind capacity in Great Britain by 2030, supported by the Onshore Wind
Taskforce Strategy published in July 2025, which addresses barriers including planning, grid connections and
routes to market. These developments present opportunities for the Company's turbine supply, installation and
maintenance activities, although the timing of growth remains dependent on project approvals, grid availability
and investment decisions.
This is an important development to allow the UK to meet its 2030 and 2050 climate targets, and the Company
expects to compete and obtain a sustained share of this revitalised UK onshore market.
Geopolitical
The Company's performance remains exposed to trade barriers between the UK and the EU, particularly due to
its reliance on EU-sourced materials. Additionally, geopolitical tensions including those arising from international
conflicts, trade disputes, shifts in political alliances, and changes in global economic policy may adversely
impact supply chains, trade tariffs and logistics. While tariffs remain low or absent for most of the ENERCON
Group’s goods, increased administrative complexity and customs checks can result in longer lead times and
elevated logistical costs for imported goods. Any further increase in trade friction, either globally or between the
UK and EU, could negatively impact the cost of goods sold, stock availability, and overall profitability.

2.5 Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Company and the turnover can be reliably measured. Turnover is measured as the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes.
Turnover from supply and installation contracts
Turnover from supply and installation contracts of standard wind turbines is recognised when risks
and rewards are passed to the customer which is deemed to be when the wind turbine is fully
operational and a completion certificate has been issued. Differences between agreed progress
billings to date and cumulative revenue of a supply and installation contract is recognised as
