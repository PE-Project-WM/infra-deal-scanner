Principal risks and uncertainties
VodafoneThree, created by the merger of Vodafone UK and Three UK, brings significant strategic
opportunities alongside potential risks associated with integration and scale. The Company, as part of
Vodafone UK, has maintained a consistent approach to risk management during this period of change,
supported by its Risk Management Framework and dedicated governance arrangements, including the
VodafoneThree Board Audit and Risk Committee and Executive Risk and Compliance Committee.
The Company has identified the following principal risks which, due to their potential impact on customers,
operations, financial performance and reputation, could affect the delivery of our strategy. These risks are
monitored throughout the year, with in depth reviews presented to the relevant oversight committees. The
principal risks, together with the compensating controls and mitigations.to support effective operations
within appetite, are set out below.
a) Cyber threat :
The Company processes and stores significant volumes of customer and commercial data across its
Business and Consumer operations. A major cyber incident could result in data loss, service disruption,
and damage to customer trust. The merger has increased the scale and complexity of the technology
, and data environment, creating additional cyber integration risks that are being actively managed™
through enhanced security controls and governance, continuous vulnerability scanning, security
testing, and threat intelligence alerts.
: b) Technology resilience and service availability
~The Company relies on the resilience and availability of its IT systems to support critical business
processes and deliver services to customers. A significant system outage could lead to service .
interruption, reduced customer satisfaction and loss of revenue. The integration of two large
technology estates increases complexity, system interdependencies and operational change activity.
: These risks are being managed through structured integration planning, resilience testing and the
progressive adoption of common technology standards and controls.
‘ c) Network resilience and delivery :
The Company is dependent on the resilience and ongoing development of its network to deliver
services to customers and meet regulatory expectations. A prolonged network outage or delay to our
network delivery plan could result in loss of customer and regulator confidence. Network integration
activities increase delivery and operational complexity, with governance and resilience controls in place
to maintain service stability throughout. This risk is managed through life cycle management of
network assets, capacity monitoring and forecasting, operational monitoring, resilience testing, and
strong delivery governance. .
__ Managing risks and uncertainties is an integral part of successfully executing our strategic objectives and
delivering long term success following the merger. Risks are not static and as the internal and external
environment changes, some risks may increase or diminish while new risks may emerge. The Company also
continues to monitor current macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty and any ‘potential impact to
VodafoneThree. . .
As part of the risk management process, several additional risks with varied likelihood, potential impact and
: priority are managed on an ongoing basis; the risks listed above are those considered the highest priority.

Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. :
In assessing the appropriateness of the going concern basis, the directors have considered a period of
twelve months from the date of approval of these financial statements, through to 30 November 2027 (the
‘Going Concern Period’). The directors also considered material events and conditions immediately beyond
that period up to 31 December 2027.
As outlined in the Strategic Report on page 3, the Company, as part of Vodafone UK merged with Three UK,
together with VodafoneThree Holdings Limited as the parent company, forming VodafoneThree. Given
VodafoneThree’s integrated funding structure, the directors have considered the cash flow forecasts of
VodafoneThree on a consolidated basis to determine whether it is appropriate to prepare the financial
statements of the Company on a going concern basis.

“As part of the going concern assessment, the directors have considered: :

e¢ The Company's business model, principal risks and uncertainties, and future developments,
including the wider macro-economic environment as outlined in the Strategic Report; .

e VodafoneThree’s ‘Base Case’ cash flow forecast for the Going Concern Period as well as significant
events and conditions immediately beyond that period;

e The letter of support provided by Vodafone Group Plc, confirming they will provide financial support
to the Company, if required, to ensure the Company is able to meet its obligations as and when

. required throughout the Going Concern Period;

e The assessment that under the Base Case forecast VodafoneThree has sufficient liquidity
throughout and immediately after the Going Concern Period without the need for mitigating
actions or additional funding beyond the letter of support;

¢ The results from a severe but plausible downside scenario on VodafoneThree’s available liquidity;
and .

- @ The ability of Vodafone Group Plc to provide financial support and remit cash to VodafoneThree
under the cash pooling arrangement, based on their solvency and liquidity positions using publicly
available information.

Based on this assessment, the directors concluded no material uncertainties exist that could cast significant

doubt on the Company's ability to continue as a going concern throughout the Going Concern Period.

Accordingly, these financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. :

1.5 Revenue recognition
When the Company enters into an agreement with a customer, goods and services deliverable under the
contract are identified as separate performance obligations (‘Obtigations’) to the extent that the customer
can benefit from the goods or services on their own and that the separate goods and services are considered
distinct from other goods and services in the agreement. Where individual goods and services do not meet
the criteria to be identified as separate Obligations they are aggregated with other goods and/or services in
the agreement until a separate Obligation is identified. The Obligations identified will depend on the nature
of individual customer contracts, but might typically be separately identified for mobile handsets, other
equipment such as set-top boxes and routers provided to customers and services provided to customers
such as mobile and fixed line communication services. The Company’s digital services and Internet of
Things (‘loT’) customer offers typically include separate Obligations for communication services, as well as
equipment and software or software as a service (‘SaaS’). Where goods and services have a functional
dependency (for example, a fixed line router can only be used with the Company's services) this does not,
in isolation, prevent those goods or services from being assessed as separate Obligations. Activities relating
to connecting customers to the Company's network for the future provision of services are not considered
to meet the criteria to be recognised as Obligations except to the extent that the control of related
: equipment passes to customers. ;
The Company determines the transaction price to which it expects to be entitled in return for providing the
promised Obligations to the customer based on the committed contractual amounts, net of sales taxes and
