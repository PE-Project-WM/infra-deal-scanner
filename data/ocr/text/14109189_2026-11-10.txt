Business Description
TP Global Operations Limited (the “Company’), trading as 1GLOBAL, is a technology enabled, global mobile
communications services provider. The wider 1GLOBAL group (the “Group”) serves some of the world's largest financial
institutions, multinational businesses, mobile network operators, connected device manufacturers and consumers. As a
fully regulated telecommunications operator active in 43 countries, 1GLOBAL provides reliable voice, data and messaging
mobile communication services to millions of consumers and connected devices across more than 200 countries
worldwide. {n 12 of these markets, 1GLOBAL operates as a fully licensed, facilities-based Mobile Virtual Network Operator
(MVNO) while in the remaining 31 jurisdictions 1GLOBAL is a registered telco services provider.
The Company delivers regulated mobile communications services in the United Kingdom and supports selected central
functions within the Group's global operating model. The Company's activities are focused predominantly on the UK -
market, where it serves financial institutions, multinational enterprises and consumer segments. In particular, the
Company exploits its strong position within London as a leading global financial centre to provide network-level mobile
recording and compliance services to major banking institutions and regulated firms. In addition to enterprise and
compliance services, the Company supports UK consumer connectivity through both direct-to-consumer offerings and
business-to-business-to-consumer (“B2B2C”) partnerships that enable consumer-facing brands to embed regulated
mobile services within their own digital platforms. :
Strategic Focus and Corporate Reorganisation
The Group operates a technology-enabled global mobile communications platform built around a proprietary mobile core
network and API-driven delivery model. The Group's headquarters relocated to the Netherlands during 2025 as part of a
broader corporate reorganisation.
The Group's strategic focus since the acquisition in 2023 and turnaround of the Truphone assets has been to transition
from a traditional telecom operator model to a technology-led, API-exposed Telecommunications-as-a-Service model.
Within this framework, the Company has historically played a central role in enterprise connectivity, compliance services
and the development and deployment of key network capabilities.
During 2025, the Group executed a multi-phase corporate reorganisation designed to align legal structure, governance
and intellectual property ownership with the geographical distribution of management and commercial activity. A new
Dutch holding structure was established, and non-UK subsidiaries were transferred under that parent entity. However, the
Company continued to perform key central functions and remained integral to Group operations throughout 2025.
This process was completed in 2026, when certain customer contracts, intellectual property rights and central functions
previously located within the Company were migrated to other Group entities. This will result in a reduction in revenue
and certain central cost allocations within the Company compared to 2025.

General Data Protection Regulation
The General Data Protection Regulation 2016/679 (“GDPR") has applied since 25 May 2018 and establishes the
regulatory framework governing the processing and protection of personal data within the European Union and the
European Economic Area. In the United Kingdom, the UK GDPR, as incorporated into domestic law under the Data
Protection Act 2018, operates in parallel with EU GDPR requirements. In addition, the Group is subject to other
international data protection and privacy regimes, including applicable US and jurisdiction-specific telecommunications
and data handling regulations in the countries in which it operates.
The Group maintains a dedicated Data Protection Officer function and continues to review and enhance its data
governance and information security framework. During 2025, further refinements were made to data processing
controls, cross-border data transfer mechanisms and incident response procedures to ensure continued compliance
with GDPR, UK GDPR and other applicable legislation. This includes established processes to manage data subject
rights requests, mandatory training and awareness programmes for staff, periodic internal audits, and formalised
reporting protocols for personal data breaches to the relevant supervisory authorities where required. The Directors
consider data protection and information security to be critical components of the Group's operational and regulatory
framework.
The policies applied by the Company are consistent with those of the Group and are applied uniformly throughout the
Group.

Key financial and other performance indicators
The Group monitors performance and allocates capital on a consolidated basis, using clearly defined financial and
operational key performance indicators. These KPIs are not managed at an individual subsidiary fevel, including the
Company, but are assessed and reviewed at Group level in line with the integrated operating model and residual profit split
framework.

number of countries in which regulated electronic communication services are offered, the number of eSIM profiles

telecommunications operators.

Financial Risk Management
The Company's operations expose it to limited financial risks including price risk, credit risk, liquidity risk and foreign
exchange risk. The Directors have delegated the responsibility of monitoring financial risk management to the Audit
Committee of the Board. The Audit Committee reviews quarterly key risks metrics presented to it by management and
adjusts the Company policies accordingly. The policies set by the Board are implemented by the Company's finance
department. They include bid reviews and approvals, initial and ongoing monitoring of the creditworthiness of clients,
centrally managed ovemight cash pooling, access to sophisticated FX treasury solutions with strict trading limits for each
employee and daily monitoring of FX exposure.
. Price Risk
The Company undertakes detailed pricing and margin analysis for all major customer opportunities and authorizes all
major contracts by a Director of the Company. Smaller contracts below a certain threshold are approved by a member of
the finance department. After the implementation of a contract the commercial team undertakes a review the profitability
of each major customer contract periodically.
Credit Risk .
The Company's customer base is comprised predominately of larger organizations as well as some private individuals.
The Company offers directly contracted consumers on the BetterRoaming™ platform only pre-paid plans thus eliminating
any credit risk from individuals. In B2B2C contracts the credit risk is normally allocated to the B2B2C partner. For corporate
clients, management has implemented appropriate credit scoring policies prior to contracting with these clients.
Liquidity Risk
The Group closely monitors its working capital requirements. The Company finished on 31 December 2025 with £2.1m
of cash and cash equivalents. The Group generated net cash from operating activities of USD 38.6m (approximately

2.12 Non-current assets held for sale
Non-current assets (and disposal groups) classified as held for sale are measured at the lower of carrying amount
and fair value less costs to sell. Non-current assets and disposal groups are classified as held for sale if their
carrying amount will be recovered through a sale transaction rather than through continuing use. This condition is
regarded as met only when the sale is highly probable and the asset (or disposal group) is available for immediate
sale in its present condition. Management must be committed to the sale which should be expected to qualify for
recognition as a completed sale within one year from the date of classification.
When the group is committed to a sale plan involving loss of control of a subsidiary, all of the assets and liabilities
of that subsidiary are classified as held for sale when the criteria described above are met, regardless of whether
the group will retain a non-controlling interest in its former subsidiary after the sale.
