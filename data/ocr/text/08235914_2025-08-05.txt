The Directors present their strategic Report for the year ended 31 October 2024.

Principal activities

The company remains focused within the Data Centre Sector, specialising in providing technical fit-out services,

bespoke manufactured containment solutions, and managed services. These services support Global High-

Performance Cloud Computing Clients and Global Data Centre Providers.

The company has an industry-leading reputation for delivering its work and projects responsibly. It boasts a large

specialist offsite manufacturing facility, further enhancing its capabilities.

Operating as a global business with a local presence, the company maintains a balanced portfolio of projects

within the Global Data Centre Sector. This includes large, strategically important projects for some of the world's

largest cloud computing companies, as well as smaller projects delivered by regional branches. The company

continues to build its credentials in the sector, seeking to expand into new regions and areas.

Business review

The Group continues to experience challenging conditions, due to significant inflationary pressures, FX

movement and political uncertainty. These factors created some of the toughest market conditions for the

industry. 2024 saw high-profile names within the UK’s industry's top 10 construction firms, folding with thousands

of jobs lost.

Despite a slight easing of inflationary pressures, trading conditions remain difficult. Ongoing economic and

political uncertainty, have made customers cautious, impacting demand. Additionally, the industry continues to

grapple with a labour and skills shortage, as well as material disruptions affecting supply chains still suffering

from the effects of Brexit.

The Group has reported a turnover of £161.8m (2023: £116.5m) and a pretax profit of £3.1m for the year ended

October 2024 (2023: £7.6m).

Performance was further impacted by a small number of fixed price contracts, which encountered significant

challenges due to inflationary pressures, foreign exchange volatility, supply chain disruptions, and differing

commercial interpretations with customers. These contracts have weighed heavily on the Group's profitability,

underscoring the need for rigorous management and strategic foresight in navigating such agreements amidst

turbulent market conditions.

Despite some challenges, we continue to deliver a majority of contracts profitably. We have invested in our sales

team so that we can focus on quality margins and only take on work with an acceptable risk profile.

In response to current market conditions, The Group continues to invest in contract risk management. This
. initiative aims to identify and address potential risks proactively rather than reactively. The Group continuously

refreshes and strengthens its approach at all stages of contract lifecycles to ensure robust risk mitigation and

management strategies are in place.

The medium to long-term outlook within the sector remains strong with increased demand globally for data

centres. With the increased investment in Al, the data centre industry is increasingly looking for new sites and

regions to build and base their infrastructure. The Group continues to monitor the expansion of our customers

and ensure that we grow with them by the continued servicing of their needs.

One of our biggest challenges throughout our sector is a short to long term skills shortage, with the recruitment

and retention of people being crucial for the delivery of our future growth plans. The Group has an excellent

track record of retaining its employees. We aim to be a great place to work through market-competitive

remuneration, training and development. The company continues to invest and explore new ways to attract,

retain and develop its greatest asset. We are actively looking at new schemes including apprenticeships,

Principal risks and uncertainties

Health and Safety

DPI acknowledges that the increased working hours and exposure in different regions elevate the level of risk for —

our business. The Group recognises that Health and Safety is an integral part of our daily operations and a key
. component of our success. With significant stakes in the Data Centre Industry regarding H&S on the shop floor,

and potential adverse consequences to DPI's reputation should a critical event occur, we are committed to a

consistent and effective approach to managing health and safety.

Due care and consideration for all our SHE (Safety, Health, and Environment) undertakings are paramount. Our

dedicated SHE department promotes a comprehensive approach to SHE management, consistently advocating

the 'Health is our mission, Safety is critical!’ message.

. DPI implements several initiatives to advance SHE and wellbeing, such as monthly SHE Forums open to all
organisational levels. Regular health, safety and wellbeing communications are disseminated through our poster
campaign, H&S Alerts, Bulletins, and access to our extensive toolbox talk library containing over 190 toolbox
talks.

To support and promote positive health and safety reporting culture, we utilise QR code and Helmet Sticker QR
code reporting systems, enabling prompt reporting and addressing of any SHE issues, suggestions, or matters
arising. The generated information provides metrics to identify potential trends, allowing for proactive issue
resolution before impacting the company. While our SHE procedures are comprehensive, we also advocate a
‘Hearts & Minds’ philosophy, empowering employees to halt tasks and seek assistance if necessary.

In our pursuit of continual improvement and best practice standards, we have developed robust systems
including a high-risk activities protocol, DPI Visual Standards — 'Do It The DPI Way’, and DPI Bitsize training
modules on specific activities.

Regarding wellbeing, the Group maintains a substantial ratio of Mental Health First Aiders and Champions
supporting our workforce. We fully endorse support for all employees in their mental health needs.

. Effective leadership is fundamental to our SHE strategy. Senior leaders uphold the belief that it is unacceptable
for anyone to suffer harm or ill health from work activities, ensuring high health and safety standards across
projects.

DPI and its Health & Safety Team are recognised by the British Safety Council's International Safety Awards,

achieving the Distinction Award for 2025, covering the 2024 period.

Market Conditions ; ;

The Group's services are susceptible to economic downturns and fluctuations in the private sector. The Group's

strategy focuses on recession-resilient sectors, particularly critical infrastructure. Projections indicate that the

global Data Centre market will grow at a compound annual growth rate of 14.4% from 2024 to 2030. Leveraging

its global subsidiary presence, the Group is well-positioned to capitalise on these opportunities.

Notably, both the current UK government and opposition parties have publicly committed to accelerating the

development of critical infrastructure for energy, transport, and technology, recognising its importance for

economic growth.

Despite easing inflationary pressures during 2023/24, market conditions remain challenging due to persistently

high inflation, struggling supply chain and ongoing economic and political uncertainty in both the UK and

Globally. The construction industry has been particularly hard-hit, with prices rising faster than in other sectors,

putting pressure on operational delivery and trading margins. The combined effects of, Brexit, the Ukraine

conflict, uncertainty in the Middle East, and the cost-of-living crisis have exacerbated material and labour

Competition
. The Group operates in a highly competitive sector, where price significantly influences contract awards. The ‘
. Group balances quality, capability, stability, and reputation with cost considerations. To manage these dynamics,
the Group rigorously evaluates bids, ensuring margins align with risk. This approach is crucial in today’s
environment, where some competitors accept lower margins to secure turnover despite inflation-related risks.
A large proportion of next year’s sales are already secured, supporting the Group’s selective approach. Quality
and profitability take precedence over chasing turnover. The Group's focus on cost efficiency, investments in
people and technology, and strong financial position contribute to its competitive edge. High customer retention
and repeat business reflect consistent excellence.
Customers and Supply Chain . .
The construction industry is facing growing risks of insolvency, driven by rising costs from high inflation, supply
chain disruptions, and high interest rates. In response, DPI remains focused on managing its cashflows through
strict contract administration and routine credit checks on customers and suppliers.
To enhance supply chain resilience, the company builds strong relationships with suppliers and subcontractors,
ensuring high-quality standards at competitive prices while avoiding over-reliance on any single supplier or
subcontractor.
Our commercial, procurement, and project teams are dedicated to meeting forecast targets, maintaining
’ financial stability amid these challenges.-

demand for highly skilled technicians and engineers as increasing, leading to intensified competition among
companies. To address this, we form partnerships with trusted providers to ensure ethical service delivery in our

Financial instruments
The company’s principal financial assets consist of cash and trade debtors, with credit risk primarily associated
with trade debtors and amounts recoverable on contracts. To manage this risk, the financial strength of
customers is assessed before entering into contracts and regularly reviewed throughout the contract's duration
to monitor exposure.
Liquidity risk management is achieved through careful monitoring and forecasting of cash flow, aligning creditors
and debtors with contractual obligations, and implementing effective cash collection techniques. The company
mitigates liquidity constraints by closely managing cash flows, including daily cash level monitoring and monthly
cash flow forecast reviews. Accurate forecasting, retained profits, and project delivery modelling contribute to
effective liquidity management. .
Research and development activities

deliver compliant and efficient solutions within tight deadlines. Data Centre providers are constantly exploring

. . innovative ways to meet market capacity needs. In response, DPI actively engages in R&D activities to support

the bespoke builds required by our clients. These custom projects often involve extensive testing and
development to ensure that the solutions align with the specific structural needs of each Data Centre.

Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and
the acquirer's interest in the fair value of the Group's share of its identifiable assets and liabilities of
the acquiree at the date of acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost
less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a
. straight-line basis to the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income over its useful economic
life.
Other intangible assets : :
Intangible assets are initially recognised at cost. After recognition, under the cost model, intangible

losses.

indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is determined which is the higher of its fair
value less costs to sell and its value in use. An impairment loss is recognised where the carrying
. amount exceeds the recoverable amount. - : : : .

cannot be made, the useful life shall not exceed ten years. .

Short-term debtors are measured at transaction price, less any impairment. Loans receivable are

2.17 Cash and cash equivalents : . : co
Cash is represented by cash in hand and deposits with financial institutions repayable without penalty
, on notice of not more than 24 hours. Cash equivalents are highly liquid investments that mature in no
more than three months from the date of acquisition and that are readily convertible to known
amounts of cash with insignificant risk of change in value.
In the Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows, cash and cash equivalents are shown net of bank
. overdrafts that are repayable on demand and form an integral part of the Group's cash management.
2.18 Creditors . .
Short-term creditors are measured at the transaction price. Other financial liabilities, including bank
loans, are measured initially at fair value, net of transaction costs, and are measured subsequently at
amortised cost using the effective interest method. :
: 2.19 Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are recognised when an event has taken place that gives rise to a legal or constructive
obligation, a transfer of economic benefits is probable and a reliable estimate can be made.
Provisions are measured as the best estimate of the amount required to settle the obligation, taking
into account the related risks and uncertainties.
Increases in provisions are generally charged as an expense to profit or loss.
