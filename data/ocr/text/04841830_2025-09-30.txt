REVIEW OF THE BUSINESS AND FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
Digital Space Group Limited (the Company or Digital Space) is a connected cloud managed service provider serving the UK mid-
market and enterprise segments across both Private and Public Sectors. Digital Space’s core value proposition is empowering
corporate and mid-market organisations to drive transformation thereby enhancing productivity, maximising and safeguarding
the value of their data, and accelerating growth.
Digital Space’s core assets include its 24/7 network operations centre, private cloud data centres, 100Gb-enabled core network,
white-labelled security operations centre, and multi-cloud management portal, with core services and solutions comprising:
. Connected Space — SD-WAN, PWAN, managed LAN and Wi-Fi
. Productive Space — AWS, Azure and IBM public cloud; virtual data centre and private cloud hosting; unified
collaboration and communications (cloud desktops and cloud contact centres}
. Secure Space — Managed Detection and Response, Security as a Service
° Customer Space — a secure management platform for ordering, reporting and billing of our services.

During 2024, the business faced a series of external challenges that impacted performance at both the revenue and margin

. Service reductions from key customers driven by a combination of their subdued trading performance, merger and
acquisition activity, and changes to property footprints or operating models.
. Persistent high borrowing costs, with UK interest rates remaining elevated throughout the year, placed pressure on
capital allocation across our customer base. When combined with sluggish economic growth, rising operational costs,
and weakened business confidence, this environment led to a marked decline in discretionary IT investment and a
general hesitancy to upgrade or expand infrastructure.
These external factors created a more cautious market landscape, requiring the business to intensify its focus on retention,
operational efficiency, and value-led service delivery.

The Board of Directors of Digital Space Group Limited have had regard to the matters set out in the Companies Act Section 172(1)
(a) to (f) when performing their duties under Section 172. Both individually and collectively, the Directors and the Board act ina
manner they consider most likely to promote the long-term success of the Company for the benefit of its members, while
considering the interests of employees, fostering business relationships with suppliers and customers, and ensuring the impact of
the Company’s operations on the community and the environment is managed responsibly.

: The Board has clearly defined the Company’s purpose, culture, and values through its articulated vision, strategy, and
implementation plans. These are communicated consistently and transparently, including through weekly updates from the CEO
and Executive Team, and regular all-staff meetings, fostering alignment and engagement across the organisation.

The strategy focuses on delivering long-term, sustainable value through managed growth, both organic and acquisitive. The Main
Board and Executive Team regularly review performance against this strategy to ensure value is created and shared across all
stakeholders, including shareholders, employees, customers, and partners.
Employee engagement is critical to the execution of our strategic objectives. The Company is committed to being a responsible
employer, with a strong emphasis on health, safety, and well-being—both physical and mental. Ongoing investment in individual
and team development ensures that we continue to meet evolving customer needs, particularly in service and technology, while
offering fulfilling and rewarding careers to our employees.
Employee engagement is central to the successful delivery of the Company’s strategic plan. Key performance measures—those
which employees can directly influence—are embedded in daily operations, supporting a high-performance culture underpinned
by clearly defined values and behaviours. These form part of a wider cultural programme designed to inspire existing employees
and attract new talent. Engagement and communication are facilitated through a tiered approach, including departmental team
meetings, Executive ‘all hands’ updates, local team briefings, and weekly CEO updates, all integrated via the Group intranet, the
‘Digital Lounge’.
Strong and constructive relationships with partners—particularly customers and suppliers—are also vital to the Company’s
continued success. The Board ensures that these relationships are carefully considered in all relevant decision-making. Customer
expectations are monitored through regular satisfaction surveys and feedback mechanisms, while the Company remains
committed to treating all suppliers fairly and equitably.
The Board also recognises its responsibility to minimise the environmental impact of the Company’s operations. Environmental
considerations are integrated into operational planning and strategic decision-making wherever possible. The Company is
committed to conducting business in a responsible, ethical, and transparent manner, maintaining the highest standards of
corporate governance and integrity.
By upholding these principles, the Company continues to build a strong reputation among all stakeholders and ensures that its
growth strategy is delivered in a sustainable, inclusive, and long-term manner.
PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The Board regularly reviews the principal risks and uncertainties facing the Company and has identified the following as the
most material to the effective management and strategic direction of the business. The Company acknowledges that other risks
not currently identified, or currently deemed immaterial, may also impact future performance.
Cost of living crisis
Although the direct impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic have lessened, broader economic consequences continue to affect the
Company and its customers. Contributing factors, including geopolitical instability and rising inflation, have led to supply chain
challenges, increased interest rates, and a heightened cost of living.
To support our employees, we have adopted hybrid working to reduce commuting costs and enhance work-life balance. A salary
review was also conducted during the year to ensure compensation remains competitive. Additionally, all employees benefit from
discounts at major grocery and retail outlets, addressing everyday cost-of-living pressures. Employee sentiment is monitored
through regular one-to-one feedback sessions and an anonymous weekly employee survey.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES (continued)
Economic conditions and interest rates
We remain acutely aware of the pressure economic volatility places on our customers. We continue to offer competitive pricing
and have focused on driving internal efficiencies, including renegotiating supplier contracts and optimising business processes.
We proactively support customers through financial uncertainty, providing flexible and responsive IT infrastructure solutions.
Credit risk is managed through regular reviews of public credit ratings and debtor ageing. Following the secondary buyout in
November 2022, Group debt decreased from £46.4 million to £28.0 million, reducing the impact of rising interest rates.
Supplier and Contract Risk
The Company resells a variety of third-party products and remains particularly reliant on major network providers such as BT,
TalkTalk, and Vodafone. Although substitution is often possible, the Company mitigates supplier risk through rigorous contract
management, strategic vendor relationships, and a multi-vendor procurement strategy.
Business continuity planning includes technical alternatives for critical services. By remaining vendor-agnostic, the Company
limits exposure to supplier dependency and minimises disruption.
Technological changes
Rapid innovation within the digital services market presents both opportunity and risk. The Company may be exposed if it fails to
adapt to emerging technologies or shifting customer expectations.
We mitigate this risk through ongoing R&D, competitor benchmarking, and early customer engagement. We also collaborate
closely with technology partners to stay ahead of market trends. Strategic initiatives include development of 5G solutions,
expansion of SD-WAN capabilities, and investment in our Multi-cloud Management Portal.
Talent Attraction and Retention
As a direct sales and service-led organisation, our success depends on the recruitment and retention of high-calibre individuals.
The Company has implemented structured succession planning and talent development programmes, as well as reviewing and
enhancing employee benefits to remain competitive. Engagement initiatives include quarterly staff roadshows, CEO Breakfast
meetings, and third-party-supported programmes on leadership, culture, and operational excellence. Recruitment metrics and
staff turnover are regularly reviewed at Board level.
Regulatory and Compliance Risk
The Company is subject to ongoing regulatory changes, particularly around mobile and data service pricing. Regulatory changes
at the retail level may compress margins if wholesale prices do not adjust accordingly.
Management proactively monitors potential regulatory developments and adjusts pricing models where necessary. The
Company also engages in lobbying efforts, where applicable, and scenario planning to anticipate and manage regulatory impact.
IT Infrastructure and Operational Risk
The Company relies on robust IT infrastructure to deliver customer services and maintain business operations. Risks are
mitigated by employing skilled IT personnel, internal system audits, and external certifications including ISO 9001 - Quality
Management, ISO 27001 - Information Security Management, and {SO 27017 - Cloud Security; our Newark Data Centre is also
PCI compliant.

FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT, OBJECTIVES AND POLICIES
On 10 November 2022, funds managed by Graphite Capital completed a Secondary Buy Out (SBO) of the Digital Space group of
companies from Lyceum Capital Partners LLP (LCP).
Following the SBO, the Group has a £28 million term loan (repayable in November 2028) and has put a £3 million Revolving Credit
Facility (RCF) in place (repayable in March 2028). The Group has also issued £8,300,000 10% A loan notes and £21,216,000 B loan
notes at an issue price of £1.00 per note. On 14 August 2024, a further £1,300,000 A loan notes were issued at £1.00 per note.
On 5 December 2024, a further £2,000,000 A loan notes were issued at £1.00 per note. The loan notes are redeemable in
November 2032.
The Group operates within a framework of debt covenants, which are monitored and reported on a monthly or quarterly basis,
with forward-looking projections covering at least the next 12 months.
Credit risk is effectively managed through a combination of robust credit checks for new customers, advance invoicing and
payment for fixed-recurring revenue streams, and rigorous credit control procedures.
Liquidity risk is mitigated through proactive cash flow management measures, including advance billing for recurring services, tight
control of working capital, and the maintenance of significant cash headroom above operational requirements.
Foreign exchange risk is limited, with exposure primarily arising from Euro and US Dollar-denominated contracts and bank
accounts. FX risk is managed by aligning customer and supplier contracts in the same currency wherever possible. Where this is
not feasible and exposure is material, forward contracts are used to mitigate the risk.

Leases .
The Company as a lessee
For any new contracts entered into on or after 1 January 2022, the Company considers whether a contract is, or contains a lease.
A lease is defined as ‘a contract, or part of a contract, that conveys the right to use an asset (the underlying asset) for a period of
time in exchange for consideration’. To apply this definition the Company assesses whether the contract meets three key
evaluations which are whether:
¢ the contract contains an identified asset, which is either explicitly identified in the contract or implicitly specified by being
identified at the time the asset is made available to the Company;
¢ the Company has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from use of the identified asset throughout the
period of use, considering its rights within the defined scope of the contract the Company has the right to direct the use of the
identified asset throughout the period of use;
* the Company assess whether it has the right to direct ‘how and for what purpose’ the asset is used throughout the period of
use.
Measurement and recognition of leases as a lessee
At lease commencement date, the Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability on the Statement of Financial
Position. The right-of-use asset is measured at cost, which is made up of the initial measurement of the lease liability, any initial
direct costs incurred by the Company, an estimate of any costs to dismantle and remove the asset at the end of the lease, and
any lease payments made in advance of the lease commencement date (net of any incentives received). .
The Company depreciates the right-of-use assets on a straight-line basis from the lease commencement date to the earlier of
the end of the useful life of the right-of-use asset or the end of the lease term. The Company also assesses the right-of-use asset
for impairment when such indicators exist.
At the commencement date, the Company measures the lease liability at the present value of the lease payments unpaid at that
date, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease if that rate is readily available or the Company’s incremental
borrowing rate.
Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability are made up of fixed payments (including in substance fixed),
variable payments based on an index or rate, amounts expected to be payable under a residual value guarantee and payments
arising from options reasonably certain to be exercised.
Subsequent to initial measurement, the liability will be reduced for payments made and increased for interest. It is remeasured
to reflect any reassessment or modification, or if there are changes in in-substance fixed payments.

includes past events, current conditions and reasonable forecasts in respect of the collectability of future cash flows of the
instrument.

4. CRITICAL ACCOUNTING JUDGEMENTS AND KEY SOURCES OF ESTIMATION UNCERTAINTY
The preparation of Financial Statements in conformity with adopted FRS 101 requires management to make judgements,
estimates and assumptions that affect the application of policies and reported annual amounts of assets and liabilities, income
and expenses. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an
ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised if the revision
affects only that period, or in the period of the revision and future periods if the revision affects both current and future periods.
The major areas of judgement within the Financial Statements are as follows:
Alternative Performance Measures (APMs)
Management use Alternative Performance Measures (APMs) to provide a clearer understanding of the Company’s operational
performance. These APMs involve adjustments to IFRS-reported figures, and the Directors exercise significant judgement in
determining which adjustments are appropriate. The purpose of these adjustments is to remove items that may distort the
Company’s performance trends, thereby giving a more consistent basis for comparison.
Management use adjusted EBITDA as an Alternative Performance Measure. This metric is critical to performance analysis and
decision-making by the Board. However since APMs are not defined by IFRS, they are not directly comparable to similar metrics
used by other companies. They are meant to supplement, not replace, IFRS measurements and provide management and
stakeholders with additional insights.
Revenue (note 5)
Revenue is recognised in accordance with the substance of the Company’s contractual arrangements and when the significant
risks and rewards of ownership have been transferred to the customer. In determining how and when to recognise revenue,
management makes key judgements for the timing of revenue recognition and the assessment of performance obligations ie
judgement is applied identifying whether each element is distinct and should be recognised separately or as part of a combined
obligation.

Exceptional items (note 6)
Exceptional items are those which, by their size and nature, are unusual or non-recurring and require separate disclosure in the
Financial Statements. Identifying these items involves a significant level of judgement by management, as they are presented on
the face of the Statement of Comprehensive Income to enhance the reader’s understanding of the Company's underlying
performance.
Typical transactions categorised as exceptional include acquisition-related costs or significant restructuring activities. By
separating these items, the Company aims to present its operational performance without the distortion of large, one-off events.
Deferred tax assets (note 21)
A deferred tax asset represents a reduction in future tax payments that a company can apply based on deductible temporary
differences, tax losses, or credits that it can carry forward to future periods. These assets are recorded when it is probable that
the Company will generate sufficient future taxable profits to utilise the tax benefits. However, the recognition of deferred tax
assets requires management to make critical judgements about the Company’s future profitability and the timing of taxable
income. In this case, the Company has decided not to recognize a deferred tax asset due to uncertainty regarding when it will
achieve taxable profits.
Extension options for leases (note 24)
When the entity has the option to extend a lease, management uses its judgement to determine whether or not an option
would be reasonably certain to be exercised. Management considers all facts and circumstances including their past practice and
any cost that will be incurred to change the asset if an option to extend is not taken, to help them determine the lease term.
The incremental borrowing rate used to discount lease liabilities is 6% for leased property and 9% for leased vehicles.
Useful economic lives of intangible and tangible assets (notes 14 and 15)
The useful lives of both intangible and tangible assets determine how much of the asset's value is amortised or depreciated each
year. These estimates are reviewed at the end of each reporting period to ensure they reflect the actual consumption of the
asset's economic benefits.
For intangible assets such as patents or trademarks, and tangible assets like machinery or buildings, revisions in the useful life
can impact the expense recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income and the asset's carrying value in the Statement
of Financial Position. Significant changes in technology, market conditions, or operational use could lead to adjustments in these
estimates.
In summary, management's use of judgement and estimation uncertainty is inherent to financial reporting, with critical areas
involving performance metrics, exceptional transactions, lease term decisions, and asset valuations. Regular reviews and
updates help mitigate these uncertainties and provide a more accurate financial representation of the Company.
