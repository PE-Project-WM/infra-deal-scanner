INTRODUCTION . .
The Directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES
The principal activities of the Company are the construction and upgrade of the energy centres and
the energy networks, as well as its operation and maintenance. This includes initial connection
services and the supply of heating, cooling and electricity to customers.
BUSINESS REVIEW
The year ended 31 December 2024 was the first full year of operation under new ownership
following the acquisition of the UK district energy business from Equans Holding UK Limited by
Bring Energy Group Limited. The Company is one of the subsidiaries within this transaction and
operates district energy concessions.
- Turnover for the year was £16.6 million (2023: £18.7 million)
- Profit before tax was £1.7 million (2023: £4.7 million)
During the year, the Company completed a carve-out from its former parent holding company,
establishing standalone systems, processes and controls appropriate for an independent operating
entity. A revised recharge arrangement was also implemented with Bring Energy Group Limited,
whereby central and shared service costs are borne at holding company level rather than within the
Company.
FINANCIAL KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS
The Directors use a combination of financial and non-financial Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
to monitor the Company's performance, financial position and operational effectiveness. The KPls
selected are relevant to understanding the development, performance and position of the
Company and are either derived directly from, or are readily reconcilable to, the Company's
financial statements and underlying operational records.
Use of KPIs
The KPis disclosed below are those used by the Directors to monitor the Company's performance
and financial position during the year. While management also uses additional internal metrics,
including cash-based and concession-level measures, these are derived from the same underlying
financial and operational information and are not presented as principal KPIs in this report.
The KPls relevant to the Company's performance and prospects are as follows:
Revenue .
Revenue is used by management to monitor activity levels, customer demand and the scale of
operations across the Company's concessions. Revenue for the year ended 31 December 2024
was £16.6 million (2023: £18.7 million), a decrease of £2.1 million (11%). The decrease in turnover
compared to the prior year is primarily attributable to lower energy prices.
Profit before tax
Profit before tax for the year ended 31 December 2024 was £1.7 million (2023: £4.7 million), a
* decrease of £3.0 million (64%). .

Profit before tax decreased primarily due to lower gross profit resulting from reduced energy prices

electricity to satisfy customer demand and reduced energy margins. Administrative expenses also
increased as a result of higher management fees charged by Group companies.
EBITDA
EBITDA represents operating profit/(loss) before depreciation, amortisation and impairment of
non-current assets. It is used by the Directors to assess operating performance before non-cash
charges relating to the Company's net asset base and to support comparability between periods.
‘The Company's EBITDA for the year was £2,403,000 (2023: £5,218,000), a decrease of
£2,815,000 (54%). The decrease was principally driven by lower gross profit arising from reduced
energy prices and lower CHP availability during the year. As a result, a greater proportion of
customer electricity demand was supplied through imported electricity purchases rather than
higher-margin CHP generation, reducing overall energy margins. EBITDA was also impacted by
management fees of £1,586,000 (2023: £1,228,000) charged under the Group recharge
arrangements.
Cash at bank and in hand
Cash at bank and in hand represents cash balances held by the Company at the reporting date, as
disclosed on the Company's statement of financial position. This KPI is monitored to ensure the
Company represents sufficient liquidity to meet its obligations as they fall due.
Cash at bank and in hand at 31 December 2024 was £958,000 (2023: £327,000) an increase of
£631,000 (193%). The increase in cash reflects the change in cash management across the wider
Bring Energy Group. Prior to the group's acquisition on 31 December 2023, cash was managed as
part of a wider cash pooling arrangement with previous owners, which is why the balance was _ kept
at a minimal level as at 31 December 2023.
Non-financial KPIs
Sources of Energy (MWh)
Sources of energy represent the total volume of energy generated or procured by the Company's
concessions during the period, measured in megawatt hours (MWh). This includes energy
generated from on-site assets such as CHP plant, as well as energy imported from third-party
suppliers.
Sources of energy for the year ended 31 December 2024 was 141,051 MWh (2023: 144,830
MWh).
Management uses this KPI to monitor energy generation and procurement levels, assess
Operational performance of generation assets, and understand the drivers of revenue and energy
costs reported in the Company's income statement.
Uses of Energy (MWh)
Uses of energy represent the total volume of energy supplied to customers and consumed across
the Company's concessions during the period, measured in megawatt hours (MWh). This reflects
customer demand and network utilisation.
Uses of energy for the year ended 31 December 2024 was 102,462 MWh (2023: 105,749 MWh).

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The identification, assessment, pursuit and management of opportunities and the associated risks
is an integral part of the management and processes of the Company. The Company has rigorous
processes in place for managing the exposure within a specified opportunities and risk

External and Regulatory Risks
The Company operates in a sector that is transitioning from a historically lightly regulated
environment to a formal regulatory regime. The UK Government has introduced legislation to
regulate heat networks, with Ofgem appointed as the statutory regulator.
The regulatory framework is being implemented on a phased basis and is still developing. Initial
stages focus on consumer protection and dispute resolution, with further requirements expected to
be introduced over time, including operator registration or authorisation, service standards, pricing
transparency and reporting obligations. As the detailed scope, timing and practical application of
these requirements continue to evolve, there remains a level of uncertainty for heat network
operators.

The introduction of regulation may result in additional compliance, operational and reporting costs

for the Company and could place constraints on pricing structures, customer engagement

processes and service delivery arrangements. Future regulatory developments may also affect the
commercial assumptions underpinning existing assets or the design of new projects.

Health, Safety and Environmental Risks
The health and safety of employees, contractors and the public is a key priority for the Company.

FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS
The Company monitors its exposure to risk on an ongoing basis. The Company's activities do not
expose it to any material price risk, interest rate cash flow risk or foreign exchange risk. Owing to
the nature of the Company's business and the assets and liabilities contained within the statement
of financial position, the financial risks the Directors consider relevant to the Company are credit
tisk and liquidity risk. -
Credit risk
Credit risk arises on the Company's principal financial assets, which are cash at bank and in hand,
trade and other debtors and amounts owed by group undertakings. The credit risk associated with
cash is limited, as the Company uses financial institutions with a high credit rating for banking
requirements. All customers are credit checked prior to any sales and only customers with an
appropriate credit rating are offered credit terms.
The Company has a concentrated customer base and therefore monitors counterparty credit risk
closely. The majority of the Company's exposure is to public sector and higher education
customers, which management considers to present a low risk of default due to their financial
strength and payment history. The credit risk on amounts owed by group undertakings is not
considered to be significant, given that all companies within the Bring Energy group benefit from
access to both short-term liquidity and longer-term financing support from the Bring Energy group.
Liquidity risk
The Company is exposed to liquidity risk on its financial liabilities, including trade and other
creditors, and amounts owed to group undertakings. In order to maintain liquidity to ensure
sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations and future developments, the Company
benefits from a Group cash flow management which allows cash to be managed intragroup where
needed.

GOING CONCERN
At the time of approving the financial statements, the Directors had a reasonable expectation that .
the Company has sufficient resources to continue in operational existence for the period to 31
December 2027. -
The Company has also received an uncapped letter of support from Bring Energy Group Limited
(formerly Burnie Bidco Limited), confirming that it will provide financial support as needed to enable
the Company to maintain adequate financial resources to meet its liabilities as they fall due and to
continue to trade, to the extent that funding is not otherwise available. In addition, it is expected
that other subsidiaries within the Bring Energy Group may also provide support to group
companies, either directly or indirectly through Bring Energy Group Limited, for a period to 31
December 2027.
The Directors manage the cash flow forecasting at a Group level and have reviewed the latest
approved detailed monthly cash flow forecasts through to 31 December 2027, taking into account
the Group's current operating performance, contractual arrangements, and committed capital
expenditure. The forecasts incorporate assumptions relating to customer demand, energy prices,
operating and maintenance costs, grant funding, and working capital movements, and are
supported by the Group's established cash management and forecasting processes.
The base-case forecast indicates sufficient liquidity headroom throughout the assessment period.
The Directors have also considered severe but plausible downside scenarios that would
significantly reduce liquidity headroom, and under these scenarios, the Group still forecasts to
maintain positive liquidity throughout the assessment period. On this basis, the Directors confirm
that Bring Energy Group Limited has the capacity to provide financial support to the Company in a
manner it considers appropriate.
Accordingly, the Directors consider it appropriate to prepare the financial statements using the
going concern basis of accounting.

2.5 Revenue
For all contracts with customers, the Company recognises revenue when performance
obligations have been satisfied. For most of the Company’s contracts, revenue is recognised
over time as the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits provided by
the Company.
IFRS 15 provides a five step-model which the Company has applied to all sales contracts
with customers to identify the revenue which can be recognised. The model is applied at
contract inception and on the assumption that the contract will operate as defined in the
contract and that the contract will not be cancelled, renewed or modified. After contract
inception, a change in the scope or price (or both) of a contract that is approved by the
parties to the contract is a contract modification.
Step 1 - Identify the contract with the customer
First, the Company determines if a contract exists and whether it is in scope of IFRS 15. The
: arrangement must create enforceable rights and obligations. Typically, this will be a signed

contract with the customer. The Company and customer must be committed to perform their
respective obligations, each party’s rights regarding the goods or services to be transferred
should be identifiable, the payment terms for the goods or services to be transferred should
be identifiable, the arrangement must have commercial substance and it must be probable
that the Company will collect the consideration to which it will be entitled in exchange for the
goods or services that will be transferred to the customer. This assessment is completed on
a case by case basis in line with IFRS 15.
Step 2 - Identify the performance obligations in the contract
At contract inception, the Company assesses the goods or services promised in a_ contract
with a customer. It identifies the performance obligations and contractual promises to
transfer distinct goods or services to a customer. For contracts with several components,
judgment is necessary to determine the performance obligations by considering whether
those promised goods or services are:
a) agood or service (or bundle of goods or services) that is distinct; or
b) a series of distinct goods or services that are substantially the same and that have the

same pattern of transfer to the customer.
Based on the contractual arrangements entered into by the Company with its customers,
management has identified the following performance obligations that are considered
distinct:
a) construction or upgrade services - this relates to services provided to the grantor for

the construction or upgrade of the energy centres and the energy networks.
b) connection services - this relates to services provided to the customers for the initial

connection to the energy networks to enable supply of heating, cooling and energy.
