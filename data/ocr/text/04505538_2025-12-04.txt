The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Business review
The principal activity of the company is the provision of telecommunications network services. Epsilon Global
Communications Pte. Ltd owns 100% of the shares of Epsilon Telecommunications Limited. and is the
immediate parent of Epsilon Telecommunications Limited.
. The loss for the year, after taxation, is £6,446,859 (2023: £7,521,879 loss). Epsiton’s historic customer base _ .
. has been predominantly the wholesale market, which we continue to serve and target as a key sector for the
business. KT Corp. is the leading telecommunications provider in South Korea and is looking to grow its
business internationally in support of Korean businesses expanding around the world as well as the
opportunity to provide services for companies looking to do business in Korea. By being part of KT, Epsilon
is able to leverage KT's network, services, and enterprise customers and serve them outside of Korea. KT
will also help Epsiton expand its portfolio of services with access to high-bandwidth subsea cable systems,
hyper-scale collocation and carrier hotel capabilities in Korea.
The Company reports under FRS 101 — ‘Reduced Disclosure Framework’ and has taken advantage of the
disclosure exemptions allowed under this standard, as set out in Note 2 to the financial statements.

Future developments
Epsilon has established a clear strategy for its three business areas, data, colocation and voice. The data
interconnection services are core to our portfolio, and we will leverage our Network as a Service platform
Infinity by facilitating the connection of data centres, cloud service providers and Internet Exchanges on a
Global basis. This removes the need for businesses to manage multiple vendor relationships and to procure
and manage network services in real time. Colocation and Voice remain important parts of the portfolio, and
we will continue to develop these areas to capture market opportunities. Additionally, both services are
complementary to our data services.
Going Concern
The directors continue to pay exceptionally close attention to cashflow projections and have reviewed a range
of scenarios. The assumptions modelled are based on the current business outlook and regulations, along
with our proposed responses over the period to 30 June 2026.

(m) Revenue recognition
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the Company

: Revenue represents the fair value of provision of contracted telephony services provided to its customers in ;

Timing of revenue recognition
Revenue is recognized at an amount that reflects the consideration to which the Company is expected to be
entitled in exchange for transferring services to a customer. For each contract with a customer, the Company:
identifies the contract with a customer; identifies the performance obligations in the contract; determines the
transaction price which takes into account estimates of variable consideration and the time value of money;
allocates the transaction price to the separate performance obligations on the basis of the relative stand-alone
selling price of each distinct service to be delivered; and recognizes revenue when or as each performance
obligation is satisfied in a manner that depicts the transfer to the customer or the services promised. Payment
for services is not due from the customer until the required services are completed.
Under IFRS 15, performance obligation from subscription of telecommunication network services are
recognized over the subscription period, as the performance obligation have been satisfied when the service
is rendered to customer. Performance obligation for interface set-up are recognized at a point in time, as the
performance obligation have been satisfied when the set-up completed.

probable.
