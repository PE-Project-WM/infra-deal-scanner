DIRECTORS' REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
The Directors present their report and the financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Introduction
The principal activity of Veolia Water Infrastructure Services Limited ("the Company") was to design, project
manage, construct and install utility assets related primarily to the water, gas and electric non-regulated markets.
However, the Company ceased entering into new contracts in 2014 which has meant that the principal activity of
the Company has been to manage any potential post contract defect obligations.
The Company is registered and domiciled in the United Kingdom.
The Company is part of the Veolia Group, ("the Group") which is defined as all companies under the control of
the ultimate parent company, Veolia Environnement S.A., headquartered in Paris. The Company is also a
member of the "UK&I group", a division of the Group, based in the UK and Ireland and involved in the provision
of waste, water and energy services, and directly or indirectly under the ownership of Veolia UK Limited ("VUK").
Business review
All of the Company's contracts have achieved financial closure. However, the Company has potential post
contract defects obligations and therefore the Directors continue to assess its portfolio of contracts for such risk
and provide accordingly within the financial statements.
Operating profit in the year was £83k (2024: loss £7k). Furthermore, during the year, a dividend of £900k (2024:
£nil) was paid to the Company's parent undertaking, Veolia Water Capital Delivery Limited.
Results and dividends
The profit for the year, after taxation, amounted to £98k (2024: £37k).
Dividends paid in the year under review amounted to £900k (2024: Enil).

Going concern ;

The Company's Balance Sheet shows net assets of £187k (2024: £989k), and at year end reported net current

assets of £187k (2024: £989k). The Company participates in the Group-centralised treasury arrangements and

so shares banking and intercompany loan arrangements with the Group, VUK and other UK-based Group fellow

subsidiaries. The Company is managed as part of the UK&I group and budgets and forecasts are prepared at

that level, of which the UK is the major component. The UK group's forecasts and budgets identify that the UK

group is expected to remain cash generative and therefore be able to meet its liabilities as they fall due for the

period until 31 December 2027. A key assumption in the UK group’s forecasts is the continuing availability of

funds that are swept into the Group Treasury cash pooling arrangements and the intercompany loans provided

by the Group to VUK and other UK-based Group fellow subsidiaries.

VUK has received a letter of financial support from the UK&l group's ultimate parent company, Veolia

Environnement S.A., which confirms that the Group, if required, will provide financial support to VUK for the

period until 31 December 2027. VUK has in turn provided a letter of support to the Company to confirm that VUK

will, if required, provide financial support to the Company for the period to 31 December 2027.

The Directors of VUK have considered the information regarding the Group's ability to provide support to VUK.
. This includes reviewing the most recent publicly available financial information and making enquiries of the

Group to confirm the position through to the date of approval of these financial statements. As a result, the

Directors of the Company have concluded that the Group has sufficient liquidity to provide any required support

and to withstand reasonable stress testing.

Further to the above assessment, the Directors of the Company have concluded that, if required, the Group will

be able to provide financial support to VUK, who in turn will be able to provide financial support to the Company,

for the period until 31 December 2027. Accordingly, the Directors are satisfied that they have a reasonable basis

upon which to conclude that the Company is able to meet its liabilities as they fall due in the foreseeable future

and that it remains appropriate to prepare the financial statements on a going concern basis.

Future developments

Following the Company's decision not to pursue new opportunities and with all remaining project work complete,

management will continue to focus on the wind down of its portfolio of contracts.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Directors consider the principal risks and uncertainties to which the Company is exposed are intrinsic to the

business it operates and its ability to provide the relevant contracted services.

The following highlights some of the Company's principal risks, but is not intended to be an extensive analysis of

all risks affecting the business. Some risks may be unknown to the Company and other risks, currently regarded

as immaterial, could turn out to be material. All of them have a potential to impact the Company's business,

revenue, profits, assets, liquidity and capital resources. The Company manages the majority of these risks by

utilising the resources and processes developed and operated within the UK&! group as a whole.

Latent defect risk

Given the Company has achieved project completion across its portfolio of contracts, the Company recognises

that post contract latent defects and any associated changes to risk profiles across its portfolio of contracts may

result in financial obligations that may crystallise at some future date. The Company regularly reviews its risk to

ensure the financial statements give a robust view of the financial performance of its contracts.

Business continuity

The UK&I group is accredited with ISO 22301 (Business Continuity Management) and is benefitting from the

programme of enhancements to the business continuity management system, which commenced in 2024 and

are now fully embedded. These enhancements include sharing of site infrastructure within the UK&lI group, the

use of external suppliers who specialise in disaster recovery scenarios and leveraging other Group relationships.

Business continuity (continued)
IT continuity planning is closely aligned with the UK&l business continuity management system and forms a key
component of its overall resilience strategy. The UK&! group's continuity plans are linked to recovery point and
recovery time objectives and are regularly tested and subject to programmes for continuous service
improvement. These plans cover critical IT systems and infrastructure, ensuring that essential services can be
restored within defined timeframes in the event of a disruption. |
The UK&l group recognises the critical importance of managing cyber risks effectively. Aligned with the NIST
Cybersecurity Framework's six functions — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover, and now, Govern — the
UK&l group conducts regular risk assessments across its digital estate. These assessments evaluate the
robustness of the UK&! group's cybersecurity controls, encompassing technical, operational, and managerial
aspects, prioritising confidentiality, integrity and availability in the face of potential cyber threats.
The UK&l risk management approach considers a range of key areas, including:
e Vulnerability and patch management;
e Identity and access management;
« Network security (including perimeter defences and segmentation);
« Security monitoring, threat detection, and incident response;
« Data security and protection (including backup and recovery);
« Third-party risk management and supply chain security; and
« Cybersecurity awareness training and education for staff.
Key findings from these assessments inform the UK&I cybersecurity strategy and continuous improvement plan,
ensuring that appropriate and timely corrective actions are prioritised. This proactive approach strengthens the
UK&l group's overall cybersecurity posture and complements the Group's broader risk management framework.
Financial risks
It is the Company's objective to manage its financial risks so as to minimise the adverse effects of fluctuations in
the financial markets on its profits and cash flows. The Company utilises the resources of the UK&l group to
implement risk controls and loss mitigation plans to manage exposure to these risks. Policies for managing
these risks are summarised below:
¢ Interest risk
The Company lends funds via VUK, at floating rates of interest, priced according to a GBP index based on
Sterling Overnight Interbank Average Rates ("SONIA"). Changes in the Bank of England base rate may
therefore impact interest charges and income.
¢ = Liquidity risk
The Company, along with other entities within the UK&l group, is party to cash pooling arrangements with
VUK, whereby each member deposits cash excesses and may borrow money in the form of short-term
advances. Amounts are swept on a daily basis and held on overnight deposit with the Group. At the year end
VUK was in a net surplus position. VUK has an additional overdraft facility with the Group which remains
unutilised. Cash forecasts are regularly prepared and submitted to the Group for review to ensure that the
liquidity profile of the UK&I group is actively monitored.
The Group has confirmed that the UK&! group will continue to have access to these balances as required for
their activities. As outlined under Going Concern (page 1), the Directors of the Company have concluded
that, if required, the Group would be able to provide financial support to VUK, which in turn would be able to
provide financial support to the Company for the periods stipulated.

The Company classifies its financial liabilities into one of the categories discussed below, depending

Financial liabilities at amortised cost
Financial liabilities at amortised cost including bank borrowings are initially recognised at fair value
net of any transaction costs directly attributable to the issue of the instrument. Such interest bearing
liabilities are subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method,
which ensures that any interest expense over the period to repayment is at a constant rate on the

Derecognition of financial liabilities

different terms, or the terms of an existing liability are substantially modified, such an exchange or
modification is treated as a derecognition of the original liability, such that the difference in the
respective carrying amounts together with any costs or fees incurred are recognised in profit or loss.
