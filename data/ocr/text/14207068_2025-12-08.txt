For the Year Ended 31 March 2025

The Directors present their report together with the audited financial statements for the year ended 31 March
2025.
Business and Principal activity
The Company was incorporated on 30 June 2022 as a wholly owned subsidiary of Vodafone Global Enterprise
Ltd. There was an initial share capital investment of 100 x £1 ordinary shares. On 30 May 2023 a further €10
million cash was invested in return for 10 million series A preference shares. On 22 June 2023 an asset transfer
to the value of €6Qmillion was made from Vodafone Global Enterprise Ltd to the Company in return for 60
million ordinary shares. On 2 August 2023 there was a further contribution by Vodafone Global Enterprise Ltd of
€10million for 10 million Series A preference shares. On 4 August 2023 the Company became a Joint Venture
upon further contribution by Sumitomo Corporation of €20 million for 20 million Series A preference shares.
Both Vodafone Global Enterprise Limited and Sumitomo Corporation carry equal voting rights in the Company.
The Company develops software services via its Platform as a Service (PaaS), which acts as middleware for
connected Things. The platform enables secure, fast, and more autonomous transactions between connected

: devices, systems, people and the greater services and payments ecosystems around them. These services
include, device digital identity, security and data protection, and data transactions and payment facilitation. The
Company also conducts research and development activities to further develop and expand these services. The
Company continues to incur set up and admin costs in financial year ended 31 March 2025 with the major
expenses relating to payroll costs, software development expenses. The Company is currently in the
development stage and such expenditure is going towards building a commercially viable platform.
On 14 March 2025, a wholly owned subsidiary of DABCO Limited, named "Pairpoint Asia Pacific," was
established. The subsidiary is registered at Shiba Daimon Center Building 10F, 1-10-11, Minato-Ku, Tokyo. It
has an authorized share capital of JPY 100,000,000, divided into 1,000,000 shares of JPY 100 each. As of 31
March 2025, the entity has issued 15,000 shares at JPY 100 each. The nature of business of the Company
encompasses sales, marketing, and application development of platforms and services related to the Economy
of Things. The Company also offers technical, operational, and related business support services.
The major assets of the Company are IP assets classified under Intangible assets. Following a detailed
assessment of the commercial performance, market outlook, and legal protection of the underlying assets,
management has determined that the expected period over which these assets will generate economic benefits
has extended. As a result, the estimated useful life has been revised from 10 years to 20 years, effective 1 April
2024, This revision constitutes a change in accounting estimate under !AS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes in
Accounting Estimates and Errors and has been applied prospectively from the effective date.
As part of our strategic realignment to accelerate commercial growth, the Board of Directors has approved a
restructuring program in September 2025 and leadership transition at the executive level. As part of this, the
chief executive officer (Jorge Bento) stepped down from his role in September 2025. Ken Uesugi will take over
as the interim CEO until the Group is able to recruit a successor with a proven track record in commercial
leadership and business development.
Results and dividends .
The loss for the year, after taxation and minority interests, amounted to €13,694,340 (2024 - €10,425,175).
The Directors did not recommend the payment of a dividend in the current year or prior period.
Future developments
The Company is set to initiate pilot projects with three key customers: Madini, Continental (a German-based
fleet company), and Vodafone IOT, during Q3-Q4 of FY26. The management anticipates that these projects will
provide further clarity regarding market acceptance by the customers. Additionally, the patents are valid for 20
years, effectively mitigating the risk of market competition.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The financial risks the Directors consider relevant to the Company include currency risk, credit risk and liquidity
risk. The Company follows the board approved policies to manage these principal financial risks and follows a
framework of policies and guidelines authorised and reviewed annually by the DABCO Limited's Board.
The entity has no outstanding customer balance as of 31 March 2025. However, management frequently
reviews risks related to credit, equity, and currency, taking necessary actions during management and Board
meetings.
The entity's parent companies, Vodafone Global Enterprise Limited and Sumitomo, have sales channels
currently working in the Internet of Things market. The entity is leveraging these channels to create an
Enterprise of Things. Management believes that these sales channels will help the entity acquire new
customers. Sumitomo has assets in Logistics and Mobility, which are the two areas the entity is targeting.
Therefore, management believes there is minimal market risk. The Company is set to initiate pilot projects with
three key customers: Madini, Continental (a German-based fleet company), and Vodafone IOT, during Q3-Q4

acceptance by the customers. Additionally, the patents are valid for 20 years, effectively mitigating the risk of
market competition.
The Directors have considered all the above risks as part of the Company's risk and going concern
assessment. In reaching its conclusion, the Directors also considered the findings of the work performed by the
Company to support the statement on the long-term viability of the Company. The Directors at the date of
signing the accounts have concluded that the Company is continuously monitoring and mitigating any risks
associated with the current geopolitical climate and have not identified any material consequential impact on the.
Company. .

Financial position and liquidity
The Directors consider that the Company has sufficient funding to meet its financial needs as they fall due.
Accordingly, the Directors have prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis.
The Directors have reviewed the financial position of the Company, including the arrangements with the
shareholders for further funding requirements. On the basis of their assessment of the Company's financial
position, the factors likely to affect its future development and performance, the Company's Directors have no
reason to believe that a material uncertainty exists that may cast doubt about the ability of the Company to
continue as a going concern. Accordingly, they expect that the Company will be able to continue in operational!
existence for the foreseeable future and hence continue to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in
preparing the annual financial statements.
Financial Risk Management
The Company is exposed to various financial risks arising from its operations and financial instruments. These
risks include market risk, credit risk, and liquidity risk. The Company's risk management framework is designed
to identify, assess, and mitigate these risks in a structured and proactive manner.
e Market Risk
The Company stays informed about the market trends and also diversified into different geographies such
: as Japan, Portugal and UK. This balances the risk of localized economic status. The Company has also
made efforts to further mitigate market risk by registering trademarks and patents to protect its intellectual
property in each jurisdiction that it intends to operate in.
« Credit Risk
Credit risk arises from the possibility that counterparties may default on their contractual obligations. The
Company manages credit risk by conducting credit evaluations of customers and counterparties, setting
credit limits, and maintaining provisions for expected credit losses in accordance with applicable accounting
standards. .
e Liquidity Risk
Dabco received cash injection of €30m in July 2025, therefore there is sufficient liquidity during the going
concern period. The Company maintains adequate liquidity through cash flow forecasting, access to credit
lines, and investment in liquid assets to ensure sufficient funds are available.
¢ Risk Management Policies
The Company's financial risk management policies are approved by the Board of Directors and reviewed
periodically. These policies establish clear guidelines for risk tolerance, control mechanisms, and reporting
procedures.
e Capital Management :
The Company manages its capital structure to ensure financial stability and support future growth. This
includes monitoring debt-to-equity ratios, optimizing the cost of capital, and maintaining compliance with
covenants under financing arrangements.

Research and development
During the financial year, the Company undertook qualifying Research and Development (R&D) activities aimed
at advancing technological capabilities within its core operations. In alignment with HMRC guidelines, eligible
expenditures—including staff costs, software licenses, and subcontracted R&D—were identified and assessed.
Based on the nature and scope of the projects, the Company claimed R&D tax relief under the RDEC Scheme.
The claim resulted in a reduction of Corporation Tax liability and/or payable credit, thereby improving the
Company's cash flow and supporting reinvestment into future innovation.
All documentation, including project descriptions, cost breakdowns, and technical justifications, has been
maintained to support the claim and ensure compliance with HMRC audit requirements.
- Indemnification of directors

in accordance with the Company's articles of association and to the extent permitted by law, the Directors may
be granted an indemnity from the Company in respect of liabilities incurred as a result of their office. In respect
of those matters for which the Directors may not be indemnified, DABCO Limited maintained a directors and

. officers’ liability insurance policy throughout the financial year. This policy is renewed annually. Neither the
Company's indemnity nor the insurance provides cover in the event that the Director is proven to have acted
dishonestly or fraudulently.
Going concern
The Directors are satisfied that, at the time of approving the financial statements, it is appropriate to adopt the
going concern basis in preparing the financial statements. The Group’s business activities, together with the
factors likely to affect its future development, performance and position are set out on page 2 of the financial
statements.
The Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Group has adequate resources to continue in operational
existence for the foreseeable future. The net current assets position as at 31 March 2025 was €2.21million, and
the net assets position was €75.89million. The net current assets position as at 31 October 2025 was
€20.29million, and the net assets position was €94.70million. .
The Directors have reviewed the financial performance and position of the Group and have assessed cash flow
forecast through to 31 December 2026, which includes the additional capital contribution of €30million in June
2025, €20million from Sumitomo Corporation and €10million from Vodafone Global Enterprise Limited.
The Directors are satisfied that, due to the headroom in the cash flow forecast and the ability of the Group to
control its capital expenditures, the business will continue to have sufficient cash available even in the event of
any reasonably possible downturn in Group's operations. The costs have to increase by approximately 20% in
order to deplete the cash to nil on 31 December 2026. The Directors view this as a remote scenario as
management can implement mitigating actions such as reductions to capital expenditures, employee costs by
freezing hiring, and non-employee costs such as sales and marketing expenses.
On the basis of their assessment, the Directors of DABCO Limited expect that the Group will be able to continue
in operational existence for the period until 31 December 2026 and hence continue to adopt the going concern
basis of accounting in preparing the annual financial statements.

Explanation as to what extent the audit was considered capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud
Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design
procedures in line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect irregularities, including fraud. The risk of
not detecting a material misstatement due to fraud is higher than the risk of not detecting one resulting from
error, as fraud may involve deliberate concealment by, for example, forgery or intentional misrepresentations, or
through collusion. The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud is
detailed below. However, the primary responsibility for the prevention and detection of fraud rests with both
those charged with governance of the entity and management.

2.6 Leased assets: the Group as lessee
Group and Company .
The Group accounts for a contract, or a portion of a contract, as a lease when it conveys the right to
use an asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration. Leases are those contracts that
satisfy the following criteria:
(a) there is an identified asset; ,
(b) the Group obtains substantially all the economic benefits from use of the asset; and
(c) the Group has the right to direct use of the asset.
The Group considers whether the supplier has substantive substitution rights. If the supplier does
have those rights, the contract is not identified as giving rise to a lease.
In determining whether the Group obtains substantially all the.economic benefits from use of the
asset, the Group considers only the economic benefits that arise from the use of the asset, not those
incidental to legal ownership or other potential benefits.
In determining whether the Group has the right to direct use of the asset, the Group considers
whether it directs how and for what purpose the asset is used throughout the period of use. If there
are no significant decisions to be made because they are pre-determined due to the nature of the
asset, the Group considers whether it was involved in the design of the asset in a way that
predetermines how and for what purpose the asset will be used throughout the period of use. If the
contract or portion of a contract does not satisfy these criteria, the Group applies other applicable
IFRSs rather than IFRS 16.
All leases are accounted for by recognising a right-of-use asset and a lease liability except for:
e leases of low value assets; and
e leases with a duration of 12 months or less.
Lease liabilities are measured at the present value of the contractual payments due to the lessor
over the lease term, with the discount rate determined by reference to the rate inherent in the lease
unless (as is typically the case) this is not readily determinable, in which case the Group’s
incremental borrowing rate on commencement of the lease is used. Variable lease payments are
only included in the measurement of the lease liability if they depend on an index or rate. In such
cases, the initial measurement of the lease liability assumes the variable element will remain
unchanged throughout the lease term. Other variable lease payments are expensed in the period to
which they relate.
On initial recognition, the carrying value of the lease liability also includes:
« amounts expected to be payable under any residual value guarantee;
«the exercise price of any purchase option granted in favour of the Group if it is reasonably
certain to assess that option; and
« any penalties payable for terminating the lease, if the term of the lease has been estimated on
the basis of termination option being exercised. .

2.11 Taxation :
Group and Company
The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the
Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income, except where a charge attributable to an item of .
income and expense recognised as other comprehensive income or to an item recognised directly in
equity is also recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date in the countries where the Company and the
Group operate and generate income.
Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not
reversed by the reporting date, except that:
. the recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits;
. any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
allowances have been met; and
. where they relate to timing differences in respect of interests in subsidiaries, associates,
branches and joint ventures and the Group can control the reversal of the timing differences
and such reversal is not considered probable in the foreseeable future.
Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of
business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair values
of assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences between the
fair values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax. Deferred tax is
determined using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the
reporting date.

2.13 Intangible assets
Group and Company
Intangible assets are initially recognised at cost. After recognition, under the cost model, intangible
assets are measured at cost less any accumulated amortisation and any accumulated impairment
losses.
Capitalised overheads are considered to have a useful life of 6 years and are amortised over this
period using the straight-line method from the date the asset is ready to use. Development
expenditure is not amortised until it is ready for use.
Amortisation is provided on the following bases:

Patents - Over 20 years

Development expenditure - Over 6 years

Computer software - Over 10 years
No amortisation is recorded on internally developed intangible assets (work in progress) until the
testing phase for additions is complete.
During the financial year ended 31 March 2025, Dabco management reviewed the estimated useful
life of the patents and computer software assets, which had previously been amortised over a period
of 10 years.
Following a’ detailed assessment of the commercial performance, market outlook, and legal
protection of the underlying assets, management has determined that the expected period over
which these assets will generate economic benefits has extended. As a result, the estimated useful

. life has been revised from 10 years to 20 years, effective 1 April 2024.
This revision constitutes a change in accounting estimate under IAS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes
in Accounting Estimates and Errors and has been applied prospectively from the effective date.
The impact of this change in estimate on the current period's financial statements is as follows:
. FY2025 (Actual)

Decrease in loss before tax €1,693,741
