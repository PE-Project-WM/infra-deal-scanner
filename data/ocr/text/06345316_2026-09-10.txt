Strategic report

For the financial year ended 31 December 2025

Introduction . .

The directors present the strategic report of Shared Access Ltd ("the Company") for the financial year ended 31
December 2025.

Shared Access Ltd is the principal UK operating entity within the Shared Access group of companies. The
Company designs, builds, owns and manages shared telecommunications infrastructure across the United Kingdom,
including outdoor tower sites, in-building Distributed Antenna Systems ("DAS") and private mobile networks.

- The Company works with mobile network operators, landlords, venue owners and enterprise customers to deliver
reliable, future-ready connectivity. Infrastructure assets ate typically owned by the Company and generate long-term,
recurring licence fee income under contracts with built-in escalation mechanisms. This is supplemented by services
(one off) revenue.

Shared Access Ltd forms part of a wider group that also operates in Ireland, Spain and the United States. While this
teport focuses on the Company as a standalone entity, certain references to group-level developments are included
where they provide context for the Company's operations and strategic direction.
As of 31 December 2025, the Company employed 54 people across its UK operations, spanning commercial,
technical, operational and support functions. The wider group employed 104 people.
Business review
Performance during the year

: The year ended 31 December 2025 was a period of significant growth for the Company. Turnover increased to

. £11,270,028 (from £10,318,337 in 2024), representing growth of 9.2% year-on-year.
This was driven by a combination of new infrastructure coming on-stream, contractual rent escalations on the
existing portfolio, and a material increase in services activity.
Gross profit increased to £7,193,815 (from £6,439,633 in 2024), with the gross profit margin increasing to 63.8%
(from 62.4% in 2024). .
EBITDA improved to (£404,553) from (£1,904,931) in 2024, demonstrating that the Company’s operations are
approaching breakeven as the revenue base continues to scale. The operating loss narrowed to £4,504,843 from.
£5,486,090, with the remaining gap driven primarily by non-cash depreciation and amortisation charges on the
. Company’s growing infrastructure asset base.

"The statutory loss for the year of £12,834,649 has reduced from £15,134,210 in 2024 and continues to reflect the
capital-intensive nature of the business model. The Company invests heavily in building infrastructure assets that are
depreciated and amortised over their useful lives, while the recurring revenue they generate grows over time.
Intercompany financing costs, which reflect the group's centralised funding of the Company's capital programme,
represent a significant component of the reported loss. The directors consider the underlying commercial trajectory
of the business to be positive. .

Infrastructure growth
The Company continued to expand its infrastructure portfolio during the year, with particularly strong momentum
in its DAS portfolio.
Tn the towers business, revenue remained stable. The UK towers portfolio is mature and well contracted, providing
a teliable base of recurring income. Services revenue remained consistent with the previous year, although timing of
operator-led upgrades continues to create vulnerability in the delivery schedule. The Company has introduced a
differentiated urban tower solution that it believes offers significant advantages over traditional street-level
infrastructure and is working to convert this into a larger-scale programme.

Business review (continued)

‘ 2025 was a pivotal ycar for the DAS business. The Company saw a step-change in both the volume and profile of
in-building connectivity projects, with several landmark venues in central London entering the contracted pipeline.
These projects, which will be constructed and begin generating recurring revenue in 2026, reflect the strengthening
market position of the Company's DAS platform and the increasing importance of in-building mobile coverage for
ptemium commercial teal estate, and is a direct result of the Company’s key account strategy which focuses on .
building deep, long-term relationships with targeted groups of multi-building owners and mobile operators.

The Company also continued to develop its Base Station Hotel model (to a total of 6 across all geographies),
whereby base station equipment is centralised into shared, secure facilities, serving up to 20 connected buildings via
dark fibre, which helps to reduce overall costs and improve operational efficiency.
Funding and capital structure
The growth of the Company's infrastructure portfolio is funded through the group's centralised financing
arrangements. In September 2024, the group secured a debt facility with a major institutional lender, and in
December 2025, a further drawdown was completed to support continued capital deployment.
As of 31 December 2025, the Company reported net liabilities of £84,380,070 (from £71,545,421 in 2024). This
position is driven by intercompany balances owed to fellow group entities, which fund the Company's infrastructure
investment. Repayment of these balances is at the discretion of the group, and the Company's ultimate parent has
provided a letter of support confirming its intention to continue funding the Company for at least pwelve months
from the date of signing these financial statements. The directors are satisfied that the Company has adequate
resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future.

Principal risks and uncertainties : .

The directors have identified the following principal risks and uncertainties facing the Company. The Board

monitors these risks on an ongoing basis and takes appropriate steps to manage and mitigate them. :

Capital and funding

© The Company's business model requires significant upfront capital investment to build infrastructure assets that
generate returns over long time horizons. The Company's growth is dependent on continued access to funding,
both through the group's existing debt facilities and through future rounds of capital raising. While the group
has an established relationship with its institutional lender and is actively pursuing additional sources of both
debt and equity, there is a risk that the pace of capital deployment could be constrained if additional funding is
not secured in line with the Company's pipeline. The directors are actively managing this through ongoing
engagement with potential capital partners.

Cost environment and geopolitical uncertainty :

e ‘The Company operates in an environment of rising costs, including increases to employer National Insurance
contributions, upward pressure on wages driven by the cost of living, and inflation in the cost of materials and
supplier services. While the Company's recurring revenue benefits from contractual escalation clauses linked to
inflation indices, cost pressures on the overhead base require careful management. The directors continue to
focus on operational efficiency and cost discipline to protect margins as the business scales.

Supply chain and capital timing

e The Company's DAS business model requires significant upfront capital investment, particularly in equipment,
to design and build in-building connectivity infrastructure, with recurring revenue commencing only once sites

; are operational. For larger projects, equipment often needs to be ordered well in advance, resulting in cash
outflows materially ahead of revenue generation. This creates inherent working capital risk, which the Company
manages through disciplined project governance and cash flow forecasting.

Customer concentration and operator timing

e A significant proportion of the Company's recurring revenue and services activity is derived from a small
number of mobile network operators. The timing of operator-led upgrade programmes directly affects the
Company's services revenue, and this has been compounded by consolidation activity within the UK mobile
market. Delays or changes to operator investment plans can create variability in revenue and resourcing
requirements. The Company mitigates this through the diversification of its revenue base across both tower and
DAS platforms, and by expanding its customer relationships to include venue owners, landlords and enterprise
clients.

Going concern

The financial statements of the Company have been prepared by the directors on a going concern basis. The

directors have considered the performance of the Company against expectations and projections, as well as the

capital and liquidity position of the Company. Management monitors the performance of the Company closely and

continues to take actions to ensure that the Company continues to generate positive EBITDA and to be profitable

in the future through an increase in activity and turnover together with managing expenses and overheads,

preparation of forecasts of expected performance and cashflows to assist in managing the capital and liquidity

position.

As at the reporting date, the Company is in a net liability position, a significant part of total liabilides represents

amounts owing to fellow group undertakings, and the demand for repayment of this is wholly within the control of

the Shared Access Group. As such the Board is satisfied that this does not present any liquidity issues for the

Company. The Company has received a Letter of Support from its ultimate parent company who have agreed to

provide continuing support for 12 months from the signing date of these financial statements. In the context of the

current facilities, the Company’s parent entity has confirmed that the loan will not be recalled for a petiod of at least

12 months from the approval of the financial statements. The Group currently have a financing facility amounting

to £400,000,000 from its current lender and significant portion of which is not yet drawn as of year end. Taking all

this into consideration the Directors are satisfied the Group has sufficient funding in the form of debt facilities and

: equity to meet the working capital requirements as they fall due. .

Accordingly, the continued support of the shareholders, the directors have a reasonable expectation that the

Company will have sufficient financial resources available to it to continue in operational existence for the
. foreseeable future. Therefore, the directors have concluded it is appropriate to prepare the financial statements on

the going concern basis. .
