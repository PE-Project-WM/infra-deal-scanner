Strategic Report

Introduction

Sinch UK Limited (the Company) is a mobile network service provider that accelerates mobile commerce by enabling
rich mobile interaction and communication between brands and consumers. The Company manages the transmission,
mobile billing, and financial settlement of Short Message Services ("SMS") mobile messaging, or text messaging as it
is commonly referred, as part of its broader suite of Messaging and Voice products. These services support the delivery
and monetization of communication solutions across both text and voice channels. The Company’s customers are
primarily in UK, Europe and Australia.

Business Review

During 2025, the Company made a profit after tax for the year of £10,289k (2024: £7,338k) The increase was primarily
attributable to reductions in operating costs.

Revenue for the year increased by £1,313k to £198,659k (2024: £197,346k), largely reflecting the impact of internal
sales, which comprised the majority of the growth,

Cost of sales decreased by £3,012k to £157,223k (2024: £160,235k). This reflects both lower overall traffic volumes
and a change in traffic mix away from higher-cost destinations, resulting in a proportionately greater decline in
associated direct costs.

Administrative expenses increased marginally by £391k to £27,981k (2024: £27,590k), This increase occurred despite
a reduction in salary costs partially offsetting higher underlying administrative costs incurred during the year. Foreign
exchange gains were broadly consistent with the prior year.

The Company’s operating profit increased to £13,455k (2024: £9,521k). Profit before taxation amounted to £14,316k
(2024: £10,234k), and profit for the financial year, after taxation of £4,026k (2024: £2,896k), was £10,289k (2024:
£7,338k). This reflects improved operational performance, with gross profit improved to 20.9% (2024: 18.8%).

The Company’s financial position at the year-end is set out in the statement of financial position on page 14. The
Company reported net assets of £45,925k at 31 December 2025 (2024: £35,635k), reflecting the profit generated during
the year and an improvement in working capital balances.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Competitive risks

Competitive pressure in the UK is a continuing risk for the Company. There is significant pressure on the price per
message the Company can charge, and the potential result is lost sales to its competitors. To manage this risk, the
Company strives to provide added-value products and services to its customers; prompt response times in the supply of
products and services and in the handling of customer queries; and through the maintenance of strong relationships with
customers.

The Company’s business may be affected by the price of marketing activities which may fluctuate, although purchasing
policies and practices seek to mitigate, where practicable, such risks.

General economic conditions and the financial! health of our customers affect the performance of our business; however,
the Company is not overly reliant on any single customer, and the business has performed well in this competitive
environment.

Regulatory risks ,

The Company is monitored by regulatory bodies, Phone-paid Services Authority (PSA), which monitors phone-paid
services in the UK and Ofcom, the UK communications regulator. There is a risk of fines through the non-compliance
with regulatory guidelines of customers using our platform. The Company has developed technology and processes to
detect such traffic in its network and has terminated business with customers judged to be wilfully breaching
regulations. The Company has taken measures to comply with the GDPR regulations that came into effect in May 2018.

Foreign exchange risks
The Company sells products into international markets in Europe and beyond and it is therefore exposed to currency
movements on such sales. The Company regularly reviews this exposure and assesses how to manage this risk. The
Company uses forward contracts to reduce the currency exchange movements for accounts receivable and accounts
payable were significant.
Credit risk
The Company’s principal financial assets are bank balances and trade and other receivables. The Company’s credit risk
is primarily attributable to its trade debtors. The amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of allowances for
doubtful debts. An allowance for doubtful debt is recorded if recoverability is not probable. During the year, the
Company continued to focus on the collection of aged receivables. The credit risk on liquid funds is considered limited
because the counterparties are banks with high credit ratings. The bad debt charge for the current year is £548k (2024:
reversal of £320k). The Company’s credit risk is diversified across a wide range of counterparties and customers, and
this exposure is monitored on a regular basis.
Liquidity risk
To maintain liquidity and ensure that sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations and future developments, the
. Company relies on support from its parent Company. This is disclosed further in the going concern section below and
in note | to the financial statements.

Employees .
The Company recognises that the knowledge, skills, and experience of its employees are critical to its continued success.
A rigorous recruitment process is applied to ensure that individuals with appropriate skills and experience are attracted
and appointed.
The Company seeks to support and retain employees by fostering a positive working culture and encouraging
professional development. Employees participate in annual performance and development reviews, during which
individual objectives and training requirements are discussed and agreed with line managers. Each employee is expected
to maintain a professional development plan, with support from the HR function where leadership or specialist
development is required.
A consistent recruitment framework is applied across the organisation to maintain high standards. Employee
engagement is monitored through regular employee surveys, which provide feedback on working practices and areas
for improvement.
The Company also encourages internal mobility as part of its approach to retaining skills and experience. All vacancies
are advertised internally in the first instance, and internal applicants are given the opportunity to apply and progress
within the organisation where appropriate.
Customers
Customer service is central to the Company’s operations at Sinch., For all Enterprise accounts, the sales team assigns a
dedicated account manager and an executive sponsor, who together are responsible for the overall customer
relationship. This includes oversight of commercial matters, technical support, and the introduction of new products
and services.
Customer performance and service delivery are monitored through regular commercial and service review meetings,
helping to ensure that customer requirements and expectations are consistently met.
Suppliers
We maintain an open and transparent approach to engagement with our suppliers. The Company meets its obligations
relating to the settlement of supplier invoices and adheres to agreed contractual terms. In addition, ongoing
communication with suppliers is maintained to support clarity, alignment, and the effective management of supplier
accounts.

Revenue
Revenue has increased from £197,346k to £198,659k in 2025, as noted above, primarily driven by an increase in transfer
pricing revenue, partially offset by a decline in messaging traffic.
Gross Profit Margin .
The overall gross profit margin increased from 18.8% in 2024 to 20.9%, resulting in a gross profit of £41,436k for the
year. Sinch UK’s largest clients are global aggregators sending traffic worldwide. The increase in gross margin is
primarily driven by a shift in traffic mix from lower-margin destinations to higher-margin destinations, combined with
selective price rate increases. While traffic volumes to certain countries declined, contributing a reduction in overall
margin pressure, revenues did not decrease proportionately due to these pricing actions. Together, these factors
contributed to a 2.1% point improvement in gross margin.
Current Ratio
The current ratio has further increased to 2.35.as at 31 December 2025 (2024: 1.73). This was primarily driven by
higher cash balances and trade debtors, together with a reduction in current liabilities, reflecting lower trade creditors
compared with the prior year.
Administrative Expenses
Administrative expenses increased marginally by 1.4%, from £27,590k in 2024 to £27,981k in 2025. The increase was
modest and occurred despite a £279k reduction in salary costs and a net favourable impact from intercompany transfer
pricing recharges, which partially offset higher underlying costs across professional services, bad debt provisions, and
other operational expenses. Foreign exchange gains were broadly consistent with the prior year.
Average Headcount
The average employee headcount declined from 85 in 2024 to 80 in 2025, representing a 5.9% reduction, reflecting the
centralisation of specific functions and organisational restructuring initiatives implemented during the year.
Energy and carbon information
During the reporting period the Company has consumed less than 40,000 kilowatt-hours (kWh) of energy and
accordingly energy and carbon information has not been included in this report. The Company’s ultimate parent, Sinch
AB, provide additional information within its sustainability report in its 2025 consolidated financial statements.
Future Developments and Post Balance Sheet Events
The company plans to continue to grow and expand its market share by providing added-value products and services
to its customers; prompt response times in the supply of products and services and in the handling of customer queries;
and through the maintenance of strong relationships with customers.

2.4 Revenue
The Company’s sales of services are made up mainly of transaction fees for sending messages, connecting calls and
subscription services for sending emails. Revenues are generated from the sale of services that are sold separately from
services sold in package deals. Revenue is recognized based on the contract with the customer and is measured based on
the consideration Sinch expects to be entitled to in exchange for transferring promised services. Revenues are distributed
across performance obligations and are recognized at a point in time or over time as the performance obligation is
satisfied, which is determined based on how control is passed to the customer. The promised consideration in a contract
with a customer may include fixed or variable amounts or both and is recognized only to the extent that it is highly
probable that a reversal of revenue will not occur.
Revenue represents the value, net of Value Added Tax, of services provided to customers. The Company’s revenue
comprises revenue from two types of SMS messages to mobile phones or SMS-related devices:
¢ Mobile Terminated (MT) — MT refers to a message sent from a content provider or enterprise to the end user’s SMS
device; and
e Premium Rate Short Message Service (PSMS) — PSMS refers to a message sent from a content provider containing
content requested by an end user via his/her SMS-enabled device, which the mobile operator sends to the end user’s
SMS device.
Revenue from both MT and PSMS is recorded when services have been rendered.
The MT service enables the content providers to send messages to a large number of recipients concurrently. MT service
revenue is recognised on a gross basis. :
PSMS messaging allows the content providers to reach subscribers and monetise their content. Mobile operators
facilitate the transport and billing of the PSMS messages to the end users. Under FRS 102 Section 23 “Revenue”, the
Company transfers to the buyer the significant risks and rewards of ownership of a transaction once the messages reach
the intended end user.
Other revenue comprises of transfer pricing revenue, monthly fee revenue non traffic fee and messaging fee. This
revenue is distributed across performance obligations and are recognized at a point in time or over time as the
performance obligation is satisfied, which is determined based on how control is passed to the customer.
