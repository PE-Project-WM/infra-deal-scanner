Strategic report
The Directors present their annual report and financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025 for
Dorenell Windfarm Limited (the "Company").
Business review
Revenues for the year were lower than the previous year due to lower prices and lower wind volumes.
Management are confident that wind speeds in the UK will maintain their medium term averages and therefore
future results are anticipated to be in line with expectations. Prices during 2025 were lower due to market
fluctuations and the long term Power Purchase Agreement and Contract for Difference (CfD) in place gives
management confidence that revenue will be sustained.
Direct costs have generally remained steady and increased slightly. However, the Company has maintained
profitability and a strong net asset position.

Principal risks and uncertainties ,
The principal risks to the profitability of the Company are as follows:
Health and safety
The health and safety of all contractors and the public is a key risk given the nature of the Company's business.
To minimise this risk, the Company is committed to creating a culture that views safe working as the only way
of working and to reviewing all processes and procedures to ensure it delivers this. Training is provided to 7
ensure the safety of the contractors that are set to work. In addition, a confidential helpline has been set up for
the use of anyone within the organisation to help eradicate unsafe practices and safeguard all contractors who
work at the wind farm site. The Directors also review health and safety performance at each of its scheduled
Board meetings.
Wind Volumes .
Lower wind speeds than anticipated result in less electricity being generated. Wind conditions will vary across
seasons and years. Although the Company does not have any control over wind resource it ensures that there are
adequate reserves in place to ensure it can withstand significant short term variability in production relating to
wind.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Operational issues
Technical issues may arise on plant and equipment which can cause significant unavailability of turbines,
business interruption and lost revenues. To mitigate this risk, regular reviews are undertaken in order to ensure
that maintenance is performed on all wind turbines to ensure that they are in good working order and fit for
purpose over their expected life spans. In addition, the Company has taken out appropriate plant and equipment
and business interruption insurance to reduce the potential financial impact of operating risks.
Offiake arrangements and power prices
The Company has a long term PPA in place which guarantees sales of electricity. In addition revenue stability is
secured through a long term Contract for Difference (CfD) with the Low Carbon Contracts Company (LCCC).
The Company does not consider counter party credit risk to be a principal risk as there has never been a credit
default on the PPA contract and the credit worthiness of the counterparty is assessed on a regular basis in line
with the Company's operating procedures.
Climate Change :
The Company, as a part of EDF power solutions UK group, is committed to accelerating a net zero future where
clean energy powers our lives. The Company generates renewable energy products and the principal activities of
the Company aim to directly combat the impact of climate change by leading the way in the delivery and
operation of renewable technologies. The current UK regulatory and political environments are aligned with the
objectives of the Company and the Directors see the commitment to a growth in renewable energy as a
significant opportunity for the Company.
Regulatory Risk - Energy Generators’ Levy:
The EGL is a government policy. This levy is paid by relevant electricity generators, including power generated
by grid connected renewables, based on their electricity output. The EGL is charged on generation receipts
above a benchmark price of £77.94 per MWh from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025 and £79.95 per MWh from
April 2025 to 31 March 2026. The benchmark price is subject to inflation annually. The EGL is intended to end
by 31 March 2028. The EGL does not apply to revenue generated at an agreed strike price under a Contract for
Difference (CfD), nor to revenue generated under Renewables Obligation Certifications (ROCs) or Renewable
Energy Guarantees of Origin (REGOs).
Dorenell Windfarm Limited was not subject to EGL for the year ended 31 December 2025.

Methodology:
The information in the tables above relates to the energy use and associated carbon emissions (Scope 1 and
Scope 2) from the operations of EDF Power Solutions in the U.K. The adopted methodology is based on the
Greenhouse Gas Protocol and UK Government Environmental Reporting Guidance.
Gas and electricity consumption data was largely gathered from utility supplier invoices and meter readings.
Estimates on gas and electricity consumption were applied to data sets that were either missing or part-missing
data throughout the year. Fuel card and mileage claim reporting allows the tracking of company vehicles and
transport data. Electricity import associated with BESS charging is tracked through on-site meters and accessed ~
via a Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system (GEMs). All energy data has been converted
to tCO2e using the published UK Government's 2025 Conversion Factors for Company Reporting.
The intensity ratio is calculated by dividing the total tCO2e carbon emissions (excluding emissions associated
with charging BESS assets & activities) by the electricity generated. .

Turnover
Tumover comprises the value of electricity generated, priced per megawatt hour ("MWh"), and includes two
components: the Power Purchase Agreement ("PPA") and the Contract for Difference ("CfD").
The PPA ensures a fixed price for electricity sold under long-term agreements, providing a stable revenue
stream.
CfDs are agreements between a low carbon electricity generator and the Low Carbon Contracts Company
("LCCC"), a UK Government owned entity responsible for delivering support mechanisms for low-carbon
electricity generation. These agreements are not considered to be contracts with a customer, as the LCCC does
not receive any goods or services from the generator. These arrangements are instead considered to be
Government Grants, with income arising from these grants recognised in the income statement in the period in
which generation takes place. Where the CfD strike price falls below the spot price of generation and payments
are made to the LCCC, these payments are netted against grant income, as allowable by IAS 20. The CfD
mechanism guarantees a fixed "strike price" by compensating for the difference between market prices and the
strike price, further stabilising revenue by mitigating market volatility.
Revenues from both the PPA and CfD are recognised in the period when the electricity is delivered. At
year-end, electricity generated but not yet invoiced is transferred to receivables and recognised as accrued
income.

Decommissioning Provision
A provision is made for the net present value of the estimated future decommissioning costs at the end of the
operating life of the wind farm. The provision is calculated using estimated costs of decommissioning, and these
estimates are arrived at by consideration of the expected costs of contracts to remove the installed plant. The
estimates are discounted at pre-tax rates that reflect current market assessments of the time value of money. The
assumptions used to calculate this provision are updated by management on a regular basis. See note 13 where
these assumptions are presented. Management have also assessed the sensitivity of the assumptions: A 100bps
increase in the inflation rate assumption increases the provision and asset by £1,291,000, a LOObps increase in
discount rate reduces the provision and asset by £1,107,000, a 10% increase in the cost assumptions applied
increases the provision and the asset by £676,000.
