Strategic report
Principal activities
Deutsche Telekom Global Business Solutions UK Ltd. (‘the Company”) provides information and telecommunication
services to its customers.
Review of the business
The Company’s result for the year is set out on page 16. The reported tumover was £20.0m for the year ended 31
December 2025 (2024: £19.5m), reflecting modest growth driven by contract renewals and new customer wins. Gross
profit was £3.6m (2024: £3.8m), representing a modest reduction compared with the prior year.
The Company recorded a loss before taxation of £637k (2024: £602k loss). This reflects continued investment in
organisational capability, alongside ongoing cost pressures from inflation and increased employment-related taxes.
Net assets increased to £87k (2024: £24k), supported by capital contributions from the parent company and
improved working capital dynamics.
Capital expenditure reduced to £389k (2024: £1.2m), reflecting the absence of significant one-off infrastructure
deployments undertaken in the prior year.
The Company continues to operate within a targeted investment phase, with a focus on achieving sustainable long-
term growth.

Future Developments
The Management Board of Deutsche Telekom Business Solutions GmbH has decided to adapt its international strategy
towards an increased focus on multinational corporation ("MNC") customers, The revised operating model affects the
Group's local business units in different ways depending upon their respective roles and market requirements.
Under the planned MNC strategy, Deutsche Telekom Global Business Solutions UK Ltd. is expected to transition
towards a supporting LBU model, while continuing to provide the local contracting, billing and commercial
capabilities required to support multinational customer engagements in the UK. A wider Business to Business (B2B) :
transformation programme has been established by the Deutsche Telekom Group and the detailed operational and
organisational impacts for individual countries, including the UK, are currently being developed.
Whilst macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty is expected to continue, the directors will continue to monitor
the Company's position as the revised MNC strategy and supporting Local Business Unit (LBU) model are
implemented.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Company's operations expose it to a variety of risks, principally delivery risk and market and economic risk.
Specific business risks are properly monitored and reported as they arise. Where a risk exposure is identified, suitable
counteractions are implemented to mitigate the risk. The risk management system is a vital part of the overall
Company governance system.
Organisational Risk
Leadership changes took place during 2026, including the resignation of the UK Managing Director, Andrew Weedon
as a director on 31 January 2026 before departing the company on 31 March 2026 and being replaced by Vikash
Ramnewash. The new CFO Ivan Gilbert commenced employment on 01 September 2025. As part of the revised
operating model, responsibility for the Company's finance leadership is expected to transition to the existing T-
Systems Limited and T-Systems Nordic CFO from 1 February 2027. Appropriate transition arrangements are being
discussed and developed implemented to maintain continuity of financial oversight, governance and statutory
compliance.
Delivery Risks
The Company operates a series of long-term contracts which expose it to changes in technology and shifts in customer
business circumstances over the lifetime of the contracts. Technology risks are managed through working closely with
the Deutsche Telekom Group as a whole to make the UK operations an integral part of the global delivery function of
the group, thereby being able to provide services to UK customers from the most appropriate point of production.
Changes in customer business circumstances are managed through working closely with customers to understand their
requirements and amend contracts to suit their needs as they develop. .
Market & Economic Risk
Various scenarios are modelled to establish the financial outcome on a worst-case basis of deterioration in the market
in which we operate. Specifically, the main factors which impact the Company, such as, trends in the market, foreign .
exchange and interest rates. At this time there is no business impact from the ongoing geopolitical uncertainty across
the globe. Exposure to US imposed tariffs on the UK and European partners has not created immediate concern but
both the ongoing geopolitical developments and potential tariff impacts will continue to be monitored for any material
fiscal effect on the business.
Financial risk management
The Company's financial instruments principally comprise trade receivables, cash balances, trade payables and
amounts due to and from group undertakings arising in the normal course of business. The Company is not exposed
to material commodity price or equity price risks and does not utilise derivative financial instruments.
Credit risk arises primarily from trade receivables and cash deposits. This risk is managed through ongoing
monitoring of customer creditworthiness, the application of expected credit loss provisioning and the placement of
cash deposits with established financial institutions.
Liquidity risk is managed through regular cash flow forecasting and access to funding from the Deutsche Telekom
Group. The Company had no external borrowings during the year, and the directors are satisfied that sufficient
liquidity is available to meet obligations as they fall due.
The Company has limited exposure to interest rate and foreign exchange risks arising from routine trading activities.
These exposures are monitored by management and are not considered material to the Company's financial position
or performance. :

Going concern ,
The directors have reviewed the Company’s financial position and considered its ability to continue as a going
concern for a period of at least twelve months from the date of approval of these financial statements.
As described under Future Developments, during 2026 the Deutsche Telekom Group determined a revised strategic
direction for the Company, under which the Company is expected to transition towards a supporting LBU model as
part of the Group’s increased focus on multinational corporation (“MNC”) customers. In assessing going concem,

: the directors have considered the implications of this revised operating model, including the Company’s continuing
obligations under existing customer contracts and the operational and organisational changes associated with the
wider Group transformation.

On 28 September 2026, Deutsche Telekom Business Solutions GmbH, the Company’s sole shareholder, passed a
written shareholder resolution formally approving the reorganisation. The resolution provides for existing customer
contracts to be performed and run to their natural conclusion and confirms the shareholder’s intention that the
Company should continue as a going concern following the reorganisation. The resolution does not authorise or
contemplate the winding-up, dissolution or striking-off of the Company.
As part of the resolution, Deutsche Telekom Business Solutions GmbH made a binding commitment to make
available financial support of up to €3.6 million to the Company for the purposes of the reorganisation. The
commitment remains in place for so long as the Company remains a subsidiary of Deutsche Telekom Business
Solutions GmbH and is intended to enable the Company to meet its liabilities as they fall due during the
reorganisation and to continue as a going concern.
The directors have also considered the Letter of Support dated 21 August 2026 from Deutsche Telekom Business
Solutions GmbH, which states the Deutsche Telekom Group’s current business policy of maintaining the liquidity of
its subsidiaries required pursuant to their budgets for at least twelve months from the date of signing the financial
statements. The directors have further considered the Company’s continued access to Deutsche Telekom
Aktiengesellschaft treasury arrangements and intra-group funding facilities. As at 31 December 2025, the Company
had no external debt finance. ,
In forming their conclusion, as detailed planning for the reorganisation has not yet commenced, the financial forecasts
incorporating the detailed effects of the revised operating model are not currently available. The directors have
therefore considered the Company’s existing financial position, the continuation of existing customer contracts, the
shareholder’s stated intention that the Company should continue as a going concem and the financial support available
from Deutsche Telekom Business Solutions GmbH, including the binding commitment of up to €3.6 million. Having
considered these factors, the directors have a reasonable expectation that the Company will have adequate resources
to continue in operational existence and meet its liabilities as they fall due for a period of at least twelve months from .
the date of approval of these financial statements.
Accordingly, the directors continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements.

214 Leases
At the inception of a contract, the Company assesses whether a contract is, or contains, a lease. A contract is, or
contains, a lease if the contract conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in
exchange for consideration.
As a lessee
At commencement or on modification of a contract that contains a lease component, along with one or more other
lease or non-lease components, the Company accounts for each lease component separately from the non-lease
components. The Company allocates the consideration in the contract to each lease component on the basis of its
relative stand-alone price and the aggregate stand-alone price of the non-lease components.
The Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability at the lease commencement date. The right-of-use
asset is initially measured at cost, which comprises the initial amount of the lease liability adjusted for any lease
payments made at or before the commencement date, plus any initial direct costs incurred less any lease incentives
received.
The right-of-use asset is subsequently depreciated using the straight-line method from the commencement date to the
end of the lease term, unless the lease transfers ownership of the underlying asset to the Company by the end of the
lease term or the cost of the right-of-use asset reflects that the Company will exercise a purchase option. In that case,
the right-of-use asset will be depreciated over the useful life of the underlying asset, which is determined on the same
. basis as those of property and equipment. In addition, the right-of-use asset is periodically reduced by impairment
. losses, if any, and adjusted for certain remeasurements of the lease liability.
The lease liability is initially measured at the present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the
commencement date, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, if that rate cannot be readily determined,
the Company’s incremental borrowing rate.
Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability comprise the following:
e fixed payments, including in-substance fixed payments;
e variable lease payments that depend on an index or a rate, initially measured using the index or rate as at the
. commencement date;
. * amounts.expected to be payable under a residual value guarantee; ,
«the exercise price under a purchase option that the Company is reasonably certain to exercise;
e lease payments in an optional renewal period if the Company is reasonably certain to exercise an extension option;
the company has one facility lease agreement with annual extension options.
* penalties for early termination of a lease unless the Company is reasonably certain not to terminate early.
The lease liability is measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. It is remeasured when there is a
change in future lease payments arising from a change in an index or rate, there is a change in the Company's estimate
of the amount expected to be payable under a residual value guarantee, if the Company changes its assessment of
whether it will exercise a purchase, extension, or termination option or if there is a revised in-substance fixed lease
payment.
When the lease liability is remeasured in this way, a corresponding adjustment is made to the carrying amount of the
right-of-use asset, to the extent that the right-of-use asset is reduced to nil, with any further adjustment required from
