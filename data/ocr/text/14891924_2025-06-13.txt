Strategic report

For the year ended 31 December 2024

The directors present the strategic report for the year to 31 December 2024. The comparative period presented
is from incorporation on 24 May 2023 to 31 December 2023.

Review of the business

The Group, comprising Ensco 1506 Limited and its subsidiaries, continues to provide industry-leading enterprise
database managed services and technical consultancy in Oracle, SQL server, and multi-cloud technology. Our
client base includes start-ups, mid-market growth organizations, and large enterprises, all of which operate
mission-critical database architectures.

. The 2024 financial year marked the first full 12 month contribution from Meursault I] Topco group inciuding the
Canadian business Eclipsys Solutions Inc, which was acquired in October 2023. During the year, the Group ,
invested in additional corporate infrastructure to support future scale. A decline in Canadian hardware resale
activity offset growth in UK revenues.

Looking ahead, the Group is forecasting double digit percentage growth in both revenue and Adjusted EBITDA for
. 2025. This outlook is driven by (i) a sharpened focus on larger enterprise customers, (ii) accelerating demand for

Oracle Cloud Infrastructure - now including the ability to access Oracle databases through other hyperscale

cloud platforms - and (iii) a leaner cost base as synergy and cost efficiency initiatives mature.

At year-end, the total loans and other borrowings, inclusive of capital and interest, were £108.3m (2023:

100.0m), contributing to a net liability position of £23.4m (2023: £4.4m). Finance costs for the year totalled

£13.2m (Period ended 31 December 2023: £2.9m), resulting in a loss before tax of £17.7m (Period ended 31

December 2023: £4.7m). ;

Principle risks & uncertainties

The Group continues to operate in an environment influenced by macroeconomic conditions, including rising

interest rates, inflationary pressures on salaries, and potential economic recession risks. However, with a

diverse customer base spanning multiple sectors and a well-established global footprint, the Group remains

resilient and positioned to manage these uncertainties while pursuing further growth.

Future Developments
In addition to the initiatives outlined above, the Group plans to complete several bolt-on acquisitions in 2025 to
strengthen its geographical footprint and broaden its service offerings. Organic growth will be supported by a
targeted push into the SME segment through Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI). Continued shareholder backing
and strategic acquisitions will remain critical drivers of the Group’s growth strategy.
Post Balance Sheet Events
There are no material post-balance sheet events impacting the Group's financial position since 31 December
2024.
Going Concern
At 31 December 2024, the Group had net current liabilities of £6.6m (2023: £4.8m), net liabilities of £23.4m
(2023: £4.4m), and a cash balance of £9.9m (2023: £8.8m).
The Group's Bank Loan facilities mature in 2029 & the Group's Loan Notes mature in 2030 (refer Note 22). The
Group's Bank Loan facilities are subject to covenants based upon cashflow cover and leverage ratios. The
banking covenants are tested quarterly and monitored on a regular basis by the Directors. The terms of the
Group's Bank Loan's facilicies allows for a portion of interest due each period to be settled via capitalisation onto
the loan principle, and the under the terms of the Loan Notes the default settlement of annual interest each year
is via capitalisation onto the Loan Note principle balance. This substantially reduces the cash requirement to
settle interest in the going concern forecast period, and each future period until maturity.

. In performing its going concern assessment, the Directors have considered a forecast up to 31 May 2026, which
is a period of at least 12 months from the date these financial statements are authorised for issue. The directors
have reviewed the Group's cash flow forecasts, including stress testing for various economic scenarios.
Subsequent to year end, the Group successfully passed covenant compliance for Q1 2025 with headroom, and
continues to forecast adequate headroom across the going concern forecast period.

The Board remains confident that the Group and Company have adequate resources to continue operating for
the foreseeable future, including through to at least 12 months from the date these financial statements are
approved for issue. The Directors therefore consider the Group and Company to be going concerns and have
prepared the financial statements on this basis.
Financial Risk Management
Liquidity Risk
Liquidity risk remains a key focus for the Group, with cash flow and liquidity closely monitored. The refinancing of
debt post-acquisition has enhanced the Group’s financial position, enabling it to meet all obligations as they fall
due.
Interest Rate Risk
The Group remains susceptible to interest rate fluctuations. At the balance sheet date, total debt included
£41.0m in bank loans (2023: £41.3m). The interest rates on bank debt vary with the SONIA & CORRA rates, while
loan notes are fixed at 12% or Nil%. The Group continues to monitor interest rate trends and manage its
exposure accordingly.

Financial Risk Management (continued) .
Customer credit exposure
The Group may offer credit terms to its customers which allow payment of the debt after delivery of the goods or
services. The Group is at risk to the extent that a customer may be unable to pay the debt on the specified due
date. This risk is mitigated by the strong on-going customer relationships and selling principally to large credit
worthy organisations. The business has historically experienced low levels of bad debts.
Foreign exchange transactional currency exposure
The Group is exposed to currency exchange rate risk due to a significant proportion of its trade receivables, and :
trade payables, being denominated in non-sterling currencies principally through its Canadian based subsidiary
Eclipsys. The net exposure of each currency is monitored is managed by the use of forward foreign exchange
contracts.
Exposure to Risks
The Group recognizes the importance of proactive risk management. The board regularly reviews and discusses
key risks, with action plans established to mitigate any potential negative impact. These include risks related to
macroeconomic changes, technological advancements, and market competition.

3.3 Business combinations
In the parent company financial statements, the cost of a business combination is the fair value at the
acquisition date of the assets given, equity instruments issued and liabilities incurred or assumed, plus costs
directly attributable to the business combination. The excess of the cost of a business combination over the fair
value of the identifiable assets, liabilities and contingent liabilities acquired is recognised as goodwill.
Contingent consideration is initially recognised at an estimated amount where the consideration is probable and
can be measured reliably. Where (i) the contingent consideration is not considered probable or cannot be
reliably measured but subsequently becomes probable and measurable or (ii) contingent consideration
previously measured is adjusted, the amounts are recognised as an adjustment to the cost of the business
combination.
Deferred tax is recognised on differences between the value of assets (other than goodwill) and liabilities
recognised in a business combination accounted for using the purchase method and the amounts that can be
deducted or assessed for tax, considering the manner in which the carrying amount of the asset or liability is
expected to be recovered or settled. The deferred tax recognised is adjusted against goodwill.

3.17 Retirement benefits
The Group operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a pension plan
under which the Group pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the contributions have been paid
the Group has no further payment obligations.

in accruals as a liability in the Balance Sheet. The assets of the plan are held separately from the Group in
independently administered funds. :
3.18 Leases
Rentals payable under operating leases, including any lease incentives received, are charged to profit or loss on
a straight-line basis over the term of the relevant lease except where another more systematic basis is more
representative of the time pattern in which economic benefits from the leased asset are consumed.
3.19 Provisions
Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the Group a legal or constructive obligation that
probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, and a reliable estimate can be made of the
amount of the obligation. .
Provisions are charged as an expense to profit or loss in the year that the Group becomes aware of the
obligation, and are measured at the best estimate at the balance sheet date of the expenditure required to settle
the obligation, taking into account relevant risks and uncertainties. When payments are eventually made, they
are charged to the provision carried in the Balance Sheet.
3.20 Interest income & expense
Interest income is recognised in profit or loss usirig the effective interest method.
Finance costs are charged to profit or loss over the term of the debt using the effective interest method so that
the amount charged is at a constant rate on the carrying amount. Issue costs are initially recognised as a
‘reduction in the proceeds of the associated capital instrument.
All borrowing costs are recognised in profit or loss in the year in which they are incurred.
