STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
The directors of Freshwave Services Limited (the "company") present their Strategic Report of the company for the
year ended 31 December 2025.
Business review
The company has continued to grow organically, supported by mobile and wireless coverage demand. The
-development of new products and solutions for our customers, coupled with expansion into new verticals such as
Stadia and Public Sector has supported a strong pipeline of new business.
The results of the company for the year ended 31 December 2025 show turnover of £15.7m (2024: £13.6m), gross
margin of £8.2m (2024: £7.9m) and an operating loss of £20.9m (2024: £16.3m). Recurring revenues have
increased a result of organic growth. This growth alongside an increase in non-recurring one-off revenue led to an
overall increase in turnover of 15.2%. Gross margin percentage has decreased by 6.4% as a result of lower-margin
one-off revenue in the year. Administration expenses of £29.1m (2024 - £24.2m) reflect our investment for future
growth and include £14.7m asset depreciation and amortisation (2024 - £10.6m).
The business continued its significant investment phase in the year ended 31 December 2025 deploying £16.25M
to expand infrastructure capability, increase service coverage and support future revenue growth.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The section below highlights the primary risks and uncertainties inherent within the company and the associated
mitigations in place. .
Market and Competitive Environment
Changes to the economic conditions of our customers, suppliers, and the markets in which the company operates
may impact the business. This is mitigated by regular monitoring of industry, customer and supplier developments.
The company also continuously engages with its customers, suppliers and partners (including mobile network
operators) to understand their needs and constraints.
Product and Technology
The company is a provider of neutral host digital infrastructure. The company's products and solutions may become
less relevant to its customer base and allow its main competitors gaining market share at the company’s expense.
This is mitigated by regular monitoring of industry, customer and supplier developments. The company is able to
respond to changes through the introduction of new technology and solutions.
Supply Chain :
Supply Chain disruption and our reliance on key partners may impact our ability to procure the equipment to either
upgrade existing customer’s technology or activate new customer accounts and restricted our revenue growth. This
is mitigated by the identification and active engagement with our strategic suppliers together with a focus on
diversifying our supplier base.
Cyber Security / Data Protection .
Inadequate defences may result in a material security breach with the potential loss of access to internal systems,
customer data being compromised and GDPR breaches that result in significant fines for the company. These risks
are mitigated through considerable executive focus in this area led by the COO and supported at all levels across
the company, including the delivery of regular training programmes on cyber security and data protection to our
employees. We also work closely with our technology partners to ensure that our cyber defences are fit for
purpose.

Foreign exchange ‘
Transactions in currencies other than the functional currency (foreign currency) are initially recorded at the
exchange rate prevailing on the date of the transaction.
Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the translation at
period-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are
recognised in profit or loss except when deferred in other comprehensive income as qualifying cash flow
hedges.
Foreign exchange gains and losses that relate to borrowings and cash and cash equivalents are presented in
the profit and toss account within ‘finance income or costs’. All other foreign exchange gains and losses are
presented in the statement of comprehensive income within ‘administrative expenses’.
Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the company and
the turnover can be reliably measured. Turnover is measured as the fair value of the consideration received or
receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales taxes.
Turnover in relation to the provision of cellular and wireless connectivity services is recognised over the life of
the contract on a straight line basis from the live service date.
Turnover in relation to projects is recognised by reference to the stage of completion of a contract determined
by the value of the services provided at the balance sheet date as a proportion of the total value of the
engagement.
Services provided to the client which at the balance sheet date have not been billed have been recognised as
turnover and are included in debtors as accrued income. Service not yet provided to the client which at the
balance sheet date have been billed are not recognised as turnover and are instead included in creditors as
deferred income.
Employee benefits
The costs of short-term employee benefits are recognised as a liability and an expense, unless those costs
are required to be recognised as part of the cost of stock or fixed assets.
The cost of any unused holiday entitlement is recognised in the year in which the employees' services are
received.
Termination benefits are recognised immediately as an expense when the company is demonstrably
committed to terminate the employment of an employee or to provide termination benefits.
Sales commissions
This is periodically reviewed and the estimate was adjusted prospectively in the current year to recognise
commissions over 5 years (2024: 3 years). Historically, the majority of contracts had a term of approximately
three years, and commission costs were therefore recognised over that period. However, in recent periods,
there has been a shift towards longer contract durations, reflecting commercial strategy. As a result, the
expected period over which the related economic benefits are realised has increased. Accordingly, the
amortisation period for commissions has been extended to five years to better align the recognition of these
costs with the longer-term nature of the underlying contracts.
Retirement benefits
For defined contribution schemes the amount charged to profit or loss is the contributions payable in the year.
Differences between contributions payable in the year and contributions actually paid are shown as either
accruals or prepayments.

Taxation

Current tax assets and current tax liabilities and deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities are offset, if
and only if, there is a legally enforceable right to set off the amounts and the entity intends either to settle on
the net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
