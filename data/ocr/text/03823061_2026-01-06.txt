The directors present the Strategic Report of AA Access Partnership Limited (the “Company”) for the
year ended 31 December 2024.
. Principal activities
Access Partnership is a global specialist consultancy, providing regulatory and public policy advisory .
services to the world’s leading innovation companies and the public sector. The Group also plays a
significant role in supporting the space and communications industry, including drafting and adapting
regulations that facilitate market access and grow access connectivity. Access Partnership Group has
over 140 employees who have collective expertise in over 200 jurisdictions and speak 35 languages
across 6 global offices. :
Business review
Revenue was below 2023 with macro challenges from the technology sector, including many cost
reduction programmes being implemented across the sector. As noted in the 2023 financial
statements the Group lost a material customer when a major client programme was discontinued
during 2024 which meant revenue was below expectations for the year. However, across the rest of
the client base we continued to deliver high quality work with a stable base of revenue to build on for
future years.
Although EBITDA was down compared to last year, some cost mitigation was possible with a
restructuring and redundancy programme approved and implemented in April 2024.
The Company finished the year with cash of £1,029,281 (2023: £2,034,089) and a loss after tax of
£1,539,949 (2023: profit of £3,218,993). . ,

Financial risk management
Currency Risk
The Company is exposed to currency risk in respect of all vendor payments, assets and liabilities
denominated in currencies other than the Great Britain Pound Sterling (GBP). The main currencies to
which the company is exposed include United State Dollar (USD), The Euro (EUR), Saudi Riyal
(SAR), South Africa Rand (ZAR) and Singapore Dollar (SGD). The Company seeks to mitigate the
risk as far as possible by matching the estimated foreign currency denominated liabilities with assets
denominated in the same currency. ,
Liquidity/Cash flow Risk

; Liquidity and cashflow risk is the risk that cash may not be available to pay obligations when due. The
Company very meticulously monitors its cash flow requirements to ensure it is able to meet its working
capital requirements.
Credit Risk
Credit risk represents the potential loss arising from an obligor's failure to meet the terms of any
contract or otherwise perform as agreed. The risk depends upon the probability of a counterparty
defaulting, the size of the exposure when default occurs and the potential recovery amount. The
Company seeks to eliminate credit risk by ensuring full legal agreements have been put in place to
ensure the risk of non-payment of fee is minimized, whilst cash balances have been placed with
reputable credit institutions with a high credit rating.

Going concern
The directors have considered the cash flows, forecasts, and projections of AA Access Partnership
Limited (the “Company’) for the period up to and including 31 December 2026, taking into account their
experience of current trading and the impact of prevailing market conditions and the economic
environment. The Company is a subsidiary of Alpha Midco 1 Limited (the “Parent Company”) and forms
part of the group that is headed by this Parent Company (the “Group”). Based on the going concern
assessment performed for the Company, it is determined that the Company is reliant on the Group for
funding to meet its obligations as they fall due.
Consistent with the disclosure in the consolidated financial statements of Alpha Midco 1 Limited, at the
date of approval of these financial statements; the directors note that the Group has sufficient financing
available to meet their existing requirements for at least the next 12 months of trading. The facilities
available to the Group include a revolving credit facility of £5m, with a remaining undrawn balance of
£2m as at the date of signing the financial statements. The directors of the Company therefore consider
that, based on the support of the Group, the business has sufficient liquidity to continue trading for at
least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements.

Going concern (continued)
The reviewed forecasts contain assumptions on the ability of the Group to continue to generate
revenues through the forecast period. The directors have also considered the continued availability of
lending facilities within the Group and other factors, both within and outside of the review period, which
may impact the going concern assessment for the Company. In considering the appropriateness of the
going concern basis in preparing these financial statements, the directors have also assessed the cash
flows and covenant compliance of the Group under a base case scenario and several severe but
plausible downside scenarios. One of the plausible downside scenarios includes a 10% reduction in
revenues and a cessation of current M&A activity.
The terms of the Group’s facilities include covenants which must be adhered to in order for the facilities
to remain available. If the covenants are breached, lenders have remedies up to and including the right
to require repayment of the loans. The main covenants relate to Minimum adjusted EBITDA, Debt
‘ ; service ratio and Gross leverage, which are required to be below certain levels at the end of each
calendar quarter. Lenders have provided support by resetting covenants and allowing certain interest
payments to be Paid in Kind. As part of this, required leverage levels were increased to less stringent
levels in 2024 and after the year end in 2025, but are set to reduce in the first quarter of 2026. In
addition, post year end, shareholders have injected additional equity into the Group.
Based on current trading, it is forecasted in the base case that certain covenants in the Group’s facilities
agreement, such as the Debt service ratio, Gross leverage covenant and Minimum adjusted EBITDA,
will not be satisfied for the quarter ending 31 March 2026. The directors of the Parent Company have
commenced discussions with lenders and shareholders to potentially reset covenants and/or take other
measures ahead of the forecast breach. The directors are confident that these discussions will provide
the Group and therefore also the Company with the necessary support as there is clear historical track
record of cooperation between the shareholders and lenders of resetting covenants which is in the best
interests of the business. However, given the proximity of when any required renegotiations need to be
finalised and the uncertainty of the outcome of discussions to date, the directors consider there is a
material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt about the Group’s ability to continue as a going
concern.
This, consequently, indicates the existence of a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt
about the Company's ability to continue as a going concern as the Company is reliant upon funding
from the Group.
Notwithstanding this material uncertainty, the directors believe that the Group’s discussions with
shareholders and lenders will lead to a resetting of covenants or such appropriate measures from 31
March 2026 and will allow the Group to continue as a going concern and providing funding to the
Company for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the financial statements. Therefore, the
directors consider that the going concern basis of preparation of these financial statements remains
appropriate, based on the expectation of continued support from the Group. The financial statements
do not include the adjustments that would result if the Company were unable to continue as going
concern.

2.4 Going concern
The directors have considered the cash flows, forecasts, and projections of AA Access
Partnership Limited (the “Company”) for the period up to and including 31 December 2026,
taking into account their experience of current trading and the impact of prevailing market
conditions and the economic environment. The Company is a subsidiary of Alpha Midco 1
Limited (the “Parent Company”) and forms part of the group that is headed by this Parent
Company (the “Group”). Based on the going concern assessment performed for the Company, ;
it is determined that the Company is reliant on the Group for funding to meet its obligations as
they fall due.
Consistent with the disclosure in the consolidated financial statements of Alpha Midco 1 Limited,
at the date of approval of these financial statements, the directors note that the Group has
sufficient financing available to meet their existing requirements for at least the next 12 months
of trading. The facilities available to the Group include a revolving credit facility of £5m, with a
remaining undrawn balance of £2m as at the date of signing the financial statements. The
directors of the Company therefore consider that, based on the support of the Group, the
business has sufficient liquidity to continue trading for at least 12 months from the date of
approval of these financial statements.
The reviewed forecasts contain assumptions on the ability of the Group to continue to generate
revenues through the forecast period. The directors have also considered the continued
availability of lending facilities within the Group and other factors, both within and outside of the
review period, which may impact the going concern assessment for the Company. In
considering the appropriateness of the going concern basis in preparing these financial
statements, the directors have also assessed the cash flows and covenant compliance of the
Group under a base case scenario and several severe but plausible downside scenarios. One
of the plausible downside scenarios includes a 10% reduction in revenues and a cessation of
current M&A activity.
The terms of the Group's facilities include covenants which must be adhered to in order for the
facilities to remain available. If the covenants are breached, lenders have remedies up to and
including the right to require repayment of the loans. The main covenants relate to Minimum
adjusted EBITDA, Debt service ratio and Gross leverage, which are required to be below certain
levels at the end of each calendar quarter. Lenders have provided support by resetting
covenants and allowing certain interest payments to be Paid in Kind. As part of this, required
leverage levels were increased to less stringent levels in 2024 and after the year end in 2025,
but are set to reduce in the first quarter of 2026. In addition, post year end, shareholders have
injected additional equity into the Group.
Based on current trading, it is forecasted in the base case that certain covenants in the Group’s
facilities agreement, such as the Debt service ratio, Gross leverage covenant and Minimum
adjusted EBITDA, will not be satisfied for the quarter ending 31 March 2026. The directors of
the Parent Company have commenced discussions with lenders and shareholders to potentially
reset covenants and/or take other measures ahead of the forecast breach. The directors are
confident that these discussions will provide the Group and therefore also the Company with
the necessary support as there is clear historical track record of cooperation between the
sharehalders and lenders of resetting covenants which is in the best interests of the business.

2.6 Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Company and the turnover can be reliably measured. Turnover is measured as the fair value of
the consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and
other sales taxes. The following criteria must also be met before turnover is recognised:
Rendering of services
Turnover from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services
are provided in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all of the following
conditions are satisfied:
+ the amount of turnover can be measured reliably;
+ itis probable that the Company will receive the consideration due under the contract;
+ the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be
measured reliably; and ;
+ the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.
Turnover realised in the financial year includes the different revenue classes listed below:
+ Retainer

Retainer - Retainer as a revenue stream refers to a fixed, recurring payment made by the client
to a service provider, in exchange for ongoing access to the provider's expertise and services.
The terms of the retainer will specify the fixed payment amounts and the scope of services that
will be provided under the retainer agreement. Revenue is measured on a straight-line basis
over the contract duration.
: Time & Material - Revenue derived from client billing using actual time worked and charging the
client on pre-defined rates based on various roles or skill levels.
Percentage of Completion - The Percentage of Completion (PoC) method is an accounting
approach that allocates a portion of the total contract revenue to each period in which the project ~

+ Reliable measurement of project completion to determine progress of project.

+ Delivery obligations associated with respective projects.
+ When it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract turnover, the
expected loss is recognized as an expense immediately.
+ Variations in contract work, claims and incentive payments are included to the extent
that the amount can be measured reliably and its receipt is considered probable.

2.11 Impairments
Capital assets, other than those measured at fair value, are assessed for indicators of
impairment at each Balance Sheet date. If there is objective evidence of impairment, an
impairment loss is recognised on the Statement of Comprehensive Income.
An asset is impaired when there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events
that occurred after initial recognition, the estimated recoverable value of the asset has been
reduced. The recoverable amount of an asset is the higher of its fair value less costs to sell and
its value in use. ‘
Where indicators exist for a decrease in impairment loss, the prior impairment is tested to
determine reversal. An impairment loss is reversed on an individual impaired asset to the extent
that the revised recoverable value does not lead to a revised carrying amount higher than the
carrying value had no impairment been recognised.

2.12 Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the Company a legal or
constructive obligation that probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, and
a reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation.
Provisions are charged as an expense to the Statement of Comprehensive Income in the period
that the Company becomes aware of the obligation and are measured at the best estimate at
the balance sheet date of the expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account
relevant risks and uncertainties.
When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the Balance
Sheet.

was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying
amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been had the impairment not
previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
