Introduction
The directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Business review
There have not been any changes in the principal activities of the Company in the year under review. The
Company continues to roll out its business plan and the directors expect revenues to continue to ramp up
and results to improve as more premises are fully commissioned and the network expanded.
During the year, investment in the network increased substantially by £35.6m (2024 - £24.7m) as our
network expanded past 66,000 premises.
The Company's revenue for the year ended 31 December 2025 of £8,508,538 increased by 47.7% in
comparison to 2024, driven primarily by the growth in the Company’s customer base. The operating loss
was £8,939,432 compared to an operating loss of £7,845,578 for the prior year largely driven by the
increased investment in staff to drive the growth of the organisation.
The Company also monitors EBITDA, defined as operating loss before depreciation and amortisation, as a
measure of the underlying cash-generating performance of the network. The EBITDA loss narrowed to
£2.1m (2024: loss of £3.3m), reflecting the continued growth of the live network and improving gross
margin, notwithstanding the increased investment in headcount during the year. The Company expects to
be generating positive EBITDA during the second half of 2026 as the customer base on the live network
continues to grow.
At 31 December 2025 the Company had net liabilities of £37,557,115 (2024 - £22,272,795) as shown on the
balance sheet on page 12. Net liabilities included £58,983,767 (2024 - £47,757,910) owed to the Company’s
parent, Wessex Midco Limited.
During the year the Company secured significant additional funding to support its continued rural network
expansion. In June 2025 the Company signed a £50m senior debt facility with the National Wealth Fund,
taking total committed senior debt to £68m and supporting the Company's plan to continue the significant
expansion of its network from its current level of 66,000 premises as at December 2025.
The Company also agreed expansions of two of its existing Project Gigabit contracts with Building Digital
UK during the year: the South Wiltshire contract was expanded by £5.2m to £24.0m, with target premises
increasing from 14,523 to 18,239; and the New Forest contract was expanded to £17.4m / 15,120 premises.
Together with the Company's existing Dorset and South Somerset contract (£38.2m / 19,557 premises) and
the recently completed North Dorset contract (£6.4m / 6,483 premises, completed November 2025), the
Company now holds Project Gigabit contracts worth approximately £86m in total state-aid subsidy.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Company operates in a highly competitive marketplace, which is a continuing risk to the Company as it

could lose sales to its key competitors. The Company manages this risk by focusing on quality of service

and reaching areas where it expects to be the only physical infrastructure provider.

The Company's activities are complex and expose it to several financial risks including credit risk, cashflow

_risk and liquidity risk. The Company does not use derivative financial instruments for speculative purposes.

Health and Safety risk

Network build and operational management of networks carries health and safety risks. Failure to manage

these risks properly could result in injuries to our workforce, impact our customer service and damage

equipment. Management of health and safety risk is a key priority for the Company. The Company has a

comprehensive risk management system in place and the effectiveness of this is continually assessed. A

training and development programme is maintained to continually improve working practices.

Competition risk

Where multiple suppliers have incurred similar costs in laying similar infrastructure in the same place

uptake and returns on investment will suffer. The Company primarily builds in rural areas, which are by :

their nature more expensive to build and less competitive. The Company regularly reviews the competitive

landscape in its target areas to maintain first mover advantage and ensure overbuild is limited. Take-up of

our network is best-in-class, exceeding 30% overall and 50% in our most established networks.

Contract delivery risk

The Company has been awarded significant funding as a contribution to roll out fibre to the premises to

hard to reach areas. In exchange for this contribution the Company must achieve the milestones in the

respective funding contracts. Key to our management of this risk is our own delivery team. We are proud to

have a construction team over 180 strong to undertake the ploughing, cabling, splicing and testing required

as part of the contract delivery. We were up to date on all delivery milestones that fell due during 2025 and

to the date of these financial statements.

Network resilience risk

Customer numbers and satisfaction are at risk from disruption and outages. The company has its own

customer support and network operations teams and monitors performance through the key performance

indicators below. Our Trustpilot rating of “Excellent” is a testament to the hard work of all of our

engineering and customer facing teams.

Liquidity risk

The company funds the expansion of its network through a variety of sources, including Government

contracts that subsidise delivery, bank debt and equity. Severe delays in either network construction (to

ready for servicé status) or growth in customer numbers could result in lower cash flows from the

Government contracts or bank debt. The performance of the company has enabled it to attract new

sources of funding (Government subsidy and debt) during 2025. The company is fully funded until 2029.

Credit risk
The Company’s principal financial assets are trade and other receivables and cash. The Company’s credit
tisk is primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The amounts presented in the Balance Sheet are net of
any allowances for doubtful receivables. The Company has no significant concentration of credit risk, with
exposure spread over many counterparties and customers.
Interest rate risk
The Company's interest-bearing borrowings comprise a senior debt facility and finance lease obligations.
Finance lease obligations are at fixed rates. The senior debt facility totals £68m across two committed
facilities, of which approximately 70% bears interest at fixed rates, with the balance bearing interest at
floating rates referenced to SONIA or the Bank of England base rate. The directors consider the residual
interest rate risk to be modest.
Foreign currency risk
The Company is exposed to limited foreign currency risk arising from a small number of purchases
denominated in Euros and US dollars. The Company does not currently hedge this exposure on the basis
that aggregate Euro and USD-denominated spend is immaterial in the context of the Company's overall
cost base. Management monitors exposure on an ongoing basis and will reassess the position if relevant
spend becomes material. .

offset these costs, the Company applies for grant funding and charges rates that are above industry
standards in these locations. The Company believes this strategy allows it to expand its reach while
maintaining financial sustainability.
