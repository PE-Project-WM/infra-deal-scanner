GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT

FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 APRIL 2025

Introduction :

The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 April 2025.

Business review

The Group provides outsourced revenue management services, including billing collection, account
maintenance, investigation services, to the Utility, Telecoms, Public Sector/Central Government and Financial
Services sectors across three UK sites. We are uniquely positioned to undertake large volume highly complex
account management, correction, and resolution tasks on behalf of clients in these sectors.

Over the past 26 years, we have built our services on a foundation of fairness and empathy, ensuring that
customers are treated with respect and that complex queries are approached positively. This philosophy has
strengthened collaboration with clients, improved engagement with their customers, and enabled us to adapt
successfully to an evolving social and economic landscape.

Group sales increased by around 14%.. The Directors consider this is a strong result given ongoing labour
market challenges in recruiting and retaining skilled resources to fully maximise contractual opportunities.

We remain committed to being a responsible employer and are fully accredited by the Living Wage Foundation
as a Real Living Wage employer.

While most costs remained stable, payroll expenses increased due to workforce growth and the implementation
of the latest Real Living Wage increase. ‘

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Group is exposed toa range of risks arising from normal business activities, with operational and conduct
risk being the most significant. .

‘Operational risks are defined as financial !oss or impairment to reputation because of failure of internal
processes. The Group relies heavily on its Actius system, and any prolonged outage would have a material
impact on operations. This risk is mitigated through a comprehensive Data Recovery Plan and robust internal
controls. The Board defines the overall risk framework, while line managers manage the day-to-day risks within
this structure. . :

Conduct risk arises from actions, behaviours, or decisions that could lead to poor customer outcomes. The
Group manages this through a risk, audit, and compliance framework that monitors staff activity and ensures
adherence to legal, regulatory, and contractual obligations. Our approach is underpinned by the six Treating
Customers Fairly (TCF) principles, ensuring that fair outcomes for consumers remain central to our objectives
and operations.
Financial performance : :
Revenue has increased from £15.5m to £17.7m, representing growth of just over 14%. This improvement was
driven by an increase in client numbers, higher account volumes serviced, and the growth of the income stream
from debt purchase activities. :
Profit before tax rose to £884k, an increase (+13%) on the prior year, reflecting strong revenue growth and
disciplined cost management.
Net assets continued to strengthen, reaching £4.2m at year-end (2024: £3.8m), primarily due to the acquisition
of several debt purchase portfolios.

Risk management and exposure: financial Instruments
As a result of its business activities, the Group has a normal level of exposure to price, credit, liquidity, and cash
flow risks arising from trading activities which are conducted in sterling. The Group does not enter into any
hedging arrangements. .
Liquidity risk represents the possibility that the Group may be unable to meet its financial obligations as they fall
due because of insufficient cash resources. Liquidity and cash flow risks are managed by maintaining adequate
reserves and banking facilities, alongside continuous monitoring of both forecast and actual cash flows.
Future developments
The Group is actively investing in the development of self-service options to enhance customer experience and
operational efficiency. A key part of this strategy involves leveraging artificial intelligence to automate routine
processes, provide intelligent support, and deliver personalised interactions. By introducing Al-driven tools, the
Group aims to reduce dependency on manual intervention, improve response times, and create a seamless,
user-friendly service environment. :

Employee engagement
The Directors continued to place significant emphasis on the interests and well-being of employees, recognising

2.14 Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting period end date, the group reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and
intangible assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an
impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in
order to determine the extent of the impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the
recoverable amount of an individual asset, the Group estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-
generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value
in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount
rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to
the asset for which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its
carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its
recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the
relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a
revaluation decrease.
Recognised impairment losses are reversed if, and only if, the reasons for the impairment loss have
ceased to apply. Where an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the asset
(or cash-generating unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but so that
the increased carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been
determined had no impairment loss been recognised for the asset (or cash-generating unit) in prior
years. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the
relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the reversal of the impairment loss is
treated as a revaluation increase. .

Bad debt provision
