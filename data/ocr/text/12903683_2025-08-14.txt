Who we are

We are the UK's leading value for money connectivity provider. We believe that simple, affordable, reliable and fair connectivity should
be available to everyone.

Since entering the market in the early 2000s, the Group has a proud history as an innovative challenger, ensuring customers benefit

from more choice, affordable prices and better services. We provide fibre, broadband, landline and data services to c3.3 million

customers. Our two businesses, PXC and TalkTaik, were operationally separated on 1 March 2024.

PXC is the UK's largest wholesale aggregator platform and manages the Group's network.

TalkTalk is the fourth largest ISP in the UK providing residential broadband and associated products.

This is the Annual Report of the Group which consolidates both business units with key metrics being as follows:

+ Our fixed line network currently is able to reach approximately 96% of UK homes.

* Our unbundling equipment (such as digital subscriber line access multiplexers, multi-service access nodes and Ethernet switches)
are installed in over 3,000 exchanges — the largest such deployment in the UK.

+ We are playing a key role in facilitating the ro!l-out of Full Fibre across the UK as network builders seek immediate access to high
volumes of customers to support their investment.

« We access discounted FTTP terms with Openreach following signing up to the Equinox deal.

+ We also access an extensive FTTP footprint via AltNets on attractive commercial ternis, including CityFibre, Community Fibre and
Freedom Fibre, with a number of new AltNet partners being onboarded.

We offer customers access to faster, more reliable fibre broadband, through wholesale agreements with Openreach, CityFibre and

other AltNets , provided via our FTTC network, and next generation FTTP networks. We continue to provide copper-based broadband

connectivity (i.e. ADSL) for those customers who do not yet require or have access to the increased download speeds offered by a fibre

connection. Finally, we offer fixed line voice connectivity, including digital voice, to residential and business customers.

TalkTalk

Our strategy
Our purpose is to offer good value and reliable fixed line connectivity across the UK to our direct customers and via our wholesale
partners. Our strategic focus is on strengthening our next generation fibre product offering, providing excellent customer service and
delivering future-proof network operations with a cost base that allows us to continue to be competitive.
In FY24, the Group announced its intention to separate its main operating businesses into three standalone businesses to create
clearer business priorities and greater strategic optionality. The Group subsequently disposed of its direct B2B arm, TalkTalk Business,
and proceeded to operationally separate TalkTalk and PXC. The separation was operationally and commercially effective on 1 March
2024 for the start of the FY25 financial year we are reporting on.
Our Group strategy is twofold:
1. Transition towards the next generation of connectivity products supported by our AltNet strategy and supporting the market's
transition to Full Fibre.
2. Focus on free cash flow generation with a disciplined approach to new acquisition and cost rationalisation.
Clear strategic focus set in PXC
PXC's strategy is to deliver future-proof connectivity through both Openreach and a differentiated AltNet offering with complementary
over-the-top services.
We are uniquely positioned to be the wholesale connectivity provider of choice. Our AltNet partnerships in both the next generation
broadband and Ethernet markets unlock a material economic advantage and provides differentiation in the market.
Our over-the-top services offering has been designed to monetise the underlying value of our network assets — selling and leasing IP
addresses, leasing backhaul access and selling security, cloud and other complementary services to connectivity products.
- This strategy is underpinned by a focus on improving free cash flow and profitability. We are building a simpler PXC by removing legacy

products and technologies and simplifying our operations through responsible cost management.
Customer focused transformation in TalkTalk
This year saw a strategic reset in TalkTalk, delivering the start of a transformation focused on improving customer service. We continue
to focus on disciplined new customer acquisition, acquiring new customers on sustainable economic terms with a focus on customer
retention and the transition to Full Fibre.
We continue to invest in customer satisfaction and digital customer experience, as well as partnering with several leading technology

: providers to support our transition to Full Fibre. During the year, we focused on cost management, as well as strategic initiatives that
unlock profitable customer acquisition.
We identify four pillars to best articulate our mission to become a low-cost, market-disrupting, customer-obsessed business:
* innovative and flexible technology stack;
* empowered customer service;
«value creation powered by data and Al; and
* creation of a lean, skilled and agile workforce.
Refinancing
The Group completed its Refinancing with existing shareholders and lenders on 19 December 2024, with agreement for an extension of
existing facilities. As part of the Refinancing process, existing shareholders contributed £235m in cash as well as certain trading assets,
including the acquired Shell customers base, into the Group. The Group now includes TFP Telecoms 2 Limited which was previously
held outside of the Group but owned by existing shareholders.

Business and financial review

Overview

The business has continued to go through a period of significant change. However, our commitment to fast, reliable and affordable
connectivity has never been stronger and our focus remains on accelerating the shift to Full Fibre. As noted earlier, our two businesses,
PXC and TalkTalk, were operationally and commercially separated on 1 March 2024.

We remain in the midst of market change, with the competitive landscape evolving as our AitNet footprint expands and the copper to
fibre transition gathers momentum. Whilst these changes bring exciting longer term opportunities for PXC, TalkTalk and their
customers, there are nearer term challenges which continue to affect the performance of the businesses such as market competition
and our ability to bring new product offerings to market on a timely basis.

FY25 performance

FY25 results reflect significant change across our key business metrics of EBITDA, customer base size, churn and ARPU, as shown on
pages 6 and 10. The markets in which our businesses operate have remained fiercely competitive. As a value challenger brand, we
have continued to provide a value-based proposition to both Wholesale and Consumer customers and have remained focused on
product reliability and improving customer service.

During the year, the Group has reassessed conclusions made in respect of the co-location space at exchanges not being in scope of
IFRS 16 ‘Leases’. The Group has concluded it has a sufficient level of control and has therefore concluded the assets should be
accounted for as leases. The Group has restated the prior year resulting in an offsetting increase to the right of use assets and lease
liabilities as weil as an increase to EBITDA in the prior year due to the rentals paid no longer being recognised as operating expenses.
Overall, our broadband customer base reduced by c420k. The reduction was predominantly on the TalkTalk side of the business
(c277k) due to our decision to refocus our acquisition strategy and deliberately retrench from unprofitable segments and customers.
Monthly churn increased year on year from 1.9% to 2.0%. Due to the reduction in our customer base, the increased chum and the
ongoing market-wide decline in voice, revenue has declined to £1,412m (FY24 restated: £1,518m). On-net ARPU slightly declined to
£25.46 (FY24: £25.74), due to contractual price rises offset by the reduction of usage and boosts. Cost of sales decreased due to the
reduced customer base size. This has resulted in a decrease in gross profit to £692m (FY24 restated: £746m). Headline operating
costs decreased to £431m (FY24 restated: £510m), mainly due to cost transformation activities. In FY25, the non-headline loss for the
year increased to £327m (FY24 restated: £32m), driven by the goodwill impairment expense and higher strategic project costs,
decrease in other income from lower sales of |Pv4 addresses and higher net finance costs arising from refinancing activity in the year.
In summary, the Group made a statutory loss before tax of £607m (FY24 restated: £276m). These pressures, along with the movement
in payables and investment in capex, have led to an increase in net debt (excluding leases) from £1,429m to £1,813m.

The Group was fully compliant with all its debt covenants during FY25. Going concern is discussed on page 28.

PXC

The PXC business, underpinned by our network, is a truly differentiated aggregator, offering multiple services across connectivity, data,
cloud and security. Our Ethernet base continues to grow albeit slower than expectations, increasing to 76k (FY24: 75k), along with our
mix of 1Gb connections and use of AltNet providers. In FY25, we focused on finalising the development of SoGEA, FTTP and digital
voice to our customers on our strategic system stacks, ready for delivery in early FY26. In addition, we are in the process of onboarding
more Full Fibre AitNets, which increases choice, expands our overall FTTP footprint coverage and furthers our commercial advantage.
We further enhanced the delivery of key value add services in cloud, security and voice solutions, including the launch of an integrated
Zoom product. Our network continues to excel in reliability with an underlying connectivity performance of 99.997%. The ever growing
demand for streaming services for entertainment, gaming or hybrid working have been met.

TalkTalk

The consumer broadband market remains highly competitive. Whilst our disciplined acquisition strategy has resulted in a smaller
customer base, this is a more profitable and cash generative outcome for the business. NPS was +21.7 and we continued to
experience positive movement in customer experience with another year on year improvement in NPS of 6 points, adding to the 9 point
improvement achieved in the prior year. This performance is underpinned in part by investment in customer experience and our
transition to increased customer self-service and contact through more convenient digital channels. Our digital channels are popular
with customers and show a 44 point positive NPS differential (Digital +41.2 NPS vs. Voice NPS -2.6). Managing complaints has been a
key focus in our customer experience strategy, where we have invested in systems, process and the skills of our colleagues.
Acceleration of FTTP

The UK broadband market is part way through its transition to Full Fibre. The proportion of our overall broadband base on FTTP has
grown to 22% (FY24: 15%). FTTP services provide significantly greater speeds and enhanced reliability. This results in fewer faults,
fewer calls to our contact centres and reduced demand for engineer visits. Customers have access to vastly superior products that
reduce our cost to serve, enabling us to deliver competitive prices and retain customers for longer.

Copper to fibre

We began our multi-year copper to fibre programme in FY22, to manage the decommissioning of the legacy copper infrastructure. The
costs to reduce footprint in exchanges and transition customers to fibre infrastructure continue to reflect exceptional transformation
costs, outside the normal course of business. In FY25, the programme has closed 56 co-mingles, has completed the voice services
migration and decommissioned the legacy voice platform. Footprint reduction continues to be managed through a time-bound
programme, with completion expected in FY27. These costs continue to be considered non-headline.

Relationships with key suppliers and customers/AltNet strategy
Openreach remains our biggest supplier with contractual incentives in place to support Full Fibre sales in FTTP enabled areas, further
supporting our copper to fibre transition programme.
Our AltNet strategy differentiates us from our competitors. AltNets create a more competitive connectivity marketplace and improved
value for our customers. 25% of our FTTP base is served by AltNets and we are the scale aggregator of simple, affordable Full Fibre
connectivity. We have partnerships with several AltNets, including a long term wholesale agreement with CityFibre, to which we are
now connecting an increasing proportion of our customers, as well as Community Fibre in London and Freedom Fibre across the UK.
Additional AltNet partners are expected to be onboarded in FY26 to expand our footprint, and we continue to support the development
of alternative networks where it is advantageous to do so.

Regulatory environment

Wholesale Fixed Telecoms Market Review

Ofcom's Wholesale Fixed Telecoms Market Review came into effect in April 2021. This sets regulation for both the Business

Connectivity Market and Wholesale Local Access Market for a five year period until March 2026. The rules are aimed at encouraging

FTTP investment whilst also protecting customers.

Telecoms Access Review

Ofcom's 2026 Telecoms Access Review will set regulation for Openreach broadband and Ethernet products from April 2026 to March

2031. Ofcom has published its first consultation, and the Group will continue to engage closely with Ofcom to influence the outcome.

Openreach Equinox offers ,

In October 2021, Openreach launched a wholesale FTTP offer (called Equinox) that provided discounts in return for meeting certain

FTTP targets. The Group has signed up to the Equinox terms. Openreach has proposed an overlay to Equinox (called Equinox 2),

which provides additional discounts. In May 2023, Ofcom confirmed that it does not intend to prevent Equinox 2 being implemented.

Duct and pole access

Openreach is required to provide wholesale access to its ducts and poles so that its rivals can use these assets to roll out their own

FTTP networks and Ofcom expects that this will be widely used by non-Openreach FTTP builders to make more roll-out viable.

Effective duct and pole access will benefit us by reducing the cost and increasing the speed of the roll-out of non-Openreach FTTP

: networks on which we will rely in certain areas.

FTTP policy

The Government's goal is for at least 85% coverage of ‘gigabit-capable’ broadband by 2025. This term would cover both FTTP

connections and Virgin Media's Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification (DOCSIS) 3.1 network. As part of this goal, the

Government is committed to promoting infrastructure competition and bringing down barriers to network deployment. The key

components of the strategy are:

* reducing build costs through bringing forward legislation to ensure Full Fibre deployment to new builds and simplifying wayleaves so
that telecoms companies have a ‘right to entry’ like other utilities. It is also seeking to streamline street works by issuing a
standardised national framework for operators and local authorities;

* supporting access to passive infrastructure through Openreach delivery of the ducts and poles access improvements and reviewing
the regulations for access to third party infrastructure;

* supporting roll-out in rural areas through an ‘outside-in’ strategy to connect the final 20% using public investment;

* requiring Ofcom to oversee an industry-led switchover from copper to Full Fibre networks; and

* improving access to spectrum and cell sites to enable increased mobile coverage and to enable SG deployment.

Telecommunications security

In 2021, the Telecommunications Security Act was passed to establish an enhanced legislative framework for telecoms security, and to

provide the Government with the powers to take action on the use of high-risk vendors on national security grounds. The Act gives

Ofcom new powers to oversee security provision within telecommunications companies. Ofcom has confirmed its approach to

monitoring compliance and has issued several statutory information requests to assess compliance with the Act's provisions. In

addition, telecoms companies are required to report back to the Department of Science, Innovation and Technology on their progress

on removing Huawei equipment from their network with a number of deadlines between now and 2027.

Introduction

The Company is the ultimate parent company for the Group. During the year, the Group carried out the Refinancing. Up until the
completion of the Refinancing on 19 December 2024, the Board of Directors of TalkTalk Holdings (‘Holdings Board’) oversaw
operational and corporate governance of the Group. Following completion of the Refinancing, operational governance of the Group is
undertaken by the Board of Directors of TalkTalk Telecom Group Limited (‘Group Board’), including all day to day governance and
ordinary course management matters of the Group.

Pursuant to The Companies (Miscellaneous Reporting) Regulations 2018, for the year ended 28 February 2025, the Group Board has
prepared this Corporate Governance Statement under the Wates Principles as our corporate governance framework. The Group Board
has continued to build upon best practice corporate governance, appropriate to a group of our size, recognising that robust corporate
governance practices underpin effective management of the Group, which is in the best interests of alt our stakeholders. Details on how
we have applied the six Wates Principles for our corporate governance arrangements for the period are set out below.

Our purpose is to offer affordable and reliable fixed line connectivity across the UK to our direct customers and via our wholesale
partners. Our strategic focus is on strengthening our next generation fibre product offering, providing excellent customer service, and

Principle 4 — Opportunity and risk

Strategic opportunities were highlighted and assessed for Board approval as they arose, with shorter term opportunities to improve
performance reviewed during the normal course of business.

The Boards viewed management of risk as integral to good business practice. The Group had established an ongoing risk management
programme to identify, assess and mitigate business, financial, operational and compliance risks. The programme was designed to
support management's decision making and to improve the reliability of business performance. The risk management process operated
throughout the Group, being applied equally to the main businesses and corporate functions.

The nature of risks identified and assessed was wide-ranging, covering risks arising from the regulatory environment, strategy,
financial, counterparties and organisational change associated with major projects. Action plans and controls to mitigate identified risks
were put in place where possible and, if considered appropriate by the Boards, took account of costs and benefits. Reports were
provided to the Directors at relevant Board meetings setting out key risks, changes in the status of the key risks and updates on
mitigation.

The Directors had overall responsibility for the Group's systems of internal controls and for reviewing their effectiveness. The Holdings
Board delegated to the leadership teams the responsibility for designing, operating and monitoring these systems. The systems were
based on a process of identifying, evaluating and managing key risks and included the risk management processes set out above and
channels to enable employees to raise concems about possible irregularities in financial reporting and other issues, and associated
processes for those matters to be investigated. Further details are contained in the Strategic Report on pages 14 to 18.

The systems of internal control were supported by the respective internal audit and risk functions. Any significant risks identified in the
period were given appropriate priority.

The systems of Internal control were designed to manage, rather than eliminate, the risk of failure to achieve business objectives. They
could only provide reasonable and not absolute assurance against material errors, losses, fraud or breaches of law and regulations.

across the businesses.

Going concern

The Directors are required to satisfy themselves that it is reasonable for them to conclude that it is appropriate to prepare the Group

and Parent Company financial statements on a going concern basis. This going concern assessment has considered the Group's

current committed financing facilities and liquidity management over the going concem period (12 months from the date of signing
these financial statements).

The key factors considered by the Directors in making the assessment of going concern are:

+ The Group's budget and business plan for the going concern assessment period through to July 2026.

* The Group's requirements and its ability to comply with certain minimum liquidity covenants under the terms of its financing
agreements, which are required to be measured throughout the going concern assessment period.

* Execution of planned initiatives to enhance the Group's liquidity during the going concern assessment period, including the
expected sale of certain non-core assets.

* Continued constructive engagement with key suppliers to the Group regarding supplier terms and the agreement of payment
schedules.

* Continued operating and capital cost reductions across the Group's businesses in line with programmes that have been under
implementation over the last twelve months.

* The continued support of the Group’s key financial stakeholders, including its shareholders and secured creditors, with regard to
optimising the Group's liquidity profile. This includes engagement of financial stakeholders in funding discussions linked to the
Groups strategic initiatives.

In considering the above factors in making their going concem assessment, the Directors have also considered the following key risks:

* The potential for breach of minimum liquidity covenants or a liquidity shortfall in the event that necessary supplier terms or payment
schedules cannot be agreed.

* The Group's ability to fund the first cash interest payment under its first lien financing facilities due in May 2026, in the absence of
sufficient liquidity enhancing initiatives being concluded in advance and if the above risks to liquidity materialise.

+ The impact on liquidity of the Group incurring higher costs by failing to reduce costs as rapidly as it plans.

+ Unfavourable trading performance or cash flows compared to budget given the inherent uncertainty with respect to forecast financial
information, the highly competitive nature of the industry and the uncertain economic environment.

The Directors have further given due consideration to the mitigating actions that could be undertaken to counteract the effects of the

above risks, including:

«Near term reductions in discretionary spend including but not limited to capital expenditure and/or spend on new customer
acquisition.

+ Assessing further opportunities for non-core asset disposals.

* Continued engagement with the Group’s secured lenders regarding covenants under the Group's financing agreements where
necessary to enhance liquidity.

+ Engagement with the Group's financial stakeholders, including its shareholders and lenders, regarding medium term working capital
support for the Group which would allow a normalisation of current extended payment terms.

Should any of the above risks materialise, this could result in a breach of the Group's minimum liquidity covenant and / or liquidity being

under significant pressure since there is limited headroom available. In these circumstances, the Group would require a waiver of its

minimum liquidity covenant as well as potentially additional funding, which is currently being discussed but is not currently committed.

The Directors therefore acknowledge that these risks represent a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt upon the Group's

ability to continue as a going concer.

Having assessed the mitigating actions available and as a result of continued constructive engagement with the Group's financial

stakeholders regarding their ongoing support for the Group, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Group will have

sufficient resources to continue its operations for the going concem assessment period, and accordingly they continue to adopt the
going concern basis in preparing these financial statements. Further, in preparing the financial statement, they do not include any
adjustments that would be required to be made if they were prepared on a basis other than going concern.
