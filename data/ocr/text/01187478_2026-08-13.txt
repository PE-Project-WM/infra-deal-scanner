STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

Introduction
The directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the Company is that of the provision of a comprehensive range of information -
management services allowing secure access to hard copy files in electronic format.
Business review including financial key performance indicators
2025 was a good year with a healthy profit achieved despite challenging market conditions. Whilst not as
significant as the previous two years, the minimum wage rise (c6%) was again a major factor in our
increased cost base but as with the previous year, improved pricing on new contracts and renewed
business helped to offset this.
The closure of the Bradford office in October 2025 will improve our net profit once the redundancy costs
are absorbed and going forward will save over £120K in 2026.
Additional changes in the senior management will further reduce the cost base in 2026 as our MD and HR
Directors both reduce working weeks whilst offering the Company the benefit of flexibility in responding to
any challenging circumstances. This will save approximately £125k per annum once fully implemented.
Due: to the unpredictable flow of work throughout the year the Sales figures have remained on a par to
2024 with a 1% reduction in value. The increased cost of employment and the general increase in costs
have reduced the Net profitability (profit before tax) by 45% £719k to £399k. As a result action has been
taken to streamline the operations with the unfortunate closure of Bradford and ongoing reviews of agency
staffing to reduce overheads going forward. .
The NHS is still a major source of revenue and as a key supplier we have continued to provide
uninterrupted services to our hospital client base. Volumes of work remain a key area to effect with any
certainty due to the pressures that our clients have in ensuring the supply is available in line with agreed
timeframes. This impacts productivity and whilst a concern and difficult to manage, overall performance
has not been significantly affected.

Principal risks and uncertainties
There are several elements of uncertainty. As always the actual flow of work into our operation which is
driven by the clients' abilities to supply raw documentation is often a challenge. The availability of trained
labours allows us to ramp up when required to meet the demands of new projects without negatively
impacting the service level agreements of existing ones. More recently the cost of employment has been
adversely affected by Government taxation and minimum wage legislation the full impact showing an
increase in costs of circa 6%.
The Company is reliant upon a skilled and flexible workforce. We have a core of employees fully trained in
all aspects of the processes and in addition it is essential to have access to a pool of temporary staff who
can be quickly trained into effective operators when capacity needs to be increased. The emphasis on
productivity is always under scrutiny. The application of Al within the reporting mechanism is helping to
improve the speed at which any such improvements can be achieved and maintained.
Investment in IT and cyber security is ongoing utilising systems for network and system monitoring The IT
infrastructure is backed up within mirror imaged systems to minimise any loss of output which would
otherwise need to be redone. This is backed up by stringent disposal and destruction methodology prior to
releasing any original documents for destruction.
Other performance indicators
Al usage and benefits: Improve speed and quality across analysis, coding support, document drafting,
planning, compliance and tender work, delivering faster turnaround times, improved consistency and 25-
50% efficiency gains in development and design activities.
Tools and systems: Regular use of ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot alongside a growing set of in-house tools
(15+ developed since Summer 2025) supporting end-to-end operational workflows from timesheets
through production, QC, planning, and reporting.
Maturity and next steps: Copilot is live with key managers, with further training planned for advanced use
cases and impact measurement ongoing.
Monthly flash results and prompt accurate management accounts are used to confirm or otherwise the
production figures. These together with the document throughput per man hour gives management a clear
indication of profitability ahead of formal figures.
Weekly reviews of production and sales/marketing activities keep the business focused on short-medium
and long-term progress. We have a mid to long term goal of steady returns 7.5% to 10% being the norm,
plus a recovery of any Project Specific Capital Expenditure which we strive to achieve.

Financial risk management objectives
The principal risks facing the business are similar for all.companies in the market in which we operate. The
principal financial risks are credit risk and liquidity risk.
Credit risk
The Company's principal financial assets are bank balances and cash, trade and other receivables. Credit
risk arises from the possibility of customers and counterparties falling to meet their obligations to the
Company. However, the public sector nature of our client base reduces this to a very low level. The
amounts presented in the Balance Sheet are net of allowances for doubtful receivables. An allowance for
impairment is made where there is an identified loss event which, based on previous experience, is
evidence of a reduction in the recoverability of the cash flows.
The Company has no significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread over a large number of
counterparties and customers.
Liquidity risk
During the year, the Company's policy was to ensure continuity of liquidity by building up cash reserves
within the Company.

2.4 Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to
the Company and the turnover can be reliably measured. Turnover is measured as the fair value
of the consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and
other sales taxes. The following criteria must also be met before turnover is recognised:

, Rendering of services
Turnover from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are
provided in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all of the following
conditions are satisfied:
° the amount of turnover can be measured reliably;
e it is probable that the Company will receive the consideration due under the contract;
. the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be
measured reliably; and

. the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.

2.5 Operating leases: the Company as lessee
Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the

Finance costs are charged to profit or loss over the term of the debt using the effective interest
method so that the amount charged is at a constant rate on the carrying amount. Issue costs are
initially recognised as a reduction in the proceeds of the associated capital instrument.

2.11 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The Company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution
plan is a pension plan under which the Company pays fixed contributions into a separate entity.
Once the contributions have been paid the Company has no further payment obligations. .
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not
paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the Balance Sheet. The assets of the plan are held
separately from the Company in independently administered funds.
2.12 Financial instruments

Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised in the Balance Sheet when the Company
becomes a party to the contractual provisions of the instrument.
Trade and other debtors and creditors are classified as basic financial instruments and
measured on initial recognition at transaction price. Debtors and creditors are subsequently
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method. A provision is established
when there is objective evidence that the Company will not be able to collect all amounts due.
Cash and cash equivalents are classified as basic financial instruments and comprise cash in
hand and at bank, short-term bank deposits with an original maturity of three months or less and
bank overdrafts which are an integral part of the Company’s cash management.
Financial liabilities and equity instruments issued by the Company are classified in accordance
with the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into and the definitions of a
financial liability and an equity instrument. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a
residual interest in the assets of the Company after deducting all of its liabilities. Equity
instruments issued by the Company are recorded at the proceeds received, net of direct issue
costs.
Interest bearing bank loans, overdrafts and other loans which meet the criteria to be classified

2.13 Current and deferred taxation :

The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in profit or

loss except that a charge attributable to an item of income and expense recognised as other

comprehensive income or to an item recognised directly in equity is also recognised in other
comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.

The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been

enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date in the countries where the Company

operates and generates income.

Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated

but not reversed by the balance sheet date, except that:

e The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they
will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable
profits; and

e Any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated
tax allowances have been met.

Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect

of business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair

values of assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences
between the fair values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax.

enacted by the balance sheet date.
