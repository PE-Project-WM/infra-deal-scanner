PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES

The Company is wholly owned by Redwood Technologies Group Ltd (Group).

The principal activities continue to be the provision of cloud CX (Customer Experience) and CCaaS (Contact

Centre as a Service) solutions, which the Group and Company delivers via its storm® CPaaS

(Communications Platform as a Service) environment. The four key product pillars within Group’s CX

portfolio are as follows;

- Omni-automation services, which allow tasks to be automated using technology such as Al (artificial
intelligence). These are delivered using Group's brain® Al orchestration software, which processes huge
amounts of data, automating work previously performed by humans, via brain Machine Agents”, or as Al
assistants supporting people in their work;

- Omni-channel services, which enable customers to manage any form of communication, such as video,
social media, voice, email, chat from a single interface;

- Omni-data services, which enable different systems of information such as CRM (customer relationship
management) and specialised systems of record in industries such as finance, logistics, retail, utilities and
public sector to be integrated into a single environment and turn them into ‘systems of action’;

- Omni-customer services, which enable information from people (‘external’ and ‘internal’ customers) and
loT (Internet of Things) devices (known as ‘digital customers’) to be processed into tasks.

In connection with the delivery and ongoing operation of storm and brain services, Group also provides

consultancy, development, programme management, testing, training, operational and support services.

The Group and Company’s target customer base is large enterprises and public sector organisations who

need mission-critical, feature-rich, highly-scalable, highly-available, highly-flexible CX and Al solutions.

Customers include central and regional government bodies, financial services institutions (including insurance

and banking), retailers, travel organisations, utilities providers, CSPs (communications service providers),

business process outsourcers (BPOs) and Sls (System Integrators). Services are run in tens of countries and

supported from regional offices in the Americas, Asia Pac and Europe (including the UK).
~ STRATEGIC REVIEW .

Group defines its strategy over three time periods:

- Short term - 12 months, planned and budgeted in great detail and with high confidence

- Mid-term - 3-5 years, planned with medium confidence in market trends

- Long-term - 15-20 years, planned with a focus on key top-level themes

The first company in Group, Redwood Technologies Ltd, was formed in 1993 with the long-term objective of

leveraging the emerging internet and open-standards technology. Content Guru was founded in 2005 with

. the long-term objective of leveraging the cloud. Over the past 5 years Group has put in place the foundations
for its third long-term plan, which is to leverage the growth opportunity in CX (customer experience), with Al

{artificial intelligence) being an increasingly important component of this.

In pursuit of this long-term objective, over the past 4-5 years, Group significantly boosted its engineering

capabilities, nearly doubling its headcount, whilst also using Al tools to significantly increase productivity.

During that timeframe it still managed to maintain double-digit growth and profitability. Increased team

performance, both through experience gained and business process automation, meant that hiring could be

slowed in 2025 and this will continue to be the case in 2026, resulting in further profitability gains.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
Principal risks and uncertainties are the same for the Company as for Group
Group continuously tracks risk through its risk-management registers and management strategy reviews.
Group's risk management does not eliminate risk; it identifies risk and devises strategies for mitigating and
managing.
Principal risks and uncertainties raised in last year’s reports were:

- Discounting from CCaaS competitors unable to command value with a strong service offering,
impacting market prices and potentially impacting Group’s profitability

-  Business-as-usual technology risks such as security, data protection, maintaining reputational
standing, keeping up with advances in technology

- Cost inflation

- Foreign exchange fluctuations (e.g. euro, US dollar, Japanese yen vs British pound)

Brexit has been removed from the list as a principal risk but will continue to be monitored as a risk.
Covering each item briefly in turn:

- Discounting from CCaaS competitors continues to be a risk. Most of Group's major competitors have
reduced their headcount as they pivot from achieving growth to achieving profitability. Group has
won a number of customers who have moved from cheaper competitors and have experienced that
‘you get what you pay for’.

-  Business-as-usual technology risks, such as security and data protection, continue to command the
necessary high levels of attention from all levels of Group. Major investment continues in Group’s
cloud infrastructure to ensure the business has best-in-class protection for itself and its customers.

-  Incommon with many other businesses, Group has experienced higher cost inflation during recent
years including higher energy and wage costs. Group continues to invest in automation of its
processes, e.g. using Al development tools, to ensure it can scale without a directly-proportionate
increase in headcount. In addition, Group will continue to near-shore some of its hiring to maximise
cost advantage and resource availability within other labour markets.

mo - Foreign exchange rate fluctuations continue to be one of the principal risks for Group and one which
Group continues to monitor and ensure that it has sufficient controls to mitigate. Group generates
, euro, US dollar, Japanese yen and British pound revenues, so is not over-exposed to a single
. currency.
Overall Group’s directors continue to feel confident about the coming years but are cautious about the
overall global economic outlook. Group's rolling three-year (mid-term) business plan (‘the Business Plan’)
forecasts strong growth for 2026-28. The Business Plan provides strategic planning, including product
development, infrastructure investment, sales and marketing activities and financial requirements to support
those activities. The Business Plan evaluates risks and uncertainties alongside new opportunities, and factors
these into its planning.
: Group's credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade debtors, with this risk mitigated by upfront payments .
for projects, credit limits set by reference to credit rating agencies and close monitoring of each customer's
. account. Group’s services are furthermore considered mission-critical by most customers. Liquidity risk is
minimised by Group’s policy of maintaining cash surpluses and minimising debt.

FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT OBJECTIVES AND POLICIES
, The Company’s risk management objectives and policies are the same as for Group.

Group's activities expose it to a number of financial risks including in relation to competition, technological
development, price, credit, foreign currency and liquidity risk. Group’s primary goal is to continue a policy of
sustainable and controlled growth allied to an acceptable level of profitability. Group believes these

"objectives can best be met by securing long-term relationships with customers who recognise the high value
added nature of Group's products and services.

. Competitive risk
Market demand for Group's products continues to be strong, with services such as cloud contact centre
driven by the current demand for migration from legacy, on-premise services and for automation through Al.
Group tracks competitive threats through its own research and via a number of analyst companies. Group
will continue to ensure its products and services are best-in-class and constantly monitor its pricing position.
Technological risk
Group carries out a significant amount of research and development in the technological development and
some of this research effort may take several years to deliver a return on investment or may fail to deliver a
return. The level of risk is always kept at a financially acceptable level and is further mitigated by ensuring
that research projects are chosen according to mid to long-term business objectives. Some experimental
products have no expected return on investment and most commercial products are typically launched on a
‘fast follower’ basis to further ensure their commercial success.
Foreign currency risk
Group’s activities expose it primarily to the financial risks of changes in foreign currency exchange rates and
: interest rates. It continues to balance its cost and revenues in non-sterling currencies to minimise the effect :
of currency variations. Group does not use foreign exchange forward contracts to hedge the profit element
derived from non-sterling currencies.
Credit risk
Group's principal financial assets are bank balances and trade receivables. Bank balances are held only with
major banks and bad debt risk is minimised by Group's credit management procedures and the fact that its
services are often critical to its clients, which encourages timely payments.

Company as a lessee
The Company applies a single recognition and measurement approach for all leases, except for certain short
term leases and leases of low-value assets. The Company recognises lease liabilities to make lease payments
and right-of-use assets representing the right to use the underlying assets.
(i) Right-of-use assets
The Company recognises right-of-use assets at the commencement date of the lease (the date that the
underlying asset is available for use). Right-of-use assets are measured at cost, less any accumulated
depreciation and impairment losses, and adjusted for any remeasurement of lease liabilities. The cost of right-
of-use assets includes the amount of lease liabilities recognised, initial direct costs incurred, and lease
payments made at or before the commencement date less any lease incentives received. Right-of use assets ,
are depreciated on a straight-line basis over the lease term (or the estimated useful life in the event of this
being shorter).
(ii) Lease liabilities

At the commencement date of the lease, the Company recognises lease liabilities measured at the present
value of lease payments to be made over the lease term. The lease payments include fixed payments less any
lease incentives receivable, variable lease payments that depend on an index or a rate, and amounts expected
to be paid under residual value guarantees.
In calculating the present value of lease payments, the Company uses either the incremental borrowing rate
or obtainable borrowing rate, on a lease-by-lease basis, at the lease commencement date because the
interest rate implicit in the lease is not readily determinable. After the commencement date, the amount of
lease liabilities is increased to reflect the accretion of interest and reduced for the lease payments made. In .
addition, the carrying amount of lease liabilities is remeasured if there is a modification, a change in the lease
term, a change in the lease payments (e.g., changes to future payments resulting from a change in an index or
rate used to determine such lease payments) or a change in the assessment of an option to purchase the
underlying asset.
The Company’s lease liabilities are included in creditors: amounts falling due within one year and creditors:
amounts falling due after more than one year (see Note 11 and Note 12).

Identification of performance obligations within customer contracts
Customer contracts may include a combination of recurring platform access, implementation and :
configuration services, onboarding activities, training, support services, usage-based services and, in some
cases, hardware. Management applies judgement in assessing the promises in each contract to determine
which goods or services represent distinct performance obligations.
In particular, management considers whether implementation and configuration activities transfer a distinct

. service to the customer or instead represent activities required to fulfil the contract and enable provision of
the hosted platform service. The identification of distinct performance obligations determines whether
revenue is recognised at a point in time or over time and therefore affects the timing of revenue recognition.
Certain customer contracts include implementation, activation or onboarding activities performed at or
shortly after contract inception, such as configuring the platform environment and establishing user access.
Management applies judgement in determining whether these activities represent distinct services .
transferred to the customer.
Where these activities represent a distinct service and control transfers to the customer when the work is .
completed, the associated revenue is recognised at that point in time. In making this assessment,
management considers whether the customer can benefit from the service on its own or together with other
readily available resources and whether the service is separately identifiable from the ongoing hosted
platform services.
