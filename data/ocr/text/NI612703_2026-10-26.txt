Strategic report
For the year ended 31 March 2026
Introduction
The directors have pleasure in presenting the strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2026.

Principal Activity and Strategy
Fibrus’ principal activity is the deployment of new full fibre broadband infrastructure to homes and businesses
across Northern Ireland and parts of the North of England.
Our strategy is to deploy our full fibre infrastructure to regional and rural areas that have been underserved by
legacy networks. Fibrus has focused on regional towns and villages with lower fibre coverage. In addition, in both
Northern Ireland and Cumbtia, Fibrus has won government funding to partly finance the deployment of network to
rural premises which would not otherwise be commercially viable, enabling our network to reach deeper into rural
areas.
We anticipate that the majority of homes and businesses in the areas that we operate will eventually have access to
other fibre networks in the future, so we focus on being first to get to market and proactively acquire the customers
based on better value and better service.
Operating Review
Fibrus has continued to establish itself as one of the best performing full fibre operators in the UK and Ireland due
to a track record of delivering on build and on customer take-up.
At a time when a number of its peers are struggling, Fibrus continues to deliver on its business plan, evidenced by
us reporting our first full year of EBITDA profitability.
Build programme delivery
During the year we were awarded further UK government contract (“NI Gigabit”) to provide service to around
10,000 additional, hard to reach premises in Northern Ireland. This followed on from the delivery of the Stratum’
programme which brought full fibre connectivity to 81,148 homes across Northern Ireland. This large scale public
infrastructure project was delivered on time and on budget and has transformed access high speed connectivity in
rural areas and delivered significant economic benefits to the local economy. . .
The addition of NI Gigabit will further boost Northern Irelands fibre coverage to 95%, helping to futureproof its
workforce by enabling access to remote and hybrid job opportunities across the UK and beyond.
In Cumbria we continued to deliver our expanded BDUK funded Gigabit on time and on budget. By the end of
the year we had delivered 32,400 premises, significantly expanding our rural footprint during the year, and already
ahead of the scope of the initial contract.
The remaining 20,000 premises will largely be delivered over the next 18 months. Once completed, alongside other

* commercial expansion plans, 99% of homes and businesses in Cumbria will have access to next-generation
broadband.
For a full strategic review of the wider group please refer to Fibrus Ltd.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of the business and the execution of the Company’s strategy is subject to a number of risks and .
uncertainties. The key risks and uncertainties affecting the Company is considered to relate to competition with
other broadband providers, customer take-up, churn and technological advance. The key risks, and the activities in
place to manage such risks, are monitored on a regular basis.
Economic risk
The Company has interest bearing liabilities with credit institutions and investors, and therefore movement in
interest rates or inflation may impact these liabilities. Interest is accounted for through the profit and loss account.
Competition risk
The directors of the Company manage competition risk through close attention to customer service levels and
providing an effective service.
Financial risk
All key financial figures are monitored on an ongoing basis. The Company continues to have access to funding from
investors to continue its investment in the roll out of its network.
Credit risk
The Company is exposed to its customers defaulting on the payment of their debts. The Company mitigates this
risk through taking deposits on orders and practices robust credit control in collecting this debt.
Liquidity risk
The Company has obtained financing from its investors and lenders in order to meet its funding requirements. This
mitigates the risk to the Company.
Interest rate risk
The Company has interest bearing liabilities. Interest bearing liabilities relate to bank loans, which pay interest at
variable rates. The Company actively manages its interest rate risk by entering into interest rate swaps to help
mitigate interest rate rises.

Directors’ statement of compliance with duty to promote the success of the Company
The Board acknowledges its responsibility under section 172(1) of the Companies Act 2006 and below sets out the
key processes and considerations that demonstrate how the Directors promote the success of the Company.

, The below statement sets out the requirements of the Act, section 172(1), and note how the Directors’ discharge
their duties.

The Board meets at least quarterly with papers circulated in advance to allow the Directors to fully understand the
performance and position of the Company, alongside matters arising for decision. Each decision that is made by the
Directors is supported by papers which analyse the possible outcomes so that an educated decision can be made
based upon the likely impact on the Company, so a decision can be made which best promotes the success of the
Company and considers the impact on the wider stakeholder group. :

Factors (a) to (f) below, are all taken into account during the decision making process.

(a) The likely consequences of any decision in the long term

Supporting each decision, the Board are given access to management papers which will set out the potential
outcome of decisions. The papers include diligence on the financial impact via forecasts, as well as non-financial
factors and how the decision fits with the strategy of the Company. The Company has a 3-year plan, which is a
financial plan supported by a Growth Optimisation Plan, which is reviewed regularly to benchmark performance
and achievements against.

(b) The interests of the Company's employees

The Directors actively consider the interest of employees in all major decisions. People is a regular agenda item at
Board meetings and employee satisfaction and other employee related matters are discussed.

(c) The need to foster the Company's business relationships with suppliers, customers and others

The Directors have identified the stakeholders of the Company and review regularly to ensure adequate
communication and engagement is ongoing with each group. One of the Company's stated Strategic Priorities is to
be customer centric and this is harboured through quality account management and customer service processes.

; Whilst the majority of our cost base is people, we maintain strong working relationships with our suppliers and ~
continually monitor supplier payment days. For key suppliers we perform diligence around their working practices
and ethics as well as their financial stability and viability.

(4) The impact of the Company's operations on the community and environment

The Company takes its responsibility within the community and wider environment seriously and acknowledges that

more can be done. The Company has a relatively low carbon footprint, but acknowledges improvements can always

be made and the Directors encourage video calling rather than air/road travel. The Company also looks to engage

local communities through charity work and donations, where appropriate.

(¢) The desirability of the Company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct

The Directors and the Company are committed to high standards of business conduct and governance. Where there

is a need to seek advice on particular issues, the Board will seek advice from its lawyers and nominated advisors to

ensure the consideration of business conduct, and its reputation is maintained.

Engagement with suppliers
The Company recognises that the prompt settlement of amounts due to suppliers is fundamental to maintaining a
resilient and sustainable supply chain, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). The Board is

invoice approval and payment processes, ensuring that suppliers are paid in accordance with agreed contractual
terms. The Company continually monitors its payment performance and seeks to promote fair, transparent and
responsible payment practices, thereby supporting the long-term sustainability of its suppliers and fostering strong
commercial relationships.
Disabled employees
The Company gives full consideration to applications from all disabled persons where the requirements of the job
can be adequately fulfilled by a disabled person. Where existing employees become disabled, it is the Company's
policy wherever practicable to provide continuing employment under normal terms and conditions and to provide
training and career development and promotion to disabled employees wherever appropriate.
Human resources
The Company's most important resource is its people; their knowledge and experience is crucial to meeting
customer requirements. Retention of key staff is critical and the Company have invested increasingly in employment

2.2 Going concern
The Company has incurred a loss for the financial year of £30,587,067 (2025: £45,320,952) and had net
liabilities of £119,899,773 (2025: net liabilities £89,312,706) at the balance sheet date. The Company is
primarily focused on its rapidly growing broadband business for its future prosperity and is actively
engaged in accelerating the roll out across Northern Ireland. This will result in a significant increase in
turnover and EBITDA in future years. During the year, the Company continued to use the funds from
its primary investor in the form of interest bearing and interest free loans and the Company has access
to significant additional funding from investors and credit institutions to fund the continued roll out and
expansion of the network.
For this reason, the financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis, which presumes
the realisation of assets and liabilities in the normal course of business. The year under review has seen
significant growth as the Company progresses in its goal to bring meaningful connectivity to rural and
regional communities. The Company previously was able to secure £220m with an additional £100m
secured in August 2024 from a consortium of bank to fund the continued expansion of the network and
has drawndown £280.3m as of year ended March 31, 2026. In November 2020, the Company was
successful in bidding for Project Stratum under which it will receive £165m in order to bring
connectivity to 76,000 premises across Northern Ireland and, in January 2022, an additional £32m was
awarded to bring a further 8,500 harder to reach premises into scope of the project. In December 2025,
the Company was successful in bidding for Project Gigabit under which it will receive £34.6m to bring
connectivity to 9,333 additional homes in hard-to-reach communities. By 31 March 2026, the Company
had passed over 334,000 premises across its portfolio of projects. The Company is in a period of rapid
growth, investing in network expansion and customer acquisition, as such it‘made an operating loss of
£5.5m for the year while it invested over £25.9m in building its hyperfast broadband network.

2.3 Business combinations
Acquisitions of subsidiaries and businesses are accounted for using the purchase method. The cost of
the business combination is measured at the aggregate of the fair values (at the date of exchange) of

: assets given, liabilities incurred or assumed, and equity instruments issued by the Company in exchange _

for control of the acquiree plus costs directly attributable to the business combination.
Any excess of the cost of the business combination over the acquirer's interest in the net fair value of
the identifiable assets and liabilities is recognised as goodwill. If the net fair value of the identifiable
assets and liabilities exceeds the cost of the business combination the excess is recognised separately on
the face of the consolidated balance sheet immediately below goodwill.

2.5 Foreign currency translation
Functional and presentation currency
The Company's functional and presentational currency is GBP.
Transactions and balances
Foreign currency transactions ate translated into the functional currency using the spot exchange rates at
the dates of the transactions.
At each period end foreign currency monetary items are translated using the closing rate. Non-monetary
items measured at historical cost are translated using the exchange rate at the date of the transaction and
non-monetary items measured at fair value are measured using the exchange rate when fair value was
determined.
Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the translation
at period-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are
recognised in profit or loss except when deferred in other comprehensive income as qualifying cash
flow hedges.
Foreign exchange gains and losses that relate to borrowings and cash and cash equivalents are presented
in the Statement of comprehensive income within 'finance income or costs’. All other foreign exchange
gains and losses are presented in profit or loss within ‘other operating income’.

. The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will be
