STRATEGIC REPORT FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024

Pursuant to the Companies Act 2006 (Strategic Report and Directors' Report) Regulations 2013, Avanti Space 3 Limited (the
“Company") and the Directors present a Strategic Report.

Principal Activities

The Company is part of the overall Avanti Group (the "Group", defined as Plate Newco 3 Limited and its subsidiaries) which has a
strategy of commercially exploiting its space assets, namely its spectrum rights, satellites, intellectual property and ground station assets.
The principal activity of the Company during the year was the provision of communication services. The Company generates revenue
from the commercial exploitation of the HYLAS 3 satellite.

Financial Review

The Company’s primary assct is the ITYLAS 3 communications payload. ITYLAS 3 is a highly flexible, high-throughput Ka-band
payload featuring a cluster of eight stecrable spot beams and more than 4 GHz of capacity operating across civil and military Ka-band.
Its ability to dynamically reposition its user beams and its gateway beam coverage makes it particularly well suited to sovereign satellite
communications, government networks, defence applications, and critical infrastructure connectivity.

During the year, the opportunity arose for the Company to acquire the EDRS-C satellite. The HYLAS 3 payload is on-board the
EDRS-C satellite and the acquisition of that satellite would provide the Company the ability to control the satellite on which this
payload is located.

In December 2024, the Company entered into a binding Asset Purchase Agreement with Airbus Defence & Space to purchase the
EDRS-C satellite. That transaction subsequently successfully completed following the completion of certain regulatory steps.

‘Ihe Company’s strategy following the successful completion of this transaction has been to offer to potential customers the ability to
redeploy HYLAS 3 to sovereign satellite communications, government networks, defence applications, and critical infrastructure
requirements. This is Avanti’s |‘lex-Geo Model.

“The Company believes there is growing demand for sovercign satellite connectivity architectures that provide end-to-end control
across both the space and ground segments. This includes satellites that are operated and controlled within national boundaries,
together with gateways and network operations centres managed within the same sovereign framework. Such architectures enhance
security, resilience, and operational independence, supporting critical national infrastructure, government communications, and military
operations. The Directors believe that HYLAS 3 is particularly well suited to deliver against these customer requirements.

In the second quarter of 2025, HYLAS 3 was deployed on its first Flex-Geo lease in the Asia-Pacific region to support inflight
connectivity for a major airline. In parallel, the Company continued to market the satellite to support sovereign, private, and dedicated
network solutions and generated significant interest from several parties.
This pipeline of opportunities led to the Company signing an Asset Purchase Agreement for the sale of the EDRS-C satellite and
HYLAS 3 payload on 28 April 2026 with Sky Perfect JSAT Corporation. The transaction is subject to certain regulatory closing steps
and is expected to complete in carly 2027.
2024 Financial Results
Revenue in the year decreased by $642k to $1,277k due to a decrease in the provision of bandwidth services to group entities. Cost of
sales increased slightly by $41k to $1,709k. .
EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation) has declined from positive $251k to negative $432k. The
Company made a loss before tax in the year of $986k compared with a Joss before tax of $324k in the previous year, representing an
increase in the loss before tax of $662k.
The 2025 financial year (unaudited) has delivered strong growth in financial performance, with revenues and profitability improving
significantly compared to the prior year. This improvement has been driven primarily by the successful deployment of HYLAS 3 in the
Asia-Pacific region, where the satellite commenced a customer lease agreement to provide inflight connectivity services for a major
international airline. The contract represented the first commercial deployment under the Group's Flex-Geo strategy and demonstrates
the value of HYLAS 3's flexible coverage capabilities in addressing customer requirements in high-growth markets.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties
The Company faces a number of risks and uncertainties that may adversely affect its business, operations, liquidity, financial position
. and future performance.

The principal business risks and uncertainties faced by the Company relate to competition from similar operators in the industry. The
Company also faces a number of ongoing operational risks including credit and foreign exchange risk.
To the extent that these risks are under managements control, the directors aim to manage these risks and uncertainties going forwards
in order to maintain and improve on the current level of performance.
Liquidity risk
The Company finances its business through operating cash flow. The Company is also dependent on Plate Newco 3 Limited, through
Plate Bidco 4 Limited, providing additional financial support to the Group if necessary. See the Basis of Preparation in Note 1 for
further details.
Foreign exchange risk
‘The Company has foreign currency transactions and is exposed to foreign exchange risk arising from various currency exposures,
primarily with respect to the pound Sterling and the Euro. In order to mitigate the foreign currency risk, the Group to which Avanti
Space 3 Limited belongs monitors the level at which natural hedges occur and continually reviews the need to enter into forward
contracts in order to mitigate any material forecast exposure. The Company's reported results of operations and financial condition are
affected by exchange rate fluctuations due to both transaction and translation risks.
Interest rate risk
The Company’s main source of loan finance is interest free intercompany loans from other entities in the Group. Cash and deposits
earn interest at fixed rates based on banks’ short-term treasury deposit rates. Short-term trade and other receivables are interest free. As
a result, interest rate risk is not currently a significant consideration.
Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk of financial loss arising from a counterpatty’s inability to repay of service debt in accordance with contractual
terms. Credit risk includes the direct risk of default and the risk of deterioration of creditworthiness. The Company primarily generates
its revenue by selling capacity on its satellite to other companies within the Group in order to service third party customer contracts
entered into by those entities. Those entities assess the credit quality of their major customers before trading commences, taking into
account customers’ financial position, past experience and other factors.

Going concern
The Company is a member of Plate Newco 3 Limited Group (the Group) and is reliant on Plate Newco 3 Limited for funding
and not seeking repayment of the amounts due at year end. In reaching their assessment, the Directors have also considered the
fact that in addition to the inter-company liability that is repayable on demand, the Avanti business manages its cash flows at a
Group level. Therefore, the Group, Plate Newco 3 Limited and each of the other subsidiary entities are reliant upon each other
for funding being made available as required to be able to meet liabilities as they fall due.
On 2 April 2024 the holders of the Super Senior Facility (“SSF”) exercised a call option over the equity shares in the Group. At
the date of signing these financial statements, the Company’s ultimate parent undertaking, Plate New Topco Limited, was 100%
owned and controlled by JTPS Investment Partners LLC (“TIPS”), the holder of the SSF. As a result, 100% of the Group’s
secured financial debt is owned and controlled by HPS.
It is the Directors’ belief that, given the holder of the SSF is also the sole equity owner of the Group, it is unlikely the SSF will be
called in the short to medium term. In the unlikely event the debt is called, the Group would have insufficient resources to repay
the SSF at short notice, and would need to immediately seek alternative sources of financing. In that unlikely scenario, there can
be no certainty of altcrnative sourccs of financing being found, and this represents a matcrial uncertainty over the Group’s ability
to continue as a going concern.
In performing the going concern assessment, the Directors have prepared cash flow forecasts for the Group, covering a period to
July 2027.
The Group currently meets its day to day working capital requirements from operating cash flow, and as at the date of signing
these financial statements has sufficient cash resources to meet its trading needs for the next 12 months should the business fail to
substantially meet the Business Plan.
At the date of approval of these financial statements, Plate Newco 3 Limited has provided a letter to the Company indicating that
it will continue to provide financial support as required for a period of at least twelve months from the date of approval of these
financial statements. However, this is not guaranteed.
It is the understanding of the Directors that [TPS’s current intention is to continue to support the business while it continucs to
deliver on the Business Plan, which will secure the financial stability and independence of the Group. It is the understanding of

Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised on differences between the carrying amount of assets and liabilities in the financial statements and the
corresponding tax bases used in the computation of taxable profit, and is accounted for using the balance sheet liability method. ;
Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all taxable temporary differences, and deferred tax assets are generally
recognised for all deductible temporary differences to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profits will be available
against which those deductible temporary difference can be utilised. .
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting date and reduced to the extent that it is no longer
probable that sufficient future taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered.
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are measured at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the period in which the liability is
settled or the asset realised, based on tax rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date. The
measurement of the deferred tax liabilities and assets reflects the tax consequences that would follow from the manner in which
the Company expects, at the reporting date, to recover or settle the carrying amount of its assets and liabilities.

Impairment policy
Assets that are subject to amortisation and depreciation are reviewed for impairment when events or changes in circumstances
indicate that the carrying amount may not be fully recoverable. The impairment review comprises a comparison of the carrying
amount of the fixed asset with its recoverable amount, which is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use.
Fair value less costs to sell is calculated by reference to the amount at which the asset could be disposed of. Value in use is
calculated by discounting the expected future cash flows obtainable as a result of the asset’s continued use, including those
resulting from its ultimate disposal, at a market based discount rate on a pre-tax basis.
An impairment loss is recognised in the Income Statement whenever the carrying amount of an asset exceeds its recoverable
amount.
The carrying amount will only be increased where an impairment loss recognised in a previous period for an asset either no longer
exists or has decreased, up to the amount that it would have been had the original impairment not occurred.
For the purpose of conducting impairment reviews, CGUs (cash-generating units) are identified as groups of assets and liabilities
that generate cash flows that are largely independent of other cash flow streams. The assets and liabilities include those directly
involved in generating the cash flows and an appropriate proportion of corporate assets. For the purposes of impairment,
individual satellites are treated as individual CGUs.
