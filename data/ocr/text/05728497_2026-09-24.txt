Review of the business, principal activities and results
The principal activities of China Telecom (Europe) Limited (““CTE” or the “Company”) during the year have been the
provision of a comprehensive range of global telecommunications services and information and communication
technology (“ICT”) services to customers. As a wholly owned subsidiary of China Telecommunications Corporation
(“CT”), the Company provides services to global CT group customers in Europe and is interconnected with the global
CT network, Headquartered in London, CTE currently has branch offices in Spain, Switzerland, Luxembourg, Czech
Republic, Hungary, and Sweden, and thirty-four Points of Presence (PoPs) across Europe.
During the year ended 31 December 2025, CTE continued to expand its marketing and sales activities through its
branches and representative offices to service the growing customer base. CTE has also continued to invest in additional
staff, telecommunication and data equipment in order to support the planned future revenue growth.
Due to high level of investment in business, our financial performance reflects continued strong growth. This has been .
driven by our Bandwidth, internet and ICT, MVNO and International Voice businesses. Net profit for the year ended
31 December 2025 increased to £5.77 million up from £2.64 million for year 2024 and net assets were £69.08 million,
up by 8.26% compared with last year (2024: £63.81 million).
Section 172 (1) statement .
The success of our business is dependent on the support of all of our stakeholders. Building positive relationships with
stakeholders that share our values is important to us and working together towards shared goals assists us in delivering
long-term sustainable success.
Our Company comprises a number of business units, all of which have extensive engagement with their own unique
stakeholders. The governance framework delegates authority for decision-making at business unit level up to defined
levels of cost and impact which allows the individual businesses to take account of the needs of their own stakeholders
in their decision-making.
The leadership teams of each business make decisions with a long-term view in mind and with the highest standards of
conduct in line with Company policies. In order to fulfil their duties, the directors of each business take care to have
regard to the likely consequences on all stakeholders of the decisions and actions which they take. Where possible,
decisions are carefully discussed with affected groups and are therefore fully understood and supported when taken.
Reports are regularly made to the Board by the business units about the operation, business strategy, performance and
key decisions taken which provides the Board with assurance that proper consideration is given to stakeholder interests
in decision-making. Details of the Company’s key stakeholders and how we engage with them are set out below.
Shareholder
China Telecom Global Limited (“CTG”) is the sole owner of our Company. We rely on its support and its opinions are
important to our decision-making. Discussions with CTG cover a wide range of topics including financial performance,
internal control, risk management, strategy, outlook, human resources, corporate govermance and ethical practices.
Colleagues
Our people are key to our success and we want them to be successful individually and as a team. There are a number
of ways we engage with and listen to our people including colleague surveys, forums, face-to-face briefings, Office
Assistant announcements, newsletters and through our anonymous colleague concern line. Key areas of focus include
health and well-being, development opportunities, and employee benefits. Regular reports about what is important to
our colleagues are made to the Board ensuring consideration is given to colleague needs.

“Customer First, Service Foremost” is our goal to deliver best-in-class service to customers. We build strong lasting
relationships with our trade customers and spend considerable time with them to understand their needs and views and
listen to how we can improve our offer and service for them. We promote communication with customers through

Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of business and the execution of the Company’s strategy are subject to a number of risks. The key
business risk and uncertainty affecting the Company is considered to relate to competition from other
telecommunications companies resulting in strong downward price pressure.
CTE views comprehensive risk management as an important task within CTE's daily operation. Pursuant to regulatory
requirements in the UK and the EU, CTE has formulated a unique five-step risk management approach based on risk
management theory and practice including risk identification, risk assessment, key risk analysis, risk reaction and risk’
management assessment. Following the efforts made in the past years, CTE has established a comprehensive risk
management system and has gradually enhanced its comprehensive risk monitoring and prevention mechanism.
Competitive pressure
CTE’s principal risk comes from competitive pressure in the UK and the rest of Europe. CTE manages this risk by
continually striving to provide premium quality telecommunication services, which are reliable and secure. CTE also
looks to maintain strong relationships with customers, to further increase market share.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Cyber risk
The confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information and its processing facilities are essential to the successful
operations of CTE. To protect information from a wide range of threats and vulnerabilities, and in order to ensure
business continuity, minimise business risk, and maximise return on investments and business opportunities,
management and staff of CTE are committed to comply with all applicable statutory, contractual and user requirements
related to information security, and adopt a continuous improvement approach to improve the effectiveness of
Information Security Management System (“ISMS”) that follows a Plan-Do-Check-Act continual improvement
framework as specified in the ISO 27001 international standard.
Competitive risk
The Company operates in a competitive industry with an evolving commercial environment. The Company places
substantive effort in exploring new innovative and quality solutions to meet the customers’ requirement.
Economic risk
The economic climate is challenging as caused by war in Ukraine and the increase of energy costs. Any deterioration
in the economy may have an adverse impact on the performance of the Company.
Financial risk management
Credit risk
CTE has implemented policies that require appropriate credit checks on potential customers before sales are made. The
amount of exposure to any individual counterparty is subject to a credit limit, which will be reassessed periodically.
Once a customer credit is over the limit, any future sales will be blocked until debtor is settled.
CTE mitigates the risk of bad debts by carefully assessing prospective customers and monitoring and following up
overdue receivables. A provision is recorded when there is a risk of default. It should be noted that a significant portion
of trade receivables is held with related parties within China Telecom Group, which also reduces the risk of bad debts.
‘At 31 December 2025, a provision is held against receivables from Russian customers due to the Ukraine War. The
company mitigated this exposure through the use of netting arrangements and has recovered a portion of the balances :
during the year. Management continues to monitor counterparties and apply prudent credit control measures.
Liquidity and currency risk
. CTE pays careful attention to the management of its cash flow and working capital position. Controls are also in place
to ensure that appropriate payment terms are included in contracts with clients and suppliers. Funding can be provided
via an intercompany loan from the parent company, China Telecom Global Limited, if needed.
Since most of the CTE’s sales and costs are in Euros and US Dollars (“USD”), the Company is exposed to the risk of
exchange rate movements on its trading balances in these currencies. The Company actively manages its risk to
exchange rate fluctuations by seeking to match the foreign currency cash balances with its net obligations in these
currencies.
With regards to financial performance, the Company recorded revenue growth across all three main revenue streams
during the year. Compared with the previous year, the International Voice, Bandwidth, Internet and ICT, and MVNO :
businesses each achieved positive revenue growth.

b. Taxation

Current tax is provided at amounts expected to be paid (or recovered) using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted

or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.

. that there will be a future outflow of funds to a tax authority. The provisions are measured at the best estimate of the

amount expected to become payable. The assessment is based on the judgement of tax professionals within the

Company supported by previous experience in respect of such activities and in certain cases based on specialist

independent tax advice.

Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance sheet

date where transactions or events that result in an obligation to pay more tax in the future or a right to pay less tax in

the future have occurred at the balance sheet date. Timing differences are differences between the Company's taxable

profits and its results as stated in the financial statements that arise from the inclusion of gains and losses in tax

assessments in periods different from those in which they are recognised in the financial statements. Deferred tax is

measured using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date that are

expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.

Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that, on the basis of all available

evidence, it can be regarded as more likely than not that there will be suitable taxable profits from which the future

reversal of the underlying timing differences can be deducted. ,

Deferred tax liabilities are recognised for timing differences arising from investments in subsidiaries and associates,

except where the Company is able to control the reversal of the timing difference, and it is probable that it will not

reverse in the foreseeable future.

Deferred tax is measured using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the balance

sheet date that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference. Deferred tax relating to non-depreciable

property, plant and equipment measured using the revaluation model and investment property is measured using the tax

rates and allowances that apply to sale of the asset. In other cases, the measurement of deferred tax liabilities and assets

reflects the tax consequences that would follow from the manner in which the Company expects, at the end of the

reporting period, to recover or settle the carrying amount of its assets and liabilities.

Where items recognised in other comprehensive income or equity are chargeable to or deductible for tax purposes, the

resulting current or deferred tax expense or income is presented in the same component of comprehensive income or

equity as the transaction or other event that resulted in the tax expense or income.

Current tax assets and liabilities are offset only when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the amounts and the

Company intends either to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset only if: a) the Company has a legally enforceable right to set off current tax

assets against current tax liabilities; and b) the deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities relate to income taxes

levied by the same taxation authority on either the same taxable entity or different taxable entities which intend either

to settle current tax liabilities and assets on a net basis, or to realise the assets and settle the liabilities simultaneously,

in each future period in which significant amounts of deferred tax liabilities or assets are expected to be settled or

recovered.
