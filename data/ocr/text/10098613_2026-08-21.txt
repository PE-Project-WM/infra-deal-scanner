Principal activities . ;
The Company is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Donnelley Financial Solutions, Inc. The principal activity of the”
Company is to act as a holding company for the Donnelley Financial Solutions group’s subsidiaries in Europe,
_ | Asia, Canada and Mexico. The subsidiaries are engaged in financial printing, virtual data room services and ,
- other financial document services. . : :
Review of the business . . -
As the holding company of several widely dispersed subsidiaries, the Company is effectively exposed to ,
: economic and political events and broader industry trends throughout the world. .
: . - Activity in capital markets remained weak in 2025, both in Europe and Asia, while North American markets
performed satisfactorily. Investment markets software sales retreated slightly after very strong growth in
2024, while virtual data room and compliance software grew’strongly in Europe. Dividends of £8,852,028 were
received from subsidiaries (2024: £12,187,748). .
Impairments to investments of £6,218,248 (2024: £14,350,379) were made due to the weak performance in : .
Asia; cost saving measures are planned for territories with weaker performance.

reflecting the Asia impairment. See also discussion of Key Performance indicators, below.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued) :
Business Growth ; .
The ability of the Company’s subsidiaries to grow and expand their global reach requires effective planning, ! ,

. development, integration and investment in systems and processes. A failure to manage this may harm the :
business through decreased ability to deliver quality.services in a cost-effective manner. The businesses have .
continued to develop software to improve our product offerings, and streamline or automate processes.

- Data protection / security .
The Company’s subsidiaries process personal and sensitive data on behalf of their customers. Any loss or
- breach of confidentiality of this data could result in financial loss to the Company, and damage to its .
reputation. This risk is managed through continued investment in IT systems and security, and the monitoring
-and control of the use of client data.
. Credit risk :
The Company’s subsidiaries are subject to credit risk arising from their trade debtors. In order to mitigate this :
. tisk, subsidiary management maintains close commercial relationships with key customers, and have
implemented policies that require appropriate credit checks be performed before sales are made and regular
monitoring of all amounts due to them.
: Liquidity risk : ;
The Company's and subsidiaries’ cash flows and treasury functions are managed centrally through the DFIN
- group cash pooling facility in coordination with the Company’s ultimate parent, Donnelley Financial Solutions
Inc. The’‘Company and subsidiaries rely on support from Donnelley Financial Solutions Inc as required and the :
directors are not aware of support being withdrawn. Working capital continues to be managed in line with the
needs of the businesses. See also the going concern review in the Directors' Report on pages 5-6.
Foreign exchange risk -
Treasury management including currency.and interest rate hedging is undertaken as part of the overall risk
management strategy of Donnelley Financial Solutions Inc group. The Company's subsidiaries make sales in .
several currencies and as a result are exposed to exchange rate movements. This exposure is mitigated
. through similarly sized inter-company trading and funding liabilities in matching currencies. Natural hedging is
’ achieved by holding foreign currency assets to offset the foreign currency risk. The directors consider this to be :
the Company’s only material exposure to currency risk; but will continue to monitor the risk in the future. ,
a Neither the Company nor its subsidiaries have external third-party debt nor external interest rate exposure.
Group risk .
, Group risks of the Donnelley Financial Solutions Inc group are discussed in that group’s annual report, which
does not form part of this report. : .
Future developments .
While the outlook for capital markets remains uncertain, we anticipate accelerating growth from our higher-
margin virtual data room and compliance software products. The investment companies software business :
should stabilise as client wins in 2024-25 start to bear fruit.

: Financial Assets and Liabilities . : .
Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised when the company becomes a party to the .
. contractual provisions of the instrument.
. Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
. arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the ~ :
assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities. .
. All financial assets and liabilities are initially measured at transaction price (including transaction costs), a
. except for those financial assets classified as at fair value through profit or loss, which are initially |
measured at fair’ value (which is normally the transaction price excluding transaction costs), unless the ,
arrangement constitutes a financing transaction. If an arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, the
financial asset or financial liability is measured at the present value of the future payments discounted ata =~
. market rate of interest for a similar debt instrument. oO
Financial assets and liabilities are only offset in the statement of financial position when, and only when,
there exists a legally enforceable right to set. off the recognised amounts and the Company intends either
to settle on a net basis, or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
For financial assets carried at amortised cost, the amount of an impairment is the difference between the
. asset’s carrying amount and the present value of estimated future cash flows, discounted at the financial
asset's original effective interest rate. .
For financial assets carried ‘at cost less impairment, the impairment loss is the difference between the
. asset’s carrying amount and the best estimate of the amount that would be received for the asset if it were
. to be sold at the reporting date. :
Where indicators exist for a decrease in impairment loss, and the decrease can be related objectively to an :
event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the prior impairment loss is tested to determine ,
. reversal. An impairment joss is reversed on an individual impaired financial asset to the extent that the
. revised recoverable value does not lead to a revised carrying amount higher than the carrying value had no .
impairment been recognised. .

. Deferred taxation :
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the
date of the Statement of Financial position-where transactions or events that result in an obligation to pay
more tax in the future or a right to pay less tax in the future have occurred at the date of the Statement of
Financial position. Timing differences are differences between the Company's taxable profits and its results
: as stated in the financial statements that arise from the inclusion of gains and losses in tax assssments in
periods different from those in which they are recognised in the financial statements.

Deferred taxation (continued) , :
. Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that, on the basis of

Deferred tax is measured on an undiscounted basis at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the

at the date of the statement of financial position. .
. The Group is within the scope of the OECD’s Pillar Two global minimum tax rules, as enacted in the UK and
, other jurisdictions in which it operates. These rules aim to ensure that the Group pays a minimum effective
tax rate of 15% on a jurisdictional basis. .
In accordance with applicable accounting standards, the Group has applied the mandatory exception to
recognising deferred tax assets and liabilities relating to Pillar Two top-up taxes. .
Current ,tax expense includes any top-up taxes arising under Pillar Two rules, including income inclusion,
undertaxed profits and domestic minimum taxes, recognised in the period in which they are incurred. The ~
tax charge is measured based on jurisdictional effective tax rate calculations in line with the relevant
legislation, using reasonable estimates where necessary. Pillar Two taxes are presented within income tax :
expense in the statement of profit or loss and disclosed separately where material,
The Group continues to monitor the development and implementation of Pillar Two legislation across
‘ relevant jurisdictions. Due to the complexity of the rules and the evolving nature of guidance, estimation
uncertainty may arise in determining the Group’s exposure to top-up taxes. : a
Foreign currency ‘
Transactions in foreign currencies are initially recorded in the entity’s functional currency by applying the
spot exchange rate ruling at the date of the transaction. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in
foreign currencies are retranslated at the functional currency rate of exchange ruling at the date of the
statement of financial position. All differences are taken to the profit and loss account.
