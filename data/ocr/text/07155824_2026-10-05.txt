STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
Introduction
The directors of BackOffice Associates Limited acknowledge the duty to prepare a strategic report for the year
ended 31 December 2025, in accordance with Chapter 414A of the Companies Act 2006. The report covers
BackOffice Associates Limited, which conducts business in the United Kingdom and the European continent.
Business review
The company is a wholly owned subsidiary of BackOffice Associates Europe (UK) Limited, which is the parent
company of BackOffice Associates Limited and other affiliates in Europe and the Middle East. The parent
company is BackOffice Associates LLC, which trades as Syniti, a limited liability company organised in the
United States of America. Syniti is a worldwide leader in information governance and data migration solutions.
The company engages in the business of selling software, developed internally or by affiliates, and selling and
delivering consulting services. The Company collaborates with its affiliates in the Americas, in Europe, the
Middle East, and Asia as part of a global organisation.
On 30 November 2024, Backoffice Associates LLC and its group of companies were acquired by Capgemini
USA, a subsidiary of Capgemini SE, France.
The company sells software products and services directly to customers and through partners. The company
sells its software under term and perpetual licenses to resellers and end-user customers, and hosts software on
a cloud-based platform. The company's software products are principally known as the Syniti Knowledge
Platform, encompassing data migration, data quality and master data management (the "Core Products"). The
Core Products are sold to enable the data aspects of clients’ overall digital transformation programs, which
frequently include implementation or upgrade of their ERP platforms, integration or spin-out of mergers /
acquisitions and divestitures, or overall data quality and master data management initiatives.
During the year the company averaged 81 employees in a variety of disciplines including consulting, sales, and
general management. To meet fluctuating demand, the company also deploys consultants who are employed
by other BackOffice entities, primarily from India and the United States.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The business is highly competitive. Large system integrators and small providers of similar goods and services
compete on price, terms, technology, delivery schedules, company reputation, and the specific nature of the
service offerings. The sales cycle tends to be long, and significant efforts can be spent which do not result in
customer acquisition.
Projects involve many unknowns at the time of setting the contract price or commencing work. Negotiations
may result in fixed price contracts, milestone approvals, expense caps, or time and materials arrangements with
potential of penalty clauses. In some cases, the company may commence work or may expand the scope of
work prior to obtaining formal approval of terms and conditions, resulting in significant delays between the time
resources are expended and receipt of customer payment. In all cases, delays or cost overruns may
unfavourably impact margins and cash flows.
The company employs highly skilled consultants. The inability to hire and retain consultants may unfavourably
impact revenue.
Changing technology may influence customer demand or change the competitive position of the company,
requiring additional investment in product development, partnering with other companies, or adjusting schedules
and prices.
Finally, the company conducts business in several currencies, primarily the Pound Sterling, the Euro, and the
US Dollar. In general, inflows of US Dollars and Euros exceed outflows, which are primarily payroll and related
costs denominated in Pound Sterling. The company is subject to, and has incurred, exchange gains and losses
particularly as the Pound Sterling has fluctuated in value versus other currencies.

Financial key performance indicators
Key performance indicators used to measure results include revenues, gross margins and earnings before
interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation ("EBITDA"). The company delivered revenue of £36.2 million
versus a 2024 amount of £25.1 million, a year on year increase of 44%; cost of sales increased to £27.1 million

policies for services and software, and as a net borrower of resources from affiliates outside the EMEA region,
the company has improved on the procurement of these resources, contributing to the overall profit.
Measures of the financial condition of the company also include cash flow and other balance sheet movements.
The company has made significant efforts to manage its debtor balances during the year, substantially reducing
’ its unbilled and overdue receivable amounts.

company have made and will continue to make investments in sales personnel, marketing events, infrastructure

2.4 Foreign currency translation
Functional and presentation currency
The company's functional and presentational currency is GBP.
Transactions and balances
Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency using the spot exchange
rates at the dates of the transactions.
At each period end foreign currency monetary items are translated using the closing rate. Non-
monetary items measured at historical cost are translated using the exchange rate at the date of the
transaction and non-monetary items measured at fair value are measured using the exchange rate
when fair value was determined.
Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the
translation at period-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign
currencies are recognised in the profit and loss account except when deferred in other
comprehensive income as qualifying cash flow hedges.

2.5 Turnover
The company has three categories of income, for which turnover, excluding Value Added Tax, is
recognised as follows:
Software
Income from the sale of software is recognised on completion of the sale, at the point of delivery of
the license.
Support
Income relating to support fees is recognised over the term of the support contract.
Consultancy
Income from consultancy services is recognised in the month the service is delivered. This includes
income from fixed price contracts where revenue is recognised over time based on the stage of
completion, as set out in note 3.

2.6 Operating leases: the company as lessee
Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to the profit and loss account on a straight-line
basis over the lease term.
Benefits received and receivable as an incentive to sign an operating lease are recognised on a
straight-line basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is representative of the time
pattern of the lessee's benefit from the use of the leased asset.

2.7 Research and development expenditure credit
Income receivable under the Research and Development Expenditure Credit (RDEC) scheme is
recognised as other operating income in the period in which the qualifying R&D expenditure is
incurred and the company’s entitlement to the credit is established. RDEC is accounted for on a
gross basis, meaning the credit is presented as income, with any related costs included in the
relevant expense line items.

2.8 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a
pension plan under which the company pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the company has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in the profit and loss account when they fall due.
Amounts not paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the balance sheet. The assets of the plan are
held separately from the company in independently administered funds.

2.9 Current and deferred taxation
The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the profit and
loss account except that a charge attributable to an item of income and expense recognised as
other comprehensive income or to an item recognised directly in equity is also recognised in other
comprehensive income or directly in equity respectively.
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date in the countries where the company
operates and generates income.
Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not
reversed by the balance sheet date, except that:
° The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will

be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits; and
. Any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
allowances have been met.

Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of
business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair values
of assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences between the
fair values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax. Deferred tax is
determined using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the balance
