Going concern , :
During the year ended 30 April 2025, the Etihad Topco Limited group initiated a series of operational, commercial,
and strategic actions to stabilise the business, create a platform for sustainable growth, remove exposure to
. potential volatility in wholesale markets, and improve the quality of earnings. The group board remains confident
that the group is building a strong platform for turnaround and growth with a significantly reduced risk profile.
The January 2025 amendment to the group’s banking facilities included an extension in the maturity of those
facilities and refreshed financial covenants. The company is one of the Obligors as per the Senior Facilities
Agreement (SFA), these are group-wide financing facilities taken up at Verastar Holdings Limited level which is
an intermediary holding entity. A further short-term reset of the group's EBITDA covenants was secured in October
2025 to allow flexibility whilst the restructuring of the group continues. The board believes that this demonstrates
the continued support and confidence of its financial stakeholders. ,
Due to the nature of the group's operations and the guarantees made by the company and other subsidiary
companies under the Senior Facilities Agreement (SFA), the directors have relied upon consolidated modelling of
the projected financial results and cash flows of the group through to January 2027 as part of their going concern
assessment for the company. .
Acomprehensive disclosure of the group going concern assessment, including the challenges faced, the strategy
adopted, and future forecasts to October 2026 is included in the Etinad Topco Limited group financial statements
for the year ended 30 April 2025, which were approved and signed on 27 October 2025 and are publicly available
on the Companies House website.
The group’s latest base case forecasts indicate that a further reset of the EBITDA covenant or waiver is likely to :
be required from the end of January 2027 onwards, and the latest severe but plausible downside forecasts indicate
this would be needed from the end of October 2026.
There is no guarantee a reset or waiver of the EBITDA covenant would be received from the lenders. The group
has successfully obtained covenant waivers and resets from its lenders in the recent past, and the directors
consider it reasonable to expect that similar lender support will continue to be available for the foreseeable future.
In the event that a waiver is not obtained and/or a reset of the EBITDA covenant is not secured, one potential
outcome is that the lenders may demand full repayment of the SFA, and the group does not have sufficient cash :
on hand to make this repayment, which would have consequences for the company as an Obligor.
The group's latest cashflow forecasts remain above the liquidity covenant levels throughout the going concern
period, including under the severe but plausible downside scenario, and the group's board of directors do not
expect either a reset or waiver of this covenant will be required.
The financial statements of Etihad Topco Limited and group companies have therefore been prepared on a going
concern basis with a material uncertainty disclosure. Given the operational and financial interdependence between
the company, its fellow subsidiaries, and parent companies, and the obligations of the company as an Obligor,
the directors need to consider the going concern of Etihad Topco Limited group on a consolidated basis when
concluding on the appropriateness of the going concern basis of preparation for the company.
The directors have received a letter of support from Etihad Topco Limited, the parent company, confirming its
intention to direct the provision of operational and financial support to the Company and its fellow subsidiaries,
based on available resources, for a period of at least twelve months from the date of approval of these financial
statements.
After due consideration of these circumstances, the directors have a reasonable expectation that the group will
have adequate resources to continue in operational existence and serve and settle liabilities, including those of
the company, for a period of at least 12 months from the date of approving these financial statements. Accordingly,
the financial statements continue to be prepared on the going concern basis. Further details regarding the adoption
of the going concern basis of preparation can be found in note 2.

In circumstances where a customer has unpaid debt, there can be delays and operational and legal
obstacles during the disconnection process, whereby the group is still providing services to a customer with
poor payment history and outstanding debt. These customers are classified as a ‘bad’ customer for debt

Analysis of past recovery patterns indicate that once unpaid debt reaches certain milestones, the probability

considered probable at the point that services are provided, and revenue is therefore not recognised for
these services by the licenced supply entities, nor are the related unpaid invoices recognised in gross trade
receivables by the company. Revenue is only recognised in relation to this group of customers upon
successful recovery, or where changes in recent payment patterns indicate that future recovery is probable,
and the customer is no longer classified as ‘bad’. Should the receipts from the current population of ‘bad’
customers vary by more than 5% from historical patterns, then there may be a material impact on reported
revenues, gross trade receivables, and gross bad debt provisions.
