Strategic Report for the year ended 31 March 2026
The directors present their strategic report on the Company for the year ended 31 March 2026.
(a) Principal activities

. Cornerstone Telecommunications Infrastructure Limited (“Cornerstone” or the “Company”) operates
as a UK nationwide digital infrastructure business, providing services in relation to non-radio
(passive) assets, maintenance, and management of sites on behalf of its tenants. The Company's
activities are primarily engaged in supporting the mobile wireless networks for Vodafone Limited
(‘Vodafone’) and Telefonica UK Limited, part of Virgin Media O2 group ("Virgin Media ©2” or
“VMO2") (together the “Anchor tenants”) as well as for other UK Mobile Network Operators
(“MNO's’).

(b) Background

In 2012, Vodafone Limited and Telefonica UK Limited pooled their respective networks into an evenly
split joint venture, creating the Company, with the purpose of building and operating a joint single
national grid of sites. Outside London, Vodafone is responsible for the design, deployment, operation
and active maintenance of the shared radio access network in the West Region, thereby acting as host
operator in these regions with VMO2 being the sharing operator. Conversely, VMO2 is the host operator
in the East Region where Vodafone is the sharing operator. London is governed by a different set of
rules.
In January 2021, Vodafone Limited and Telefonica UK Limited each entered into new long-term Master
Services Agreements (MSAs) with the Company, having initial terms of 8 years, with three 8-year
renewal periods and a 3-year exit period if not renewed, being together the primary ‘Anchor tenants’.
Under the terms of the MSAs, the Company has certain commitments from Vodafone and VMO2 to
facilitate the effective roll-out of 5G and other technologies, increase network capacity and to meet
regulatory coverage obligations. The construct of the MSA commits Vodafone and VMO2 as anchor
tenants on new sites to be constructed by the Company and extends passive tenancies on existing
sites with a market base fee being applied for each site occupation depending an site type and location.
Following the commencement of the MSAs, we continue to monitor the Company's leasehold
multiservice provider (“MSP”) assets, where Cornerstone lease a section of a third-party tower. These
are leased for the entirety of the remaining term to the anchor tenants and the anchor tenants have
direct control over the direction and use of these assets. .

. !n March 2021, Vodafone subsequently transferred its 50% shareholding in the Company to Central
Tower Holding Company BV a subsidiary of Vantage Towers AG (Vantage). From March 2025,
Vantage Towers AG is ultimately and indirectly co-controlled by (i) Vodafone Group Ple and (ii)
Blackrock Inc., ‘New York City, USA, and KKR Management LLP, New York City, USA (the
companies listed under (ii) also exercise joint control).

: In March 2023, O2 Cedar Limited transferred its shareholding in the Company to O2 Networks Limited
as part of Virgin Media O2’s internal restructuring. The shareholding structure of Cornerstone is Central
Tower Holdings Company BV (50%), 02 Networks Limited (50%).

In November 2023, Virgin Media O2 (through O2 Holdings Limited) disposed of 16.66% minority stake
of the Company to GLIL Infrastructure LLP (“GLIL”) (through Granada Bidco Limited). The sale has
been structured through Granstone Holdco Limited (formerly VMED O2 Networks Limited)
(“Granstone”), which wholly owns O2 Networks Limited. As a result, GLIL owns 33.33% of Granstone
and Virgin Media O2 the remaining 66.67%.

In November 2024, Virgin Media O2 sold an additional 16.66% in Granstone. As a result the beneficial
ownership of O2 Networks Limited 50% stake in Comerstone is; Virgin Media O2 (25.01%); GLIL
(16.66%); and Funds managed by Equitix Investment Management Limited (8.33%).

(d) Future outlook .

. The Company operates at the core of the UK's digital connectivity ecosystem, managing a nationwide
portfolio of telecommunications infrastructure that underpins mobile network services. The
Company's future outlook remains resilient and underpinned by strong structural demand drivers,
although growth is expected to be moderate and progressive, reflecting the maturity of the UK tower
market. .
The UK telecommunications infrastructure market continues to benefit from sustained demand for
data connectivity, driven by increasing mobile usage, digitalisation across industries, and the ongoing
rollout of 5G networks. Government policy continues to support investment in digital infrastructure,
with 5G recognised as a key enabler of long-term economic productivity and innovation.

While the market is comparatively mature, it continues to expand steadily. This growth is primarily
driven by network densification requirements rather than extensive new site build, reflecting the need
to enhance capacity, coverage, and reliability.

Cornerstone maintains a leading position in the UK market, with a large-scale portfolio of
infrastructure assets and long-term contractual arrangements with its anchor tenants, VMO2 and
Vodafone. These arrangements provide predictable, inflation-linked revenue streams and underpin
the Company's cash flow stability.

(g) Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
The principal risks and uncertainties affecting the Company include: :
Operational Risk
The effective implementation of the programme of activities under the MSA and to maintain the
performance of the consolidated network at the levels required in a cost-effective manner,
fluctuations to market rents payable on network sites, technological advances, and regulation. The
Company's operations require strong acquisition capability, commercially beneficial agreements with
landlords, capitalising upon acquisition opportunities, and delivering long term sustainable estates
management costs.
The introduction of the Building Safety Act 2022 has increased regulatory requirements for the
construction, maintenance, and management of buildings. While the Company does not directly own.
or operate residential high-rise buildings, its infrastructure assets such as rooftop installations and
shared sites may be subject to enhanced scrutiny under the new regime.
There is a risk that evolving compliance obligations, including those related to structural safety, fire
‘risk assessments, and duty holder responsibilities, could impact operational timelines, increase costs, .
or require changes to existing site management practices. The Company continues to monitor.
legislative developments and is working proactively to assess its exposure, engage with landlords
and site owners, and ensure that appropriate safety and compliance measures are in place.
Financial risk management
* The Company is exposed to financial risks arising from its operational activities. The Company's principal
non-derivative financial instruments include borrowings and cash that arise directly from its operations.
The Company's credit financing is unsecured and independent of the shareholders. .
It is the Company's policy not to enter in speculative derivative transactions or to invest in financial
products on a speculative basis but may engage in hedging through selective derivatives to manage
inherent operational financial risks. .
Liquidity risk .
The Company maintains a mixture of equity and external borrowing that is designed to ensure
: adequate liquidity for operational effectiveness and investment in property, plant and equipment.
Liquidity is monitored regularly to ensure sufficient funding is available to meet both short-term
obligations and long-term strategic needs.
Credit risk .
Concentration of credit risk with respect to customers defaulting on the payment of their debts is
considered limited given that the Anchor tenants are related parties of the Company’s shareholders, who
are contractually committed to fund the operations of the Company.
For third party customers, creditworthiness is assessed prior to engagement, and exposure is monitored
_ on an ongoing basis. The Company does not require collateral in respect of financial assets but
maintains robust credit control procedures to mitigate risk.
Interest rate risk
The Company's is exposed to interest rate fluctuations through its unsecured revolving credit facilities.
Interest rate movements are monitored regularly and the option to implement hedging strategies to ,
mitigate this risk exposure remains under consideration. In addition, the Company requests funding for
a one-month period monthly and maintains its cash balance in a range sufficient to cover its short-term
liabilities. :

(g) Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)

Cyber risk
Cyber security remains an area of focus given the impact to the Company's operational capabilities, data
management and business continuity, The Company adopts a low-risk appetite approach and manages
cyber risks through a combination of robust internal IT security controls and external assurance. This
includes dedicated InfoSec personnel and ongoing independent external assessment through ISO
27001 accreditation and regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing.
Third-party supplier dependencies
The Company relies on a network of suppliers and contractors to deliver infrastructure projects and
maintenance services. Disruptions in supply chains or contractor underperformance may impact delivery
timelines and costs. :
The operational performance of third-party suppliers is monitored through relevant KPI's and delivery
measures, Failure of our suppliers to comply with our social and environmental guidelines may result in
harm to our reputation and brands, which may have a negative impact on our business, sustainability
rating and financial position. This is managed by operating supply chain assurance activities which
include, but are not limited to, contract management, relationship management and active
creditworthiness monitoring.
Going concern
In determining the appropriate basis of preparation of the financial statements for the period ended 31
March 2026, the directors are required to consider whether the Company is a going concern, i.e.,
whether the Company is able to meet its debts as they fall due over a period of at least 12 months from
the date of approval of these financial statements. The key judgement is with regards to whether there
is sufficient available and committed funding to allow the Company to continue to be cash generative on
an on-going basis.
The directors have considered the provisions of the shareholder agreement, the MSAs with the Anchor

, tenants and the Company's independent unsecured revolving credit financing and consider that the

: Company can continue its ongoing operations with existing funding of the business for the foreseeable
future. As part of their regular assessment of the Company's financing position, Management have
prepared a cash flow forecast for a period which covers at least 12 months after the date of approval of .
the financial statements. Further analysis has been prepared which shows that on a severe but plausible
downside scenario, the business can continue to operate and discharge liabilities in the normal course
of business. These sensitivities included the impact of higher inflation and a reduction in the revenues
earned compared to forecast. The forecast shows sufficient available and committed funding to allow
the Company to continue to be cash generative on an on-going basis. While the going concern
assessment period covers the period to 31 March 2028, the Company's current projections cover the
period to 31 March 2031. The directors have considered compliance with the covenants attached to the
Company's borrowing facilities. Forecasts prepared for the going concern assessment demonstrate that
the Company is expected to remain in compliance with these covenants throughout the assessment .
period. Based on this assessment and performing scenario planning, the directors continue to consider
it appropriate to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the financial statements. :

Our purpose and leadership

The Company's purpose is to enable a digitally connected society through our nationwide network

of neutral host, shareable sites, providing unmatched opportunities for coverage and digital services.

This purpose guides our culture and strategic direction. This purpose underpins consistent decision-

making and _reflects our commitment to supporting mobile network operators, including Vodafone

and VMO2, to designing, building, and deploying shared digital infrastructure that ensures seamless
connectivity by integrating land, property, and technology.

The Company’s vision and strategy is set by the Board and is implemented by the Chief Executive

Officer (“CEO”) and the Executive Management Team through operating business plans, financial

budgets and operational strategies. The Board receives regular updates on strategic delivery and

provides oversight, challenge, and support to ensure alignment with long-term objectives.

In 2025/26, the Board maintained strong oversight of the Company's strategic and operational

priorities with a particular focus on managing Company's principal risks and to provide oversight,

challenge and support to the Executive Management Team. This included oversight and regular

reviews of the Company Risk Register, ensuring that appropriate mitigation strategies were in place;

the continued deployment of the Company’s operational resilience plans; business plans, strategies .
and budgets. The Board also remained focused on ensuring the Company continued to strengthen

its approach to workforce health and wellbeing, while delivering positive outcomes for clients and
maintaining a resilient, cost-effective supply chain that supports long-term value creation.

_ The Board has a strong understanding of the Company's culture, as it guides strategy and helps

The Company's culture is anchored in ethical business conduct by all colleagues. To encourage and

: promote practising the right behaviour at Cornerstone, the ‘Be a PRO’ (Professional, Respectful,
Open) initiative supports our Company values. PRO behaviours are committed to by our colleagues -
and stakeholders. Be a PRO guides us on how we must conduct ourselves when representing
Cornerstone either to our colleagues or to anyone outside our Company.

Management monitors culture through employee surveys, absenteeism rates and exit interviews,

business integrity and conduct through established whistleblowing processes.

The Cornerstone Board is appointed by Company's shareholders and reflects the joint venture
structure, comprising an equal number of shareholder appointed directors from diverse backgrounds
and nationalities. The Board consists of highly skilled individuals within the telecommunications
space and is considered well suited to the scale and complexity of the business.
To enhance board governance effectiveness, the Board is supported by dedicated committees,
including the Audit and Risk Committee and the Remuneration Committee. These committees
together with the Executive management team, provide focused oversight and enable the Board to
discharge its responsibilities with rigour and efficiency.

General Counsel and Property Director, Chief Sales and Commercial Officer, Chief Network Officer,

Going Concern Basis of Accounting

In determining the appropriate basis of preparation of the financial statements for the period ended 31
March 2026, the directors are required to consider whether the Company is a going concern, i.e.
whether the Company is able to meet its debts as they fall due over a period of at least 12 months from
the date of approval of these financial statements. The key judgement is with regards to whether there
is sufficient available and committed funding to allow the Company to continue to be cash generative
on an on-going basis.

The directors have considered the provisions of the shareholder agreement, the MSAs with the Anchor
tenants and the Company's independent unsecured revolving credit financing and consider that the
Company can continue its ongoing operations with existing funding of the business for the foreseeable
future. As part of their regular assessment of the Company’s financing position, Management have
prepared a cash flow forecast for a period which covers at least 12 months after the date of approval
of the financial statements. Further analysis has been prepared which shows that on a severe but
plausible downside scenario, the business can continue to operate and discharge liabilities in the
normal course of business. These sensitivities included the impact of higher inflation and a reduction
in the revenues earned compared to forecast. The forecast shows sufficient available and committed
funding to allow the Company to continue to be cash generative on an on-going basis. While the going
concem assessment period covers the period to 31 March 2028, the Company's current projections
cover the period to 31 March 2031. The directors have considered compliance with the covenants
attached to the Company's borrowing facilities. Forecasts prepared for the going concern assessment
demonstrate that the Company is expected to remain in compliance with these covenants throughout
the assessment period. Based on this assessment and performing scenario planning, the directors
continue to consider it appropriate to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the
financial statements.
