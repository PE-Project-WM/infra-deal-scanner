Introduction
The Directors present their Strategic Report for Africa Mobile Networks Limited for the year ended 31 December .
2025.
Strategic Review 2025
Business review
The Group's Revenue grew 21% from $26.0m to $31.5m during the year from Revenues generated in ten countries
across sub-Saharan Africa. Revenues have increased through a combination of traffic and subscriber growth on
existing sites and from building additional Towers at new rural site locations.
The Tower portfolio count grew by 626 sites from 4,422 at the start of the year to 5,048 at the end of the year. Most
of the new sites were added in DRC, Cameroon and Nigeria although the site count increased in nine of the ten
countries where the business is actively operating.
The Group’s Adjusted EBITDA has continued to grow throughout the year and increased significantly from $7.7m
in 2024 to $12.6m which is an impressive performance following some of the significant challenges faced by the
business in several of its markets in the previous two years. The key objectives at the start of the year were to
consolidate the existing operations and to grow EBITDA and this has been achieved at a Group level with the
business either exceeding or hitting its performance targets for the year. The results have this year been positively .
. impacted by the activities in Nigeria where we have seen a turnaround in performance and a positive EBITDA
contribution after several years of negative results and losses which were primarily caused by foreign exchange
volatility. This has been achieved through a combination of contract amendments and operational efficiencies
coupled with stability and improvements in the local currency against the US Dollar which has helped to bolster the
“ average revenues generated per site.
There has also been a very strong performance in DRC where 341 sites were added in the year, and this continues
to be the best performing network in terms of both the Revenue and EBITDA generated. There were notable
performances in several other countries, and most of the operating entities are showing healthy year on year growth.
Both Zambia and Madagascar have made positive EBITDA contributions to the Group results for the first time during .
the 2025 financial year.
During the year the Company undertook a major project to recover equipment from the 300 sites that had been
switched off and disconnected in Guinea Conakry and Guinea Bissau during 2024 following the Mobile Network
Operator selling its operations there and defaulting on the contracts. Most of the equipment was successfully
recovered and redeployed to new sites across multiple countries which has contributed to the site count growth in
the year.
The Group achieved a strong core trading performance during the year, delivering an Adjusted EBITDA of $12.6m :
(2024: $7.7m). On a statutory basis, the Group reported a loss before taxation for the year of $15.7m (2024: loss
of $4.6m). This statutory result primarily reflects the first-time recognition criteriaJbeing met under IFRS 2 (Share-
Based Payments), which resulted in a total exceptional expense of $16.4m being recognized ($14.9m non-cash
equity option valuation and $1.5m associated employer's National Insurance provisions). The result also continues
to reflect high levels of non-cash operational costs, including depreciation on owned Tower assets and equipment
($7.1m), tangible fixed asset impairments ($2.7m), and serviced and capitalised interest expenses on loan facilities
($2.0m).

Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal risks for the business are:

Foreign exchange risk

The Group faces foreign exchange risk. Revenues are typically generated in the local currency of the country where
the business operates and about two thirds of the Revenue is currently non-USD denominated. The Company
incurs costs in USD that it must fund locally so there are also some liquidity issues linked to availability of hard
currencies in certain countries which adds to overall foreign exchange costs. At a Group level this exposure is
mitigated through geographical diversification by operating in multiple territories so that no one country or foreign
currency represents more than 25% of the overall Revenue.

Logistics risk

The Company sources equipment globally from multiple suppliers so can be subject to supply chain and logistics
tisks. Much of the equipment purchased is consolidated in China and will typically be shipped via sea freight
containers which will have three-to-six-month lead times from supplier to the warehouse in country. Any delays in
the timings or breakdowns in the supply chain can then lead to delays in rolling out tower sites which can then
impact negatively on the performance of the local operating entity. The Company mitigates this risk by ordering bulk
sets of equipment well in advance of intended rollout dates.

Credit risk . ,

The Group and individual subsidiary entities have entered into contracts with customers who are Telecoms Mobile
Network Operators (MNOs). Each AMN Operating entity responsible manage the credit control and collection :
process with their own individual customer. Delays can be experienced in both invoicing and collection for a variety
of reasons; these risks are mitigated by working with well-established and highly reputable Tier 1 operators and

: then proactively working with the local MNO to agree invoicing amounts on a monthly basis to facilitate timely and

predictable cash collections.

Political and economic risk

The Group operates in multiple countries in Africa and at times political change and economic instability can lead
to unrest and some uncertainty in a country from time to time. This is mitigated at a Group level by being well
diversified across multiple jurisdictions, but it is also helped by the fact that the Towers are built in rural regions
which are seen as an essential service and a lifeline in many cases in providing communications to previously
unconnected communities and as such are generally not impacted negatively by political or government unrest.

Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis which assumes that the Group and
Company will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future.
The Group reported a loss for the year after taxation of $21.0m for the year ended 31 December 2025 (2024: $6.9m)
and Net Assets at the balance sheet date of $35.8m (2024: $41.9m). The increase in the net statutory loss compared
with the prior year is primarily attributable to the first-time recognition criteria being met under IFRS 2 (Share-Based
Payments), which generated an aggregate charge of $16.4m ($14.9m non-cash equity option valuation and $1.5m
in associated employer's National Insurance provisions). Operationally, the business demonstrated strong
expansion, with Adjusted EBITDA increasing to $12.6m (2024: $7.7m) and the tower portfolio expanding to 5,048
sites. The Group can meet all its current obligations as they fall due and in the currency in which they are payable.
The Directors have reviewed cash flow forecasts for a period of not less than 12 months from the date of approval
of the financial statements. These forecasts consider expected Revenue growth, operating costs, capital
expenditure requirements and available Cash resources within the Group.
Based on this review, the Directors are satisfied that the Group and Company will have adequate resources to meet
their liabilities as they fall due. Accordingly, the Directors consider it appropriate to prepare financial statements on
a going concern basis. :
Future developments
The prospects for both the Company and the Group continue to look very promising. The business continues to
make strong progress in building its Tower portfolio across multiple countries in Africa which has been supported
by the improvements in EBITDA and Cash generation in several of its key operating entities across the Group. In
2026, the Group is targeting further growth in both Revenues and EBITDA, alongside the addition of approximately
800 to 1,000 sites. This expansion is expected to be funded through a combination of existing cash reserves and
the funds generated by the local operating entities. :
The Group continues to develop its proprietary Radio Access Network (RAN) technology, which enables 2G, 3G or
4G services to be delivered from a single radio node referred to as AMN Radio Node (ARN). There are now over
2,800 of these units installed on the low-cost base stations in the active operating countries which represents just
under 60% of all the sites owned and managed by the business. The Group assemble and repair the ARN in-house .
in the UK and has expanded its production capacity to be able to assemble c3,000 units per year which has reduced
its dependency on third party suppliers and overall equipment cost to build a Tower. In addition, the Group’s ability
to deploy sites using both LEO and GEO satellite connectivity provides flexible connectivity solutions for both remote
and rural communities anywhere in the world.

Looking ahead, the Company is evaluating its funding requirements to support its longer-term growth strategy to
increase its presence and footprint across sub-Saharan Africa to more than 10,000 sites over the course of the next
three to four years. The existing funding and Cash reserves have allowed the business to grow to over 5,000 sites
however additional capital will be required to achieve the Group's long-term growth ambitions.
The Group has also evaluated other opportunities outside of Africa as part a global growth strategy. These initiatives
have been put on hold in the short term whilst focusing on maximising its potential and EBITDA in its existing
markets.
Research and development
The Company and Group continue to invest time and resources into the research and development of both new
and existing solutions to the rural telecommunications market. This includes the ongoing optimisation of Tower
design through close collaboration with key equipment and service suppliers. At the same time the Group continues
to develop innovating in-house technical and software solutions as well as continuing to develop and enhance its
own proprietary equipment and products to strengthen the Group's competitive position in rural connectivity while
supporting the continued expansion of the Tower portfolio.

_ Meet the conditions for recognition under IFRS 3 ‘Business Combinations’ are recognised at their fair values at the

Goodwill represents the excess of the consideration transferred, the amount of any non-controlling interest in the
acquiree and the acquisition date fair value of any previous equity interest in the acquiree over the fair value of the

of the business combination, the difference is recognised directly in profit or loss. Any subsequent adjustment to
reflect changes in consideration arising from contingent consideration amendments are recognised in profit or loss.
On an acquisition-by-acquisition basis, the Group recognises any non-controlling interest in the acquiree either at

The Group recognises revenue in accordance with IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers. Revenue is ~

Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised at the point in time when the relevant performance obligation is

consideration received or receivable. The Group assesses whether contracts contain a significant financing
component; currently, the Group's contracts do not contain a financing component.
Sale of Goods

A contract asset is recognised when the Group has transferred goods or services to a customer but has not yet
received payment, and the right to consideration is conditional on something other than the passage of time (e.g.,
future performance or another performance obligation).
A contract liability is recognised when a customer pays consideration or the Group has a right to an amount of
consideration that is unconditional, before the Group transfers a good or service to the customer. If payments
received from customers exceed the services rendered, a contract liability is recognised.
Customers are typically invoiced on a monthly basis, and consideration is payable when invoiced. Payment is
generally due within 30 days of delivery or service provision.

A loss allowance is recognised on initial recognition of financial assets held at amortised cost, based on expected

Subsequent Measurement

impairment and Expected Credit Losses (ECLs) .

3. CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES AND JUDGEMENTS

The preparation of financial information in accordance with generally accepted accounting practice, in the case of the

Group being UK-adopted International Financial Reporting Standards, requires the Directors to make estimates and

judgements that affect the reported amount of assets, liabilities, income and expenditure and the disclosures made in

the financial statements. Such estimates and judgements must be continually evaluated based on historical experience
and other factors, including expectations of future events.

The significant judgements made by management in applying the Group's accounting policies as set out above, and

the key sources of estimation, were:

* — Impairment of goodwill:

The Group tests goodwill annually for impairment, in accordance with the accounting policy stated in note 2.12
above. The value in use calculation requires the Group to estimate the future Cash flows expected to arise from
each cash generating unit and also to estimate a suitable discount rate in order to calculate the present values of
the anticipated future Cash flows. Management has estimated cash flow projections values beyond a five-year
period at a growth rate of average 3%. The forecasts are consistent with past nature of Cash flows. The Cash
flows are discounted at a rate of 9%. Carrying amount of goodwill is in note 13.

e Residual values and useful economic lives of intangible and tangible assets:

The Company depreciate tangible assets over their estimated useful lives. The estimation of the useful lives of
assets is based on historic performance as well as expectations about future use and therefore requires estimates
and assumptions to be applied by management. The actual lives of these assets can vary depending on a variety
of factors, including technological innovation, product life cycles and maintenance programmes.

e  Recoverability of debtors:

Debtors are carried at amortised cost less accumulated credit losses. Expected credit losses (ECL) are determined
by applying an estimated loss rate to the debtor's balance. Debtors are all deemed recoverable, except for those
specifically identified as not recoverable. Intercompany balances are deemed recoverable, except for those
specifically considered not recoverable, and represent the funding provided to each operating Company to build
Towers and support the growth of operations and it is anticipated that as the individual businesses start to generate
surplus profits over time that these balances will be repaid.
12 months ECL is the same as lifetime ECL because debtors are expected to be settled within 12 months.
Assumptions that are used include exposure at default, discount rate of 9%, estimated portion of exposure that is
. expected not to be recovered in the event of a default in next 12 months., forward looking events like profitability,
ability to generate cash, economic factors impacting the debtor.

* — Hyperinflation environment .
The preparation of financial information in accordance with IAS 29 Financial Reporting in Hyperinflationary
Economies requires management to apply significant judgement and estimates.

Management exercised judgement in determining if a subsidiary’s functional currency operates in a
hyperinflationary economy. This assessment is based on a range of qualitative and quantitative indicators,
including the cumulative inflation rate over a three-year period, trends in price indices, and other economic factors
such as changes in purchasing power and pricing practices.

Once hyperinflation is identified, management will apply IAS 29 by restating non-monetary assets, liabilities, equity,
and results of operations using a general price index to reflect the current purchasing power at the reporting date.
This process involves significant estimation, particularly in selecting an appropriate general price index,
determining the timing of transactions for restatement purposes, and calculating the gain or loss on the net
monetary position.

The fair value of the share options was determined using the Black-Scholes option pricing model. This model

incorporates various assumptions, including the share price at grant date, expected volatility, risk-free interest rate,

successful exit event occurring, given that the exercise of most options is contingent on such an event. While the

Directors acknowledge the inherent uncertainty in estimating the probability of an exit event, the total fair value of

the 2.7 million options outstanding as of 31 December 2025 was determined to be $15.1m (2024: $5.4m). .

Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk of economic loss should any of the Group's customers fail to fulfil their obligations and is mainly
prevalent in the Group’s core Revenue as a result of debtors failing to pay at a time they should pay.
Credit risk is managed on a collective basis. At Group and Company level exposure to credit risk is from Cash and
Cash equivalents and trade and other receivables from customers. These are the main two Groupings to exposure.
Trade receivables are Grouped per country. Cash and Cash equivalents and deposits are held at banks and
financial institutions. The Group reviews its banking arrangements carefully to minimise such risks and has put in
place credit control procedures to mitigate against risks arising from customers including the obtaining of references,
setting of credit limits and monitoring of timits.

Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is defined as the risk of failure to meet all financial obligations on a timely basis and in the currency
due without incurring above normal costs. In the case of the Group this risk specifically would arise from an inability
to honour obligations with respect to borrowings from related parties.
The Group’s objective is to maintain a balance between the availability of sufficient continued funding and flexibility
through the use of shareholder funding. Management monitors monthly forecasts of the Group’s expected Cash
flows to ensure that a sufficiently liquid position is maintained.
