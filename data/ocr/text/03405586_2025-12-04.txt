REVIEW OF THE BUSINESS
The Company, which services the micro and SME market with a range of products including domain names, shared
hosting, email services, dedicated and virtual servers, has continued to perform well within an extremely competitive
environment.
To grow the Company significantly would mean competing in a more commoditised market with the need for a
substantial marketing budget. As a result, our target for the Company is to retain our existing presence in the UK
market via selective marketing and responding to market conditions with dynamic pricing. As in previous years, the
Company continues to deliver strong profitability with an adjusted EBITDA! of £4,926,901 being 43% of revenue
(2024: £5,153,814 being 44% of revenue). Profit before tax was £4,449,726 (2024: £4,680,751), a decline of 5% on
the prior year driven by an increase in administration costs. The business benefits from sharing of the iomart Group
ple (“the Group”) infrastructure, meaning this profitability translates to strong cash flow.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES (CONTINUED)

Staff

As with any service organisation, Easyspace is dependent on the skill, experience and commitment of its employees

and especially a relatively small number of senior staff. The performance of the Company could be adversely affected

if the required staffing levels are not maintained or senior staff are not retained. The Company seeks to recruit and

retain suitably skilled and experienced staff by offering a challenging and rewarding work environment. This includes

competitive and innovative reward packages and a strong commitment to training and development. The Company

also has the ability to manage and recruit resource across multiple locations which creates, to some degree, flexibility

on where it recruits and deploys its resources.

Data and Cyber Security

There has been a sharp rise in recent years in cyber and data related crime. The security of customer, commercial and

personal data presents both a reputational and financial risk to the Company. The Company continues to make

investment in physical and data security systems and to promote a culture within the organisation which embeds

security across all of our operations. The Group continues to develop its security portfolio to equip its customers with

the means to counter the types of security threats that clients face. The Group, and so the Company, continues to :

enhance its internal process improvement, security awareness and training to ensure it can provide solutions which

customers can rely on. The Company also carries specific insurance in relation to cyber related crime. The Company’s

contracts and associated schedules with customers make it clear where responsibilities lie in relation to the roles and

responsibilities of each party for the security of data and data protection in general.

Competition and product portfolio -

Easyspace operates in a competitive and fluid marketplace and while the Directors believe the Company enjoys

significant strengths and advantages in competing for business, some of the competitors are significantly larger which

could allow them to offer similar services for lower prices than the Company would be prepared to match, or launch
. new product offerings with significantly enhanced features. Consequently, these competitors could materially

adversely impact the scale of the Company’s revenues and its profitability. In response to this, the Company maintains

a broad customer base. The Company also mitigates the risk by establishing strong relationships with its customers,

developing value-creating solutions and detivering excellent service performance while being cost competitive in its

day to day business. The development team are continually working towards both enhancing, and augmenting, the

services the Company currently offers. The product board meet regularly to keep abreast of the new technology which

could enhance the Company's service portfolio.

Network

The Company provides an essential service to an extensive client base, many of whom rely on the provision of that

service for their major internet presence. The service we provide to customers is dependent on the continued operation

of our diverse fibre network which connects our data centre estate. Should the network fail there would be an adverse

impact on customers and any diminution in the level of service could have serious consequences for customer

acquisition and retention. The Company has implemented a resilient network throughout its data centre estate with no

single points of failure to ensure the likelihood of network failure is minimised.

Inflationary pressure

The largest cost base is the people cost which is managed centrally with annual salary awards and reviews of our

benefits packages to staff. Monthly reviews are undertaken of the cost base and the Group, and so the Company, is

in discussion with customers and suppliers where it is seeing any inflationary impact. In addition, the Group’s pricing

model is regularly reviewed to ensure that contracts are priced adequately to cover inflation risk.

FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT OBJECTIVES AND POLICIES
The Company’s financial instruments comprise cash and liquid resources and leases together with various items such
as trade debtors and trade creditors that arise directly from its operations. The main purpose of these financial
instruments is to provide finance for the Company’s operations.
Liquidity risk
The Company seeks to manage financial risk to ensure sufficient liquidity is available for ongoing operations and to
meet foreseeable needs and to invest cash safely and profitably. As discussed in Note 20, the Group parent company
secured a new Revolving Credit Facility ("RCF") in which the Company acts as a guarantor. The RCF totals £145
million and was secured from a syndicate of three banks. Liquidity risk from this is considered low as the Group was
in compliance with all covenants under its original banking facility arrangements throughout the 31 March 2025
reporting period. In addition, Directors of the Group have prepared robust budgets and forecasts for the years ending
31 March 2026 and 31 March 2027 respectively. These budgets and forecasts take into account the financial covenant
requirements as well as possible downside scenarios. Even under the downside scenario, the Group is forecast to
maintain sufficient liquidity and comply with all financial covenants without requiring mitigating actions. As a result,
risk associated with this is deemed to be low.

liability in the statement of financial position. The right-of-use asset is measured at cost, which is made up of

commencement date to the earlier of the end of the useful life of the right-of-use asset or the end of the lease
term. The Company assesses the right-of-use asset for impairment under IAS 36 ‘Impairment of Assets’ where
such indicators exist.

. Taxation
Current tax is the tax currently payable based on taxable profit for the year. Deferred income taxes are
calculated using the liability method on temporary differences. Deferred tax is generally provided on the
difference between the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities and their tax bases. In addition, tax losses
available to be carried forward, as well as other income tax credits to the Company, are assessed for recognition
as deferred tax assets.
Deferred tax liabilities are provided in full, with no discounting. Deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent
that it is probable that the underlying deductible temporary differences will be able to be offset against future
taxable income. Current and deferred tax assets and liabilities are calculated at tax rates and laws that are
expected to apply to their respective period of realisation, provided they are enacted or substantively enacted
at the reporting date. Deferred tax assets and liabilities arising in the same tax jurisdiction are offset.
Changes in deferred tax assets or liabilities are recognised as a component of tax expense in the statement of
. comprehensive income, except where they relate to items that are recognised directly in other comprehensive
income or equity in which case the related deferred tax is also recognised in other comprehensive income or
equity accordingly.
Share based payments
The Company operates equity-settled share-based remuneration plans for its employees. All goods and services
received in exchange for the grant of any share-based payment are measured at their fair values. Where
employees are rewarded using share-based payments, the fair values of employees’ services are determined
indirectly by reference to the fair value of the instrument granted to the employee. This fair value is appraised
at the grant date and excludes the impact of non-market vesting conditions (for example, profitability and sales
growth targets).
All share-based remuneration plans are ultimately recognised as an expense through profit or loss with a
corresponding credit to ‘retained earnings’.
For share-based payment awards based on market conditions, such as total shareholder return %, the grant date
fair value of the share-based: payment is measured to reflect such conditions and there is no true-up for
~ differences between expected and actual outcomes.
If vesting periods or other non-market vesting conditions apply, the expense is allocated over the vesting period,
based on the best available estimate of the number of share options expected to vest. Estimates are subsequently
revised if there is any indication that the number of share-based incentives expected to vest differs from
previous estimates. The two main vesting conditions that apply to share options relate to the achievement of
annual objectives and continuous employment. Any cumulative adjustment prior to vesting is recognised in
the current period. No adjustment is made to any expense recognised in prior periods if share-based incentives
ultimately exercised are different to that estimated on vesting.
Upon exercise of share-based incentives the proceeds received net of attributable transaction costs are credited
to share capital, and where appropriate share premium.
