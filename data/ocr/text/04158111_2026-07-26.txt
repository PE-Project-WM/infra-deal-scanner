Strategic Report
For the year ended 30 April 2025
The directors present their Strategic Report of Sports Loyalty Card Limited (‘the Company’), for the
year ended 30 April 2025.
REVIEW OF THE BUSINESS .
The year ended 30 April 2025 represented a pivotal transition for the Company, marking the point at
which the Company moved from a period of platform investment and operational strengthening into a
phase of accelerated, scalable growth. During FY2025, the Company delivered strong financial
performance while laying the strategic and organisational foundations required to support its next stage
of expansion across the UK and international markets.
Akey milestone in FY2025 was the Company’s engagement with Alix Partners to support management
in the development of a comprehensive Long Range Plan (LRP) and medium-term business strategy.
This work provided an external, rigorous validation of Reward’s strategic direction, financial
trajectory, and investment requirements. The LRP establishes a clear roadmap for sustainable,
profitable growth and underpins management’s ambition to scale the business while improving margin
quality, operational leverage, and long-term value creation.
The LRP is anchored around the Company’s differentiated flywheel business model and the synergies
between its three interconnected business units: Customer Engagement, Commerce Media, and
Consumer Insights. By deepening integration across these capabilities, the Company’s aims to create
a self-reinforcing growth engine in which:
* customer engagement with banks increases addressable audiences;
* those audiences drive higher retailer and advertiser activation through Commerce Media;
* the resulting transaction data fuels richer Consumer Insights; and
¢ enhanced insights further improve engagement, activation, and monetisation across the ecosystem.
This flywheel model is central to the Company’s competitive differentiation and underpins the
Company’s ability to deliver high-quality, recurring revenues with attractive margins.
FY 2025 also saw the Company strengthening its leadership team with James House stepping in as the
Company’s Chief Commercial Officer and Yekaterina Gusin as Chief Financial Officer. Under the
refreshed leadership team, the Company refined its strategic priorities and aligned them closely with
the outputs of the AlixPartners LRP process. Management focus is firmly on executing against a clearly
articulated growth plan, prioritising disciplined investment, margin expansion, and international
scalability.
LONG RANGE PLAN AND GROWTH STRATEGY
The Long Range Plan (LRP) developed in collaboration with AlixPartners defines a growth strategy
designed to deliver sustained, profitable expansion. The plan leverages the Company’s established
platform, proprietary data assets, and strategic partnerships to support organic growth, margin
expansion, and cash generation.
The strategy is built around three strategic pillars:
Elevate Customer Engagement: the Company will deepen relationships with existing banking
partners while expanding its footprint through new bank wins in the UK and priority international
markets. The strategy extends beyond traditional personalised card-linked offers, delivering next-
generation, end-to-end engagement programmes that increase customer lifetime value and strengthen
long-term bank partnerships.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
Economic/marketplace
During the 12 months to 30 April 2025, the UK economy continued to experience heightened risks and
uncertainty, characterised by persistent inflationary pressures, elevated energy and living costs, and
subdued consumer sentiment. GDP growth projections for 2025 remained modest, around 0.8%, as
households faced higher utility bills and businesses passed on increased labour costs, all of which
constrained spending power during the period... International conditions further amplified uncertainty,
with escalating global trade tensions and volatile policy environments weighing on business investment
confidence. For UK-focused companies reliant on domestic consumer spending, these headwinds
underscored ongoing exposure to fluctuating demand conditions, reinforcing the need for strategic
resilience and, where possible, diversification beyond the UK market. The Company continues to
expand into Europe, the Middle East and Asia, aiming to deliver its model to a diverse customer base,
in an area that is ripe for growth in the card-linked offers industry. This will reduce the reliance on the
UK retail market and position the Company for future success.
Credit risk
Large receivables with retailers expose the Company to insolvency risks, heighted by economic
pressures. While credit insurance mitigates some risks, it does not cover all retailers. To address this,
the Company uses advance payments, direct debits, shorter credit terms, and closely monitors
accounts. Risks also include holding programme funds in bank accounts. These measures, alongside
continuous review, help manage credit risk effectively.
Business resilience and reliance on suppliers
Operational disruptions, whether intemal or external, could lead to revenue loss and additional cost.
disaster recovery measures and operational redundancies mitigate these risks. The Company relies on
third-party providers for key functions under strict service level agreements (SLAs). Due diligence
ensures supplier reliability, and service credits partially offset potential losses.
Long-selling cycle to achieve growth with banks and other financial institutions.
Lengthy selling cycles with financial institutions and other large organisations increase the risk of non-
recovery of business development expenses. The Company mitigates this through multi-year
commitments and limited termination rights. By improving its security posture, compliance processes,
and certifications, the Company can reduce onboarding timelines and increase probability of successful
agreements.
Data security and cyber threats.
Handling personal (and payment) data requires strong controls to reduce the risk of operational
disruption, reputational harm, regulatory penalties and customer loss. Any material weakness in
technical or operational controls could increase remediation costs, affect service delivery and hinder
growth, particularly as hybrid working continues to expand the attack surface.
The Company applies layered security controls, including strict access management through Role
Based Access Control (RBAC) to enforce need-to-know access, alongside ongoing monitoring and
enhanced assurance over cloud service providers. The Company also uses Cato, a cloud-delivered
Secure Access Service Edge (SASE) platform, to provide secure connectivity for sites and remote users
and to apply consistent security policy across the network. Security governance is supported by
recognised standards and assurance, including maintaining PCI DSS Level 1 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022
certification.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES (continued)
Data access and regulatory developments
Access to spend data is fundamental to loyalty and consumer engagement programmes, but creates
risk if third-party data arrangements are not fully consistent with customer terms and permissions.
, Misalignment could result in regulatory action, contractual disputes, and reputational harm.
Data protection requirements continue to evolve across the jurisdictions in which the Company
operates, including the UK, EU, UAE and other parts of the Middle East. Changes to privacy and cross-
border data transfer rules could affect commercial arrangements, product design and operational
processes. The Company maintains a privacy compliance framework aligned to applicable
requirements, overseen by a Data Protection Officer, supported by ongoing horizon scanning and
updates to policies, controls and supplier governance as needed. ISOMEC 27001:2022 certification
supports the Company’s ability to evidence effective information security governance and
accountability.
Competition
The card-linked offer (CLO) marketplace continues to grow with more brands adopting CLOs, drawing
more competition into the field. Larger competitors leverage their scale, while smaller firms secure
niches through emerging technologies and trends. The result is that the Company’s market share could
come under pressure in the future from either type of competitor. To stay competitive, the Company
must monitor consumer preferences, technological advancements, and regulatory changes. Introducing
customer satisfaction surveys can help refine offerings and align services with customer needs.
Additionally, maturing controls with the quality management framework will enhance reliability and
reinforce the Company’s reputation for excellence. Combined with an agile approach to market trends
and diversified revenue streams, these efforts position the Company to outperform competitors and
: maintain a strong market presence.
Furthermore, enhancing controls within the quality management framework will strengthen
operational reliability and reinforce the Company’s reputation for high standards.
Combined with an agile response to market trends and a broadening of revenue streams, these
measures aim to mitigate competitive risk and support the Company in sustaining a strong and
differentiated market position.
Risks inherent in international operations
Expanding into international markets introduces both currency exposure and operational complexity,
as the liabilities arising from customer loyalty and consumer engagement programmes are typically
denominated in the local currencies of the regions in which they operate. Fluctuations in exchange
rates can increase the cost of fulfilling these obligations and adversely affect profitability. As the
Company grows its global footprint, it will need to align programme assets with these locally
denominated liabilities to mitigate exchange-rate risk. This creates additional operational demands,
as it may require holding funds in multiple currencies or implementing hedging strategies—both of
which involve management expertise, ongoing oversight, and associated costs. Furthermore, net
revenue generated from international operations may vary significantly from expectations due to
movements in exchange rates over time.

Financial risk management
The Company’s activities expose it to a number of financial risks including credit risk and liquidity
risk.
Credit risk
The Company's principal financial assets are cash at bank and trade and other receivables, which
represent the Company's maximum exposure to credit risk in relation to financial assets.
The Company's credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The Company trades
principally with recognised, creditworthy parties. Receivable balances are monitored on an ongoing
basis with the result that the Company's exposure to bad debts has not been significant.
The amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of allowances for doubtful receivables. An
allowance for impairment is made where there is an identified loss event which, based on previous
experience, is evidence of a reduction in the recoverability of cash flows.
The credit risk on liquid funds is limited because the counterparties are banks with high credit-ratings
assigned by international credit-rating agencies, although the Board recognises in the current economic
climate these indicators cannot be relied upon exclusively.
The Company has no concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread over a large number of
customers.
Liquidity risk
The Company manages its risk to a shortage of funds by maintaining a minimum level of cash at bank.
The Company has a credit facility with Barclays Bank (see Note 18). This enables the business to
develop without increasing risk to a shortage of funds in the future.

that the loss event had a negative effect on the estimated future cash flows of that asset that can be

estimated reliably.

An impairment loss in respect of a financial asset measured at amortised cost is calculated as the

difference between its carrying amount and the present value of the estimated future cash flows

discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. For financial instruments measured at cost less

impairment an impairment is calculated as the difference between its carrying amount and the best

estimate of the amount that the Company would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the reporting

date. Interest on the impaired asset continues to be recognised through the unwinding of the discount.

Impairment losses are recognised in profit or loss. When a subsequent event causes the amount of

impairment loss to decrease, the decrease in impairment loss is reversed through profit or loss.

26. Restatement of prior period
The Company has restated its financial statements as follows: :
a) Revenue from retail transactions within loyalty programmes not correctly recognised
The Company previously incorrectly recognised retail revenue arising from loyalty
programme transactions on a gross basis, with rewards earned by loyalty programme
members recorded as part of revenue and as a corresponding expense within cost of sales. In
accordance with FRS 102, Section 23 Revenue, rewards credited to loyalty programme
members represent consideration payable to customers and do not constitute payment for a
distinct good or service received by the Company. Accordingly, such amounts should be
recognised as a reduction of revenue rather than as a cost of sale. In addition, amounts
relating to the release of the reward redemption liability (breakage income) were recognised
as a reduction in cost of sales rather than within revenue. The impact of the adjustment
: required for the year ended 30 April 2024 was to decrease revenue by £26,485,395 from
£49,799,046 to £23,313,651; and decrease cost of sales by £26,485,395 from £32,132,604
to £5,647,209. The adjustment has no impact on the Company’s profit for the year, total
assets, total liabilities, or net assets as at 30 April 2024.
b) Reclassification of Reward redemption liability
The Company’s Reward redemption liability of £19,208,719 as at 30 April 2024 was
previously presented within provisions as a non-current liability in the statement of financial
position. Following a review of the nature of this balance, it has been reclassified and is
. now presented within creditors in current liabilities. This resulted in decrease in the
Company’s net current assets of £19,208,719 as at 30 April 2024. The reclassification has
no impact on the Company’s profit for the year ended 30 April 2024. :
