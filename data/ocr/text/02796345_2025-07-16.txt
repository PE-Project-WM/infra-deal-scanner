Principal activities
Arelion UK Limited (‘Arelion’, ‘the Company’) is a private Company incorporated and registered in England &
Wales and is limited by shares.
The Company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Arelion Holding UK Limited (‘Holding’), UK. Arelion UK Limited
and Arelion Holding UK Limited are part of the globally active Arelion Group. :
The principal business activities of the Company involve providing telecommunication services and developing,
producing and maintaining infrastructure of a fibre-based network, and connecting several countries on the
European continent and in the United States (US).
Review of business
The Arelion Group (formerly Telia Carrier Group) has a strong position in the international carrier market,
serving the communications industry with quality IP and cross-border services. Arelion Group is the leading
European carrier with 70,000 kilometres of network and the fastest IP growth rate in Europe. It provides direct
internet connectivity via its global iP network to 85 percent of all European broadband service providers.
Arelion UK Limited operates in two major segments: Data and Voice. The Data segment offers a range of
products designed for different customer segments and user scenarios. For data transport at global level, the
Company offers IP Transit and capacity in the form of wavelengths. These are primarily designed for larger
operators and content providers. The Company also sells services to large multinationals. Where the most
common product is company networks (Wide Area Networks, WAN), and to a smaller extent also data centre
services (colocation) and dark fibre. The Voice segment deals with global telephone calls and messaging
services.
The Arelion Group was formed on 1 June 2021, when Arelion AB, owned by Polhem Infra, acquired Telia
Company's Carrier business. The business has a long history and since the early 1990s has been operated as
part of Telia Company. In 2021, Arelion Group maintained its expansion by opening more points of presence in
Europe (eg. in Belgrade, Hannover, Munich, Zurich), North America (Cleveland, Columbus, Memphis,
Sandston), Mexico (Merida, Santa Fe, Tuititian) and Asia (Osaka).
The Company’s gross profit decreased with £1,780K compared to the last year, it is primarily explained by
increased cost of sales by 7%. The operating profit has been reduced by £315k, primarily explained with the
increased cost of sales but the company has reduced the administrative expenses with 16%.

Principal risks and uncertainties :

The Company's activities expose it to a variety of financial risks, including competitive pressure, foreign

exchange risk, credit risk, and liquidity risk. The Company's overall risk management programme focuses on

the unpredictability of financial markets and seeks to minimise potential adverse effects on the Company's

financial performance.

Competitive pressure :

Due to the current network infrastructure and the structural organisation, the Company meets its operational
. expenses from a combination of third-party revenues and services provided to related parties. Demand from

related parties remains strong. Existing contracts signed with companies within the Arelion Group are expected

to drive future growth.

Furthermore, the continuing price pressures experienced by all telecommunications providers including

Arelion means that the increase in bandwidth traffic and associated revenues has not translated into an

increase in gross profit.

Brexit

With effect from 1 January 2022, all telecommunication services provided to an EU Member State are treated

in the same way as to a non-EU State. The same applies to import and export of telecommunication equipment

from or to an EU Member State. This does not have a significant impact on the cost side for additional customs

clearance fee and import tax. The impact of Brexit on foreign exchange risk is discussed below. The company

has so far not recorded any further effects due to Brexit.

Foreign exchange risk

Foreign exchange risk arises when future commercial transactions, recognised assets or liabilities are

denominated in a currency that is not the Company’s functional currency, Pound Sterling (GBP). Previously, the

directors of the Company have not considered foreign exchange fluctuations to be a significant risk to the

company’s operations, however hedges have been entered into due to GBP not being stable against the US

Dollar (USD) and the Euro (EUR) over the last two years. Going forward, the Directors anticipate the foreign

exchange risk to increase as a result of the United Kingdom’s decision to leave the European Union. To manage

this risk, the Company intends to continue entering into foreign exchange hedges with the parent company as

hedging partner.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Credit risk
Credit risk for the Company arises from trade and other receivables, and an amount due from parent, and cash
at bank. Individual credit evaluations are performed on all customers requiring credit over a certain amount.
These evaluations focus on the customers past history of making payment when due and current, ability to pay,
and consider information specific to the customers. The Company will make specific provision for those :
balances which cannot be recovered. The directors are of the opinion that the default risk is low. In addition,
although trade receivables consist of a small number of customers, the Company has policies in place for the
control and monitoring of the concentration of credit risk. For the Voice business the Company reduce the
credit risk based on agreements containing a master netting arrangement.
Liquidity risk
The liquidity of the Company is managed by monitoring the funding requirements needed to meet its current
financial commitments, maintaining cash on hand and assessing whether a loan is required from its ultimate
parent company. Given the Company’s historic cash and cash flow position, the directors are of the opinion
that the Company does not have significant liquidity risk. As the company is part of a cash pooling arrangement
with the parent undertaking, the cash on hand balance is nil for the year ended 2024.
War in Ukraine and the middle east conflict
On 24 February 2022, Russia launched its war of aggression against Ukraine. Western authorities swiftly
imposed sanctions and other measures on the country and organisations deemed to be contributing in various
ways to the continuation of the war. However, telecoms services, in particular access to a free Internet, were
exempted from the sanctions. Along with other global telecoms operators, Arelion decided to maintain existing
connections to operators and other customers in Russia. (Competing operators such as Lumen, Sparkle,GTT
and Cogent continued to offer services and connect more customers to their networks during the year, but
from access points in Europe.) :
Since the outbreak of the war, Arelion conducts its operations in Russia on a “best effort” basis, as the
availability of components, equipment and services from global network equipment suppliers has been
severely restricted. Arelion has suspended all new sales in Russia until further notice. Arelion is also complying
with atl applicable sanctions.
Arelion also follows the increased level of geopolitical tension closely, including monitoring of conflicts such
as recent conflicts in the middle east. Both customers and suppliers are screened and monitored to ensure
compliance with applicable sanctions.

Future developments
The market for global connectivity is expected to continue to grow over time, albeit with increasing volatility.
Demand from content and cloud service providers is expected to remain strong, particularly in relation to
capacity and dark fibre services, while the corporate market is seeing a shift from traditional WAN solutions to
Internet-, cloud- and software-based network solutions. The volume and price trend on the North American
market is expected to be more favourable than in Europe, where demand is weaker, and competition is also
increasing. In line with its business strategy, the Company will continue to increase the number of connection
points in city areas in Europe. The Company will also develop its sales and marketing channels to reach a
broader customer group.

Going concern

company's ability to continue as a going concern. This assessment includes an evaluation of the Groups current

: In satisfying themselves-that the parent company has the means and ability to provide such financial support
if required, the director has understood the available facilities and cash reserves of the parent undertaking,
have reviewed group cash flow forecasts, including sensitivities and challenged the assumptions within. He is
satisfied there is sufficient available headroom on available facilities, including covenant requirements. The
company benefits from a cash pooling arrangement, enhancing its liquidity and financial flexibility. The
company's profitability is influenced by Transfer Pricing Agreements with Arelion AB, which are secured until
31 December 2026 providing stability for the foreseeable future. The management's review of the parent
groups, Arelion AB, going concern status further reinforces the company's financial stability.

Based on these considerations and with the letter of support in place, the director continues to adopt the going

2026.
