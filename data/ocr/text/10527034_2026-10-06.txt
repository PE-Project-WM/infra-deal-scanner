Introduction

The directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025.

Principal Activities

ENKA UK Construction Limited is primarily engaged in the delivery of engineering, procurement, and

construction (EPC) services in the infrastructure and energy sectors. The Company undertakes large-scale,

technically complex projects both within the United Kingdom and internationally. Current operations focus on

nuclear power infrastructure and manpower support services.

Business Review

ENKA UK continued to strengthen its presence in the United Kingdom during 2025 through the execution of

major infrastructure and energy projects. The Company also continued to provide manpower support services to

ENKA projects in Libya.

Following the contract signed in 2021 for the Turbine Island piping prefabrication and erection works at Hinkley
. Point C (HPC) Nuclear Power Plant in Somerset, the project reached full mobilisation during the second quarter

of 2025. Engineering, procurement, prefabrication, logistics, and site construction teams were fully mobilised,

enabling the project to transition into its active execution phase. This represented a significant milestone in the

delivery of one of the UK's largest low-carbon infrastructure projects.

In line with the project timeline, prefabrication of high-pressure (HP) spools and supports was completed by

December 2024. Prefabrication progress for Unit 1 and Unit 2 intermediate and low-pressure (IP/LP) piping has

reached 86%, and overall pipe support fabrication is 76% complete. Preassembly activities on-site began in May

2025, followed by the commencement of permanent works in June 2025.

In July 2024, ENKA UK signed an additional contract to expand its scope to include the verification of thermal

insulation design, procurement of insulation materials, installation of thermal insulation on pipping and

equipment, and painting works within the turbine halls. This additional package further strengthened the

Company's role in the successful delivery of Hinkley Point C project (W13).

; In addition, during 2025 ENKA UK secured the EET Hydrogen (Vertex) project at Stanlow Refinery, marking a
significant expansion of its UK project portfolio beyond the nuclear sector. The project reinforces the Company's
position in the UK's low-carbon energy market and demonstrates its capability to deliver complex engineering,
procurement, construction, and commissioning (EPCC) projects supporting the UK's energy transition.

ENKA UK's active involvement in the Hinkley Point C and EET Hydrogen (Vertex) projects demonstrates the
Company's growing position within the UK's strategic infrastructure and low-carbon energy sectors. These
projects strengthen ENKA UK's long-term presence in the UK market while highlighting its engineering expertise,
, operational capability, and commitment to supporting the UK's energy security and decarbonisation objectives.

level resources should additional liquidity be required. There are no material uncertainties identified that would
cast significant doubt on the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern.

in operational existence for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the financial statements. The
financial statements are therefore prepared on a going concern basis. .

2.5 Turnover
Turnover represents revenue earned from the provision of engineering, procurement and
construction services and manpower support services.
Where the outcome of a construction contract can be estimated reliably, turnover and costs are
recognised by reference to the stage of completion of the contract activity at the end of the reporting
period, measured based on the proportion of contract costs incurred for work performed to date
relative to the estimated total contract costs.
Turnover arising from cost plus fee contracts is recognised on the basis of costs incurred plus a
percentage of the contract fee earned during the year.
Contract costs include all direct material and labour costs and those indirect costs related to contract
performance, such as indirect labour, supplies, tools, repairs and depreciation costs. Selling, general
and administrative expenses are charged to the statement of comprehensive income as incurred.
Provisions for estimated losses on uncompleted contracts are made in full, in the period in which
such losses are determined. Changes in job performance, job conditions and estimated profitability,
including those arising from contract penalty provisions and final contract settlements may result in
revisions to costs and income and are recognised in the period in which the revisions are determined.
Profit incentives are included in turnover when their realization is reasonably assured.
Costs and estimated earnings in excess of billings on uncompleted contracts represent turnover
recognised in excess of amounts billed. Billings in excess of costs and estimated earnings on
uncompleted contracts represent billings in excess of turnover recognised and are included in
deferred contract revenue.
