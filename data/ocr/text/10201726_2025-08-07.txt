: Principal activities

The principal activity of the Company is the provision of telecommunication network services.
Review of the business
M Group Telecom Limited (M Group Telecom”) continues to operate under long term framework
agreements with its client base within the Telecoms industry. We take pride in having valued long -
term relationships with our portfolio of clients by investing in our contracts and people to ensure we
deliver superior services to all our customers.
Company turnover for the year ended 31 March 2025 was £202.4m (2024: £213.8m) and operating
profit was £12.6m (2024: £10.5m). .
The net assets of the Company as at 31 March 2025 were £34.4m (2024: £24.2m).
With the support of its ultimate parent undertaking, M Group Limited, M Group Telecom has maintained
operating scale across our key client base and refocussed our strategy to reflect the changing strategic

, . priorities of our customers and in consideration of the adjacent service and client portfolio of our wider
division. We have successfully delivered a strong year of trading across our frameworks, despite a
changing landscape in the UK Fibre rollout, including an expansion of our in-home capabilities, being
underpinned by our delivery excellence. Business performance remains in line with our strategy, with
expanded service breadth and depth; there has been a slight step-back in revenue as the business
begins to rebalance away from Fibre Build, reflecting the strategic priorities of key clients and market
trends; repair and maintenance and Connections scopes now represent a larger share of revenues as
Network operators manage their enlarged fibre networks. As an established entity within the M Group
Limited Telecom Division, we remain committed to delivering on our strategy and vision to be the
leading Telecom services supplier, offering a complete end-to-end service portfolio across the UK.
The scope and pace of nationwide fibre rollout plans continues to drive demand for our network service

: capabilities across an expanding fibre network footprint.
Key performance indicators (KPIs)
The Board monitors progress on the overall Company strategy and trading performance by reference
to KPls, the principal measures being turnover, EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxation,
depreciation and amortisation), operating profit and accident frequency rate. Group performance
against these can be found in the consolidated financial statements of Minerva Equity Limited (the
‘Group’).
The 12 Month Rolling Accident Frequency Rate (AFR i.e. Number of Reporting of Injuries, Diseases
and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations (RIDDOR) incidents and accidents x 100,000hrs / No. of
hours worked) decreased from 0.05 for the 12-months ended 31 March 2024 to 0.02 for the 12
months ended 31 March 2025, demonstrating a positive improvement year on year.

Principal risks and uncertainties
: This Section of the strategic report has been adopted from the group. The Group's risks are reviewed
quarterly at the Group Risk Board and annually a formal review of the principal risks is completed.
The updated principal risks are then shared with the Audit Committee. Below is a summary of the
Group's principal risks, together with the mitigating actions being taken which have been considered
for the company where applicable.
"Business interruption
There are a variety of unpredictable events that could arise and cause a significant degree of
business interruption for the Company. This includes, for example, a failure of a major IT system or
a cyber-attack. .
To mitigate this risk, IT systems and infrastructure are assessed for loss of service impact at the point
of design, have appropriate levels of recoverability and redundancy built in including, where required,
non-IT alternative capabilities, and have periodic risk reviews and testing for vulnerability and
exposure.
The Group has a UK managed Security Operations centre, monitoring the networks continuously with
highly effective security solutions. These include internet-based security solutions, active monitoring,
Multi-Factor Authentication and penetration testing. In addition, the Group holds an ISO 27001
accreditation and has developed crisis management and business continuity plans to mitigate the
risk of any business interruption events. The Group has a recovery business plan to mitigate the
impact of a cyber-attack.
Contract Management
The Company's long term contracts periodically come up for renewal. There is a risk that the
. Company may not renew its framework contracts with existing clients during a competitive tender
process, impacting on turnover and profitability. Framework contract renewal risk is mitigated by
delivering a quality service, a strong health and safety performance and an effective bid process and
has resulted in an excellent renewal track record.
Skills shortages
The Company requires a large workforce, across the UK, with a range of skills to deliver high-quality
work on time for our clients. We operate in markets where skill shortages are common across specific
roles; these may vary over time as the UK skills market fluctuates dependent on demand and major
UK projects. The Company invests heavily in training and development to ensure everyone reaches
their maximum potential and stays with the Group as long as possible. Succession plans are in place
for key roles, and we have mapped top talent and identified development opportunities for these
individuals.

Health and safety

‘The Company’s operations are complex and involve working in situations that could result in

significant health and safety risk if not managed effectively. The Company promotes a culture that

puts safety first and so has established minimum standards, policies, procedures and safe working

practices that must be followed by everyone working for, or on behalf of, the Company. The Board is

provided with assurance that these practices are being followed, and health and safety risks are being

effectively managed via our in-house specialist Safety, Health, Environmental and Quality teams,

who are embedded into every Division across the Group. .

Reliance on supply chain

The nature of the work delivered by the Company means that we are reliant on being able to obtain

the materials needed, as well as access to individuals that are adequately skilled to deliver this work.

The Company uses subcontractors, alongside its large directly employed workforce, to deliver work

for clients and so there is a risk that any disruption to the supply chain (materials or labour) would

impact the ability of the Company to deliver services to its clients.

Reliance on supply chain (continued)

This risk is mitigated by establishing preferred supplier relationships with a small number of suppliers,

and subcontractors, for all key areas within the supply chain. This not only helps to ensure continuity

and stability within our supply chain but also means that we are able to deliver the best value to our

clients. Suppliers are paid promptly, and performance of suppliers is regularly reviewed with actions

taken as required.

Future developments ‘

The current year financial performance of M Group Telecom is in line with our growth strategy. We

continue to build on existing relationships with large operators, supporting critical national

infrastructure, as a preferred partner. :

The Company is expecting to see consolidation of a re-balancing of services provision in the next

year and beyond as the business continues to deliver on existing opportunities; we expect to deliver

a work mix skewed towards non-Fibre Build services however we expect to broadly maintain our

business scale. This is achievable through the increasing strength and depth in our core business

combined with Group relationships and end to-end service offerings in the next year.

Communities and the environment (continued)

To reduce the impact of our business on climate change, M Group achieved a significant milestone

with the validation of a near-term science-based target from the Science Based Targets initiative

(SBTi) in August 2024. This target forms the foundation of our Divisional decarbonisation strategy,

guiding our efforts to reduce carbon emissions.

To achieve meaningful carbon reduction, we have worked to prioritise data quality and understanding

our carbon footprint hotspots. As such, we undergo third-party audits in line with ISO 14064

greenhouse gas accounting standards and participate in the Achilles Carbon Reduce programme.

Analysing our carbon footprint allows us to identify hotspots and implement practical solutions.

Notable initiatives include transitioning to electric vehicles, optimising transport routes to reduce fuel

consumption, and integrating renewable energy sources into our operations.

Further to this work, we have extended our carbon footprint assessment beyond our own

organisational boundary to include our most significant emissions—those arising from Scope 3

Purchased Goods and Services. As part of our ambitions to continuously improve, we are shifting

from using average industry emissions factors to obtaining verified data directly from suppliers.

This supplier engagement programme is set to grow year-on-year and is further reinforced by the

updated Supplier Code of Conduct issued by Group Procurement.

To address the impacts of climate change to our business, infrastructure, and people; we applied two

internationally recognised future climate scenarios and have integrated these into our risk

management framework and business continuity plans. For each scenario, we have identified

potential impacts, associated risks, and mitigation measures to minimise adverse effects. In FY25,

we reviewed and enhanced this framework to ensure it evolves alongside new information and

technological advancements. This proactive approach ensures that climate-related risks are

reviewed annually as a minimum and updated when necessary to remain relevant.

These efforts underscore our unwavering commitment to environmental stewardship and sustainable

practice, as well as our dedication to reducing the Group's overall carbon footprint.

Financial risk management objectives and policies :
Financial risk faced by the Company include liquidity and funding risk, market risk including interest
rate risk, and credit risk. The Company reviews these risks on an ongoing basis in accordance with
internal policies.
Liquidity risk
The company cash and short-term liabilities are managed centrally by the group, Where the liquidity
and funding risk is the risk that the Group will encounter difficulty in meeting the obligations associated ,
with its financial liabilities. The Group finances its liquidity position and its operations by a combination
of retained profits, bank borrowings and leases. The Group policy is to maintain a level of cash together
with sufficient committed bank facilities to meet liquidity needs and to fund acquisition opportunities as
they arise, to maintain a smooth debt profile and to ensure senior bank debt does not exceed the
covenants ratios set in the senior facility agreement.
Market risk
The Company is exposed to various elements of market risk, which include interest rate risk and :
inflation risk. The Company is not exposed to significant foreign exchange risk as it operates in the UK
and has no overseas subsidiaries. Interest rate risk is the risk that debt issued at variable interest rates
will give rise to cash flow risk, management reviews debt commitments and cash flow forecasts on a
regular basis to manage this risk. Inflation risk is the risk that inflationary uplifts in rates will give rise
to cash flow risk, the Company manages this risk mainly via framework agreements with a contractual
: allowance for annual inflationary uplift.
