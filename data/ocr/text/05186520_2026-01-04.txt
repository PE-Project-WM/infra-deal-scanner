Strategic Report .
for the year ended 31 December 2024

The director presents his strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.

Business overview

Med Cable Limited ("Med Cable", "The Company") is a wholly owned subsidiary of the VEON group. The Company is

100% owned by VEON Algeria Holdings B.V. and its ultimate parent company is VEON Ltd. On 9 September 2019 100%

of Med Cable's issued share capital was transferred from Global Telecom Holdings S.A.E. (GTH) to VEON Algeria Holdings

B.V., where both companies are subsidiaries of VEON Ltd. The Company was incorporated in the UK on 22 July 2004.

The Company owns part of the international segment (Marseille - Annaba Segment) of the first private submarine fibre

optic cable system between France and Algeria, with landing stations in Marseille, Algiers, Annaba and Oran.

The cable, which is currently located in the Mediterranean Sea, is interconnected with other regional terrestrial backhauls

to provide end-to-end connectivity to customers. The system is also equipped with several branching units in order to allow

extensions to the East and West of the Mediterranean Sea.

The Marseille - Annaba segment became operational at the end of 2005 and the Algerian station started delivering services

in June 2006. The extension towards Oran became operational in June 2007.

On 24 November 2022, VEON has entered into an agreement to sell VEON’s Russian operations to certain senior

members of the management team of group entity PJSC VimpelCom and was closed in October 2023. Nonetheless, this

does not have any direct impact on the Company's operating activity.

Review of Business

In May 2010, Med Cable's fellow group company Orascom Telecom Algeria ("OTA"), which owns part of infrastructure,

received notification from the Algerian Regulation Authority of Post and Telecommunications ("ARPT") that all

telecommunications traffic going through its cables should cease effective from 30 May 2010.

Accordingly, the international gateway licence enabling OTA's licence to operate its network cable was suspended and

operation of the cable ceased, resulting in no ongoing revenue or cash inflow for the Company. Without the licence being

granted, Med Cable has no means of generating revenues.

An uncertainty exists as to whether and, if so, when the current restrictions will be removed and as a result the Company

recognised a full impairment of its fixed assets in 2010 financial year. Nevertheless, the Company's director intends to
. recommence operation of the cable and understands that negotiations by OTA continue in this respect with the Algerian

government.

The Company continued to maintain the cable in operational condition through maintenance and operational support by

third-party vendors, which were funded through intercompany loans from group undertakings.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Foreign exchange risk

The Company's foreign exchange risk has been reduced by the settlement of the loans quoted in foreign currency. The

Company is still exposed to foreign currency risk on expenditure incurred in foreign currency however this is not considered

a significant risk.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Company will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they fall due. The director

confirms that the indirect parent entity, VEON Ltd will provide financial assistance in the future to assist the Company in

managing its liquidity risk.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Regulatory risk
Regulatory risk is the risk that the Company will not comply with regulatory body requirements or that regulations imposed
will significantly affect the performance of the entity. A regulatory body in Algeria, ARPT, is responsible for the issuance of
licences to grant mobile operations permission to transmit signals internationally. Med Cable has suffered a full termination
of its transmission operations as a result from OTA not being granted such a licence. There is a risk that the governing
body will not issue the necessary licences to OTA to resume operations. Management reacts to the risk by providing
continuous maintenance on the cable to ensure it is in optimal condition in the event of a sale agreement being negotiated
or the licence being restored.

Financial key performance indicators
During the periods where no revenue is generated, the principal key performance indicator for the Company is operating
loss. Management uses the performance indicator to monitor the overall maintenance and administrative costs incurred to
ensure that the minimum amounts of expenses are incurred during the period of zero revenue generation.
Other key performance indicators
As the Company has no way of generating its revenues, the key performance indicator is to maintain the submarine cable
infrastructure and avoid transmission interruption or down time.

2. Accounting policies - continued
Turnover
Turnover consists of leasing a capacity of the Mediterranean cable and is recognised over the life of any service
agreements in place. .
Cost of sales
Cost of sales are recognised as any cost attributable to the fair maintenance and day to day running expenditure of
the submarine cable. These costs are recognised as and when the expense is incurred.
Cost of sales also includes provisions recognised in respect of obligations arising from estimated costs to complete
contracts. Provisions are recognised when the Company has a present obligation as a result of a past event, it is
probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation, and a reliable estimate can
be made.
Where a provision previously recognised within cost of sales is no longer required—due to changes in estimates,
the resolution of the underlying obligation, or new information becoming available—the reversal of that provision is
recognised as a credit within cost of sales, insofar as it relates to cost estimates associated with the current financial
period.
Administrative expenses
Expenses are recognised in the period that they are incurred on an accruals basis. Where expenses are paid in
advance of the service being received then the amount paid in advance is recognised as an asset on the statement
of financial position and released to the statement of comprehensive income in the period to which it relates.
Operating leases
Payments under operating leases are charged to the statement of comprehensive income on account on a straight-
line basis over the expected term of the lease.
Finance income and expenses
Financing expenses comprise interest payable, unwinding of the discount on provisions and net foreign exchange
losses that are recognised in profit or loss. Financing income comprises interest receivable on funds invested and
net foreign exchange gains.
Interest income and interest payable is recognised in profit or loss as it accrues, using the effective interest method.
Foreign currency gains and losses are reported on a net basis.
Taxation
Taxation for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in profit or loss, except to the extent
that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity.
Current or deferred taxation assets and liabilities are not discounted.
Current tax is recognised at the amount of tax payable using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or
substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.
Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance
sheet date.
Timing differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from
those in which they are recognised in financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that
have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the
timing difference.
Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they
. will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.

Financial assets
Basic financial assets, including trade and other receivables and cash and bank balances are initially recognised at
transaction price, unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at
the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.
Financial assets are derecognised when (a) the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are
settled; or (b) substantially all the risks and rewards of the ownership of the asset are transferred to another party;
or (c) despite having retained some significant risks and rewards of ownership, contro! of the asset has been
transferred to another party who has the practical ability to unilaterally sell the asset to an unrelated third party
without imposing additional restrictions.
Financial liabilities
Basic financial liabilities, including trade and other payables and loans from fellow group companies are initially
recognised at transaction price, unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the debt
instrument is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.
Debt instruments are subsequently carried at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method.
Trade payables are obligations to pay for goods or services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of
business from suppliers. Accounts payable are classified as current liabilities if payment is due within one year or
less. If not, they are presented as non-current liabilities. Trade payables are recognised initially at transaction price
and subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method.
Loans from group companies are initially measured at fair value which is the transaction price. Loans with a fixed
maturity of more than one year are subsequently measured as the present value of future payments discounted at
a market rate of interest. Loans with no fixed maturity are considered to be repayable on demand and are recognized
at transaction price. Any gain on discounting of loans granted by the Company's parent with interest rates that are
not market related are treated as a capital contribution to the Company and recognized directly in equity.
Financial liabilities are derecognised when the liability is extinguished, that is when the contractual obligation is
discharged, cancelled or expires.
Offsetting
Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is
a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to
realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Provisions
A provision is recognised in the statement of financial position when the Company has a legal or constructive
obligation as a result of a past event that can be estimated reliably, and it's probable that an outflow of economic
benefits will be required to settle the obligation. If the effect of time value of money is material, provisions are
determined by discounting the expected cash flows at a pre-tax rate that reflects a current market assessment of
the time value of money and, where appropriate, the risks specific to the liability.
Restoration and dismantling provisions
An obligation to incur restoration and dismantling costs arises when environmental disturbance is caused by the
installation of the submarine cable on the sea floor.
The future costs generaily include the dismantling and scrapping charges for the infrastructure’s total removal.
The majority of dismantling expenditure is incurred at the end of the life of the cable. Although the ultimate costs to
be incurred is uncertain, the Company estimates its respective costs based on feasibility and engineering studies
using current dismantling standards and techniques.
Provision for dismantling costs are recognised at the net present value of the estimated outflow of economic
resources to settle the obligation.
The amortisation or "unwinding" of the discount applied in establishing the net present value of the provision is
charged to profit or loss in each accounting period. The amortisation of the discount is shown as a finance cost,
rather than as an operating cost.

- Impairment of tangible fixed assets
The Company determines whether property, plant and equipment are impaired at least on an annual basis. This
requires an estimation of the value in use of the assets. Estimating the value in use requires management to make

to calculate the present value of those cash flows. As at 31 December 2024 and 31 December 2023 tangible fixed
assets were fully impaired (Note 8).
- Useful life of tangible fixed assets
The useful life of property, plant and equipment is determined by the Company's management based on their
estimate of the period over which an asset is expected to be available for use by the Company along with the
impairment assessment. Their estimate is reviewed and adjusted if appropriate at each financial year end or in -
case of restart of operation of the main asset of the Company
- Dismantling provision
Management has estimated the useful life of the submarine cable at 34 years from the day it first became
operational as set out in the underlying contract. Management has based their estimate from similar estimates
within the telecommunications industry. A contingency of 20% in the budgetary estimate has been included to
provide confidence that the estimate is conservative to fully cover the potential cost which contributed to €141,918
in the provision estimate. The estimate is prepared by management's expert DRG taking into account the
Company's membership in MECMA association. At the end of the cable's useful life a present obligation exists for
dismantling the cable.

tefer to Note 18. The underlying bottom up assessment of the dismantling costs included in the provision calculation

The Company evaluates at every reporting date the existence of contracts with suppliers where their setttement

expected revenue generation over the lifetime of minimum contractual obligation terms and recognises a provision
amount as an amount of the lower of contract termination terms and exit penalties. At the end of the year
management recognised an onerous contract provision of €327,693 (2023: €545,887). Judgment is required to
estimate the economic benefit of the Company's operations which is dependent on the existing licence restrictions
to operate the cable.
