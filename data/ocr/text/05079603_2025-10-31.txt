Introduction
The directors present the strategic report on the affairs of EJ Peak Technology Solutions Limited for the year
ended 28 February 2025.
EJ Peak Technology Solutions Limited specialise in providing innovative digital solutions designed to transform
process control systems. By introducing cutting-edge technologies, we harness the power of data to enhance
workflows, increase output, and reduce costs.
EJ Peak Technology Solutions Limited is part of the wider MARCH® Group which is a leading provider of critical
engineering services, specialising in delivering comprehensive solutions across high-tech, complex, and
: regulated environments. Our expertise spans various sectors, including food and drink, nuclear, defence, and
utilities. We focus on providing critical engineering services that enhance the efficiency of buildings, processes,
and digital control systems. The MARCH® Academy plays a pivotal role in addressing the industry's growing
skills gap by developing multi-skilled engineers who will drive the future of the industry.

have been strong in the period, with gross profit of £4,714k (2024: £4,650k) demonstrating the robustness of
the company’s business model. Net assets have reduced compared to the previous year following the payment
of £2m of dividends. ,
The company continues to develop its strategy and focus on key client relationships. The business continues to
invest in its infrastructure including health & safety, apprenticeships, human resources and finance to
demonstrate its commitment to improving the service offering to its customer base.

Principal risks and uncertainties ,
Asa multi-disciplinary engineering services business operating in an outsourced contracting environment, the
Group considers the following to be its key risks:
Health and safety. The contracts environment is a high-risk environment where failure to follow processes and
procedures can put people’s safety at risk. The Group has an established health and safety structure with
robust systems and processes and regular reviews in place to ensure they remain relevant.
Losses arising on contracts from pricing errors or poor commercial management. The Group has clearly
established procedures for the pricing of individual contracts with approval /authorisation levels, as well as
regular board level reviews within each trading entity.
Recoverability issues on contracts due to the credit risk related to any of the group customer base. The Group
monitors the credit risk of its customers closely, putting in place appropriate actions to address any areas of
concern.
Retention of key staff. MARCH® has a strong ethos of investing in future talent with 158 apprentices and 200
employees in training at any one time. The Group uses this embedded culture of continuous development as a
strategy to attract and retain high quality talent amidst an increasing demand for skilled labour resource. The
Group has three purpose-built MARCH® Academy facilities and a virtual learning Academy.
MARCH@® sees investment in future talent as a key part of our growth plans and future development.
Supply Chain. MARCH® has a disciplined approach to governance and controls. Pricing models are structured
to be protected from inflationary pressures after the bid initiation stage to contract sign off. Clear business
authority levels and commercial policies are regularly updated and communicated to set out the parameters
within which the business can operate and engage commercially. MARCH® has a diverse and well managed
supply chain.
Inflation. The group works with a variety of revenue models which are designed to ensure that the business is
protected from inflationary pressures which could have an impact upon margins. MARCH® complements those
commercial contractual agreements by operating a defined governance structure which is consistently applied
across all MARCH® companies including clear commercial, financial and operational controls to ensure robust
and consistent margin performance.

1.3. Turnover and profit recognition
The turnover shown in the profit and loss account represents amounts due on contracts completed
during the year adjusted for turnover attributable to long term work in progress, exclusive of value
added tax and trade discounts.
Profit on long-term contracts is taken as the work is carried out if the final outcome can be assessed
with reasonable certainty. The profit included is calculated on a prudent basis to reflect the
proportion of the work carried out at the year end, by recording turnover and related costs as
contract activity progresses. Turnover and cost of sales are calculated on a percentage of direct
costs used. Turnover derived from variations on contracts are recognised only when it is probable
that they will result in turnover and that they are capable of being reliably measured. Full provision is
made for losses on all contracts in the year in which they are first foreseen.

, Cumulative turnover is compared with total payments on account. If turnover exceeds payments on
account an amount recoverable on contracts is recognised and separately disclosed within debtors.
If payments on account are greater than turnover to date, the excess is classified as a deduction
from work in progress, with any residual balance in excess of cost being classified within creditors.
Rendering of services
Turnover from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are
provided in accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all of the following
conditions are satisfied:
. the amount of turnover can be measured reliably;
. it is probable that the company will receive the consideration due under the contract;
. the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured
reliably, and

. the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.

1.4 Exceptional items
Exceptional items are transactions that fall within the ordinary activities of the company but are
presented separately due to their size or incidence.

1.5 Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable
of operating in the manner intended by management.
Repairs and maintenance are charged to the profit and loss account during the year in which they
are incurred.

At each balance sheet date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible fixed assets to

amount of the asset is reduced to its recoverable amount. Impairment loss is recognised as an
expense immediately.

1.7 Work in progress
Work in progress is stated at the lower of cost and net realisable value. Cost includes all direct

1.12 Provisions

becomes aware of the obligation, and are measured at the best estimate at the balance sheet date
of the expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account relevant risks and
uncertainties.
When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the balance sheet.
