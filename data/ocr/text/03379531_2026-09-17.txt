STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

Introduction

The Company’s principal activity remains the provision of Secure IT Managed Services and Secure

Collaboration for both Government and Commercial clients.

Business review

Despite a challenging trading environment throughout the year, the Directors consider the Company's overall

financial performance to be satisfactory. Market conditions remained difficult across the SME secure technology

sector, driven by increased regulatory and legislative costs together with continued delays in UK Government

defence spending and an effective freeze on the commencement of new MOD projects. Against this backdrop,

the Company remained profitable, continued to invest in its products and infrastructure, and maintained a strong

balance sheet.

The business continued to improve productivity across all areas through the adoption of Agile development

methodologies and the increasing use of artificial intelligence within its software development processes. These

initiatives have accelerated the delivery of new product capabilities and enhanced the Company's ability to

support secure government clients, major defence contractors and commercial organisations operating in highly

secure environments. Investment also continued in refreshing the Company's internal technology platforms and

managed service infrastructure.

The Company now offers a portfolio of secure technology solutions across five principal markets:

. Defence and Critical National Infrastructure (CNI);

. Industry partners delivering secure capabilities to government;

. Homeland Security;

. Secure warehousing, logistics and distribution; and

: Higher Education and Research.

These solutions are built on modern technology platforms, with a significant proportion of the existing client

base already benefiting from these developments. The Company also secured new commercial customers

during the year while continuing to provide a fully sovereign managed service from its two UK data centres.

The Board was strengthened following the appointment of Sara Sharkey CBE as Strategy Director. Her

appointment brings additional expertise to the Company's strategic direction, product development and market

positioning, supporting the next phase of growth.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal commercial risk continues to be the delayed publication and implementation of the Government's

Defence Investment Policy (DIP), which has resulted in reduced levels of new MOD procurement activity. In

response, the Company has increased its business development activity across adjacent government and

commercial sectors, broadened its customer base and strengthened strategic partnerships. The Directors are

encouraged by the level of contracted revenue, a growing pipeline of opportunities and a number of significant

new customer engagements, providing confidence in a stronger performance during 2026.

Subsequent to the year end, the Company secured a number of important new customer wins and continued to

strengthen its leadership team, further reinforcing the Directors’ confidence in the Company's future prospects.

Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include trade and other debtors, cash and bank balances, are initially
measured at their transaction price (adjusted for transaction costs except in the initial measurement
of financial assets that are subsequently measured at fair value through profit and loss) and are
subsequently carried at their amortised cost using the effective interest method, less any provision
for impairment, unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is
measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.
Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial. The Company's cash and cash
, equivalents, trade and most other debtors due with the operating cycle fall into this category of
financial instruments.
Impairment of financial assets .
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference
between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the
asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.
