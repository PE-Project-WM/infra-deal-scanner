The Directors present their strategic report on Hyperoptic Limited (the “Company”) together with the audited
financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activity
The Company’s principal activity during the year was to develop facilities for the provision of high-speed internet
broadband by deploy and operate a Fibre to the Premise (FTTP) network and through fibre optic cabling in
buildings.
Strategy and business model .
The Board believes it has established a strategy and business model that promotes long-term value for
shareholders.
The Company's strategy is to deploy and operate a FTTP or “full fibre” network in buildings and geographic areas
with a high customer acquisition potential.
Hyperoptic has a focus on multiple dwelling units in both existing and new development and works closely with
freeholders, developers, property managers and residents to bring award-winning gigabit services to residential
and business developments that increases the attractiveness and value of each property. Hyperoptic broadband
is available at selected sites in 64 UK towns & cities. The business model will deliver long-term value by increasing
penetration rates at existing sites, targeting attractive new sites across the UK and further extending the Company's
network reach through its new off-net retail offering.
The Company's goal is to continue to grow its customer base by providing exceptional customer service and
competitive product offerings whilst extending its national fibre network footprint to more than two million homes
and businesses.

Business review
Hyperoptic is on a mission to be the broadband provider of choice for partners, residents and businesses alike,
giving its users the connectivity they deserve, underpinned by five-star customer experience. Hyperoptic continues
to successfully expand its FTTP network reach by working as a true partner to property owners, developers, and
professionals, designing and installing dedicated fibre infrastructure to new buildings and existing developments.
The results for the twelve months to 31 December 2025 show that Hyperoptic is delivering on its ambitions. The
Company continued to grow its full fibre network, turnover and customer base compared with the previous twelve-
month period. This growth has driven an increase in penetration of the existing network footprint and the continued
increase in homes passed during that period.
Total homes passed reached 1,979,771 at 31 December 2025, and the Company took a focused approach to the
continued increase in the network footprint, working with New Build developers and our Build partners.
Hyperoptic’s subscriber base grew by 66,352 (18%) to a total of 440,270 for the year ending 2025.

Financial Review
Turnover for the year was £138.8 million (2024: £113.6 million), an increase of £25.2 million or 22% (2024: £20.2
million or 22%). This represents revenue from subscribers in new homes passed as well as an increase in
penetration in the existing network. The primary driver of the revenue increase was from the increase in subscribers
compared to 2024.
The gross margin increased to 95.3% in 2025 from 76.9% in 2024, primarily due to a reduction in network operating
costs recognised within cost of sales. This reflects a change in lease accounting adopted from 1 January 2025,
under which costs including Ethernet Access Direct (‘EAD’) rentals, Duct and Pole access (‘DPA’) rentals, and Data
Centre leases are no longer presented within cost of sales. While these costs continued to increase on a cash
basis, driven by network expansion and growth in homes passed, their reclassification has resulted in a higher
reported gross margin in 2025.
Distribution costs increased by £2.3 million to £12.0 million (2024: £9.7 million), reflecting growth in subscribers
and sales activity. :
Administration costs increased by £23.8 million to £177.3 million (2024: £153.5 million). The increase was primarily
driven by higher depreciation associated with continued fibre network investment in 2025, as well as depreciation
on newly recognised right-of-use assets following the lease accounting changes. Depreciation increased by £31.8
million to £108.7 million (2024: £76.9 million), of which £16.8 million relates to right-of-use assets.
This increase was partially offset by the absence of a £5.3 million provision for obsolete and slow-moving stock
recognised in 2024.

Financial risk management
Hyperoptic has significant long-term funding requirements to deliver its fibre rollout to two million homes, requiring
active liquidity management, efficient use of committed facilities, and mitigation of interest rate risk.
Treasury monitors financial risk closely, producing rolling 13-week cash flow forecasts to manage short-term
liquidity and optimise drawdowns while minimising interest costs.
Liquidity is supported by a robust governance framework, including monthly covenant and cash flow reviews,
alongside a sensitised long-term forecast to assess funding needs. These are overseen by the CFO and reviewed
by the Board. The business plan is updated annually and reviewed quarterly, with advisors engaged early to support
capital raising and ensure funding is secured ahead of requirements.
The Company is exposed to SONIA- and EURIBOR-linked floating rates. Treasury mitigates this through
derivatives, with 94% of drawn debt (£1,015.2 million of £1,079.3 million) hedged via interest rate swaps, providing
cost certainty. Hedge positions are reviewed regularly to align with forecasts and risk appetite.
The Company remained fully compliant with all debt covenants during the year.

Trends and factors affecting future performance
The UK Government had confirmed the previously set target for 85% of premises to have access to a gigabit-
capable internet connection by the end of 2025. This target has now largely been achieved, with gigabit-capable
coverage reaching approximately 88-89% of UK premises during 2025. Additionally, the UK Government's 2025
Spending Review pushed back the previous Government target for gigabit-broadband to be available nationwide,
the target is now for it to be available by 2032, with nationwide coverage defined as “at least 99%” of premises.
According to the latest data from thinkbroadband, full fibre rollout now covers approximately 84.2% of UK premises,
reflecting continued rapid expansion of FTTP networks.
The industry regulator, Ofcom, is also actively encouraging fibre network investment. Through its most recent
market review, Openreach pricing is regulated to protect competitor cost recovery. Mechanisms are in place to
review. Openreach pricing changes to assess any potential impact on competition. Ofcom has now published its
final Telecoms Access Review (TAR 2026-31), setting out the regulatory framework for UK fixed telecoms markets
from April 2026 through to March 2031, largely maintaining the existing approach while expanding areas subject
to lighter-touch regulation to reflect increased network competition. The review aims to ensure that the UK’s
broadband infrastructure is fit for the future and promotes competition and investment in gigabit-capable
broadband. This sits alongside wider industry transitions, including the ongoing retirement of legacy copper-based
networks. Ofcom has also introduced new consumer protection rules (effective January 2025) requiring that any
mid-contract price increases be clearly specified in pounds and pence at the point of sale, rather than linked to
uncertain future inflation. In addition, the ‘One Touch Switch’ gaining-provider-led switching process for residential
customers was launched in September 2024 and is now operational across the market, although further
enhancements for business switching are still being developed.
Hyperoptic Limited, established in 2011, has targeted buildings and geographic areas underserved by fast internet
connections. Demand for faster, more reliable internet connectivity is growing rapidly, driven by the increased use
of higher bandwidth applications like video communication services, high definition & 4k video streaming, and
gaming. Subsequent changes in home working practices post-Covid-19 have highlighted customers’ reliance on
broadband services. It is likely that more customers in the UK will seek faster, more reliable fibre connections in .
the coming years.
Hyperoptic Limited is one of several companies, both large and small, deploying FTTP networks, and while
significant build activity has taken place in recent years, the market is now entering a more mature phase where
the focus is shifting from network expansion to customer acquisition, utilisation and monetisation of existing
networks. Recent industry developments indicate that investment conditions have tightened, with reduced
availability of funding for smaller alternative network providers (“altnets”), alongside emerging consolidation across
the sector. This presents both opportunities and challenges for the company. Increased awareness of and demand
for fibre services will enhance the potential for the company to expand its customer base and market share. At the
same time, competition is intensifying due to network overbuild in many areas, placing pressure on pricing and
customer acquisition, while overall market growth in connections is beginning to stabilise. There is also an
increasing strategic emphasis across the industry on improving take-up rates and achieving sustainable returns on
invested capital.
In parallel, the UK telecoms industry is undergoing a major structural transition as Openreach retires the legacy
Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN), with all analogue and ISDN-based services due to be switched off by
31 January 2027. This transition presents operational and commercial challenges for providers and customers
reliant on legacy copper-based services, while also acting as a catalyst for increased customer switching, service
upgrades and fibre adoption. As a result, the period leading up to the switch-off is expected to see elevated levels
of market activity and competition as providers seek to retain and acquire customers during the migration process.

In 2025, the size of the vehicle fleet reduced from 451 vehicles in 2024 to 361 vehicles, representing a reduction

Intensity ratio
The majority of emissions created by the Company are from the fuel used in its vehicle fleet and the electricity use
of its equipment. The emissions are driven by the need to install, maintain and run the network equipment so a
primary metric for emissions intensity is emissions per £ million of capex spent. As the business is scaling and
maturing, and the operation of the network becomes a larger proportion of emissions, the Company will put more
emphasis on intensity metrics that track the intensity of ongoing operations so it can increase its assurance on
energy efficiency initiatives, and is therefore also measuring intensity for emissions per 1,000 subscribers.
Capital expenditure for 2025 was £133.9 million (2024: £173.3 million). As a result, total CAPEX intensity for the
period from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2025 was 26.5 tonnes of CO2e per £1 million of CAPEX (2024: 25.4
tonnes of CO2e per £1 million), representing a year-on-year increase of approximately 3.9%. This marginal
increase primarily reflects continued growth in the customer base and the associated rise in operational emissions,
alongside a reduction in overall CAPEX investment during the year. In 2025 the intensity metric presented above
includes only Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions.
The total subscribers for 2025 were 440,270 (2024: 373,918) and the metric for the period from 1%! January 2025
to 315 December 2025 was 8.5 tonnes of CO2e per 1,000 average subscribers (2024: 11.8 tonnes of CO2 emitted
per 1,000 average subscribers). As the business continues to successfully penetrate the constructed network and
drive revenue growth, the effective carbon intensity for delivered product continues to fall on a like for like basis as
a result of improved operational efficiency and reduced Scope 1 and 2 emissions.

expenditure requirements.

Revenue represents network services provided to external customers, at invoiced amounts less value added

Performance obligations and timing of revenue recognition
A significant portion of the Company's revenue is derived from sales of internet and broadband services and
associated installation or termination charges. Internet and broadband services and installation or termination
revenue are considered part of the main obligation to provide network services and hence are recognised
evenly over the period to which the services are granted, as customers simultaneously receive and consume
fibre connectivity over the service period. Revenue is recognised from the date at which the network service
becomes available for use by the customer. Installation or termination services are recognised at a point in
time as they are identified as a distinct performance obligation and is fully completed and transferred to the
customer upfront. Revenue is recognised from the date at which the network service becomes available for
use by the customer.
Where there are multiple connections within a contract, each connection is considered part of a series of
distinct services that are substantially the same and have the same pattern of transfer to the customer and
hence form one performance obligation. There are no obligations for refunds, returns or warranties.
Customers are billed for network services on a monthly, quarterly or annual basis, with installation services
being billed up front. Deferred revenue arises where services are billed at the beginning of the contract,
however are recognised over the period during which the service is granted. Accrued income is recognised in
relation to services recognised as revenue but yet to be invoiced. This typically arises where the timing of the
related billing cycle occurs in a period after the performance obligation is satisfied.
