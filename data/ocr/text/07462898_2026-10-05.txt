STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
Introduction
The directors present their strategic report on the financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the Company during the year was that of promoting the products and services of the
immediate parent undertaking. These products and services together provide customers with visibility and
control over network traffic. :
. The Company, Gigamon UK Ltd, is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Gigamon Inc.
Business review
The Company delivered a turnover of £23,222,395 for the year ended 31 December 2025, which has decreased
from £24,947,973 in the year ended 31 December 2024. Administrative expenses have also decreased in the
year to £22,643,054 (2024 - £23,375,393). There has also been a decrease in the profit before tax to £1,157,980
(2024 - £2,084,161). , .
The Company has various overseas branches in Europe, Asia, North America and Australia. During the year the
Company operated in nine separate countries outside the UK, and closed its existing branches in France and
Germany by transferring the assets and liabilities to Gigamon Netherlands B.V. on 1 November 2024. The
Company expects to continue to hire new employees to support its anticipated growth, particularly with respect to
an anticipated increase in sales and marketing efforts.
The Company's profits in the current and previous periods have resulted in a net asset position of £730,013
: (2024 - £12,049,512), and a net current asset position of £527,701 (2024 - £11,788,833).
Principal risks and uncertainties :
A principal risk for the Company is the impact that foreign exchange variances will have on the profits of the
Company. During the year exchange rates resulted in foreign exchange loss of £1,086,562 (2024 - Loss of
£261,447) being recognised in the financial statements, as the Company makes purchases in various
currencies, including remuneration to employees of the branches detailed above, and so is exposed to potential
adverse foreign exchange movements.
The Company minimises its actual exchange losses by holding current accounts in the various currencies in
which it operates. However, a significant proportion of its closing assets and liabilities are denominated in non-
‘sterling currencies, and the translation of these at the period end results in adjustments to the Company's
financial position and performance, which remains outside the control of the Company.
The Company's business and operations have experienced rapid growth in the recent years, and if it does not
appropriately manage its future growth, or is unable to increase the productivity of its existing employees and to
hire, train and manage new employees, as needed, or improve its operational, financial and management
controls and reporting processes and procedures, its operating results will be negatively affected.
Failure to comply with laws and regulations could also harm its business. Due to the global nature of its
business, the Company is subject to the regulation by various foreign governmental agencies, including agencies :
responsible for monitoring and enforcing employment and labor laws, workplace safety and environmental laws,
anti-bribery laws, and tax laws and regulations.
Ultimately, the Company is reliant on its immediate parent Gigamon Inc. As the Company exists solely to
promote Gigamon Inc products and services and does not receive direct sales income, the performance of the
immediate parent is considered a key risk to the Company.

3.2 Revenue

, Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Company, the revenue can be reliably measured, it is probable that the Company will receive the
consideration due, and the costs incurred can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair
value of the consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and
other sales taxes.

3.3 Tangible fixed assets .
Tangible fixed assets under the cost model are stated at historical cost less accumulated
depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is
directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of
operating in the manner intended by management.

At each reporting date the Company assesses whether there is any indication of impairment. If such
indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is determined which is the higher of its fair
value less costs to sell and its value in use. An impairment loss is recognised where the carrying
amount exceeds the recoverable amount.

Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference
between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the
asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.

: Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the
estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The
impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of
