The directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Introduction
Genius Sports UK Limited (‘the Company”) is a leading provider of sophisticated data-driven technology and
services to licensed sportsbooks worldwide supplying sportsbook content and management solutions that
enable customers to maximise performance and revenues.
Business review
The Company's revenues grew to £283m for the year representing an increase of 20% against the previous
year (2023: £235m) driven by the Company's continued leveraging of the Group's key rights agreements and
growth across multiple revenue streams including Inplay, streaming and risk services.
The Company's gross profit margin has increased by 4% to 55% (2023: increased by 9% to 51%) impacted by
changes in the revenue mix.
The Company's earnings before interest, taxation, depreciation, amortisation and Impairment of investments
("Adjusted EBITDA’) for the year increased to an Adjusted EBITDA of £11.3m (2023: £3.9m).
Increased revenues were offset by growth in variable costs such as Hosting and Ad-serving expenses. Further
impacts to administrative expenses included growth in costs associated with key rights deals, wages and
salaries as well as increased recharges from Group entities reflecting the Group's transfer pricing policy and
growth in their underlying costs.

Principal risks and uncertainties
‘The key risks facing the Company are outlined below.
Financial risk management
The Company's debt financing exposes it to a variety of financial risks. In the course of its business, the
Company is exposed to interest rate risk, risk associated with liquidity risk, credit risk, foreign currency risk,
technology risk and rights risk.
Interest rate risk
Interest rate risk arises due to the fluctuations in interest tates, which the company is exposed to through its
borrowing and lending. This is mitigated through using fixed interest rates. ;
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk represents the risk that the Company will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they fall
~ due. The Company's approach to managing this risk is to ensure, as far as possible, that it will always have .
sufficient liquidity to meet its liabilities when due, under both normal and stressed conditions, without incurring
unacceptable losses or risking damage to the Company's reputation. Should the Company ever have difficulty
maintaining liquidity, it will be supported by its ultimate parent entity.
Credit risk
The Company's policy is to provide for any debtor balance whose collection is deemed doubtful. The Company
is exposed to credit risk on its trade and other debtors to the extent they are not specifically provided for as
-doubtful debts. The Company is exposed to credit risk on its intercompany balances, where the recoverability is
assessed based on the performance of its subsidiaries and the group's ability to support these balances.
Foreign currency risk
Foreign currency risk arises both where sale and purchase transactions are undertaken in currencies other
than the respective functional currency of the Company (transactional exposures). The Company does not
have any significant foreign currency exchange transactions other than intercompany balances, therefore this
does not represent a significant risk to the wider group.

Technology risk .
Our future business and financial success will depend on our ability to continue to anticipate the needs of
customers and potential customers, to achieve and maintain broad market acceptance for our existing and
future products and services, to successfully introduce new and upgraded products and services and to
successfully implement our current and future geographic expansion plans. To be successful, we must be able
to quickly adapt to changes in technology, industry standards and regulatory requirements by continually
enhancing our technology, services and solutions. Developing new services and upgrades to products and
services, as well as integrating and coordinating current products and services, imposes burdens on our
product development team, management and researchers. These processes are costly, and our efforts to
develop, integrate and enhance our services may not be successful. In addition, successfully launching and
selling a new or upgraded product or service puts additional strain on our sales and marketing resources.
Expanding into new markets and investing resources towards increasing the depth of our coverage within
existing markets impose additional burdens on our research, systems development, sales, marketing and
general managerial resources. If we are unable to manage our expansion efforts effectively, in obtaining
greater market share or in obtaining widespread adoption of new or upgraded products and services, we may
not be able to offset the expenses associated with the launch and marketing of the new or upgraded product or
service, which could have a material adverse effect on our financial results.
Rights risk
If we lose any official accreditation from one of our league or federation partners, we could lose our exclusive
tights to collect certain data, streams or other properties and our services would be less attractive to
customers. Our revenue may decrease as a result, which could have a material adverse effect on the results of
our operations.
We must use all efforts to retain and acquire key sports data, streaming and other properties and to protect and
enforce our rights. If we are unable to do so or otherwise unable to provide products and services that
customers want, then customers may become dissatisfied and use our competitors’ services. If we are unable
to continue offering innovative services, we may be unable to attract additional customers or retain our existing
customers, which could harm our business, results of operations and financial condition.
Non-financial risks
Regulatory risk
There is a risk that the Group's business model is impacted by future changes in regulations in the online
sports betting industry. The Group regularly reviews activity in this area through proactive discussions with key
industry officials, professional advisors and regulatory bodies where appropriate. The Group is a member of the
World Lottery Association, the European Lotteries and the Toto Association, and is a subscriber to the
Gambling Compliance publications.
A large number of the Group's current customers are online sportsbook operators who market and sell to their
customers on a worldwide basis. Jurisdictions continue to regulate the online gambling market through the
continuation or introduction of licensing regimes. The Group liaises regularly with its customers regarding their
active management of this risk.

The Company's functional and presentational currency is GBP. ;
Transactions and balances
Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency using the spot exchange
rates at the dates of the transactions.
At each period end foreign currency monetary items are translated using the closing rate. Non-
monetary items measured at historical cost are translated using the exchange rate at the date of the
transaction and non-monetary items measured at fair value are measured using the exchange rate
when fair value was determined.
Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the
. translation at period-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign
currencies are recognised in profit or loss except when deferred in other comprehensive income as
qualifying cash flow hedges.
2.18 Operating leases
Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the
lease term.
Benefits received and receivable as an incentive to sign an operating lease are recognised on a
straight-line basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is representative of the time
pattern of the lessee's benefit from the use of the leased asset. :
2.19 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The Company operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is
a pension plan under which the Company pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the Company has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not
paid are shown in accruals as a liability in the Statement of Financial Position. The assets of the
plan are held separately from the Company in independently administered funds.
2.20 Finance income
Interest income is recognised in profit or loss using the effective interest method.

2.21 Share-based payments _ : ,
The Company measures the cost of share based awards including restricted shares and share
options granted to employees and directors based on the grant date fair value of the awards. For
share based awards subject to service and market conditions, the Company recognises the cost for
these awards on a tranche-by-tranche basis (the accelerated attribution method). The Company

Where the terms and conditions of awards are modified before they vest, the increase in the fair
value of the options, measured immediately before and after the modification, is also charged to
profit or loss over the remaining vesting period.
2.22 Capital contribution from parent
Share based compensation charges incurred at a parent company level are recharged to the
subsidiary entities of the Group. Where a share-based payment charge relates to the employees of
this Company, this is not expected to be reimbursed to the Parent and recognised in the income
statement as an expense and recognised as a capital contribution in equity.
Where the share-based payment charge relating to employees of other companies in the Group is
included in the management recharge to the Company, this is recognised in the income statement
as an expense and intercompany payables, as the Company is required to reimburse the Parent for
this expense.

3. Critical accounting estimates and judgements
Estimates and judgements are continually evaluated and are based on historical experience and other
factors, including expectations of future events that are believed to be reasonable under the
circumstances. Although these estimates are based on management's best knowledge of the amount,
events or actions, actual results ultimately may differ from those estimates. Management consider the
following areas to be subject to estimation and judgement.
Capitalisation of Development Costs
. Staff costs are capitalised where they are directly involved in the software development process.
Management estimate the amount of time each employee has spent on software development at each
reporting period and prorate the staff costs accordingly.
Amortisation of software and license costs
Careful judgement by the Directors is applied when deciding whether the recognition requirements for
capitalised development costs have been met under FRS 102 section 18 ‘Intangible assets other than
goodwill’. Before capitalisation commences on a specific project, a business plan is prepared and
approved in order to ascertain that the project meets all criteria of the standard as well as to estimate the
asset's useful life. Judgements and assumptions are made using all information known at the end of the
reporting period.
Impairment of investments
Investments are held in both domestic and overseas subsidiaries. Impairment of the valuation of the
subsidiary needs to be considered when such indicators of impairment arise to ensure that the asset is
not overstated within the accounts. This is performed with reference to and review of the subsidiary
company's performance in the year.
Impairment of software and license costs
: Impairment of the valuation of software and license costs needs to be considered to assess any
indicators of impairment to ensure that the asset is not overstated within the accounts. This is performed
with reference to and review of the carrying value included within the accounts. Under FRS102
management are required to perform impairment: reviews where there is evidence that the value of
intangible assets may have been impaired. If there is evidence of impairment an assessment of software
and license costs value in use (or fair value less costs to sell) is performed. As a result, estimates of
future cash flows are required, together with an appropriate discount factor for the purpose of
determining the present value of those cash flows.
