Strategic Report
The Directors present the Strategic Report of VivoPower PLC (the “Company”) and its subsidiary
undertakings (together, the “Group”) for the year ended 30 June 2025.
Business review and principal activities
VivoPower is an international sustainable energy solutions group. Following the completion during
the year of the divestment of the Critical Power Services business, the Group’s activities are
organised into five reportable segments: Electric Vehicles, Sustainable Energy Solutions, Solar
Development, Digital Assets and Corporate Office. Segment information is set out in Note 4.
The Electric Vehicles segment, conducted principally through the Tembo group of companies in
the Netherlands, Australia and the United Arab Emirates, designs and supplies electric utility
vehicle conversion kits and related products for customised and ruggedised fleet applications.
During the year Tembo continued development of its EUV conversion kits and the Tembo Tusker
electric pickup truck, secured full on-road homologation for the Tembo Tusker in Australia and
New Zealand, executed a definitive Business Combination Agreement with Cactus Acquisition Corp
1 Limited, and entered into a definitive agreement with Sarao Motors in the Philippines in ‘
connection with an all-electric Jeepney programme.
The Sustainable Energy Solutions segment designs, evaluates, sells and implements renewable
energy infrastructure, both on a standalone basis and in support of Tembo electric vehicles. The
Solar Development segment holds the Group’s remaining United States solar development
interests through Caret, LLC and its subsidiary. The Digital Assets segment was established during
the year and reflects the Group’s entry into digital asset activities. Corporate Office comprises the
Company and the central service entities.
The Critical Power Services segment, previously conducted through Aevitas Group Limited and its
subsidiaries, was discontinued following the sale of Kenshaw Electrical Pty Ltd to ARA Group
Limited, which completed on 2 July 2024. The results of that segment are presented as
discontinued operations. The final gain on disposal recognised in the year was $1.648 million,
comprising an initial gain of $0.827 million based on disposal consideration of $0.791 million and
net liabilities disposed of $0.036 million, together with a $0.821 million adjustment to the
estimated costs to sell. Further detail is given in Note 18.
Business model
Tembo combines product design, engineering and homologation capabilities with a capital-light
supply-chain model. Its electric vehicle platform comprises conversion kits for ruggedised and
customised fleet applications and the Tembo Tusker electric pickup range. Tembo relies on external
manufacturing and sourcing partners, including suppliers in Asia, which reduces the need for owned .
assembly facilities and related capital expenditure. Sustainable Energy Solutions complements the
electric vehicle business through the design, evaluation and implementation of renewable-energy
infrastructure, while Solar Development comprises the Group’s remaining U.S. solar-development
interests.

The results for the year reflect the completion of the Critical Power Services divestment and the
Group’s strategic focus on Electric Vehicles and Digital Assets.

Cash flows and funding
Net cash used in operating activities was $8.5 million, offset by net cash generated from financing
activities of $8.8 million, reflecting the Group’s continued reliance on external funding to support
its operating activities.
Based on the Group’s current forecasts, the Directors estimate that the Group will require
approximately $9.7 million of funding to meet its cash requirements over the twelve-month period
to September 2027. The Group continues to actively manage its liquidity through the control and
prioritisation of expenditure and working capital, alongside its capital raising activities and :
existing committed funding arrangements.
The Directors expect the Group’s forecast cash requirements to be met through available cash
resources and the receipt of committed and other funding. However, the timing of receipt of
certain funding remains outside the Group’s direct control, giving rise to uncertainty over liquidity
during the forecast period. Further details of the Directors’ going concern assessment, including
the material uncertainty related to the timing and receipt of funding, are set out in Note 2.2.

The principal risks and uncertainties considered by the Board are:
Q liquidity risk and the ability to raise capital on acceptable terms;

and Digital Asset activities; .
® market demand, competition, regulatory approvals and product quality or safety matters;

The Board monitors these risks through regular review of operating performance, cash flow

Operational scale-up and supply chain
The Group’s strategy depends on Tembo scaling delivery while maintaining technical
specifications and regulatory standards. The capital-light supply-chain model reduces direct
assembly investment but increases reliance on third-party manufacturing, logistics and supplier
performance. Disruption to international supply chains, inflation or geopolitical conditions could
affect component availability, lead times and costs.
The continuing businesses require funding for product development, working capital and public-
company costs. The Group therefore remains exposed to the availability, timing and terms of debt
and equity capital. Liquidity is monitored through cash-flow forecasts, funding plans and ongoing
engagement with lenders, investors and creditors. During the period following the year end, the
timing of remittances under the Group’s private investment placement has been slower than
originally anticipated, including as a result of heightened geopolitical tensions and conflict in the
Middle East, which have contributed to additional restrictions and delays affecting the transfer of
funds from investors in the region. While the relevant funding commitments remain in place, the
timing of receipt is outside the Group’s direct control. Accordingly, management continues to
actively manage expenditure and working capital while progressing the receipt of committed
funding and other financing initiatives.

working capital demands of that business and the ability to concentrate management attention
and funding on the Electric Vehicles and Sustainable Energy Solutions strategy. The Board
concluded that the long-term prospects of the Group were better served by the exit.

3. The capital raising and the Tembo subscription

During the year the Company entered into subscription arrangements under Regulation S of the
United States Securities Act of 1933 giving rise to receivables of approximately $60.5 million,
together with a $10.0 million subscription receivable relating to Tembo, of which $8.7 million
remained unpaid at the reporting date. Where the subscription conditions had not been
completed at 30 June 2025 the corresponding shares had not been issued.

The Board had regard to the interests of existing shareholders, who faced dilution, and to the
Company's obligation under Nasdaq Listing Rule 5550(b)(1) to maintain stockholders’ equity of at
least $2.5 million, non-compliance with which would have put the listing at risk to the detriment of
atl members. It also had regard to the position of employees and suppliers, for whom the Group’s
continued solvency was the primary concern. The Board weighed the dilution and the
counterparty risk attaching to unpaid subscriptions against the alternative of a materially smaller
balance sheet and a listing in jeopardy, and concluded that proceeding was in the interests of
members as a whole.

4. Adoption of the going concern basis and approval of the financial statements

In approving these financial statements, the Board considered the Group’s forecast cash
requirements of approximately $9.7 million for the twelve-month period to September 2027,
together with the Group’s available and committed sources of funding and the timing of their
expected receipt.

In reaching its conclusion, the Board considered the interests of the Group’s key stakeholders,
including employees, suppliers and shareholders, as well as the importance of maintaining
appropriate standards of business conduct and transparent financial reporting. Having considered
the Group’s forecasts, funding arrangements and available mitigating actions, the Board
concluded that the going concern basis of preparation remains appropriate. However, the timing
of receipt of certain committed funding remains outside the Group’s direct control and,
accordingly, a material uncertainty related to going concern exists. Further details of the Directors’
assessment, including the principal assumptions and uncertainties considered, are set out in Note
2.2 to the financial statements.

2.2 Going concern
The financial statements have been prepared ona going concern basis. In making this assessment,
the Directors considered the Group’s forecast cash requirements of approximately $9.7 million for
the twelve-month period to September 2027, together with available cash resources, operating
requirements, creditor and lender obligations, available and committed sources of funding, the
expected timing of their receipt and mitigating actions available to the Group.
At 30 June 2025, the Group had unrestricted cash of approximately $0.1 million and net current
assets of approximately $18.4 million. The Group’s forecast cash requirement is approximately

issuances and entered into further committed funding arrangements, including the Ceres private
placement and subsequent PIPE transactions, as disclosed in Note 28.
The Group’s ability to continue as a going concern is dependent on the timely receipt of funding
under existing committed funding arrangements, including amounts contractually committed
under the Ceres private placement, together with the availability of additional sources of funding
where required. The timing of receipt of certain committed funds remains uncertain. Management
continues to monitor cash resources and forecast funding requirements against the Group’s
operational requirements and strategic priorities. The Group retains flexibility over the timing and
scale of certain discretionary expenditure, including product development, commercial expansion
and other growth initiatives, allowing management to align the pace of investment and
operational activity with available tiquidity. Where necessary, management may prioritise core

expenditure, manage creditor and lender payments and seek additional debt or equity capital.

initiatives adjusted where necessary to preserve liquidity.
