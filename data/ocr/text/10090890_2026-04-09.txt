The Directors present their strategic report on the Company for the year ended 30 June 2025.

Principal activities and review of the business

The principal activities of the Company is to design, build and operate neutral host communications systems within the London

transport network in the United Kingdom (U.K.). Our primary customers include Transport for London (‘TfL’), and the Mobile

Network Operators (‘'MNO’s’) in the U.K. The Company is a 100% owned subsidiary of Boldyn Networks PNE Holdings UK

Ltd, and its ultimate parent and controlling party is Boldyn Networks Global Limited.

The Company's reported financial performance reflects the progress on the build-out of the communications network in the

TfL Underground. The Directors note that the business has performed within its expectations, progressing the core TfL

Commercial Mobile and Emergency Services network construction builds throughout the year.

Key performance indicators

The key performance indicators for the Company include revenue, operating profit, cash flow and net assets, which are

regularly monitored and discussed as follows:

e Revenue: the Company’s revenue for the year ended 30 June 2025 was £11,620,278 (2024: £39,236,224). This
represented a decrease of 70% which is due to emergency services network construction revenue, as disclosed in note
4.

e Profit and loss: the loss before income tax for the year ended 30 June 2025 was £41,202,276 (2024: £29,579,202) and
the loss after tax was £31,488,034 (2024: £22,332,542). The loss was due to the increased cost of operations (fixed cost
base) and progress on the build-out of the communications network in the Transport for London (‘TfL’) Underground,
including operation of the base station hotels, electricity and fees payable to TfL. The Directors note that as the build-out
of the network progress, associated costs will increase accordingly. Administration expenses has also increased in the
year ended 30 June 2025, partly due to the rise in staff costs.

e Cash flow from investing activities is an important metric to-understand the investment in capital expenditure throughout
the year. For the year ended 30 June 2025, payments for property, plant and equipment were £109,757,306 (2024:
£88,678,881) reflecting the progress of the build throughout the year. Capital expenditures relate primarily to the
construction of the TfL network. As at 30 June 2025, the Company's net asset position was £206,448,059 (2024:
£182,936,093). The increase in net assets was driven primarily due to increased capital expenditures.

e The Company was in a net current assets position for 30 June 2025 of £17,214,199 (2024: net current liabilities
£99,660,278). This was largely due to strengthened cash position versus prior year.

e Average number of employees: the monthly average number of employees during the year was 111 (2024: 110).

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Directors acknowledge their responsibility for the Company’s internal control and risk management systems, and for

monitoring their effectiveness. The Directors monitor relevant risks to the business on a regular basis. The purpose of these

systems is to manage, rather than eliminate, the risk of failure to achieve business objectives, to provide reasonable assurance
as to the quality of information, and to maintain proper control over the income, expenditure, assets, and liabilities of the

Company. .

However, no system of control can provide absolute assurance against material misstatement or loss. The Directors have

regard to what controls, in their judgement, are appropriate to the Company’s business and to the relative costs and benefits

of implementing specific controls.

The management of the Company is subject to a number of risks which could adversely affect the Company's future

development. The following is not an exhaustive list or explanation of all risks and uncertainties associated with the Company,

but those considered by management to be the principal risks: :

TfL build program . ,

The Company is part-way through a significant network Build program and, therefore, potential failure to complete this program

within the agreed timeframe or budget poses financial and reputational risk to the Company. The Company mitigates this risk

by ensuring projects are appropriately planned, resourced and coordinated with external parties and key stakeholders. Where
delays are experienced, the Company works with partners to revise build and delivery timelines. The status of all major
projects, including budgets, is reviewed regularly, and the Company shares lessons between projects.

Market environment
Changes to the economic conditions of our customers in the markets in which the Company operates may impact our business.
The Company regularly monitors industry and customer developments, and also continuously engages with its customers to
understand their needs and constraints. In particular, the announced pending merger of two of our major customers Vodafone
and Three UK is a development that we will continue to monitor including potential impacts to future projects.
Technology :
The Company is a provider of neutral host digital infrastructure. New or emerging technology including artificial intelligence
(Al) could have an impact on the demand from our customers and the types of digital infrastructure required.
The Company monitors trends in technology including Al, and the impact on our products and services.
Compliance risks :
The Company's operations are covered by government or industry regulations. The Company has committed resources,
through a dedicated risk and compliance specialist and legal counsel, which supports maintaining compliance with these
requirements presently and in the future. Policies and procedures are in place, mandatory employee training is carried out
and forma! Risk Committee meetings are held to discuss risk and compliance.
Political climate
The political climate within which the Company operates may create challenges and uncertainty. However, the Company's
expansion and capital investments align with the UK government's Wireless Infrastructure Strategy, which aims to improve
the country’s wireless digital infrastructure and promote the widespread adoption of 5G technology. The Company will
contribute significantly to this industry's development.
Global economy, inflationary environment and interest rates
Persistent global economic pressures, such as fluctuations in inflation and interest rates, remain a challenge. Further, in the
current inflationary environment, the Company is exposed to the risk that it is unable to pass cost increases on to customers,
thereby, adversely impacting profitability as some of our customer contracts have fixed or capped escalation clauses. :
Data security and data privacy
The Company is subject to data and privacy regulations, particularly General Data Protection Regulation ('GDPR’). Failure to
comply with legal or regulatory requirements relating to data security or data privacy in the course of the Company's business
activities can result in reputational damage, fines or other adverse consequences, including criminal penalties and
consequential litigation, adverse impact on the Company's financial results or unfavourable effects on the Company's ability
to do business. The Company has appropriate data security policies and procedures in place to mitigate these risks.
Loss of people
The Company's senior executive team is instrumental in implementing the Company's business strategies and executing
business plans which support its operations and growth. Service agreements are in place and the risk of the loss of key
personnel is mitigated by regular reviews of remuneration packages and succession planning within the team.
Health and safety .
Harm to our employees, contractors and/or the public because of our actions and operations can impact our reputation. The
Company has health and safety management systems in place including policies, safety plans, incident reporting and regular
monitoring.
Sustainability
Sustainability is essential to the world and our business. Through providing resilient communication infrastructure, we believe
we can positively impact communities and move society forward. An inability to meet our sustainability targets and continue
to evolve and mature our initiatives could impact our reputation with our investors, customers, employees and the communities
in which we operate. We publish an annual sustainability report and monitor our progress against targets monthly. Boldyn
Networks has also joined the United Nations Global Compact, the world’s largest corporate sustainability initiative aimed at
mobilising a global movement of sustainable companies.
Exposure to price, credit, liquidity and cash flow risk
‘The Company is not exposed to any significant price risk.
Credit risk refers to the risk that a counterparty will default on its contractual obligations resulting in financial loss to the

until the uncertainty associated with the variable consideration is subsequently resolved. Amounts received that are subject
to the constraining principle are recognised as a refund liability.

would have been £1,162,028 higher (lower).

Note 12. Right-of-use assets (continued)
Additions to the right-of-use assets during the year were £7,911,274 (2024: £3,915,082).
The Company leases commercial space around London for its network assets, known as base station hotels, and offices.
These agreements range from 4 to 25 years with, in some cases, options to extend. Some leases have escalation clauses.
During the year, the entity assessed its portfolio of right-of-use lease assets for indicators of impairment in accordance with
IAS 36 Impairment of Assets. This assessment was undertaken whenever events or changes in circumstances suggested
that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. As part of this review, an impairment indicator was identified in
relation to the leased premises at 2 Kingdom Street, which the entity exited during the year and which had previously served
as the registered office. Following the decision to vacate the property, the recoverable amount of the associated right-of-use
asset was determined to be nil. The carrying amount was therefore written down in full, resulting in a total impairment charge
of £1,132,674 recognised in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income for the year.
