Review of business ;
Inmarsat Global Limited (the “Company”) is a private company limited by shares’ incorporated in the United Kingdom and
domiciled in England and Wales. The results of the Company are consolidated into Inmarsat Group Holdings Limited (the -
“Group”).
The Company is a subsidiary of Viasat, Inc. (“Viasat”), a global communications company listed on the NASDAQ stock exchange.
On 30 May 2023, Viasat completed the acquisition of Connect Topco Limited (“Topco”, together with its subsidiaries, the “Inmarsat - ,
Group”) (which indirectly owns 100% of the share capital of the Company), in exchange for approximately $550.7 million in cash
. and 46.36 million shares of Viasat common stock valued at $2.1 billion at the closing of the transaction (the “Viasat Transaction”).
Since then, the Company has actively progressed its integration into the wider Viasat group organisation. .
; These financial statements cover the period from 1 January 2024 to 31 March 2025, which is a period of 15 months. This is not the
- company’s first accounting period. The company has changed its accounting reference date from.31 December to 31 March to better -
align its financial reporting with its ultimate parent company, Viasat. In accordance with Section 391 of the Companies Act 2006, the
. financial period has been extended and does not exceed the maximum permitted period of 18 months. Comparative figures presented
in these financial statements cover the 12-month period ended 31 December 2023. As a result, the amounts presented in the primary’
statements are not entirely comparable, but provide more relevant and reliable information.
The principal activity of the. Company, as a leading provider of global mobile satellite communications services (“MSS”), is to
provide data and voice connectivity to end users worldwide.
The Company has an in-orbit fleet of owned and operated satellites in geostationary orbit. This includes satellites using our L-band :
and Ka-band platforms, and also the S-band platform. In addition to the established L-band satellite services business, Global Xpress
(“GX”) services offer seamless global coverage and delivery of Ka-band satellite services with broadband speeds for users in the
Government, Maritime, Enterprise and Aviation business lines. The Company specialises in connecting commercial mobility and
: government customers, particularly vessels, aircraft, vehicles and people, often in areas that conventional telecommunications
- networks cannot reach. The Company offers a diverse portfolio of secure, resilient mobile satellite communications services across
the globe, supporting mission- critical operational systems, essential safety communications, core office applications, a growing range
of Internet of Things (“loT”) applications, and in-flight connectivity.
The Company’s complementary fleet of 15 in-service or operational satellites spans Ka-, L- and S- bands, with five Ka-band
satellites, eight high-availability L-band satellites (three of which are contingency L-band satellites that are operational but not .
in commercial service), an S-band satellite that supports the European Aviation Network to provide in-flight connectivity (“IFC”)
"services to commercial airlines in Europe, and an I-6 class hybrid Ka-/L-band satellite (the I-6 F1 satellite).
“In May 2025, subsequent to fiscal period end, two Ka-band highly-elliptical earth orbit satellite payloads (GX10A and GX108B) were put ,
in service to provide polar coverage for government customers, with commercial maritime and aviation services anticipated to follow
during fiscal period 2026. In addition, the Company has six additional geostationary earth orbit (“GEO”) satellites under -
development: three additional adaptive Ka-band GEO satellites (GX 7, GX 8 and GX 9) and three I-8 L-band GEO safety service satellites. _
The Company’s extensive satellite fleet enables it to provide a wide array of high-quality broadband and narrowband services
with near global coverage (including strong oceanic coverage) with greater redundancy and resiliency.
: The Directors are of the opinion that the current level of activity and the period-end financial position are satisfactory. and will remain
so in the foreseeable future. Please refer to the results of the Company on page 39 and the financial key performance indicators review
on page 22. The Directors do not envision any material changes to the nature of the business in the foreseeable future.

Section 172 statement Oo
Section 172 (1) of the Companies Act 2006 requires the Directors to act in the way which they consider, in good faith, would be most
: likely to promote the success of the Company for the benefit of its members as a whole. , .
In doing so, Section 172 (1) requires a Director to have regard (among other matters) to:. : : co
: : (a) The likely consequence of any decision in the long term . :
(b) The interests of the Company’s employees : : .
: (c) The need to foster the Company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others .
a (d) The impact of the Company’s operations on the community and the environment :
(e) The desirability of the Company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business .
- (f) The need to act fairly between members of the Company. .

We consider “principal decisions” as decisions and discussions, which are material or strategic to the Company and those that are
- significant to any of our stakeholder groups. . a a ,

set out below: . : : ; . ue
Inmarsat Group capital & debt consolidation .
O During March 2025, the Board entered a series of transactions to consolidate the Inmarsat Group’s financial and corporate structure.
This included the consolidation of the Group’s debt capital to streamline financing arrangements to align with Viasat Group’s
, strategic growth plans. In addition, the consolidated Inmarsat US Group entities were sold and repositioned within the corporate
structure under a direct subsidiary. of the Company, Inmarsat Holdings Limited, to enhance operational alignment and reporting
efficiency across jurisdictions. , :
Key stakeholder groups considered: Employees, investors (shareholders and lenders}, partners and customers ‘ .

individual comments and generating an overall engagement score of 74%, which places Viasat in the “high engagement” category
and reflects the strength and resilience of the workforce. : : : . a

Principal risks and uncertainties — . :
: Effective risk management is a key part of the Company’s ability to reach both its short-term and.long-term goals. Risk often goes hand
in hand with opportunity, and while it cannot be avoided, it can be ‘thoughtfully identified, assessed, and managed. The
Company's focus is on creating a culture’ that encourages smart risk-taking atid supports sound decision-making to achieve
objectives. Since acquiring the Inmarsat Group, Viasat has been working to establish an overarching risk management policy and
: process. A Viasat group-wide framework is being rolled out to align risk management with business goals and address major risks.
* Viasat has also introduced risk registers at the departmental level, with many completed, as part of a larger plan to develop an
enterprise-wide risk management programme. : .

Climate-related risks and opportunities were identified as part of the Company’s Double Materiality Assessment (“DMA”) process,

sustainability information for financial materiality. This list of IROs and stakeholders was mapped against our value chain business

_ activities, and stakeholders were invited to assess the scale, scope, likelihood, irremediable character and financial impact of each IRO
using a survey format. . : ,

The IRO inventory, stakeholders, assessment process, weighting, scales, scoring and results were reviewed and approved by members
of a cross-functional DMA team, which included representation from legal, enterprise risk management, internal audit, finance, and ~ :
. other teams. The output of this DMA process used statistical analysis to determine the material IROs for the Company, including those
, risks which were climate-related. Material risks are being integrated into the Enterprise Risk Management processes and systems,
which maintains ‘relevant risk registers to support the disposition, review and follow up on risk mitigation activities, as well as .
effectiveness and changes in post mitigation tisk profiles over time. Management of Sustainability and Social Impact, Regulatory :

process, which is overseen by the ESG Steering Committee and subsequently, members of the Executive Management team.

oo Revenue — . .
The Company applies the 5 step-model as required by IFRS 15 in recognising its revenues. A contract's transaction
price is allocated to each distinct performance obligation and recognised as revenue when, or as, the performance
obligation is satisfied. Revenue is only recognised when it is probable that the entity will collect the consideration to
which it will be entitled in exchange for the goods or services to be transferred to the customer. :
Mobile satellite communications service revenues result from utilisation charges that are recognised as revenue over
the minimum contract period. The selection of the method to measure progress towards completion requires judgement | -
and is based on the nature of the products or services to be provided. Because of control transferring over time, revenue
is recognised based on the extent of progress towards. completion of the performance obligation. Deferred income
attributable to mobile satellite communications services or subscription fees represents the unearned balances
“remaining from amounts received from customers pursuant to prepaid contracts.
The Company enters into minimum spend contracts with customers, known as ‘take-or-pay’ contracts, whereby
customers agree to purchase a minimum value of mobile satellite communications services over a fixed period. Any
unused portion of the prepaid contracts or the take-or-pay contracts (‘breakage’) that is deemed highly probable to occur
__ by the expiry date is estimated at contract inception and recognised over the contract period in line with the pattern of .
actual usage of units by the customer. : a
‘Revenue from the sale of prepaid credit is deferred until such.time as the customer uses the airtime and subsequently |
: recognised over time. Breakage from prepaid credit deferrals which is considered highly probable is estimated and
recognised from contract inception. Mobile satellite communications service revenues from capacity sold are recognised
_ ona Straight-line basis over the term of the contract concerned, which is typically between one and 12 months, unless
another systematic basis is deemed more appropriate. :
_ Revenue from spectrum coordination agreements, is recognised at a point in time based on standalone selling prices.
Revenue from service contracts is recognised as the service is provided over time based on the contract period. 7
Revenue of terminals and other communication equipment sold are recognised at the point in time when control is :
: transferred to the customer. Installation revenues relating to this are also recognised at a point in time. Revenue from :
installation of terminals and other communication equipment owned by Inmarsat and used in the delivery of the service :
to the customer is however recognised over the contract term. 7 :
