Strategic report
For the financial year ended 31 December 2025
Introduction
Pico Global Ltd. ("the Company") and Subsidiaries (collectively known as "Pico" or “the Group") is an established
infrastructure service provider, focused on today's global capical markets. Utilizing third party providers, Pico
provides innovative solutions to clients via low latency networking and state of the art infrastructure technologies.
Pico's client base encompasses a full spectrum of market participants including banks, technology vendors, asset
managers, hedge funds, proprietary trading firms, exchanges and liquidity venues.
Services provided include:
e hosting and colocation services,
e hardware and software procurement, installation and management;
¢ monitoring and analytical services;
e = market data collection and redistribution (real time, normalised, historical); and
* connectivity to over 900 exchange and venues products.
Business review
The Group increased revenues by 4.5% during the financial year to £101.3m (2024 - £96.9m) reflecting further roll
out of the infrastructure services, monitoring and analytical services, market data, connectivity and related services
within Europe and the Asia Pacific Region. The Group's loss for the financial year is £2.9m (2024 - £17.9m).
During the year, the Company undertook reviews of its vendor agreements and service pricing and commercial
arrangements, which contributed positively to revenue and gross profit performance. As a result, the gross profit
margin increased from 51.4% to 55.2%, Overall administrative expenses decreased by £9.0m due to tighter
controls on overheads. At 31 December 2025, the Group showed a net liability position of £28.9m (2024 -
£28.7m). At 31 December 2025, the total liabilities of the Group are £107.8m of which £74.1m is owed to the
; Group's affiliates. :
Future developments :
The Group is continuing to expand its global reach while consistently providing the existing customers with quality
services. Growth will also be generated from strategic acquisitions. The Group will also focus on its internal
organization to increase efficiencies and effectiveness.

Going Concern
The financial statements have been prepared on a going-concern basis notwithstanding the fact that the Company
and the Group have incurred a loss in the financial year. -
At 31 December 2025, the Group had net liabilities of £28.9m which is inclusive of intercompany payables of
£74.1m. At the end of the financial year, the Group had a cash balance of £14.9m and no third-party loans. When ~
intercompany payables are excluded, the Group's net assets are £45.2m at 31 December 2025.
The directors consider the basis to be appropriate as the Company and the Group have sufficient facilities available
from its parent company to fund its working capital requirements, including confirming in writing of its intention
not to call for payment of intercompany payables amount until the Company has sufficient available funds, for a
period of at least twelve months from the date these financial statements were approved. Accordingly, they
continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements.

2.4 Revenue

The Group provides a fully managed, multi-deployment trading solution including hosting, multi-asset
class market data, connectivity and regulatory reporting. Financial technology service fees are monthly
fees paid by customers for managed technology solutions, providing infrastructure management,
network connectivity, data centre co-location facilities, and raw and normalized market data. These fees

revenue are recorded as revenue over the course of the integration project.

. Fees received upfront and not yet earned are recorded as unearned technology services revenue and

The Group earns revenue through sale of goods which are recognised when the Group has transferred

For subscription arrangements, the software is loaded onto proprietary hardware and provided together
with related maintenance and support services. The software and hardware are considered a combined

appliances. These separately identifiable, distinct outputs promised to customets are each satisfied at a
point in time and fees related to such performance obligations are recognized as revenue when the
customer obtains control of the promised goods and services.
Principal vs agent
Since the Group often utilizes the goods or services of third parties in fulfilling customer obligations, it
is required to determine whether the nature of the Group's performance obligations is as principal or
agent. The Group has determined it is primarily responsible for fulfilling all promises requested by its’

services on behalf of customers, and has the sole responsibility of establishing the price of the goods
and services with the exception of third-party maintenance arrangements in connection with the sale of
equipment, for which the Group does not believe it is primarily responsible for fulfilling. As a result,
. the Group is in control of the goods and services before transferring those to the customer and acts as
principal for all services except maintenance arrangements in which the Group acts as an agent.
Revenue from maintenance arrangements with third parties are recognized on a net basis with expenses
paid to third parties. All other revenues are recognized on a gross basis and expenses paid to third party
providers are accounted for as a cost of sale.
