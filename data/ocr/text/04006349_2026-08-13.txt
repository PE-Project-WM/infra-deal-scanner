The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2026.
Principal activities
The principal activity of the Company during the year continued to be the provision of software and associated
services for the social care and education sectors in the UK, together with the pursuit of opportunities in selected
overseas markets where complementary market conditions exist for the Company's products and services. The
Company's solutions support customers in reducing costs and improving the quality of the services they deliver.
The Company has ten wholly owned subsidiaries (2025: two) and is part of the wider group headed by its parent
company, Asclepius Topco Limited (together, the “Group”). The increase in subsidiaries during the year principally
teflects the acquisition of MYP Group on 31 July 2025 by an Australian subsidiary within the Group. MYP Group is
an Australian provider of software solutions serving the disability, allied health and aged care sectors, with products
focused on care management, case management and learning management systems. The acquisition is consistent
with the Group's strategy to expand its international presence and strengthens its operations in Australia and New
Zealand.
Business strategy
The Company's strategy is to deliver sustainable growth through the provision and development of software
solutions for the social care and education sectors. This is supported by continuing demand for digital
transformation across public services, including the integration of care and the wider digitisation of service delivery.
The Company seeks to achieve long-term growth through organic revenue development, profitability and cash
generation.
To support this strategy, the Company continues to invest in the development of its existing products and services
in order to maintain competitiveness, support customer retention and secure new business. New products and
services are developed in response to market requirements and customer needs. The Company's strategy also
places importance on maintaining strong customer relationships and attracting and retaining skilled employees,
both of which are important to the delivery of sustainable growth.
The Company also considers selective acquisition opportunities where these would complement its existing
capabilities, broaden its product offering and support future growth. The Company continues to benefit from its
relationship with its parent undertaking, System C Healthcare Limited, and from the wider Group’s presence in the
healthcare market.
Business Review
The Company’s trading results for the year ended 31 March 2026 are set out in the Statement of Comprehensive
Income on page 12. Turnover was £37.6m (2025: £33.4m) and profit before taxation was £7.6m (2025: £5.3m).
With effect from 1 April 2025, the Group early adopted the 2024 amendments to FRS 102 in full. The most
significant impacts of adoption related to the amendments concerning revenue recognition and lease accounting.
The prior year comparatives have not been restated and, accordingly, the results for the year are not directly
comparable with those of the prior year. Further details, including the financial effect of these changes, are set out
in note 1 to the financial statements.
The Directors consider the Company's trading performance during the year to be satisfactory. The statutory result
includes significant non-operational and non-cash charges recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income,
comprising amortisation of intangible assets of £2.6m (2025: £2.5m), accrued interest on loans from Group
undertakings of £2.6m (2025: £2.5m) and share-based payment charges of £0.1m (2025: £0.1m). The Directors
also monitor operating performance using Management EBITDA, a non-GAAP measure, and a reconciliation of
operating profit to Management EBITDA is set out on page 2.
The Company's balance sheet is set out on page 13. As at the balance sheet date, the Company had net assets of
£9.9m (2025: £16.2m).

Market risk
Pressure on public spending and structure change within the social care sector continues to present challenges
and opportunities to the Company and its future revenues. The product portfolio of the Company means that it is
well positioned to support its customers in delivering quality services within public sector financial constraints. The
sales pipeline is expected to remain strong but the procurement periods are often long and complex, making
forecasting revenue streams inherently difficult to predict.
Customer risk
The Company needs to ensure it retains its existing customers and continues to win new customers to meet its
financial forecasts. Customer loss could result in erosion to business revenues and profits. The Company ensures
there are appropriately staffed support and account management teams to focus on maintaining good customer
relationships.
Product risk
The Company has built a strong reputation for the successful development and delivery of its software and services
to the UK social care market. However, product development and successful implementation and support pose
substantial continuing chalienges. Failure to deliver on time to the required quality can result in financial penalties
and reputational damage as well as having potential implications for patient safety.
To mitigate this risk, performance is continuously monitored against contracted commitments and expectations, and
a wide range of experienced personnel are deployed to evaluate performance. A project board is appointed for high
risk projects utilising a combination of expertise as appropriate from technical delivery, finance and commercial
resources.
Business systems risk
The Company needs to ensure that it has appropriate customer facing and intemal systems that enable it to
transact its business effectively. Best of breed suppliers are used to ensure systems are of appropriate quality.
There are also third party audits which review the effectiveness of the Company's systems.
Information security risk
The Company operates software and services in the healthcare sector and is therefore exposed to risks relating to
cyber security, information security and the protection of confidential, personal and sensitive data. A significant
cyber incident, data breach or failure of critical systems could result in service disruption, regulatory action,
contractual claims, reputational damage and loss of customer confidence, and may adversely affect the Company's
financial performance and prospects.
To mitigate this risk, the Company maintains information security policies, procedures and controls designed to
protect its systems, infrastructure and data. These include access controls, security monitoring, vulnerability
management, incident response procedures, staff training and the use of external assurance and certification
processes where appropriate. Information security risks are subject to ongoing review by management and form
part of the Company's wider risk management and internal control framework.
Financiai risks
The Company is exposed to a variety of financial risks in the course of carrying out its business. These can be
summarised as: Interest rate risk, Credit risk, Foreign exchange risk and Liquidity risk.
Credit risk
The Company is exposed to credit risk through its trade receivables with its social care customers. The amounts
presented in the balance sheet are net of allowances for doubtful receivables. There are internal procedures to
monitor customer credit balances, as well as much billing being ahead of service delivery to partly mitigate the risk
of credit default materially impacting business performance. As customers are mostly public bodies, being Local
Councils, the Company has not historically been materially affected by credit default. The Company has no
significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread over a large number of counterparties and customers.
Foreign Exchange risk
The Company mostly carries out its business in British pounds sterling, with minimal financial transactions in other
currencies. As a result it is not exposed to material translation and transaction foreign exchange risk. The Company
regularly reviews its exposure to foreign exchange and should the value of this exposure increase in the future
would consider hedging or other financial methods to mitigate its risk of exposure as appropriate.

Interest rate risk
The Group's external borrowing is held by the Company's parent undertaking, Asclepius Bidco Limited, and bears
interest at variable rates linked to SONIA. Although the Company is not the direct borrower, movements in interest
rates may affect the wider Group's finance costs, cash flows and available headroom, and are therefore considered
by the directors in assessing the Company's forecasts and going concern position. A 0.5% increase in SONIA
would result in additional annual interest charges of approximately £950,000 at Group level. The Group continues
to monitor interest rate exposure and may use hedging financial instruments where considered appropriate.
Liquidity risk
The Company's liquidity risk is managed as part of the wider Group's centralised treasury and funding
arrangements. Liquidity and financing risk includes the risk that insufficient cash resources are available across the
Group to meet operational and financing obligations as they fall due. The Group manages this risk through the
preparation and regular review of short-term and long-term financial forecasts, including profit and loss, balance
sheet and cash flow projections, prepared under both expected and downside scenarios.
The Group monitors forecast cash generation, liquidity headroom and compliance with external debt covenants on
an ongoing basis for all companies within the Group, including the Company. The Group's external bank facility
includes covenant requirements based on cash as a ratio of last twelve months’ adjusted EBITDA. The Group also
has access to a revolving credit facility to support short-term liquidity requirements if required.
In monitoring iiquidity risk, the directors focus principally on forecast cash flow, covenant headroom and the
availability of financing facilities at Group level, as these are the key measures used to assess the Company's
access to liquidity within the wider Group.
Modern Slavery
The Company is committed to ensuring that there is no modern slavery or human trafficking in its supply chains or
in any part of its business. Our Anti-slavery policy reflects a commitment to acting ethically and with integrity in all
business relationships and to implementing and enforcing effective systems and controls to ensure slavery and
human trafficking is not taking place anywhere in its supply chains. The full statement is published on the System C
Healthcare website https:/Avww.systemc.com/.

The Company early adopted the amendments introduced by the FRS 102 Periodic Review 2024 with effect

The Company has also early adopted the other amendments arising from the FRS 102 Periodic Review 2024.
These include amendments relating to concepts and pervasive principles, fair value measurement, financial
instruments, income taxes and other consequential changes. The adoption of these amendments has not had
a material impact on the Company's financial statements, other than as set out below, although related
disclosures have been updated where applicable.
The cumulative effect on opening retained earnings at 1 April 2025 was a decrease of £10.6m in respect of
revised Section 23, Revenue from Contracts with Customers, and £nil in respect of revised Section 20,
Leases.
Leases
The amendments to Section 20, Leases, were applied on initial adoption in accordance with the transition
requirements of the revised standard. Under revised Section 20, the Company recognises a right-of-use asset
and corresponding lease liability for leases, except where the recognition exemptions for short-term leases or
leases of low-value assets are applied.
On initial application of revised Section 20, the Company recognised a lease liability of £0.6m, measured at
the present value of the remaining lease payments, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease
where readily determinable, or otherwise the Company's incremental borrowing rate of 9.75%. A
corresponding right-of-use asset of £0.7m was recognised, measured at an amount equal to the lease liability
adjusted for prepaid or accrued lease payments and dilapidation provisions recognised immediately before 1
April 2025. Two leasehold office leases were treated as short-term leases on transition as the lease term
ended within 12 months of the date of initial application.
in applying revised Section 20, the Company used the practical expedients available on transition. For
contracts previously assessed for the existence of a lease, the Company did not reassess whether a contract :
is, or contains, a lease at the date of initial application. Leases previously classified as operating leases for
which the lease term ended within 12 months of the date of initial application were treated as short-term
leases. Information received and choices made after the date of initial application were applied in assessing
leases previously classified as operating leases, including in determining the lease term where the contract
contains options to extend or terminate the lease.
Revenue
The amendments to Section 23, Revenue from Contracts with Customers, were applied using the modified
retrospective approach and therefore only to contracts that were not completed at 1 April 2025. Comparative
information has not been restated and the cumulative effect of initial application has been recognised in
opening retained earnings at that date.
Under the Company’s previous accounting policy, perpetual software licence sales were recognised at a point
in time when the relevant performance obligations had been satisfied, while implementation services were
tecognised separately over time by reference to the stage of completion, typically based on contractual
milestones. Under revised Section 23, the Company has reassessed these arrangements by applying the five-
step model and concluded that, in certain contracts, the perpetual software licence, implementation services
and other related services provided over the contract term, including support, maintenance and hosting
services where applicable, are not distinct in the context of the contract and therefore comprise a single
performance obligation. Revenue in respect of that combined performance obligation is recognised over time
as control transfers to the customer. The revised requirements have also resulted in the recognition of cost to
fulfil assets in respect of certain implementation costs where the relevant recognition criteria are met. The
Company has elected not to capitalise costs to obtain a contract, including sales commissions, and therefore
expenses such costs as incurred.

2.7 Impairment of fixed assets
At each balance sheet date investments and other non-financial assets are assessed to determine whether
there is an indication that the asset may be impaired. If there is such an indication the recoverable amount of
the asset is compared to the carrying amount of the asset.
The recoverable amount of the asset is the higher of the fair value less costs to sell and value in use. Value in
use is defined as the present value of the future cash flows before interest and tax obtainable as a result of
the asset's continued use. These cash flows are discounted using a pre-tax discount rate that represents the
current market risk- free rate and the risks inherent in the asset. Fair value less costs to sell is defined as the
amount obtainable from the sale of an asset in an arm's length transaction between knowledgeable, willing
parties, less the costs of disposal.
If the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated to be lower than the carrying amount, the carrying amount
is reduced to its recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive
Income.
If an impairment loss is subsequently reversed, the carrying amount of the asset is increased to the revised
estimate of its recoverable amount, but only to the extent that the revised carrying amount does not exceed
the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of depreciation or amortisation) had no impairment
loss been recognised in prior periods. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of
Comprehensive Income.

arises from goodwill or from the initial recognition of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that affects
neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each reporting end date and reduced to the extent
that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available to allow all or part of the asset to be
recovered. Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the year when the liability is
settled or the asset is realised. Deferred tax is charged or credited in the Statement of Comprehensive
Income, except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to equity, in which case the deferred tax is
also dealt with in equity. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset when the Company has a legally
enforceable right to offset current tax assets and liabilities and the deferred tax assets and liabilities relate to
taxes levied by the same tax authority.

2.12 Provisions
Provisions are recognised when the Company has a legal or constructive present obligation as a result of a
past event, it is probable that the Company will be required to settle that obligation and a reliable estimate can
be made of the amount of the obligation.
The amount recognised as a provision is the best estimate of the consideration required to settle the present
obligation at the reporting end date, taking into account the risks and uncertainties surrounding the obligation.
Where the effect of the time value of money is material, the amount expected to be required to settle the
obligation is recognised at present value. When a provision is measured at present value, the unwinding of
the discount is recognised as a finance cost in the Statement of Comprehensive Income in the year in which it
arises.

2.15 Share-based payments
Equity-settled share-based payments are measured at fair value at the date of grant by reference to the fair
value of the equity instruments granted using the Black Scholes model. The fair value determined at the grant
date is expensed on a straight-line basis over the vesting period, based on the estimate of shares that will
eventually vest. A corresponding adjustment is made to equity.
When the terms and conditions of equity-settled share-based payments at the time they were granted are
subsequently modified, the fair value of the share-based payment under the original terms and conditions and
under the modified terms and conditions are both determined at the date of the modification. Any excess of
the modified fair value over the original fair value is recognised over the remaining vesting period in addition
to the grant date fair value of the original share-based payment. The share-based payment expense is not
adjusted if the modified fair value is less than the original fair value.
Cancellations or settlements (including those resulting from employee redundancies) are treated as an
acceleration of vesting and the amount that would have been recognised over the remaining vesting period is
recognised immediately.

2.16 Leases
Accounting policy as a lessee applicable from 1 April 2025
From 1 April 2025, the Company applies the revised requirements of Section 20 of FRS 102 to leases in
which it is the lessee.
At contract inception, the Company assesses whether a contract is, or contains, a lease. A contract contains a
lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for
consideration.
The Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a corresponding lease liability for leases, except where it
applies the recognition exemptions available for short-term leases and leases of low-value assets. Payments
made under short-term leases and leases of low-value assets are recognised in profit or loss on a straight-line
basis over the lease term. Where a contract contains both lease and non-lease components, the Company
accounts for the non-lease components separately where required by the terms of the arrangement.
Right-of-use assets
At the commencement date, the Company measures the right-of-use asset at cost.
The cost of the right-of-use asset comprises the amount of the initial measurement of the lease liability, lease
payments made at or before the commencement date less any lease incentives received, any initial direct
costs incurred, and any provision recognised for costs to dismantle, remove or restore the underlying asset or
the site on which it is located, where applicable.
After initial recognition, right-of-use assets are measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and
accumulated impairment losses. Right-of-use assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis over the shorter
of the lease term and the useful life of the underlying asset.

3 Judgements and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In the application of the Company's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements,
estimates and assumptions about the carrying amount of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent
from other sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are based on historical experience and other
factors that are considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting
estimates are recognised in the year in which the estimate is revised where the revision affects only that year,
or in the year of the revision and future years where the revision affects both current and future years.
Critical judgements
Revenue recognition
Following early adoption of the amended FRS 102 revenue recognition requirements, the Company applies
judgement in identifying the promised goods and services within customer contracts and assessing whether
these represent distinct performance obligations. Judgement is required in determining whether software
licences, implementation services, professional services, support and maintenance services are capable of
being distinct and separately identifiable within the context of the contract. Judgement is also applied in
determining when control of the relevant goods or services transfers to the customer and, accordingly,
whether revenue is recognised at a point in time or over time.
Lease classification and lease term
Following early adoption of the amended FRS 102 lease accounting requirements, the Company applies
judgement in assessing whether contractual arrangements contain a lease and, where applicable, in
determining the lease term. This includes consideration of extension and termination options where the
exercise of those options is reasonably certain based on the commercial circumstances of the arrangement.
Investment impairment
As per the requirements of Section 27 an entity should assess at each reporting date whether there is any
indication that an asset might be impaired. If any indication exists, the entity should estimate the asset's
recoverable amount. If there is no indication of impairment, the recoverable amount is not required to be
estimated. Management have used their judgment to perform a trigger assessment on several extemal and
internal factors to determine if there are indications of impairment for investments held in subsidiaries and
note no such triggers.
Impairment of financial assets
As per the requirements of Section 11 an entity should carry out an assessment to determine if there are
objective indicators of impairment of any financial assets, such as significant financial difficulty of the
counterparty, a breach of contract or it becomes probable that the debtor will enter bankruptcy. If there is an
indicator of impairment a review should be undertaken and provisions recognised as appropriate.
Management have performed an assessment for financial assets and noted no requirement for material
provisions outside of our general bad debt accounting policy.
Critical estimates
Amortisation of intangible fixed assets
The annual amortisation charge for intangible fixed assets is sensitive to changes in estimated useful
economic lives and residual values. These estimates are reviewed annually and updated, where necessary, to
reflect current expectations, including technological developments, future investment plans, economic
utilisation and the expected recoverability of the assets. Further details are set out in note 2.4 and note 12.
