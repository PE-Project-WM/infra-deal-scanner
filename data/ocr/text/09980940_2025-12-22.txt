Strategic Report
For the Year Ended 31 December 2024
Review of the business :
Sierra Nevada Corporation (SNC) is an international organisation that specialises in aerospace, defence,
electronics, engineering and manufacturing. Founded in 1963, the organisation has grown organically and by
acquisition to be a multi-billion dollar organisation with more than 4000 employees across more than 40 locations
worldwide.
Sierra Nevada Corporation Mission Systems UK LTD (SNC MS UK) was formed in 2019 as a wholly owned
subsidiary of SNC.
SNC MS UK delivers tailored solutions to government and commercial customers, with applications in vehicle
integrations, navigation and guidance systems, threat detection and security, tactical systems integration, scientific
research and infrastructure protection.
The company has experienced significant growth through 2024, expanding into new product lines, developing
established products and utilising the wider SNC group to expand its market share within the UK defence industry.
The business aims to continue to grow its market share and establish a firm position within the newly established
product lines while continuing research and development opportunities for both new and existing products.
Key Performance Indicators .
The business reports and monitors a series of financial and non-financial KPl’s to ensure a balanced approach to
management is maintained.
Key KPI's are monitored on a continuous basis. Management maintains a view of the order book. Order backlog
represents the volume of firm orders that will be recognised in the future as turnover.
Turnover and net profit before tax and operational cashflow are also considered to be key financial performance
. indicators for the business and current performance can be found in detail in the profit and loss statement.
The business also measures staff retention, health and safety incident reporting and environmental reporting as
non-financial performance indicators.
Notable KPI's that have materially changed between FY23 and FY24 include:

¢ Turnover: £36.5m (2023: £14.8m), this is representative of the organic growth experienced by SNC MS
UK notably entering new contracts within novel product spaces.

e¢ Operating profit: £8.6m (2023: £2.3m), strong margins have been increased relative to revenue through
robust operational processes and KP! management.

e Cash: £7.7m (2023: £3.2m), cash held by the business has increased markedly through 2024 due to an
increasing contract base along with strong working capital controls.

e Current debtors: £7.5 (2023: £3.0m), current debtors have increased through 2024 due to a notable
increase in amounts owed by group undertakings, accrued income and contract related work in progress
(WIP). While trade debtors remained consistent with 2023. The notable increase is attributable to new
contracts that span more than one financial year.

e Creditors: £5.4m (2023: £3.5m), creditors have increased through 2024 most notably due to an increasing
trade creditors balance. This increase is in line with the business and contract growth. Other increases with
creditors include amounts owed to group undertakings, due to increased intercompany project work, and
corporation tax which is consistent with the business growth and profitability.

Principal risks and uncertainties °
Interest rate risk:
Risk of fluctuations in interest rates that might give rise to changes in the company's profit or loss and the value of
its assets and liabilities. Despite the low-risk exposure as the company does not have third party loans or
borrowings, management maintains a cautious approach to interest rate risk management and continuously
monitors market conditions and adjusts the investment strategy as necessary to optimize returns while preserving
capital. The conservative investment policy prioritises liquidity and capital preservation, ensuring resilience against
unexpected changes in interest rates.
Foreign currency risk:
Risk arising from fluctuations in exchange rates that have an effect on future transactions and the valuation of
assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currency. SNC MS UK conducts business with the wider SNC group,
often making payment in USD. SNC MS UK maintains close communication and coordination with the parent
company regarding currency exposure and adhering to the corporate guidelines.
Global supply chain crisis:
Risk arising from suppliers’ ability to deliver on time can impact forecasted delivery timelines. SNC MS UK conducts
strong due diligence on supply chain risk exposure and employs supply chain diversification and localisation where
possible to mitigate risk. .
Commodity price risk:
Risk arising from changes in prices and market variables relating to commodities required in the businesses supply
chain. SNC MS UK mitigates this risk through a strong supply chain governance structure while also operating a
just in time inventory management strategy where possible to minimise risk exposure.
Political risk:
SNC MS UK recognises that changes within the international political space can present a risk to the defence
industry. The company mitigates this risk by employing trade specialists, maintaining a strong presence in the
defence industry, and by keeping a close relationship with key customer stakeholders.

~The company acknowledges the above risks and has endeavoured to implement risk mitigation measures where
appropriate when reviewed, as part of the ongoing strategic planning and review.
Future developments
SNC MS UK is committed to a continuous improvement ethos within the product and service offering. The company
employs a dedicated research and development business unit committed to improving established products and
developing new offerings.
Moving into the next reporting period, SNC MS UK will be expanding manufacturing capabilities and refining tested
and proven processes with a view to increase throughput and efficiency on established contracts while allowing
new innovative R&D work to commence.
SNC MS UK will encourage organic growth by developing a strong presence in the defence industrial sector, new
marketing initiatives and trade show opportunities will be used to demonstrate new capabilities to customers.
The company will look strategically align with other ‘companies within the SNC group to establish synergies and
potential routes into new markets.

2.3. Turnover
Turnover represents the total invoice value, excluding value added tax, trade discounts and all other
taxes of sales made during the year, and is recognised at the date the service is provided. Where
contracts have milestones, revenue is recognised according to the milestones achieved on those
contracts. Where contracts are not based on milestones, revenue is recognised by reference to the
state of completion, when the costs incurred and costs to complete can be estimated reliably. The
stage of completion is calculated by comparing costs incurred to total expected costs to complete.
Where the outcome of a construction contract can be estimated reliably, revenue and costs are
recognised by reference to the stage of completion of the contract activity at the balance sheet date.

2.10 Financial instruments
Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised when the Company becomes a party to the
contractual provisions of the instrument.
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in
the assets of the Company after deducting all of its liabilities.
All financial assets and liabilities are initially measured at transaction price (including transaction
costs), except for those financial assets classified as at fair value through profit or loss, which are
initially measured at fair value (which is normally the transaction price excluding transaction costs),
unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction. If an arrangement constitutes a financing
transaction, the financial asset or financial liability is measured at the present value of the future
payments discounted at a market rate of interest for a similar debt instrument.
Financial assets and liabilities are only offset in the balance sheet when, and only when there exists a
legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and the Company intends either to settle
on a net basis, or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Debt instruments that comply with all of the condition of paragraph 11.9 of FRS 102 are classified as
‘basic’. For debt instruments that do not meet the conditions of FRS 102.11.9, the company considers
whether the debt instrument is consistent with the principle in paragraph 11.9A of FRS 102 in order to
determine whether it can be classified as basic. Instruments classified as ‘basic’ financial instruments
are measured subsequently at amortised cost using the effective interest method.
With the exception of some hedging instruments, other debt instruments not meeting conditions of
being ‘basic’ financial instruments are measured at fair value through profit or loss.
Commitments to make and receive loans which meet the conditions mentioned above are measured
at cost (which may be nil) less impairment.
Financial assets are derecognised when and only when a) the contractual rights to the cash flows from
the financial asset expire or are settled, b) the Company transfers to another party substantially all of
the risks and rewards of ownership of the financial asset, or c) the Company, despite having retained
some, but not all, significant risks and rewards of ownership, has transferred control of the asset to
another party. :
Financial liabilities are derecognised only when the obligation specified in the contract is discharged,
cancelled or expires.
