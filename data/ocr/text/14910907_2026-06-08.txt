Going concern

We draw attention to the fact that at 31 May 2025, the Company had accumulated losses of £625,511 (2024:

£67,937 as restated).

The accumulated losses as indicated above, may cast doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a going

concern and, therefore that it may be unable to realise its assets and discharge its liabilities in the normal course

of business. .

The ability of the Company to continue as a going concern is dependent on a number of factors, the most

significant of which is the directors’ continued ability to secure additional venture-backed funding to support the

Company's operations, which is typical for software-based businesses.

In assessing the appropriateness of the going concern basis in preparing these annual financial statements, the

Directors have considered and implemented the following measures:

« Cash flow forecasting: Management has prepared detailed cash flow projections for the 12 months following
year end, which indicate that the Company is expected to have sufficient cash resources to meet its
obligations as they fall due.

e Projection accuracy: Projections for the period from 1 June 2025 to 31 March 2026 were compared to actual
results and demonstrated that management's EBITDA (including software development costs capitalised)
assumptions are reliable.

e Strategic initiatives: Management's current focus on operational efficiency, scalability, and strategic
partnerships is expected to result in the business achieving positive EBITDA by the end of 2027.

¢ Asset valuation: The Company's internally developed intangible assets are measured at cost in accordance
with IAS 38, which may not reflect their fair value.

e Post-year-end funding: The Company successfully closed the second stage of its Series A funding round
with a strategic investor during June 2025, valuing the business at approximately £20 million.

1.5 Revenue
Revenue is recognised in accordance with IFRS 15 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers.
The entity earns revenue from its joint venture arrangements by providing access to intellectual property,
data, technology, and/or related services, as set out in the relevant JV agreements.
Under IFRS 15, revenue is recognised by applying the following five-step model:
1. Identification of the contract with the customer (the JV partner)
2. Identification of the performance obligations in the contract
3. Determination of the transaction price
4. Allocation of the transaction price to the performance obligations
5. Recognition of revenue when (or as) the performance obligations are satisfied
Revenue from the JV is recognised when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to the
JV partner, which may occur over time or at a point in time, depending on the nature of the performance
obligation.
Where the entity provides ongoing access to IP, data, or platform services, revenue is recognised over
time on a straight-line basis over the contractual period, as the JV partner simultaneously receives and
consumes the benefits of the services.
JV revenue is measured at the transaction price specified in the contract, net of any variable
consideration, which is recognised only to the extent that it is highly probable that a significant reversal will
not occur.
1.6 Other operating income
Other operating income primarily relates to intercompany recharges and fees receivable.
Intercompany recharges represent amounts recoverable from related parties in respect of costs incurred
on their behalf and are recognised on an accruals basis in the period in which the related costs are
incurred.
Fees receivable comprise agreed amounts recoverable to cover the cost of legal fees incurred in
connection with the preparation and implementation of the sale of shares between the Company's
shareholders. Such income is recognised when the Company has an enforceable right to receive
reimbursement and the amount can be measured reliably.
1.7. Translation of foreign currency
Foreign currency transactions
A foreign currency transaction is recorded, on initial recognition in Pounds, by applying to the foreign
currency amount the spot exchange rate between the functional currency and the foreign currency at the
date of the transaction.
Foreign currency monetary items are translated at the end of the reporting period using the closing rate.
Cash flows arising from transactions in a foreign currency are recorded in Pounds by applying to the
foreign currency amount the exchange rate between the Pounds and the foreign currency at the date of
the cash flow.

1.8 Property, plant and equipment
Items of property, plant and equipment are measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and any
accumulated impairment losses.
If significant parts of an item of property, plant and equipment have different useful lives, then they are
accounted for as separate items (major components) of property, plant and equipment. Any gain or loss
on disposal of an item of property, plant and equipment is recognised in' profit or loss. Subsequent

1. Accounting policies (continued) .
1.10 Financial instruments (continued)
The material accounting policies for each type of financial instrument held by the Company are presented
below:
Trade and other receivables
Trade and other receivables, excluding, when applicable, VAT and prepayments, are measured,
subsequent to initial recognition, at amortised cost.
The amortised cost is the amount recognised on the receivable initially, minus principal repayments, plus
cumulative amortisation (interest) using the effective interest method of any difference between the initial
amount and the maturity amount, adjusted for any loss allowance.
The accounting policy for impairment of trade and other receivables is set out in the loss allowances and
write offs accounting policy.
Borrowings and loans from related parties
Loans from group companies, loans from shareholders and borrowings are classified as financial liabilities
subsequently measured at amortised cost.
Interest expense on borrowings is calculated on the effective interest method, and is included in profit or
loss.
Trade and other payables
Trade and other payables, excluding VAT and amounts received in advance, are classified as financial
_ liabilities subsequently measured at amortised cost.
Cash and cash equivalents
Cash and cash equivalents are stated at carrying amount which is deemed to be amortised cost.
Derecognition
The Company derecognises a financial asset only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the
asset expire, or when it transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of
ownership of the asset to another party. If the Company neither transfers nor retains substantially all the
risks and rewards of ownership and continues to control the transferred asset, the Company recognises
its retained interest in the asset and an associated liability for amounts it may have to pay. If the Company
retains substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership of a transferred financial asset, the Company
continues to recognise the financial asset and also recognises a collateralised borrowing for the proceeds
received.
The Company derecognises financial liabilities when its obligations are discharged, cancelled or they
expire. The difference between the carrying amount of the financial liability derecognised and the
consideration paid and payable, including any non-cash assets transferred or liabilities assumed, is
recognised in profit or loss.

2. Accounting estimates and judgements
The preparation of annual financial statements in conformity with IFRS requires management, from time
to time, to make judgements, estimates and assumptions that affect the application of policies and
reported amounts of assets, liabilities, income and expenses. These estimates and associated
assumptions are based on experience and various other factors that are believed to be reasonable under
the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The estimates and underlying
assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the
period in which the estimates are revised and in any future periods affected.
Critical judgements in applying accounting policies
The critical judgements made by management in applying accounting policies, apart from those involving
estimations, that have the most significant effect on the amounts recognised in the financial statements,
are outlined as follows:
Revenue recognition
Management has exercised judgement in determining the timing of revenue recognition for licence
agreements. Revenue is recognised at a point in time because the licence provides the customer with a
right to use the intellectual property as it exists at the point of transfer. There is no explicit or implicit
obligation for the entity to undertake activities during the licence period that would (a) change the form or
functionality of the intellectual property or (b) support or maintain its value. Accordingly, the licence does
not meet the criteria for recognition over time under IFRS 15.
Going concern basis
Management has applied the going concern basis of accounting in preparing these financial statements.
This judgement includes consideration of the entity's ability to continue as a going concern for the
foreseeable future and is integral to the annual impairment assessment of intangible assets. In forming
this view, management evaluated available financial resources, forecast cash flows, and other relevant
factors supporting the conclusion that the going concern assumption remains appropriate.
Key sources of estimation uncertainty
Impairment of financial assets :
The impairment provisions for financial assets are based on assumptions about risk of default and

Exposure to credit risk
Trade receivables inherently expose the Company to credit risk, being the risk that the Company will
incur a financial loss if customers fail to make payments as they fall due. Trade receivables arise from
related-party transactions.
There have been no significant changes in the credit risk management policies and processes since the
prior reporting period.
Related-party trade receivables: Related-party trade receivables arise from transactions between the
Company and entities viewed by management as related parties. These transactions are conducted on
an arm’s length basis, consistent with the terms and conditions applied to comparable transactions with
third parties, including agreed credit terms of 30 days. These balances are monitored separately due to
the nature of the relationships involved. Details of related-party trade receivables are disclosed in note 19.
Management assesses and monitors credit risk exposure separately for related-party receivables, based
on these distinct risk concentrations.
A loss allowance is recognised for all trade receivables, in accordance with IFRS 9 Financial Instruments,
and is monitored at the end of each reporting period. In addition to the loss allowance, trade receivables
are written off when there is no reasonable expectation of recovery, for example, when a debtor has been
placed under liquidation. Trade receivables which have been written off are not subject to enforcement
activities.
The Company measures the loss allowance for trade receivables by applying the simplified approach
which is prescribed by IFRS 9. In accordance with this approach, the loss allowance on trade receivables
is determined as the lifetime expected credit losses on trade receivables. These lifetime expected credit
losses are estimated using a provision matrix, which is presented below. The provision matrix has been
developed by making use of past default experience of debtors but also incorporates forward looking
