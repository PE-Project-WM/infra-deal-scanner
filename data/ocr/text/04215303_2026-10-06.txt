Strategic Report ;
For the year ended 31 December 2025
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activities
The principal activities of the company are the provision of services for the design, installation, deployment,
integration and ongoing support of in-building ‘smart’ technologies. These include network connectivity
solutions (both wired and wireless), electronic security systems, audio-visual solutions, Internet of Things (loT)
systems (including environmental, energy and occupancy monitoring), and building and workplace
management software platforms.
Fair review of the business
The company continues to maintain a strong position within the UK smart building solutions market,
demonstrating resilience during a year of transition and targeted investment. The company’s strategic focus .
remains on capitalising on growing demand for smart building enablement, driven by the Corporate Real
Estate (CRE) sector’s increasing emphasis on certifications such as SmartScore, WELL and NABERS,
alongside the need for more efficient, connected and sustainable workplaces.
During the year, turnover increased modestly to £29.62m (2024: £29.49m), reflecting continued underlying
demand for the company’s services. Managed Services revenue stabilised at £9.83m (2024: £9.14m), while
Frameworks revenue continued its positive trajectory, increasing to £7.39m (2024: £6.85m). Revenue from
multi-technology design and build contracts decreased to £11.47m (2024: £12.53m), reflecting project timing
and the close-out of a significant individual contract during the year.
Gross profit margin reduced to 24% (2024: 26%), primarily due to project mix and the impact of the contract
close-out. Administrative expenses increased slightly to £7.5m (2024: £7.4m), reflecting continued investment
in strategic growth areas. As a resuit, Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation and Amortisation (EBITDA)
reduced to £106k (2024: £642k). The pre-tax loss of £281k (2024: profit of £326k) is considered non-recurring
in nature, primarily attributable to the completion of a single contract rather than underlying trading.
Notwithstanding this, the company has continued to strengthen its strategic position and operational
capabilities. Service offerings have been expanded across long-standing client relationships, supporting
additional revenue opportunities. The company also secured a significant long-term contract renewal, entering
into a new five-year global framework agreement to provide voice support services, offering enhanced visibility
of future revenues.
The company continues to invest in innovation and sustainability. During the year, it achieved “Works with
WELL’ accreditation for its Sense IQ platform and regained Silver status in the EcoVadis sustainability rating,
“placing it within the top 15% of organisations globally for environmental, social and governance (ESG)
performance. :
The data centre sector has continued to emerge as a key growth area, with the company acting as a strategic
partner to clients and delivering long-term value through its specialist capabilities. In parallel, the company’s
smart technologies revenue stream is forecast to grow significantly, with current projections indicating an
increase of over 250% in 2026. .
Looking ahead, the directors remain confident in the company's prospects. While 2025 reflects a period of
transition, the underlying fundamentals of the business remain strong, supported by a solid pipeline of
opportunities, increasing demand for smart building technologies, and a clear strategic direction. The
company is well positioned to deliver sustainable long-term growth.

Global services .
Since 2010, the company has been the UK and European representative of LGAP, a global alliance of like-
minded service providers with combined annual sales exceeding $400m. With over 38 offices worldwide,
LGAP members.support major corporate clients across North America, South America, Asia Pacific, Europe,
South Africa, Hong Kong, India, China, Australia, New Zealand and Japan. This partnership continues to
provide access to international opportunities, including data centre developments, enterprise office fit outs and
IT integration projects.
The data centre market has continued to expand during the year, with the company increasingly acting as a
strategic delivery partner on large-scale projects, delivering long-term value to global clients through this
alliance. .
The company continues to evaluate opportunities to strengthen its presence within Europe and will maintain
close collaboration with LGAP partners to ensure consistent support for global clients.
Quality and management systems
The company places significant emphasis on quality, excellence and continuous improvement and is
accredited to ISO 9001:2015 (Quality Management System — QMS), ISO 14001:2015 (Environmental
Management System — EMS) and ISO 27001:2022 (Information Security Management System — ISMS).
These standards are embedded within the company’s core business processes and policies, ensuring
consistent service delivery, minimising environmental impact and safeguarding information assets to the
highest standards.
Research and development
The company's commitment to research and development remains focused on advancing its proprietary
digital commissioning and smart building enablement software. This solution continues to evolve, enabling the
integration and normalisation of data from multiple building systems into a unified platform. Its multi-protocol
capability and built-in MQTT broker support interoperability across a wide range of technologies, while
automation of deployment and configuration enhances efficiency and reduces delivery risk.
The smart buildings and ‘PropTech’ market continues to grow, driven by increasing demand for measurable
outcomes in energy performance, occupant wellbeing and operational efficiency. The company’s focus
remains on enabling clients to deliver intelligent, sustainable and high-performing workspaces.
Progress has continued during the year in demonstrating the value of smart building technologies, particularly
in supporting certification standards such as SmartScore, WELL and NABERS. The Smart Building Integration
& Demonstration Lab remains central to this effort, serving as both a client showcase and a development
environment for solutions including ExchangelQ and Sense IQ.
The company’s smart technologies revenue stream is forecast to grow significantly, with projections indicating
an increase of over 250% in 2026, supporting continued investment in innovation.

Financial risk management
Financial risks, including credit, foreign exchange and inflationary pressures, are actively managed through
established policies and oversight by the finance, commercial and procurement teams.
Liquidity risk management
Cash flow is closely monitored to ensure obligations are met as they fall due, supported by effective project-
level financial control. :
Credit risk management
Credit procedures remain robust, with ongoing monitoring of client exposures and prudent contract selection.
Foreign exchange currency exposure
Foreign exchange risks are assessed and managed where appropriate, including the use of forward contracts
when beneficial.
Future business prospects .
Demand for smart, connected and sustainable buildings continues to grow as industry standards become
more defined through organisations such as RIBA, WiredScore, NABERS and the International WELL Building
Institute. These frameworks are accelerating the adoption of integrated digital technologies that enhance
building performance and user experience.
The company is well positioned to benefit from this trend through its expanding portfolio of digital services,
software platforms and smart building solutions. The ongoing transition from traditional, site-based services to
integrated, centrally managed and on-demand digital service models remains a key strategic focus.
The continued growth of the data centre sector represents a significant opportunity, with the company acting
as a strategic partner to clients in this space.
The company’s progress continues to be driven by its people, with ongoing investment in training,
development and culture remaining a priority. ;
While the financial results for the year were impacted by a non-recurring contract close-out, the underlying
business remains strong, supported by a solid pipeline, strong contract retention—including a new five-year
global framework agreement with a long-standing client—and increasing demand for services.
The directors remain confident that the company is well positioned to deliver sustainable long-term growth.

Explanation as to what extent the audit was considered capable of detecting irregularities, including
fraud
Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design
procedures in line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect material misstatements in respect of
irregularities, including fraud. The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities,
including fraud is detailed below.
The objectives of our audit in respect of fraud, are; to identify and assess the risks of material misstatement of
the financial statements due to fraud; to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the assessed
risks of material misstatement due to fraud, through designing and implementing appropriate responses to
those assessed risks; and to respond appropriately to instances of fraud or suspected fraud identified during
the audit. However, the primary responsibility for the prevention and detection of fraud rests with both
management and those charged with governance of the company.
Our approach was as follows:

- We obtained an understanding of the legal and regulatory requirements applicable to the company
and considered that the most significant are the Companies Act 2006, UK financial reporting
standards as issued by the Financial Reporting Council, and UK taxation legislation.

« We obtained an understanding of how the company complies with these requirements by discussions
with management and those charged with governance.

« We assessed the risk of material misstatement of the financial statements, including the risk of
material misstatement due to fraud and how it might occur, by holding discussions with management
and those charged with governance.

» We inquired of management and those charged with governance as to any known instances of non-
compliance or suspected non-compliance with laws and regulations.

- Based on this understanding, we designed specific appropriate audit procedures to identify instances
of non-compliance with laws and regulations. This included making enquiries of management and
those charged with governance and obtaining additional corroborative evidence as required.

There are inherent limitations in the audit procedures described above. We are less likely to become aware of
instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations that are not closely related to events and transactions
reflected in the financial statements. Also, the risk of not detecting a material misstatement due to fraud is
higher than the risk of not detecting one resulting from error, as fraud may involve deliberate concealment by,

1.2 Going concern — .
The directors have carefully considered the company’s operations, trading performance and available
sources of financing when determining the most appropriate basis for preparation of the financial
statements.

. Liquidity is provided through existing cash reserves, and further supported by an invoice discounting
facility, which has sufficient headroom within the facility to the date of signing the accounts in 2026 and
is forecast to continue to be sufficient.

In assessing its ability to continue as a going concern, the directors considered various scenarios,
including severe but plausible downside forecasts, and their impact on liquidity. The assessment
considered the principal risks and uncertainties as set out in the Strategic Report and is dependent on
a number of factors, including reasonable assumptions regarding financial performance, continued
access to borrowing facilities and future market demand for the services offered by the company.

The resultant cash flow forecasts considered the effects of the principal risks and uncertainties
identified on the company's trading performance, confirming the ability of the company to operate, with
sufficient headroom, within the covenants set out in its banking agreements.

Based on these assessments, the directors confirm that they have a reasonable expectation that the
company has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for a period of at least 12
months from the date of approval of these financial statements, and have therefore concluded it
appropriate to continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements.

1.3. Turnover ;
Turnover represents amounts receivable for goods and services net of VAT and trade discounts.
Amounts recoverable on long term contracts, which are included in debtors, are stated at the net sales

. value of the work done after provision for contingencies and anticipated future losses on contracts, less
amounts received as progress payments on account. Excess progress payments are included in
creditors as payments on account.

Revenue is generally recognised as contract activity progresses so that for incomplete contracts it
reflects the partial performance of the contractual obligations. For such contracts the amount of revenue
reflects the accrual of the right to consideration by reference to the value of work performed.

An associate is an entity, being neither a subsidiary nor a joint venture, in which the company holds a

1.9 Stock
Stock are stated at the lower of cost and estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell. Cost
comprises direct materials and, where applicable, direct labour costs and those overheads that have
been incurred in bringing the stock to their present location and condition.

stock over its estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell is recognised as an impairment loss
in profit or oss. Reversals of impairment losses are also recognised in profit or loss.

1.13 Taxation
The tax expense represents the sum of the tax currently payable and deferred tax.
Current tax

or deductible in other years and it further excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The
company's liability for current tax is calculated using tax rates that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the reporting end date. ;

1.16 Leases
Leases are classified as finance leases whenever the terms of the lease transfer substantially all the
tisks and rewards of ownership to the lessees. All other leases are classified as operating leases.
Assets held under finance leases are recognised as assets at the lower of the assets fair value at the
date of inception and the present value of the minimum lease payments. The related liability is included
in the balance sheet as a finance lease obligation. Lease payments are treated as consisting of capital
, and interest elements. The interest is charged to profit or loss so as to produce a constant periodic rate
of interest on the remaining balance of the liability.
Rentals payable under operating leases, including any lease incentives received, are charged to profit or
. loss on a straight line basis over the term of the relevant lease except where another more systematic
basis is more representative of the time pattern in which economic benefits from the leased asset are
consumed.
1.17 Foreign exchange
Transactions in currencies other than pounds sterling are recorded at the rates of exchange prevailing at
the dates of the transactions. At each reporting end date, monetary assets and liabilities that are
denominated in foreign currencies are retranslated at the rates prevailing on the reporting end date.
Gains and losses arising on translation in the period are included in profit or loss.
