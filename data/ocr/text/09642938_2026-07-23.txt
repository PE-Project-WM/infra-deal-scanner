Strategic report
The Directors present their Strategic report for the year ended 31** December 2025.
Principal activities
Evero Energy Holdings Limited (the “Company”) is an intermediary holding company for operating assets within
the wider Evero group. The company and its subsidiaries (the “Group”) manage three bioenergy power generation
assets where waste wood, that would otherwise go to landfill, is used to generate energy, providing renewable,
low-carbon source of electricity to consumers equivalent to the annual power requirement of approximately
123,000 households.
The principal activity of the Company is to act as holding company for the Group.
Strategy and organisation
The Group’s strategy is to develop renewable bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS) on waste wood
assets, to assist in the decarbonisation of the economy and support the UK’s journey to net zero. This has been
endorsed by the UK Government’s selection of the Group as a Priority Project for the HyNet Carbon Capture and
Storage Cluster.
The Group operates three strategically located plants that process approximately 438kt p.a. of locally sourced
waste wood on secure long-term contracts. Electricity is exported to grid or sold directly to industrial customers
via “private wire.” .
The Group is evaluating the installation of carbon capture technology to all three plants that would result in the
capture of 619kt p.a. of carbon emissions. The permanent sequestration of carbon from biogenic sources creates
negative emissions would help offset emissions from other hard to abate sectors. Two of the Group’s plants are
within the catchment area of the UK’s most advanced CCUS cluster, Hynet, with one, Ince Bio Power Limited
being priority listed.

Results and performance
Group revenue for the year ended 31 December 2025 was £73.5 million (2024: £83.1 million), the reduction was

2025. Other income for the year was £10.5 million (2024: £1.7 million), primarily relating to income from the
settlement of a legal claim.
The Group operating profit for the year was £8.6 million (2024: loss of £126.7 million) after charging
administrative expenses of £10.0 million (2024: £11.8million).

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Directors are responsible for managing the Group’s system of internal control, risk management and for
monitoring the effectiveness of controls. The purpose of these systems is to mitigate to the extent possible, rather
that eliminate the risk of failure to achieve business objectives, to provide reasonable assurance to the quality of
the management information and to maintain proper control of the income, expenditure, assets and liabilities of
the Group.
No system of control can provide absolute assurance against the risk of potential misstatement or loss. The
Directors must consider which controls are economically effective and are appropriate to the Group’s business.
Risk management
During the year the Group’s management has carried out a refreshed evaluation of the enterprise-wide risks. This
involved a bottom-up evaluation of all risks involving risk managers from all of the business functions. Risks
were recorded with a measure of likelihood impact before and after applicable controls. The result of this analysis
was reviewed with the Board who considered the Groups tolerance for the different types of risk.
Risks which management considers to be significant are listed below:
Health, Safety and the Environment (HSE”)
The safety of staff and the effect of our operations on the environment are essential aspects of the performance of
the Group. Respect for our surroundings and seeking to prevent harm in all our activities is one of the core values
of the business. Good HSE performance supports the economic success of the business and safeguards the
wellbeing of staff and the environment.
During the year the Group undertook a safety initiative under the “Is it worth the risk?” banner to use results from
safety observations to develop campaigns to develop increased awareness, through on-site meetings and safety
themed signage.
The Group operates an Environmental Management System to demonstrate compliance with permitted emissions
limits, which includes continuous emissions monitoring.
Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk arising from the failure of a counterparty to pay its debts. Each new counterparty is analysed
individually for creditworthiness, before negotiating bespoke contracts and payments terms in conjunction with
each potential transaction. The review includes extemal ratings, where available, and in some cases bank
references. Credit risk arises under commercial contracts, notably on the power purchase agreements and
derivative contracts that are with investment grade counterparties.
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the Group will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they fall due. The
Group’s approach to managing liquidity is to ensure, that it will have sufficient liquidity to fund its liabilities. The
Group continually monitors historic and forecast cash positions, funding requirements and loan covenant
compliance. Current cash balances and future operational cash flow are expected to meet future liquidity
requirements.

Operational risk
Once built, the bioenergy assets must be run efficiently and reliably to deliver the tevels of planned capital return.
Management seeks to maximise plant availability and generation capacity when available. The facilities are
designed with system redundancy, where appropriate, and are maintained to deliver a high degree of reliability.
Single points of failure are analysed, and strategic spares are held to be able to quickly replace items that may have
long delivery lead times.
The Group assets are operated by a mix of in-house and third-party operators under an operation and maintenance
contract. The performance and management of these contracts is key to managing operational risk. Contracts
incentivise the operators to meet targets through a mix of bonus and penalty mechanisms. The recruitment and
retention of suitably skilled and experienced staff is essential to ensuring operational reliability. The Group seeks
to offer competitive employment benefits and provide sufficient training to maintain levels of competency.
Market risk :
Market risk is the risk that changes in markets, such as commodity prices or interest rates, will affect the Group’s
income. The objective of market risk management is to manage and control market risk exposures within
acceptable parameters, while optimising the return on risk. Risk mitigation in this area is governed by policy. The
principal market risks that the Group is exposed to are, fuel prices, interest rates volatility and electricity price risk.
The Group manages fuel price volatility driven by the supply or waste wood. This has been mitigated to the extent
practical through long-term supply contracts and the recruitment of industry specialists to manage this exposure.
The Group has entered into interest rate contracts to swap all floating rate exposure under the current bank loans
agreements to fixed (See Note 25). Power price volatility has been mitigated through forward contracts where
financial contracts have been used to fix the price on a nominal volume of electricity for future settlement. (see
Note 25).
Regulatory risk
The energy industry, and the energy-from-waste within the wider industry is heavily regulated. The regulatory
environment is constantly changing; government policy, the push for a low carbon economy, technology advances
and consumer needs all affect it. There is a risk of adverse impacts from changes in law and policy, such as
environmental, tax and similar legal and policy regimes, including changes in regulatory attitude and behaviours
as a result of shifts in public opinion.
The Group’s business model is in line with society’s needs for low carbon energy and sustainable waste disposal,
which generally provides opportunities for the business, with potentially adverse changes actively managed and
mitigated to the extent this is possible.

Evero Energy Holdings Limited’s business activities are the conversion of waste into energy, playing a pivotal

recovery facilities across the UK, using locally sourced waste biomass to generate low-carbon electricity.
