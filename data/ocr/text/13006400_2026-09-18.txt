FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

Introduction
The directors present their Strategic Report together with the audited financial statements for the year end 31
December 2025.
Group Restructuring
In March 2025, the group completed a corporate legal restructuring to accommodate future funding and debt
financing. Freedom Fibre Limited shareholders contributed 100% of their shares in Freedom Fibre in exchange
for newly issued share capital in Freedom Holdco 1 Limited, mirroring their former shareholdings.
Freedom Holdco 1 contributed 100% of its shareholding in Freedom Fibre to Freedom Holdco 2 in exchange for
newly issued shares in Freedom Holdco 2.
As part of the legal restructuring process a small number of management shares relating to the merger options
were vested with a resulting small dilution for all shareholders. No other ownership or share changes occurred.
Strategic Merger
Following regulator approval, on 29 April 2026, Freedom Holdco 2 Limited acquired 100% of the issued share
capital of Truespeed Communications Limited and County Broadband Limited as part of the merger signed on 4
February 2026, under which InfraBridge-backed Freedom Holdco 1 Limited and Aviva-backed Truespeed
Communications Group Limited, two of the leading UK alternative local fibre providers, signed an agreement to
combine their businesses. Aviva will control Freedom Holdco 2 Limited through Truespeed Communications
Group Limited, with the ultimate parent company becoming Aviva plc.
The transaction brings together two highly complementary full fibre networks, with the combined business
serving more than 412,000 premises Ready for Service (RFS) and around 70,000 customers across the North
West, West Midlands, South West and East of England.
By combining Freedom Fibre’s wholesale, open-access network with Truespeed’s established retail capability,
the group is well positioned to support continued growth across both wholesale and consumer markets. The
merger reflects a shared ambition to build a stronger, more resilient business that can compete effectively in an
increasingly competitive and consolidating sector.
The combined network benefits from strong regional density and limited overlap, enabling greater operational
efficiency and more effective capital deployment. The business is also lowly levered, providing a strong financial
foundation to support future investment and long-term growth.
Following completion of the merger, the combined business will operate as the Freedom Truespeed Group.
Freedom Fibre will continue as the group’s wholesale network, working with its established ecosystem of
partners, while Truespeed and LilaConnect remain customer-facing brands across the expanded footprint.
Nathan Vautier, CEO of the Freedom Truespeed Group, said:
“The completion of this merger is an important milestone for both businesses. By bringing together Truespeed
and Freedom Fibre, we have created a stronger platform with the scale, capabilities and financial strength to
compete effectively in the UK market. We remain focused on delivering high-quality connectivity for our
customers and supporting our partners as we move into this next phase.”
The businesses’ focus ‘in the near term will be on maintaining service continuity and outstanding customer
service, while planning the integration of systems, processes and teams in a phased and considered way.

Financial review
During the year the business ceased new build activity including, by mutual agreement, descoping the remaining
premises of the North Shropshire BDUK gigabit contract. From mid-2025 the company focused efforts on
commercialisation of the existing network, with build limited to a much lower level of conversion to ready for
service. A detailed review of all build areas was undertaken, with areas with no premises passed disposed of
and those with no ready for service impaired. Stock (recognised in assets under construction) is reviewed
annually and has been impaired where build related and not anticipated to be deployed. The carrying value of the
: network was also assessed as part of the Wholesale CGU, which led to a further impairment. This has led to
some one off charges being incurred in the year.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Refinancing
Freedom Fibre secured an extension of its external debt facility with Santander to 30 April 2027. The company
will take reasonable endeavours to refinance the prior to the scheduled maturity date. In the event the facility
cannot be refinanced, the shareholder agreement between Truespeed Communications Group Limited,
Freedom Holdco 1 Limited and Freedom Holdco 2 Limited, permits Freedom Holdco 2 Limited to request
shareholder funding in exchange for a share issue.
Access to funding
As part of the strategic merger (see business review), the company gained access to a further significant
investor (Aviva), a significantly larger footprint and growing customer base. The company has secured
shareholder funding which will fund the operations of the company to the point it becomes cash generative, being
beyond the 12 months from the date of signing these accounts.
Competition
From a group perspective, wholesale competition is faced from BT Openreach (BTOR), Virgin and other altnets.
Benefiting from a 30-year wholesale contract with TalkTalk along with major ISP Zen and a number of smaller
regional ISPs who have been onboarded since 2024, the company has increased its penetration over the last
year. The retail brand LilaConnect is growing against competition from national brands supported by strong
Trustpilot scores, reliable network and competitive pricing. Post year end, the company has benefitted from the
. acquisition of Truespeed, enhancing the footprint and customer base, whilst providing a recognised ISP.
