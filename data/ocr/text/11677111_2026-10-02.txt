The Directors present their Strategic Report on the Group for the year ended 31 December 2025. Paystratus
Group Limited (trading as ‘Weavr') is a private company limited by share capital, incorporated and domiciled in
* the United Kingdom. .

Overview
Embedded Finance is the integration of financial services into non-financial applications by our customers
("embedders"). The benefits are significant - it increases value to their customers, retention rates and lifetime
value - all while offering a frictionless experience and automation to their customers. Use cases include enabling
financial transactions and the issuance of payment cards within business-to-business software-as-a-service
("B2B SaaS") and enterprise resource planning ("ERP") systems to enable integrated payments to suppliers, the
handling of payments between buyers and sellers within marketplaces, and the issuance of loans (such as buy-
now-pay-later - "BNPL") and highly personalised insurance policies as part of the e-commerce checkout process.
The Group's European proposition is delivered through wholly owned subsidiary Weavr (Europe) Ltd in
conjunction with financial partners. Weavr (Europe) Ltd holds a European e-money institution ("EMI") licence and
is regulated by the Malta Financial Services Authority, alongside our established UK operations.
Business review ,
Despite challenging market conditions, including higher interest rates which dampen the availability of capital for
our target customers to invest, and a planned shift to focus on serving larger B2B customers while divesting of
smaller B2C customers, the company successfully maintained revenue levels close to the previous year. Net
revenue was consistent at £3.519m, consistent with £3.519m in the prior year.
Cost of Sales increased to £1.689m in the current year from £1.451m in the prior period, representing a £238k
movement. The primary driver of this increase was the non-recurrence of a £158k Mastercard incentive
recognised in the prior year. The remaining £80k movement reflects underlying cost base inflation and normal
business cost pressures. With revenue remaining flat, the increase in Cost of Sales resulted in Gross Profit of
£1.830m compared to £2.068m in the prior year.
Throughout the year, the business strategically shifted its focus toward embedders that offer the most value,
characterised by larger B2B embedders. This refined delivery strategy allowed for a higher standard of product
delivery and improved product quality, positioning the business for future scalability.
Administrative expenses decreased by 22.3% on the previous year, from £12.756m to £9.909m. During the year
the business undertook a focused initiative to streamline the structure of the business to enable us to better
facilitate the needs of our embedders. We still maintain our primary focus on people to deliver this service and
people infrastructure still represents 64.5% (2024: 73.5%) (including externally provided people resources) of our
Administration expenditure.
Despite challenging market conditions, we have effectively managed our available cash resources while
successfully generating additional income streams. This resulted in interest income earned on the Group's
investments of £250k for the period. Although this is lower than the £812k interest income achieved in 2024, this
reduction is a direct result of the diminishing resources following the utilisation of investment funds.
During the year, the Company submitted its RDEC R&D claim and secured a recovery of £475k (2024: £59k).
The related proceeds were received prior to the year end, resulting in no outstanding R&D claim debtor at 31
December 2025. The FY25 submission is expected to be made following this report and as such has yet to be
recognised.
As we transition towards profitability, the business remains highly focused on optimising unit economics and
keeping tight expenditure control and the strategic deployment of cash reserves. While reserves continue to
decline during this period, proactive steps have been taken to secure additional sources of finance.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Group maintains a risk register that identifies the key areas of risk that are required to be managed. This

risk register is an evolving process due to the nature of the business, evolving regulatory environment and

market conditions. These categories are Technology Risk, Regulatory Risk, Market Risk, Credit and

Counterparty Risk, Competition Risk, Operational Risk and Liquidity Risk.

e Technology risk - Our customers ("embedders") rely heavily on our technology, both external and internal
services, to run their businesses. Given that one of our subsidiaries is an authorised e-money institution,
maintaining the high reliability of these technologies is critical for ensuring continuous regulatory compliance.
The Group places significant importance on our technology provision. We have established controls,
processes, and procedures to mitigate associated risks, which are further reinforced by our PCI-DSS Level 1
and ISO 27001 certifications.

However, as a startup, our operating model is constantly evolving, and so too are our risks. We continuously
reassess these risks as we evolve to ensure proactive mitigation.

e Regulatory risk - The Group currently holds one regulated licence, being the European e-money institution
("EMI") licence held by Weavr (Europe) Limited, making it subject to the regulatory requirements of the
MFSA. Given our operations in the UK financial services sector, we are also highly aware of and committed
to meeting the requirements of UK regulations. This is essential to safeguard current activities and future
licence applications. Therefore, effective risk management, particularly in relation to regulatory compliance
alongside technology risks, is a key priority.

Significant effort, in collaboration with our partners, has been dedicated to establishing a robust regulatory
compliance framework. This strength is evidenced by our registration as an agent of Paynetics AD and
Yapily Connect Ltd, and as a distributor of Paynetics UK Limited and B4B This framework undergoes regular
assessment by our partners’ compliance teams, which we consider our third line of defence, in addition to
scrutiny from our own internal compliance team.
With our European e-money institution ("EMI") licence and Visa membership now secured, together with the
European passporting rights this affords us, we are focused on the continuous evolution and strengthening
of our risk management and compliance frameworks.

e = Market Risk - The Group's principal market risks are interest rate risk and foreign exchange risk; the Group
does not consider price risk (e.g. commodity or trading-position risk) to be significant given the nature of its
business.

e Interest rate risk - The Group holds surplus cash on interest-bearing deposit accounts, and the income
earned on these balances varies with prevailing market interest rates. This is monitored as part of the
Group's cash management process.

e Foreign exchange risk - The Group's revenue and costs are substantially denominated in GBP and EUR;

however, certain metrics are also monitored in USD.
The business seeks to operate natural hedges by matching the currency of its cost base against the
transactional currency of its embedders where possible and exposure to foreign currencies is monitored on a
regular basis with hedging to be considered where a material exposure is identified. The Group recorded a
foreign exchange loss of £398k in 2025 (2024: £12k loss), reflecting increased exposure during the year.

e Cost inflation and geopolitical risk - The Group continuously monitors cost inflation trends and the broader
geopolitical environment as part of its risk assessment process, given the potential impact on operating
costs and customer demand.

Given the Group's current location of operations, these risks are not currently considered individually
significant but are kept under regular review by the Board and the Group's finance and compliance functions.

e Credit and Counterparty risk - The Group is exposed to credit and counterparty risk through multiple
sources: cash and cash equivalent balances held with banking counterparties, receivables from embedders
(customers), and relationships with payment scheme, technology and distribution partners. To mitigate this
risk, the Group: (i) places cash with reputable financial institutions; (ii) conducts comprehensive due
diligence procedures on embedders to assess their credit worthiness and ability to meet payment
obligations; and (iii) maintains ongoing due diligence and monitoring of its key partners and counterparties.

e Competition risk - The embedded finance and payments sector remains highly competitive, with a broad
range of established and emerging providers. The Group seeks to mitigate this risk by continuing to invest in
its product proposition, technology platform and regulatory credentials, and by maintaining close
relationships with its embedders to ensure continued alignment with their evolving needs.

e Operational risk - External factors that could impact the Group's operations, such as inflationary pressures
and geopolitical changes, are continually monitored as part of our risk assessment. Identifying and
addressing these risks is crucial to ensure we can maintain uninterrupted, high-quality service for our
customers. .

Our working model supports this by offering a flexible hybrid approach. Employees have the option to work
from home while also having full access to our offices. This is designed to facilitate collaboration, support
professional development, and foster a strong company culture. This flexibility, combined with regular testing
of our business continuity plans, allows us to adapt effectively to business demands.

Key financial performance indicators
During 2025, the fifth full year of trading, revenue for the Group remained consistent at £3.519m.
There are three main key performance indicators ("KPI") for the Group which are:
1. Annualised Revenue
Annualised Revenue ("AR") is defined as all revenue from contracted customers, projecting the revenue for the
subsequent twelve months. This calculation provides a conservative baseline for the Group's achievable revenue
over that period, as it assumes no customer churn or additional growth. AR is deliberately calculated on the
basis of all contracted revenue rather than only recurring revenue, and should not be confused with Annual
Recurring Revenue ("ARR"), which is a narrower, revenue-type-specific metric. Consistent with an early-stage
growth trajectory, the Group has seen a positive trend in AR, which has risen to £4.3m from £3.9m as of 31
December 2024. This upward movement has persisted into 2026.
2. Cash and Liquidity
Effective cash and liquidity management remain crucial for our continued scaling. While the year concluded with
a cash and liquidity position of £5.02m moderately below internal budget expectations, this result actually reflects
an outperformance, reflecting deliberate deferral of planned but undrawn financing.
The variance against budget reflects the business's deliberate decision not to take up a previously budgeted
facility, demonstrating strong financial discipline and prudent resource management, and successfully supporting
ongoing growth without reliance on additional external funding.
The board continues to monitor the cash resources of the business and explore strategic options to support the
future business needs.
3. Administrative expenses
The Group successfully reduced its administrative expenses significantly in 2025, falling to £9.909m from
£12.756m in 2024. This £2.85m overall reduction in indirect costs resulted from the business-wide restructuring.
A major component of this saving was a £2.14m decrease in direct employment costs, which totalled £5.16m in
2025 (2024: £7.30m). Given that these expenses are a significant contributor to the Group's current cash burn,
they are tracked as a key performance indicator. While the Group remains committed to investment for
development and scaling, the disciplined management of administrative expenditure is a core focus, especially in
the current market environment, to maintain cost efficiency.

During the year ended 31 December 2025, the Group reviewed the presentation of interchange
income and associated cashback arrangements.

made a judgement that these costs should be reflected separately as Cost of sales. Therefore, the

rather than as an offset against Revenue noting this provides a more relevant and reliable
representation of these costs.

information to reflect the revised presentation.
The change affects presentation only and has no impact on gross profit, loss before taxation, net

2.6 Revenue

Revenue is measured at the fair value of consideration received or receivable and represents the
amount receivable for services rendered and will be reflected net of returns, discounts and rebates
allowed by the group and value added taxes.

Revenues are broken down into three different categories:

Monthly Subscriptions

Monthly subscriptions are recognised in the profit and loss accounts in line with the contractual terms.
Consideration is made of the length of the contract and the true value of the monthly subscription
contract value. This includes such things as free period and tapered subscription terms. This value of
the contract is then applied across the period of the contract. The risks and rewards of ownership are
provided to the Innovator (Customer) as they are able to integrate the services into their website and
introduce their platform to their end users (Customer's Customer) allowing them to conduct
transactional activity. The end user's ability to transact enables the flow of economic benefit to the
Innovator over the period, and this is recognised in that period.

Setup/Implementation Revenues

Setup/implementation fees are fees charged for the implementation of a service to the Innovator and
have different implementation periods. These periods are assessed and consistently applied to all
setup revenues. The main setup fees are initial integration to the Company's platform and the
company estimates that it takes three months to integrate. The implementation and set of services
enables the Innovator to introduce the platform services to the end user and also to provide additional
services. These services are provided as stated previously to enable the end user to transact and as :
a result enables the Innovator to gain the economic benefit from those transactions.

Activity-Based Revenues

This form of revenue is based on transactional activity or supply of goods to end users. Transactional
revenues are generated by the activity of the end user on an Innovators programme and we charge
the Innovator for these activities. These revenues are recognised in the period in which the activity
took place. The other form of revenue in this section is the supply of goods normally in the form of
plastic cards. Revenues are recognised in the period in which the goods are supplied to the end user.
The Innovator's ability to generate revenues from the activities of the end user implies that the risks
and rewards of the access provided by the platform have transferred to the Innovator, demonstrating
that the criteria to recognise revenue have been met.

Interchange income and cashback arrangements

Cashback revenue represents amounts earned by the Group under contractual arrangements linked
to transaction activity generated through customer and embedder programmes. Cashback revenue is
recognised in the profit and loss account when the underlying transaction activity giving rise to the
entitlement occurs and the amount of revenue can be measured reliably. Amounts payable under
cashback arrangements are recognised within cost of sales in the same period as the related
revenue is earned.

2.9 Pensions
Defined contribution pension plan
The Group operates a defined contribution plan for its employees. A defined contribution plan is a
pension plan under which the Group pays fixed contributions into a separate entity. Once the
contributions have been paid the Group has no further payment obligations.
The contributions are recognised as an expense in profit or loss when they fall due. Amounts not paid
are shown in accruals as a liability in the Balance Sheet. The assets of the plan are held separately
from the Group in independently administered funds.

2.10 Share-based payments
Where share options are awarded to employees, the fair value of the options at the date of grant is
charged to profit or loss over the vesting period. Non-market vesting conditions are taken into
account by adjusting the number of equity instruments expected to vest at each balance sheet date
so that, ultimately, the cumulative amount recognised over the vesting period is based on the number
of options that eventually vest. Market vesting conditions are factored into the fair value of the options
granted. The cumulative expense is not adjusted for failure to achieve a‘market vesting condition.
The fair value of the award also takes into account non-vesting conditions. These are either factors
beyond the control of either party (such as a target based on an index) or factors which are within the
control of one or other of the parties (such as the Group keeping the scheme open or the employee
maintaining any contributions required by the scheme).
Where the terms and conditions of options are modified before they vest, the increase in the fair
value of the options, measured immediately before and after the modification, is also charged to profit
or loss over the remaining vesting period.
Where equity instruments are granted to persons other than employees, profit or loss is charged with
fair value of goods and services received.
