STRATEGIC REPORT :
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2025
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2025.
Review of the business
4! Utility is a rapidly growing award-winning internet service provider of ‘full fibre’ broadband to residential
properties within the United Kingdom. , :
The Board has an established strategy for continued growth and nurturing of long-term value for shareholders. 4th
Utility continued to expand its presence significantly across the country over the last twelve months. The focus of
the group is to continue to deliver on one of its core strengths of driving customer take up with strong cohesive and
effective branding, coupled with sales and marketing strategy, alongside high standards of customer service and
product quality.
With the expressed consent of the Board, the directors and senior management of the group continue to explore
opportunities to increase our footprint in the marketplace through acquisitions and partnerships for the greater
development of financial performance, brand recognition and shareholder value in addition-to our organic growth.
The 2024-25 financial year was another of significant growth for the group in several key performance indicating
areas, signified by recurring revenues increasing by £1.9m (+103%) thanks to a doubling of monthly subscriptions
sold through the year. This contributed to negative EBITDA reducing to £5.0m as the group also exercised cost
efficiency measures.
£2.8m of capital expenditure was invested into the group’s infrastructure and systems to provide the foundations for :
further growth. This year’s progression was supplemented by draws from the principal investor loan fund of £6.6m
through the year.
The group continues to focus on giving customers the class-leading service they expect and an average 4.3 out of 5
Trustpilot rating is indicative of the customer satisfaction the group provides at install and activation and providing
an ongoing trusted and reliable connection.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Availability of Capital - The business is supported by the financial instruments in place through Liberatis
Investments Limited that continue to provide capital to fund operations, acquisitions and investments.
Competitive Threat — Whilst offering a superior quality product, the group finds itself often competing with FTTC
Internet Service Providers (ISPs) or cellular data operators offering alternative connectivity options to consumers.
Global Pandemic — The group is aware of the impact that a viral outbreak may have on operations if mandated
lock down measures are introduced. Office based staff have the capability to work remotely if required with little
disruption to business operations, however any field work operations may be disrupted to a greater extent with
restrictions to limit spread of infection.
Economic Uncertainty — US imposed tariffs have the potential to impact the supply of router stock if demand
increases for component parts ahead of an introduction of an increased tariff rate. Domestically, falling living
standards, ongoing cost of living pressures, plus conflicts across the globe leading to volatile markets have
impacted the disposable income of consumers which results in a marketplace where price is becoming a more
important factor in consumer choice. Inflationary pressures impact our cost base as suppliers pass on their own
cost increases to their customers and our own internal labour costs increase.
Recruitment and Retaining Talent — Success of the group is dependent on the retention of existing talent and
recruitment of additional people with the right skills, experience and expertise. The group mitigates the risk of not
being able to attract or retain talent by offering competitive remuneration packages as well as a positive and flexible
working environment, training incentives and career development opportunities. :
Regulatory Risk — The risk of increased regulatory intervention by Ofcom or other regulatory powers provides the
group with the challenge of preparing for their implementation and management of ongoing compliance. The group
is well resourced to adapt our operating processes to introduce additional regulatory requirements, however the
board is conscious of the additional costs of systems development and ongoing management that will arisc.
Outsource Partners — The group engages with outsource partners to supply some aspects of our service and is
exposed to possible failure of any one or more of those third parties to deliver the expected level of service or their
insolvency. The group contracts multiple outsource partners and can with ease shift workload between partners
and engage additional partners in case of insolvency.
Systems, Cyber Security, Data Protection — Loss of key systems through security breach, attack, or lack of
resistance could impact the groups ability to deliver key services to customers or the extraction of confidential data
which may lead to reputational damage to the group and its brand. The group mitigates against this through robust
controls and procedures as well as training staff regularly in recognising and acting on potential threats to cyber
security. Updates are conducted regularly and automatically for all group devices to be operating on the latest
software, firmware and security updates. .
Corporate Governance
The Board of 4th Utility Holdings Topco Limited are governed by the Liberatis Investments Limited shareholder
agreement. All Board members have the collective responsibility and legal obligation to promote the interests of the
group and each Board member understands their accountability and responsibilities. Good governance ensures the
decisions of the Board and Senior Management are underpinned by through consideration to internal and external
factors that safeguard the groups prospects. ©
The Board receives timely and regular information in relation to, but not limited to, group performance, risks,
opportunities, strategy and operational matters at least once monthly. Financial information and records are
collated by the group accounting system, with Management Information prepared by appropriately qualified
personnel within the Finance function.

1.2. Business combinations

Where the relative rights of shareholders are preserved in a group restructure, it is appropriate to use merger
accounting to present consolidated information for the group as if the new legal structure had always existed.
This is necessary to ensure that the shareholders receive useful information about their investment on an
ongoing basis, recognising that there has been no change in the substance of their investment.

The new shares issued as consideration for the merger, and therefore the parent's investment in the acquired
company, are recorded at their nominal amounts in the books of the acquiring company. The net assets of the
combining companies are combined using existing book values, with adjustments made as necessary to
ensure that the same accounting policies are applied to the catculation of the net assets of the companies. No
amount is recognised as consideration for goodwill or negative goodwill. The consolidated profit and loss
account includes the profits of each company for the entire period, regardless of the date of the merger, and
the comparative amounts in the consolidated accounts are the aggregate of the amounts recorded by the
combining companies. Again adjustments are made as necessary to ensure that the profits of the combining
companies have been calculated using consistent accounting policies.

1.6 Intangible fixed assets - goodwill
Goodwill represents the excess of the cost of acquisition of a business over the fair value of net assets

amount of the cash-generating unit is less than the carrying amount of the unit, the impairment loss is
allocated first to reduce the carrying amount of any goodwill allocated to the unit and then to the other assets
of the unit pro-rata on the basis of the carrying amount of each asset in the unit.

In the application of the group's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates

Key sources of estimation uncertainty
The estimates and assumptions which have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying
amount of assets and liabilities are as follows.
Accrued income — Partner Incentives
Management exercises judgement in estimating accrued income related to partner incentive arrangements,
which are governed by contractual terms. These incentives are calculated based on the volume of additional
customer connections made within the reporting month. A portion of the incentive is recognised immediately to
reflect upfront Day 1 costs incurred at the start of the customer relationship. The remaining balance is
deferred and recognised evenly over a 24-month period, aligning with the expected duration of the customer
contract. This approach ensures that income recognition appropriately offsets the impact of aggressive pricing
strategies and promotional discounts embedded in the contract, thereby matching revenue with the
associated costs and economic benefits over time.
income in Advance - Recurring Charges
The group recognises income in advance in accordance with its standard payment terms, whereby all
recurring service charges are billed from the service commencement date. Payments are collected in advance
for the upcoming billing period, resulting in the recognition of deferred income until the related services are
delivered. This approach ensures that revenue is matched to the period in which the service is provided.
Judgement is applied in determining the appropriate deferral and recognition of income, particularly in cases
involving service activation delays or billing cycle adjustments.
