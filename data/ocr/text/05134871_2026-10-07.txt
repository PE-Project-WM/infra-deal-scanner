Strategic Report for the Year Ended 31 December 2025 .
The Directors present their strategic report on Flixmedia Limited ("the Company") for the year ended 31
December 2025.
Principal activities .
The Company’s principal activities remained those of providing content syndication services to consumer
brands that help optimise sales conversion, basket value and profit margins on sales with impactful solutions to
engage shoppers.
Fair review of the business

+ The results for the year and financial position at the end of the year of the Company are as shown in these
financial statements. The business has performed wel] during the year and delivered an earnings before interest,
taxes, depreciation, amortisation, indirect foreign exchange, management fees and restructuring costs (“Adjusted
EBITDA”) of £7,710k (2024: £8,961k ). Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure which is
reconcilable to the operating profit of £7,104k for the year (2024: £7,558k) by adding back depreciation of
tangible assets, of £36k (2024: £41k), amortisation of intangible assets of £518k (2024: £1,216k), foreign
exchange (gain) / losses on bank balances of -£84k (2024: £10k), restructuring costs of £88k (2024: £99k) and
adding back the management fee of £48k (2024: £38k).
At the year-end, the Company was in a strong financial position, with net current assets of £2,656k (2024:
£9,537k) and net assets of £5,112k (2024: £11,763k).
Retention of major customers was a primary objective in the year and this objective has been largely achieved.
The Company also invested in both people and infrastructure to drive product development and gain new

_ business.
: Key Performance Indicators
The Company uses three key measures of performance: gross profit, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EBIT
(earnings before interest and tax, excluding management charges). For management reporting purposes, these
measures are computed on a constant currency rate basis rather than an actual currency rate basis in order to
remove the impact of changes in foreign exchange rates in assessing the performance of the business between
actual figures and budget/forecast figures. The Company controls financial performance by monitoring growth
as demonstrated by these measures.
The Directors are satisfied with the level of performance shown by these measures; the Company’s gross profit
margin for 2025 was 60% (2024: 63%). Adjusted EBITDA of £7,710k (2024: £8,961k) and EBIT £7,104k
(2024: £7,558k) decreased year on year but were in excess of the figures prepared for the annual budget. The
directors will continue to control financial performance by closely monitoring direct costs and implementing
measures (including employee suggestions) for improving efficiency.

Corporate and social responsibility
Environment .
The Company recognises the importance of its environmental responsibilities and endeavours to design and
implement policies to reduce any damage that might be caused by the Company’s activities. Initiatives designed
to minimise the Company’s impact on the environment include safe disposal of waste, recycling and reducing
energy consumption.
Employees
It is Company policy to communicate appropriate and relevant information to its employees and to consider
employee feedback. The executive management hold regular formal briefings with staff on strategy issues and
organisational changes and less formal briefings are held with all staff at key times during the year. .
Applications for employment by disabled persons are always fully considered, bearing in mind the specific
abilities of the applicant concerned. In the event of members of staff becoming disabled every effort is made to
ensure that their employment with the Company continues and that appropriate training and support is arranged.
It is Company policy that the training, career development and promotion of disabled persons should, as far as
possible, be identical to that of other employees.
Going concern
In preparing the financial statements, the Directors are. responsible for assessing the Company’s ability to
continue as a going concern, disclosing as applicable, matters related to going concern and using the going
concern basis of accounting, unless the Directors either intend to liquidate the Company or to cease operations,
or have no realistic alternative but to do so.
As of, and for the year ended 31 December 2025, the Company met its day-to-day working capital requirements
through cash flows from operations. The Company's forecasts and projections at that time, considering
reasonably possible changes in trading performance, showed that the Company should be able to continue to
operate within the level of its current and forecasted operational cash flow.
Notwithstanding the impact that the price inflation, the effects arising from ongoing UK and global
macro-economic uncertainties on the operations and profitability of the Company, based on forecasts prepared
up to the date of signing these financial statements it expects to make a significant trading profit during 2026
and expects to have sufficient cash on hand to meet its liabilities as they fal! due throughout 2026 and 2027.
However, despite these macroeconomic conditions, the Company remains committed to investing in its people
and technology. With strong projected adjusted EBITDA for 2026 and a highly cash-generative business model,
the Company is confident in its ability to navigate these challenges and sustain long-term growth.
Cash flow forecasts prepared by the Directors covering 12 months from the date of signing these financial —
statements do not show any negative cash balances for the Company during that time and so it remains able to
meet its liabilities as they fall due. There was no significant impact on the Company's operations during 2025
, and no such impact is expected during 2026 or 2027.
The Directors are therefore confident that the Company will continue to operate as a going concern for a period
of at least 12 months from the date of signing of these financial statements, and accordingly these financial ‘
statements have been prepared on a going concern basis.

Principal risks and uncertainties .
The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Company, together with measures in place to address them are:
- Competition risks

Competitive pressures in the UK are a continuing risk for the Company, making retaining clients and winning
new business challenging. This risk is mitigated by providing added value services to its clients, having fast
response times not only in supplying services but also in handling customer queries, and by maintaining strong

; relationships with clients. The Company aims to ensure that it gives “best in class” delivery to all clients,
thereby forging long-term relationships. This approach leads to good retention and maximises the opportunities
for gaining new business from existing clients. In addition, resources are continuing to be developed for gaining :
new business outside its existing client base.
Operational risks
Our reliance on sophisticated technology platforms exposes us to operational risks such as system failures,
disruptions, or cybersecurity threats. Ensuring the resilience and security of our technology infrastructure is
crucial to maintaining uninterrupted content syndication services. Operational risks may also arise from issues
such as contract disputes, coverage levels, retailer access and failure to meet client expectations, impacting our
business relationships and revenue streams. Building and maintaining strong relationships with Brands and
Retailers is vital to our success, as well as a robust process for reviewing and signing contracts. The Company
also maintains comprehensive insurance against relevant operational risks.
Financial risks
The main elements of financial risk to which the Company is potentially exposed are:
Credit risk - the Company’s credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The Company carries out
credit checks on clients before starting to trade, negotiates the best commercially viable credit terms and
monitors collection regularly. Where appropriate, the amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of
allowances for doubtful receivables; an allowance for impairment is made where there is an identified loss event
which, based on previous experience, is evidence of a reduction in the recoverability of the debt. The Company
has no significant concentration of credit risk.
Liquidity risk - the Company has limited liquidity risk due to strong cash flow from its core business and
absence of debt funding. The Company aims to mitigate liquidity risk by frequent monitoring and management
of its forecast cash flows. :
Cash flow risk - is the risk of exposure to variability in cash flows that is attributable to a particular risk
associated with a recognised asset or liability such as future interest payments on a variable rate debt. The
Company is not exposed to interest rate risk but has exposure to foreign currency exchange risk due to it billing
in US Dollars and Euros. This is in part mitigated by the use of bank accounts denominated in US Dollars and
Euros.
Capital risk - the Company has limited physical assets but holds insurance to cover associated risks. .

Financial instruments

The Company has chosen to adopt Sections 11 and 12 of FRS 102 in respect of financial instruments.

Financial assets

Basic financial assets, including trade, group and other receivables, cash and bank balances are initially
recognised at transaction price, untess the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction
is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.

Such assets are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method. ;

At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for objective
evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying
-amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate.
The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.

If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was
recognised, the reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount
would have been had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in
profit or loss.

Financial assets are derecognised when (a) the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are
settled, or (b) substantially all the risks and rewards of the ownership of the asset are transferred to another party
or (c) control of the asset has been transferred to another party who has the practical ability to unilaterally sell

. the asset to an unrelated third party without imposing additional restrictions.

Financial liabilities

Basic financial liabilities, including trade and other payables from fellow group companies are initially
recognised at transaction price, unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the debt
instrument is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.

Debt instruments are subsequently carried at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method. :
Trade payables are obligations to pay for goods of services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of
business from suppliers. Trade payables are classified as current liabilities if payment is due within one year or
less. [f not, they are presented as non-current liabilities. Trade payables are recognised initially at transaction
price and subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method.

Financial liabilities are derecognised when the liability is extinguished, that is when the contractual obligation is
discharged, cancelled or expires.

Offsetting

Financial assets and liabilities are offset and the net amount presented in the financial statements when there is a
legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to :
realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
