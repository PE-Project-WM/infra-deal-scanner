BUILT FOR WHAT’S CRITICAL
Who we are
Some services simply can’t stop. In a complex world, every signal, every asset, every secure connection must
work without exception. For broadcasters, utilities networks and public institutions the non-negotiables are the
same: constant performance, flawless resilience, and decisions that are secure in the face of scrutiny. Arqiva
designs, builds and operates the foundations that keep systems, sites and audiences connected. From cloud and
IP delivery to networks, monitoring and managed services, Arqiva takes on the complexity behind the scenes so
our partners can move forward with confidence. With deep expertise, disciplined operations and more than 100
years of delivery, Arqiva reduces risk, provides operational assurance and enables change without disruption.
Business model
Built for what’s critical.
Arqiva is at the heart of Media and Broadcast (M&B) and Smart Utilities Networks (SUN) in the UK, providing critical
data, network, and communications services. Arqiva works in partnership with its customers, delivering vital
connectivity. We build and operate the complex ecosystems and infrastructures through which data and content
can move effectively, securely and sustainably at scale - whether that’s through media broadcasting and
transmission services, or smart networks for energy and water.
Arqiva operates through two main commercial functions, Media and Broadcast and Smart Utilities Networks
supported by non-revenue generating Operations, Technology and Corporate functions. Arqiva earns revenue
from its customers through the provision of network access, transmission service and utilities communication
infrastructure, as well as fees for engineering services and new projects. Arqiva’s services tend to be mission-
critical for its customers, providing the network coverage necessary for the fulfilment of their requirements for
their licensed services. .
During the year the Board reviewed the Group's long-term strategy and business plan. This resulted in a refreshed
strategy focused on Evolve, Expand and Establish, underpinned by Execute. The strategy was launched following
the financial year end.
Arqiva has £888 million of property, plant, and equipment as at 30 June 2026. Our business focus, assets,
operations and markets are predominantly in the UK. We currently have a small overseas footprint and therefore
have minimal exposure to international markets and foreign exchange. However, this is evolving as we have
established new business development roles in targeted markets, aimed at growing our cloud based global
media management services.

Arqiva Business Overview and Update

Solid underlying financial performance during the year reflects the resilience of the Group’s long-term contracts
and progress delivering the Group’s strategy, despite competitive market dynamics, a more challenging
macroeconomic environment and the uncertainty caused by the Government’s ongoing reviews of the future of
TV and radio.

The Group remains well positioned to support customers with its robust infrastructure base, and portfolio of
digital, IP and cloud-based solutions.

Media and Broadcast

The Group benefits from a regulated position as the UK’s sole provider of universal television and national radio
broadcast transmission services. It operates over 1,450 transmission sites nationwide, including c.1,150 television
sites, and holds licences for two of the six national digital terrestrial television (DTT) multiplexes. In radio, the
Group maintains 100% national market share, delivering both analogue and digital services via an extensive
infrastructure of approximately 1,700 analogue transmitters and 1,020 digital audio broadcasting (DAB)
transmitters across 690 sites, providing coverage to c.99% of the UK population. The Group operates the two
national commercial DAB multiplexes (including through joint ventures), holds 25 of the Uk’s 58 local DAB licences
and serves as the service provider for the BBC’s national digital radio multiplex.

Evolve: Digital Terrestrial Television .
DTT is the only resilient, mission-critical service in the UK television market that can deliver mass reach at scale.
According to Ofcom 2026 Media Nation’s report, audiences (individuals aged 4+) watch an average of 2 hours
and 19 minutes of broadcast TV per day, with DTT delivering the majority of this viewing. DTT continues to provide
primary television access for around 10 million UK households. As the audience shift continues from traditional
platforms especially from younger audiences moving to IP delivered content; live viewing continues to remain a
substantial component of TV consumption and draws very high audiences to live events and stand-out shows.
As an illustration, the Celebrity Traitors finale in November 2025, BBC’s hit show, was watched by over 12 million
people, with 45% viewing via DTT. According to the Broadcasters’ Audience Research Board (BARB), live viewing
accounted for 45% of total identified viewing on the TV set in December 2025, including younger age groups.
The Group’s DTT platform had 94% utilisation at 30 June 2026, maintaining 34 TV streams that carry 43 channels.
The platform continues to support a broad range of national broadcast services and remains underpinned by
long-dated customer contracts with large media groups extending into the early 2030s. 18 channel renewals
were completed and three new channels were launched during the year, and two further channel launched in the
first half of the financial year ending 30 June 2027.

The number of households combining DTT with over-the-top (OTT) services continues to increase, with around
70% of UK households now paying for at least one video streaming service. The proportion of UK households
with a Subscription Video on Demand (SVoD) service continued to plateau year-on-year, and was only two :
percentage points higher than it was in 2021. Within this, there has been a significant shift toward lower-priced,
ad-supported subscription tiers. Ongoing cost-of-living pressures reinforce Freeview’s appeal as a universal,
free-to-air DTT service for households seeking to manage media spend in the United Kingdom.

The emergence of hybrid consumption by viewers splitting their time between broadcast and streaming TV
requires our customers to run both linear broadcast and streaming services (e.g., ITV & ITVX). Whilst this creates
competitive and cost pressures for the Group’s traditional linear business, it opens opportunities in our Managed

Over the last 18 months, Arqiva has played an active role in the DCMS Future of TV Distribution Forum. In June
2026, the DCMS published its Media Green Paper Watch this Space: A New Strategic Direction for UK Media. This
Green Paper opened a 10-week public consultation on-the future of television distribution and the long-term
sustainability of public service media. The Green Paper outlined two possible scenarios for the future of DTT
beyond the current licence period; either a full DTT switch off in 2034, or a fixed term extension to 2044. Under
the DCMS's 2044 option, Arqiva would reduce the cost of DTT transmission for the Public Service Broadcasters
(PSBs) from 2034, protecting universal coverage and viewers who remain heavily reliant on DTT and release the
600 MHz radio spectrum for auction by Government and use by the mobile sector.

Arqiva continues to advocate for the enduring role of broadcast services until 2044, which would provide a
modernised, cost and energy efficient hybrid DTT and internet delivered television ecosystem. This would
preserve a vital universal service and ensure access to free TV services for viewers irrespective of their location,
age or income. It would also ensure that we retain choice for viewers, support broad access to television services
and maintain a resilient national distribution model as viewing habits continue to evolve. Retaining DTT untit 2044
also ensures there are no knock-on consequences for the radio sector and that the UK retains a diverse and
resilient communications delivery infrastructure alongside internet delivered services.

Evolve: Radio

Live radio reach remains high, with 86.9% of listeners tuning into radio for an average of 19.9 hours per week,
according to Ofcom’s 2026 Media Nation’s Report. Digital radio remains the primary form of radio listening with
Digital Audio Broadcasting (DAB) accounting for 43.1% of total radio listening hours, an increase of 13.1% over 10
years, followed by AM/FM (analogue radio) at 24%. Internet-supported Smart speaker listening has remained
steady over the past year, reflecting 18.8% of all live radio listening, alongside growth in streamed music and
podcasts.

Both national DAB multiplexes remain fully occupied with 80% of Digital 1 (D1) multiplex capacity being contracted
until 2035. The second national multiplex remains full, with 100% of capacity contracted until at least 2028. Across
our 25 local muxes, capacity for all major broadcasters has been secured out to 2030 providing stability across
both the national and local platforms. FM renewals have also continued, with several contracts extended beyond
2030.

The Group is supporting customers and the industry by continuing to promote digital DAB radio as an attractive
medium for listeners and default replacement network for the eventual phase-out of analogue. While there have
been Government statements of support for no FM switch-off before 2030, AM closure is expected to be phased
over time and completed before 2030.

In April 2026, the Group announced Nation Radio as the launch partner for Arqiva’s new fully managed, cloud-
native Audio Streaming platform under a multi-year agreement to support hybrid traditional and streaming
consumption. Nation Radio broadcasts on DAB and online and is one of the UK’s fastest-growing commercial
radio groups. Arqiva will use the platform to support audience growth, enhance listener experiences and
accelerate digital monetisation, with the Group providing full control over content distribution, monetisation,
analytics and operational support. Under the agreement, Nation Radio will migrate all 33 services to Arqiva’s
platform, delivering around five million listening hours per month and up to one million targeted audio adverts per
day. The platform provides scalable, low-latency delivery, real-time audience insights, smart speaker and podcast
distribution, and enterprise-grade resilience.

Water sector update
Smart metering remains a priority area for investment for water companies and Ofwat to combat water scarcity
in the UK due to a growing population and climate change. Currently, less than 20% of UK premises have a smart
water meter and 20% of water is lost through leakage. Smart meters provide an opportunity for our water
customers to meet ambitious environmental and sustainability targets focused on reducing leaks and
consumption, as well as reducing pollution caused by sewer flooding and reducing the Uk’s overall greenhouse
gas emissions. .
The Final Determination for the current 2025-30 regulatory period (AMP8) approved £2.5 billion of investment in
smart metering as part of a £104 billion overall investment in water infrastructure. This will support the rollout of
c.10 million smart meters in AMP8 with all English water companies having targets to connect and gain smart
meter reads to agreed volumes and standards.

Energy sector update
Regulatory and policy developments in the energy market have continued to focus on enabling the Uk’s transition
: to a secure, resilient and low-carbon electricity system. Central to this has been progress on electricity market
reform and preparations for the RHO-3 price control period, covering electricity transmission and distribution
networks and gas distribution networks from 2026 to 2031.
The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) has advanced its programme of electricity market
reform, including further updates to the Review of Electricity Market Arrangements (REMA). Published jointly with
Ofgem in July 2025, these updates set out next steps to deliver a more resilient, low-carbon power market and
to ensure sufficient investment in flexible generation and storage. REMA reforms form part of the Government's
wider ambition to achieve a decarbonised electricity system by 2035 and to support energy security in the
context of increasing renewable generation.
Alongside market reform, Ofgem has progressed the RIIO-3 price control framework. During the year, Ofgem
published draft determinations and engaged with network companies on consultation responses and potential
refinements to investment allowances. RIlO-3 is intended to unlock more than £100 billion of regulated investment
across electricity and gas networks over the 2026-2031 period. A significant share of this investment is expected
to be directed towards upgrading the electricity transmission network to support the Government's Clean Power
2030 objectives, including the connection of new renewable generation and reduced reliance on gas-fired power.
Over £80 billion has been earmarked for electricity transmission, alongside substantial investment in distribution
and gas networks. RIIO-3 places strong emphasis on the deployment of smart technologies, improved monitoring
and enhanced resilience, with Ofgem reinforcing expectations around secure, interoperable communications and
high-quality data to support smarter and more flexible networks. Final determinations were published in
December 2025 and implementation began from 1 April 2026 and will apply through to 31 March 2031.

Financial Risk Management

The Board monitors the Group’s exposure to financial risks as part of its overall risk management framework.
These risks are reviewed regularly and are managed in accordance with approved policies designed to protect
liquidity, financial resilience and long-term value. .

Inflation and Cost Inflation Risk

Sustained high inflation could adversely affect operating and financing cash flows, increase the Group's cost
base and impact the financial health of customers and suppliers. Energy costs represent a significant component
of operating expenditure and are subject to volatility and inflationary pressure.

The Group mitigates inflation risk through a combination of contractual protections, operational measures and
financial hedging. A significant proportion of core television and radio revenues are linked to RPI, providing a
natural hedge against inflation. Inflation linked derivative instruments are used to align interest costs with
inflationary movements. Power cost exposure is managed through long-term power contracts, incremental
forward purchasing under an approved hedging strategy, partial customer pass-through arrangements and
demand-reduction initiatives embedded within the Group’s sustainability strategy.

Financing and Refinancing Risk :

The Group requires access to financing to service and refinance maturing debt. There is a risk that financing may
not be available when required or on terms comparable to historical arrangements, particularly in the event of
adverse market conditions or a deterioration in credit metrics. This risk is mitigated by maintaining a stable,
investment-grade capital structure with diversified medium and long-term debt maturities, limiting refinancing
concentration in any single period. This is evidenced by our BBB+/BBB (S&P/Fitch) ratings of the Group’s senior
debt, and B / B2 (S&P/Moodys) ratings of the Group’s Junior debt, which reflects our ability to service and repay
debt from our cash flows over a reasonable period of time. The Group aims to refinance well ahead of maturity
dates and maintains ongoing dialogue with credit rating agencies. Financial covenant compliance is closely
monitored through retrospective and forward-looking modelling to provide early visibility of potential risks.

Liquidity Risk

There is a risk that the Group may not have sufficient liquid resources to meet working capital requirements,
planned investment or funding obligations, including commitments to the defined benefit pension scheme.
Liquidity risk is managed through the maintenance of cash reserves and access to committed, undrawn facilities:
The Board reviews liquidity and funding adequacy as part of the long-term financial planning process, ensuring
sufficient headroom under a range of scenarios. As at 30 June 2026, the Group had liquidity of £292 million
comprising £24 million cash available and £73 million (£27 million drawdown of £100 million facility) and £195
million undrawn working capital and liquidity facilities respectively available to cover senior debt and/or interest
payment if required.

Interest Rate Risk

The Group is exposed to interest rate movements arising from variable-rate borrowings, which could result in
increased finance costs and volatility in cash flows.

Interest rate risk is managed through the use of derivative instruments, including interest rate and inflation-linked
swaps, in line with an established hedging policy. These instruments provide greater certainty over future interest
cash flows and support covenant compliance. While hedge accounting is not applied, the economic intent of the
hedging programme is to reduce exposure to adverse interest rate movements over the life of the debt.
Credit and Counterparty Risk

The Group is exposed to credit risk arising from customer receivables, liquid funds and derivative counterparties,
including the risk of non-payment or counterparty default.

This risk is mitigated through robust credit checking procedures, both at contract initiation and throughout
contract life, with higher-risk customers required to pay in advance. Aged receivables are actively monitored and
managed. Credit exposure on cash deposits and financial instruments is controlled through counterparty limits,
diversification and ongoing monitoring of credit ratings assigned by international rating agencies.

Purchase Price and Project Cost Risk

Volatility in input prices, particularly energy, and fluctuations in wider market pricing could impact project costs
and margins.

This risk is mitigated through forward purchasing strategies, inclusion of anticipated cost increases within
long-term planning, milestone-based project controls and active monitoring of third-party savings initiatives.
Financial performance against cost and revenue assumptions on major projects is reviewed regularly by senior
management and the Board.

Sustainability is an integral part of our strategy and our business operations and decision making. When thinking

Our overarching strategic purpose is social, enabling people to stay connected to the information and

To meet the requirements of the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) net zero target framework Arqiva is
committed to identifying opportunities to reduce our carbon emissions and has set science-based net zero
targets aligned to meeting the goals of the Paris Agreement which aims to limit global warming to 1.5C above
pre-industrial levels.

We have committed to near- and long-term carbon reduction targets that are aligned to the SBTi Net Zero
Standard which were officially validated in July 2025. Our overall net-zero target commits to reaching net- zero
greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions across the value chain by 2040. Our near-term target commits us to reduce
absolute scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions by 90% by 2031 from a 2023 base year and to reduce absolute scope 3
GHG emissions by 42% within the same timeframe. Our long-term target commits us to maintain a minimum of
90% absolute scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions reductions from FY31 through 2040 from a FY23 base year and to
reduce absolute scope 3 GHG emission by 90% within the same timeframe. In both cases the target boundary
includes land related emissions and removals from bioenergy feedstocks.

This financial year we reduced our energy consumption across Scopes 1 and 2 by approximately 7.4 Gigawatt
hours. This was achieved through a combination of power reductions and reconfiguration of equipment, installing
more efficient technology, and switching off some legacy services. Full details of our energy and greenhouse gas
emissions can be found in the Streamlined Energy and Carbon Report on pages 30-33.
