Introduction
Mycom (UK) Limited (‘the Company”) is part of the Mycom Group (“Mycom”). Mycom was formed in 2014 by .
combining two leading Telecom Industry Service Assurance software providers — Mycom and OSI.
The Mycom portfolio of applications are cloud native, integrated and provide real time assurance powered by
automation, analytics and artificial intelligence / machine learning. With this powerful suite of applications, the Group
continues to grow its portfolio of tier 1 Communications Service Providers (“CSPs’), including some of the world’s
largest. The Mycom technology helps manage the experience of over 2bn tier 1 CSP customers globally.
Mycom is headquartered in London, UK with 274 employees and contractors in offices worldwide serving customers
in all continents.
Strategy, objectives and business model
The Company's strategy is to be the leading specialist assurance software provider in the global telecommunications
market. The Company commits significant investment in its people and application portfolio to ensure the Mycom platform
delivers value for its customers globally and aligns with their strategic, operational and commercial objectives.
The Company provides flexible commercial models including subscription-based offerings which provide predictability of
spend for customers as part of an end-to-end service.
The Company leverages Mycom’s technically skilled and dedicated employees in centres of excellence around the
world. These teams ensure the successful delivery of the Mycom suite of products to its customers.
The board regularly review the Group’s strategy to ensure it reflects the opportunities identified in the markets in
which the Group operates, as well as emerging markets where the Group can use its experience and technology to
expand its business model. Mycom is at the forefront of the market in providing artificial intelligence services to its
customers. The Company is focused on growing the opportunity pipeline and with continued investment in its sales
and marketing teams the Company continues to develop opportunities to grow the order book and revenues.
The business is well positioned to target growth opportunities across the broader market, including targeting
customers looking for end-to-end services in the mobile and fixed line (FTTx) markets globally. In the current year,
the Company has delivered organic growth from its existing customer base with customers purchasing the full suite of
Mycom products with opportunities to deliver additional services.
Core values .
Whilst the Company uses technology and processes, the core of its business is its people who strive to keep the
Group at the forefront of its markets with their passion for innovation and customer service.
The Company could not succeed without the skill and dedication of its workforce. They embody the culture and
values and ensure that the Company delivers the excellent products and service on which its success depends.
The directors express their appreciation to all the Company's employees for their hard work and dedication.
As a values-led organisation, corporate responsibility is a consistent theme. Acting responsibly enables the Company
to be a better, more efficient business and a good corporate citizen.
The Company’s approach to responsible business influences the way it manages and develops its people. [t also
underpins the delivery of excellent customer service by ensuring all colleagues are kept safe and secure and
improves the Company's efficiency by encouraging a reduction in energy and resources used to mitigate the impact
on the environment.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Competitive environment

The Company operates in a competitive environment with competition from a broad range of organisations. Failure to

continue innovating could reduce the value of the Company's customer proposition and therefore adversely impact

on its leading competitive position.

To mitigate this risk, the Company invests to continue the innovation of its suite of assurance applications and to keep

at the forefront of an ever-changing market.

Economic environment

The cost base of the Company has been subject to increased price pressure due to the prevailing economic

environment, alongside customers looking to reduce their cost base. In order to protect growth and margins we work

with our customers to offer the best value for money propositions for their business to retain revenue streams and
grow the customer base. The Company implemented an internal cost saving review to identify measures and cost
savings to manage the cost base.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Failure to manage reputation
Failure to manage reputation will mean that customers will be less likely to award the Company new business or
renew existing business. It will also impact on the Company's ability to attract and retain high quality people as
maintaining the right staffing with the appropriate technical and administrative skills is critical to meeting the needs
of customers. To mitigate this risk, the business has implemented a Customer Success Programme to focus on
customer satisfaction, value realisation and user adoption.
Major information security breach or cyber attack
A major information security breach resulting in the loss or compromise of sensitive information (including personal
or customer) or the loss of service is likely to cause significant reputational damage, financial penalties, and loss of
customer confidence is a key risk to the business.
The Company continues to make significant investments in its cyber security systems and processes for both
customer solutions and internal systems. In addition, the Company has implemented a security training programme
for employees.
With phishing and ransomware attacks increasing, new security measures have been implemented including two
factor authentication, training and internal IT alerting systems.
Contract non-compliance
Failure to deliver contractual obligations or to meet agreed service performance levels and report against them
accurately may lead to significant financial penalties or legal notices.
Any failure in the Company's software products or solution delivery to customers could also result in increased
project delivery costs and an adverse impact on reputation risk. To address this risk, regular performance reviews
are conducted for all material contracts.
Significant failure of suppliers
Asignificant failure in a platform deliverable hosted by a supplier may expose the Company to a risk of failure to meet
its customer obligations, perform critical business operations or win new business.
Supply chain risk is carefully managed for critical customer deliverables. The Company works closely with suppliers
to understand their systems and deliverables and engage with them regularly to ensure compliance and key delivery
dates are adhered to.
Material legal and regulatory compliance failure
Failure to comply with iaws and regulations could result in significant loss to the Company including exposure to
prosecution and fines, reputational damage and the potential loss of license and authorisations, all of which may
adversely impact the prospects for future contracts.
All key contracts are reviewed on a monthly basis by the management team to ensure performance is in line with
contractual obligations and to ensure any remedial actions are delivered.
Financial risk management
Financial control failure
Financial contro! failure may result in an inability to accurately report timely financial results and meet. contractual
financial reporting obligations, increase risk of error and fraud, and adversely impact management's ability to make
business decisions and forecast accurately.
The Company is focused on maintaining an effective and well monitored control environment that minimizes
financial risk and ensures the effectiveness of systems for internal contro! and financial reporting.
Foreign exchange risk
The Company’s transactions are primarily carried out in British Pounds and US Dollars. The Company is able to net
the US Dollar amounts with other subsidiaries in the Group where-sufficient cash balances are maintained so that
transactional risk across the Group is managed.

Going concern ‘
The Company is part of the wider Magnate Midco Limited Group (‘the Group”) as detailed in note 21. The Group
as a whole is managed as a single cash generating unit, with resources deployed across the Group as needed in order
to meet individual Company obligations as they fall due.
After making enquiries, the directors have prepared forecasts and considered the Group's current trading performance,
cash position and funding arrangements. Baséd on this analysis, the directors have a reasonable expectation that the
Group has access to sufficient resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future and have
therefore adopted the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements for the Company.
The Group is supported by its ultimate parent entities, which have provided a letter of support to fund operational
liabilities as they fall due for a period of at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements.
In addition, the Group has a loan facility in place which is due to expire on 31 December 2026.
The forecasts indicate that the Group may breach certain financial covenants during 2026, which the lender is
aware of. Inthe event that the financial covenants were either breached, or not re-negotiated, the Group would require
additional support from either the lender or ultimate parent entities. The directors are actively engaged with the Group’s
lender and shareholders regarding covenant resets and ongoing funding requirements. While constructive discussions
with both parties are ongoing, and the directors are confident the position will be resolved, these matters are not yet
contractually agreed.
These conditions indicate the existence of a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt on the
Company's ability to continue as a going concern. The financial statements do not include any adjustments that
would result if the Company were unable to continue as a going concern.

In the application of the Company's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates

Critical accounting judgements
Revenue
The Company's contracts with customers may include promises to transfer multiple products and services to a
customer. Determining whether products and services are considered distinct performance obligations that
should be accounted for separately versus together may require significant judgement.
For arrangements with multiple performance obligations, the Company allocates the contract transaction price,
including discounts, to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Judgement is
required to determine the standalone selling price for each distinct performance obligation.
The Company determines standaione selling prices based on prices charged to customers in observable
transactions and uses a range of amounts to estimate standalone selling prices for each performance obligation. The
Company maximises the use of observable inputs by maintaining pricing analysis that includes the Group’s
pricing policies, historical standalone sales when they exist, and historical renewal prices charged to customers.
It was concluded that the standalone selling prices of certain performance obligations, specifically the standalone
selling prices for software licenses and SaaS arrangements, are highly variable. In these instances, the Group
estimates the standalone selling prices using the residual approach, determined based on total transaction price.
minus the standalone selling price of other performance obligations promised in the contract.
Lease accounting — determining the lease term
The Company determines the lease term as the non-cancellable term of the lease, together with any periods
covered by an option to extend or terminate the lease. In making this judgement, the Company evaluates
whether it is reasonably certain to exercise the option to renew or break the lease term.
That is, it considers all relevant factors that create an economic incentive for it to exercise the renewal and the
circumstances and facts for each lease including past experience to determine the likely lease term and whether
the break option is likely to be exercised. This includes an assessment on the length of time remaining before
the option is exercisable, current trading conditions and future trading forecasts on the ongoing profitability of
the business.
After the lease commencement date, the Company reassesses the lease term if there is a significant event or
change in circumstances that is within its control and affects its ability to exercise (or not to exercise) the option
to renew (for example, a change in business strategy).
Estimation uncertainty
Lease accounting — determination of the appropriate rate to discount the lease payments
The Company uses the wider Groups incremental borrowing rate as the discount rate for determining its lease
liabilities at the lease commencement date since the rate implicit in the lease cannot be readily determined. The
calculation of the incremental borrowing rate involves estimation and is based on the average interest rate of all
wider Group debt outstanding at the time the lease began.
