GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

Introduction
The director presents the Group Strategic Report for Index Exchange Limited and its subsidiary companies for
the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activities
The principal activities of the Group comprise of. providing sales and support functions related to its parent entity,
which hosts an exchange that enables digital publishers and suppliers to sell their online advertising impressions
programmatically and in real time. The main geographical markets of the Group are Europe (primarily France,
Germany, Italy and Great Britain), Asia (Singapore and Japan) and Australia.
Business Review
The director aims to present a balanced and comprehensive review of the development and performance of the
business during the period and its position at year end. The review is consistent with the size and nature of the
business and is written in the context of the risks and uncertainties that our Group faces.
We consider revenue. to be our key financial performance indicator which illustrates financial performance and
strength of the Group. Revenue was £32,568,000 for the year, a 36.4% increase on the prior year revenue figure
of £23,875,000. As the Group principally provides sales and support functions to its parent under a cost-plus
arrangement, revenue is largely a function of the operating costs incurred in each region. In the year of 31
December 2025, the total administrative and finance expenses of the Group were £30,814,000 (2024 -
£30,005,000).
Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risk facing the Group relates to increased competition within the programmatic advertising industry

/ and prevailing macroeconomic conditions. The Group operates in a highly competitive market. In addition, the
global economy continues to experience a period of slower growth, with inflationary pressures driven in part by
ongoing geopolitical events. Management continues to monitor these developments closely and has maintained
a disciplined approach to discretionary spend to preserve the Group's operational resilience.
Future Developments
The success of the Group can be attributed to its ability to penetrate emerging markets in Europe and Asia. The
Group continues to expand its sales and marketing initiatives to attract new customers, thus the outlook on the
future development of the Group remains positive despite the current economic climate. Management will focus
on maintaining the Group's profitability by actively managing its cash reserves and deploying austerity measures
around discretionary expenses where necessary.

3.5 Share-based payments
Share-based payment transactions of the Company
Employees of the Group receive remuneration in the form of share-based payments whereby employees
are granted equity awards for their employment services. The Group has two share-based payment
arrangements: the Participation Unit Plan (Unit Plan") and the Share Option Plan (“Option Plan”).
The fair value of share options issued under the Option Plan and the fair value of units issued under the
Unit Plan are based on the fair market value of a common share equivalent on the date of grant. The cost
of equity-settled share-based compensation is determined by the fair value at the date when the grant is
made.
That cost is recognised in administrative expenses in the statement of profit and loss together with a
corresponding increase in equity (share-based payments reserve), over the period in which the service
and, where applicable, the performance conditions are fulfilled (the vesting period).
The expense recognised for equity-settled transactions at each reporting date reflects the vesting period,
and the Group's best estimate of the number of equity instruments that will ultimately vest. The impact of
the revision of the original estimates, if any, is recognised in profit or loss such that the cumulative
expense reflects the revised estimate, with a corresponding adjustment to contributed surplus.
Service and nonmarket performance conditions are not taken into account when determining the grant
date fair value of awards, but the likelihood of the conditions being met is assessed as part of the Group's
best estimate of the number of equity instruments that will ultimately vest. Market performance conditions
are reflected within the grant date fair value. Any other conditions attached to an award, but without an
associated service requirement, are considered to be non-vesting conditions. Non-vesting conditions are
reflected in the fair value of an award and lead to an immediate expensing of an award unless there are
also service and/or performance conditions.
No expense is recognised for awards that do not ultimately vest because nonmarket performance and/or
service conditions have not been met. Where awards include a market or non-vesting condition, the
transactions are treated as vested irrespective of whether the market or non-vesting condition is satisfied,
provided that atl other performance and/or service conditions are satisfied.
