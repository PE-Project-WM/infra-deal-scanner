Review of Business
CB Payments, Ltd (the “Company’), is authorised to carry out electronic money services activity in the
United Kingdom (“UK”) by the UK Financial Conduct Authority (“FCA”) under the Electronic Money
Regulations 2011 ("EMRs”), and in Q1 2025 was registered with the FCA under the Money Laundering,
Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 (“MLRs”) to
carry out certain crypto asset services to its customers. In July 2026, the Company received further
authorisation from the FCA under the UK Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (“FSMA”) as a
Markets in Financial Instruments Directive ("MiFID") investment firm.
The Company provides E-money services and crypto assets trading services to Coinbase customers in
a number of markets, primarily the UK, to enable customers in those countries to be able to undertake
crypto transactions on the Coinbase trading platform. In exchange for the E-money services and related
obligations undertaken, the Company earns a service fee from the affiliates. The affiliate entities and
the Company are wholly owned subsidiaries of Coinbase Global, Inc. (the "Parent", "Coinbase", or
collectively, the "Group").
In February 2025, the Company received approval as a Virtual Asset Service Provider ("VASP") from the
Financial Conduct Authority ("FCA"). The VASP license enables the Company to provide crypto asset
exchange and custodian wallet services directly to its customers. Full VASP approval was received in
April 2025, following which the Company commenced migrating customers from the Company's
affiliate. As a result, the Company now generates transactional revenue directly from crypto asset
services in addition to its existing E-money service fee revenue. As the Company's VASP activities
scale, and following the completion of customer migrations, E-money service fee revenue is expected
to tail off, as the intercompany arrangements previously used to facilitate crypto activities on behalf of
the Company's affiliate are no longer required to the same extent.
For the year ended December 31, 2025, the Company recognised turnover of €270.3 million (2024
€131.7 million), with a cost of sales of €54.4 million (2024: €20.7 million). The Company incurred
administrative expenses of €212.1 million (2024: €101.0 million), and a tax charge of €6.0 million
(2024: €3.8 million), resulting in profit for the year of €0.2 million (2024: Profit: €7.8 million).
The increase in turnover was primarily due to an increase in E-money transactions as well as
transaction revenue from crypto trading following the VASP approval. The increased crypto trading
activities also contributed to higher intercompany settlement balances at year end. intercompany
settlements were also impacted by timing of settlements at year end. The decrease in profit for the year
was primarily attributable to a significant increase in administrative expenses, driven by a foreign

. exchange loss driven by volatility in exchange rates and greater exposure to USD, compared to a
foreign exchange gain in the prior year, as well as higher staff costs resulting from increased headcount
and share-based payment charges to support the growth of the business. These factors were partially
offset by the strong growth in turnover noted above.
As at December 31, 2025, the Company had €152.0 million (2024: €227.8 million) of corporate cash
and €397.6 million (2024: €336.2 million) of safeguarding customer cash assets. The decrease in
corporate cash was primarily due to the timing of settlement of amounts owed to group undertakings.
Financial Key Performance Indicators
The Company's key performance indicators ("KPI") for the year ended December 31, 2025 and 2024,
include but are not limited to turnover and profit from ordinary activities before taxation. These metrics
provide indicators of the Company's ability to generate profits for the Group.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties :
Principal risks and uncertainties are identified and summarised below along the main risk pillars.
Key Regulatory Changes in 2025 .
Authorised Push Payment ("APP") Fraud: The APP Fraud Reimbursement Scheme, introduced by the
Payments Systems Regulator ("PSR"), came into force on October 7 2024, so financial year 2025 was
its first full year in effect and has required a notable amount of attention from the Company and key
stakeholders as they deal with banking counterparties. It requires in-scope payment service providers
("PSPs") sending payments through either the Faster Payment System ("FPS") or the Clearing House
Automated Payment System ("CHAPS") to reimburse their customers if they are the victim of an APP
scam, subject to certain exceptions. Broadly, the cost of that reimbursement is shared equally between
the sending and receiving PSPs. Any material failure to prevent APP Fraud could adversely impact the
Company's business.
Crypto Asset Promotions: The UK's crypto financial promotions regime, introduced by the FCA in
October 2023, requires firms marketing crypto assets to UK consumers to comply with the financial
promotion rules, meaning promotions must be communicated or approved by an authorised or
registered firm, or rely on an exemption, and must be fair, clear, and not misleading. This is part of the
broader effort to protect consumers and maintain market integrity. Following its MLRs registration in
Q1 2025, CBPL determined it would review and self-certify its own financial promotions rather than
relying on an external third party as done previously. This required CBPL to develop and implement a
number of internal protocols to help ensure compliance. If the Company is unable to satisfy such
criteria, the Company may face regulatory action and reputational damage, which could adversely
impact the Company's financial position and the Company's business.
Consumer Duty: The FCA’s Consumer Duty rules require financial firms to act in a way that delivers
good outcomes for retail customers by ensuring products and services are fit for purpose, offer fair
value, and support customer understanding and decision-making. Firms must act in good faith, avoid
foreseeable harm, and help customers achieve their financial goals. The Duty applies to all retail
financial products and services, and came into effect in July 2023 for open products and July 2024 for
closed ones. The Company continued to build out its Consumer Duty-focused efforts in 2025, including
through the building out of its product governance framework, in order to help ensure compliance. The
Company may face regulatory action and reputational damage, which could adversely impact the
Company's financial position and the Company's business if deemed non compliant.
Legal and Regulatory Risks
Impact of upcoming UK Crypto asset Financial Services Regime
The regulatory landscape for crypto assets is rapidly evolving. Changes or new legislation can
significantly impact operations, competitiveness, and strategy.
The forthcoming regulatory framework for crypto assets within the UK, as detailed in HM Treasury's
policy note (April 2025) and the many FCA Consultation Papers and related Policy Statements that have
followed, is set to introduce a more comprehensive and detailed regime. However, while most final .
rules and guidance has been released as of July 2026, there are still areas to be clarified with the FCA
and thus the final scope is to be determined. The final shape of the regulatory framework may
significantly impact how the Company operates.

11. FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT
The Board of Directors has overall responsibility for the establishment and oversight of the
Company's risk management framework and is responsible for developing and monitoring the
Company's risk management strategy and policies. .
Liquidity risk .
The Company has exposure to liquidity risk arising from its operating activities. The Company
actively monitors current cash flow requirements and continually forecasts future cash flows in
order to maintain liquidity and ensure that sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations.
The Directors consider current cash balances sufficient to meet cash requirements. If required,
the Parent will provide ongoing financial support, and there is sufficient cash available with the
Parent.
Market risk
Market risk is the risk associated with the effect of changes in market factors on the value of
the assets and liabilities held on the Company's statement of financial position, including
interest rates, foreign exchange rates, prices of crypto assets, or volatilities such as market
volatility or product liquidity. The Company is not directly exposed to market risk, as the
Company did not hold any financial instruments subject to price fluctuation as of December 31,
2025.
Interest rate risk

- The Company had cash at banks and in hand and safeguarding customer cash assets that bear
interest.
The Company's investment policy and strategy primarily attempts to preserve capital and meet
liquidity requirements without significantly increasing risk. The Company's cash at banks and in
hand and restricted cash with banks is in interest bearing accounts. Changes in interest rates
would primarily impact interest income and other similar income included within net interest
income and other in the statement of profit and comprehensive income. A hypothetical 100
basis points increase or decrease in interest rates would not have had a material impact on total
net profit for the years ended December 31, 2025.
Foreign currency risk .
The Company has exposure to foreign currency translation and transactional gains and losses
arising from financial transactions denominated in a currency other than the Company's
functional currency. Accordingly, changes in exchange rates may negatively affect the
Company's future operating results upon translation into Euros. At this time, the Company does
not, but may in the future, enter into derivatives or other financial instruments in an attempt to
hedge the Company's foreign currency exchange risk. As of December 31, 2025, the Company's
net exposure to foreign currency risk was not significant. Accordingly, a 10% increase or
decrease in current exchange rates throughout the year would not have had a material impact
on the Company's financial results. .
