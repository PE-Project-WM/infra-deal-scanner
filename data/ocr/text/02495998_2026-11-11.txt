Strategic Report
The Directors of Lumen Technologies UK Limited (“the Company”) present their strategic report for the year ended
31 December 2025.
Principal activities and Business review
As of 1 November 2023, the Company divested from the Lumen Technologies, Inc. Group of Companies (“Lumen”
or “LUMEN US") and since forms part of Colt Group Holdings Limited (‘Colt” or “Group™). This divestiture
formed part of the acquisition of Lumen’s operations in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa (LUMEN EMEA”) :
by Colt.
Colt provides digital infrastructure services and solutions for enterprise, wholesale and capital market customers in
Europe, Asia and North America. Colt's strategy centres on ensuring that it is the force behind the critical
infrastructure needed to drive businesses forward and continuing to deliver an industry-leading customer experience.
Colt is structured along two key lines of business: Colt Core (‘Core’, including Network and Voice) and Data Centre
Services (‘DCS’), The Company maps within Core where the structure focuses on Colt's assets, providing end-to-
end ownership and responsibility for revenue, profits and cash returns with visibility of performance and delivery.
Acquisition of Lumen EMEA
Since the acquisition of the Lumen EMEA business in 2023, Colt has undergone significant transformation as it
immediately began integrating the two organisations.
In 2025, the Group continued te make progress in unifying operations, enhancing customer experience, and
. strengthening Coit’s position in the market. Colt has substantially expanded its scale and capabilities, reflecting the
impact of the enlarged business. Integration activities completed in May 2026, completing the migrating of ali
customers onto the Colt systems stack and realising the full operational and commercial benefits of the combined
organisation
Principal risks and uncertainties
The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Company are integrated with the principal risks and uncertainties
facing the Group and are not managed separately. Details of the risks and uncertainties facing the Group, the Group’s
financial risk management policy and an analysis of the performance of the Group, including key performance
indicators, can be found in the consolidated Group annual report for the year ended 31 December 2025 which does
not form part of this report. The Group annual report is available from the Company’s registered office.
Cyber incident on Colt and its impact
Colt suffered a cyber incident in August 2025, which impacted all business support systems, further requiring
: manual processes to be implemented for critical activities. While CORE network infrastructure was reported to be
unaffected, internal business support systems, Coli Online, and customer portals faced outages. Colt contained the
issue, and our core network infrastructure remained untouched.
Colt took action to remove the threat actor from our environment, and proactively took some systems offline, leading
to disruption to some elements of our services. Colt brought in specialist third-party investigation, forensic and
cyber support and has implemented enhanced containment and security measures to further harden our systems as
well as help prevent similar incidents in the future. The company took systems offline as a precaution and worked
to restore services. Colt implemented additional controls and completed the restoration of the majority of its systems
in December 2025. Financial reporting systems were not impacted by the activities of the threat actor, and no further
threat actor activity was identified within Colt's network environment since the restoration of systems.
Colt proactively notified the relevant regulators and authorities and will continue to work closely with law
enforcement agencies.

Employee Engagement
The engagement of colleagues across Colt has been of critical importance and focus during 2025 as we progressed
integration and bringing the two organisations together as One Team.
Colt believes that listening to its employees and establishing a two-way communication and feedback culture is
critical to continuous engagement with employees. Through a comprehensive listening strategy, employee feedback
shapes plans and initiatives and helps to build the employee experience. Example elements of our listening approach
are Colt’s annual employee survey (‘People Matters”); the country management function; and the European and
National Works Councils.
The results of the annua! employee survey are communicated to the entire company by Colt’s CEO; executive and
senior management then consult their functions to identify areas for improvement. Progress is tracked by each
function, inter alia by all-hands, town halls with executives and skip-level meetings.
In each country where Colt operates, it has an individual appointed to act as a ‘country manager’, in addition to their
substantive role. The country manager’s function is both to support management’s strategy at country-level; as well
as to help raise any issues at country level to senior management’s attention. The larger countries within Colt also
have a country-level local leadership team. The smaller countries have a regional management team, such as
countries in Asia.
The Colt Culture & Inclusion group (which is a network of employee volunteers who act as change agents at Colt)
works together with the country management function, to help ensure a regular rhythm of local inclusion and

Revenue recognition
Revenue represents amounts earned for services and equipment sales provided to customers (net of value added tax). On
inception of the contract, the Company identifies a performance obligation for each of the distinct goods and services
we have promised to provide to the customer. The consideration specified in the contract with the customer is allocated
to each performance obligation identified based on their relative standalone selling prices and is recognised as revenuc
as they are satisfied.
The majority of the Company’s revenue contracts have only one performance obligation, being the provision of services
to which the customer is contracting. This includes revenue from initially connecting the customer to Colt’s network,
known as installation fees. The service obligation is satisfied over a period of time by a series of distinct services that
are substantially the same and have the same pattern of transfer to the customer. Voices services revenue is based on
customer usage and is recognised at a point in time, when the service is received. No two customers are exactly the same
owing to services requested and the risk pattern of each customer, therefore the price charged to a customer is the
standalone selling price, reflecting the value the individual customer obtains from the service.
Revenue recognition across our revenue streams is considered further below.
Network services
The Company’s revenue streams typically have fixed price contracts which are invoiced on a monthly basis or quarterly
basis. For these contracts, the Company has the right to consideration from a customer in an amount corresponding
directly with the value of the entity’s performance obligations completed to date. Revenue is therefore allocated over the
life of the customer contract according to the pattem in which the customer derives the benefits of the service. In
accordance with IFRS 15, B16, the Company recognises such revenue based on its right to invoice.
Installation fees are invoiced and received upfront for the Company’s revenue streams. Installation revenue cannot be
unbundled from the underlying service, since the customer cannot benefit from the underlying services without
connecting to the Colt network (IFRS 15 p27(i)). As such, it is not considered a separate performance obligation, but
rather part of the overall service contract. Installation fees are therefore deferred and recognised as the services are
rendered over the average contract term (36 months) with the exception of contracts greater than £0.5m, where
installation fees are deferred and recognised over the contract term.
Voice
Voice services revenue is recognised when voice traffic is carried over the network based on the fair value of this traffic
at the time the call is placed.
Long-term IRU agreements
The lease of network capacity and dark fibre to third parties, pursuant to long-term IRU agreements, are accounted for
as either finance or operating leases in accordance with IFRS 16.
Principal/agency relationships

transaction, and recognise revenue net of directly attributable costs. The assessment of whether the Company represents
revenue gross as the principal or net as the agent, i.e., only in the amount of the remaining margin, requires an analysis
of both the legal form and the substance of contracts. After all of the relevant facts and circumstances of the individual
case have been weighed up, in many cases the decision also involves a degree of discretion, even if a uniform Group
method of assessment is applied. Depending on the conclusions reached, there may be material differences in the
amounts of revenues and expenses for the current and subsequent periods. This, however, has no bearing on profit or

Accounting as a lessor, including sub-leases

The Company may act as a lessor, normally in the supply of network infrastructure or when involved in a sub-lease

arrangement. How the Company accounts for the lease arrangement will depend on the lease classification. A lease is

classified as a finance lease if it transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of an underlying
asset. A lease is classified as an operating lease if it does not transfer substantially all of the risks and rewards incidental
to ownership of an underlying asset.

Factors considered when determining whether substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of an

underlying asset include:

- the lease transfers ownership of the underlying asset to the lessee by the end of the lease term;

- the lessee has the option to purchase the underlying asset at a price that is expected to be sufficiently lower than the
fair value at the date the option becomes exercisable for it to be reasonably certain, at the inception date, that the
option will be exercised;

- the lease term is for the major part of the economic life of the underlying asset even if the title is not transferred,

- at the inception date, the present value of the lease payments amounts to at lease substantially all of the fair value
of the underlying asset, and

- the underlying asset is of such a specialised nature that only the lessee can use it without major modifications.
For a finance lease, the Company derecognises the related nght of use asset or property plant and equipment and
recognises a lease receivable at an amount equal to the net investment in the lease with a related gain or loss recognised
in the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income. Income from a lessee will ordinarily relate to
revenue-attributable activities and is classified as revenue. Where the Company is acting as the principle in the
arrangement, income and costs would be recorded gross. Where the Company is acting as the agent in the arrangement,
the gain or loss would be recorded net.

Income from arrangements classified as operating leases is presented as revenue where it relates to our CORE operating

activities. For an operating lease, the Company recognises lease payments as income on either a straight-line basis or

another systematic basis.

Any upfront payments received, such as connection fees, are deferred over the lease term. Within wholesale hall

contracts, there may be fixed inflationary increases as part of the standard terms and conditions. The revenue is spread

on a straight-line basis over the term of the customer contract.

Income Tax
The income tax expense or credit for the year is the tax payable on the current year’s taxable income based on the
applicable income tax rate for each jurisdiction adjusted by changes in deferred tax assets and liabilities attributable to
temporary differences and to unused tax losses.
The current income tax expense is calculated on the basis of the tax laws enacted or substantively enacted at the end of
the reporting period in the countries where the company and its subsidiaries operate and generate taxable income.
Management periodically evaluates positions taken in tax returns with respect to situations in which applicable tax
regulation is subject to interpretation and considers whether it is probable that a taxation authority will accept an
uncertain tax treatment. Colt measures its tax balances either based on the most likely amount or the expected value,
depending on which method provides a better prediction of the resolution of the uncertainty.
Deferred tax is provided on all temporary differences that arise between the carrying amounts of asscts and liabilities for
financial reporting purposes and their tax base which result in an obligation to pay more tax, or a right to pay less tax at
a future date, at rates that are expected to apply when the obligation crystallises. These rates are determined using tax
rates (and laws) that have been enacted or substantially enacted by the end of the reporting period and are expected to
apply when the related deferred income tax asset is realised or the deferred income tax liability is settled.
Deferred tax assets are recognised only if it is probable that future taxable amounts will be available to utilise those
temporary differences and losses. The amount of deferred tax recognised is based on an assessment of future forecast
profits.
Deferred tax assets and liabilities arising on temporary differences from investment in subsidiaries are not recognised as
the timing of their reversal is controlled by Colt, and it is probable that the differences will not reverse in the foreseeable
future.
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset when there is a legally enforceable right to offset current tax assets and ~
liabilities and when the deferred tax balances relate to the same taxation authority. Current tax assets and tax liabilities
are offset where the entity has a legally enforceable right to offset and intends either to settle on a net basis, or to realise
the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Current and deferred tax is recognised in the income statement, except to the extent that it relates to items recognised in
other comprehensive income or directly in equity. In this case, the tax is also recognised in other comprehensive income
or directly in equity, respectively.

US Sales Tax Provision
The US Supreme Court's decision in the 2018 Wayfair Case established liability for state and local sales taxes where
there is economic nexus in a State. Any service originating and/or terminating in the US may create an economic nexus
and give rise to US Federal, State and Local Taxes. The provision represents the current estimate of the US sales tax
liability for the Company. The amount payable is dependent on the latest estimates of the taxability of each product and
state in which the services were delivered. The timing of cash outflows is dependent on communications with US tax
