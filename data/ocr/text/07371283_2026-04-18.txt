Principal activities and business review
The Company is mainly a contract research and development provider which also conducts a small business of contract
manufacturing. The turnover for the year was £ 125.2 million of which 97% is from contract research and development
‘ services and 3% from contract manufacturing business. The customers for the services and the owners of resulting
technology are the Huawei Group companies (‘Huawei Group, group, group company or group companies’ in the
following text, unless the context otherwise requires, means Huawei Investment & Holding Co., Ltd. and its subsidiary
companies). The Company carries out research activities, develops and produces devices in accordance with its
contractual obligations. In return, the service recipients have ownership and/or use of any intellectual property rights
created or acquired by the Company through the activities governed by these contracts.
The Company is an active collaborator strengthening the UK science base with the aim to attract the best talent to the
UK. The Company’s Cambridge Research Centre is developing innovative digital technology solutions to improve
the performance of smartphone applications by optimising the underlying core software and hardware, as well as :
developing open source software for server systems.
The Ipswich Research Centre is dedicated to the field of photonic technology, developing designs and early stage
prototype production of optoelectronic devices, initially for fibre-optic communications. The Company started to
perform manufacturing activity in Ipswich from 2019 and exported devices in small volumes to enable the first
generation of products of its customers. In 2022 the facility was upgraded to steer its research towards new fields of .
applications such as healthcare, displays, etc. In 2025, the revenue which it generated from contract manufacturing
was £4.1 million, a significant reduction (2024: £6.4 million) largely due to the site having to recover from a fire
incident in March 2025. The Company has recovered well as a result of engaging its business continuity plan. The
total damage assessment to date is £3.3 million against which insurance recoveries of £1.7 million have already been
made. A claim of £450k is currently under review and further claims will be made as remedial works are carried out.
Insurance proceeds are only recognised on receipt and netted off against the respective expenses. The Company had
to write-off some equipment due to irreparable damage and the financial effect of this is disclosed in notes to tangible
fixed assets. The Company has also reviewed its loss provisioning at the reporting date and does not consider any
further provisions are required due to the ongoing insurance claims and limited operational impact of the fire.
The team in London Research Centre specialises in Artificial Intelligence (“AI”), focusing on computer vision
research, thereby enhancing the experience from consumer devices such as smartphones.
The team in Edinburgh Research Centre, building on the successful collaboration with the University of Edinburgh,
is developing middleware software technologies for mobile devices.
The Company also continues to operate a rental business at its investment properties whilst evaluating the strategic
use and options for the site.

Principal risks and uncertainties :
The directors consider the principal risks and uncertainties faced by the Company to be as follows: :
Competitive risks — the Company operates in a competitive industry and a continually evolving environment. The
Company places great emphasis on distributing new and innovative solutions to meet ever-changing customer
requirements.
Economic risks — the economic situation remains challenging in the near future. Any deterioration in the economy
may have an impact on the Company’s financial position. The Company relies on the Huawei Group in contracting
it to perform contract research and development services and contract manufacturing on their behalf. China represents
about two-thirds of the total market of the Huawei Group which means it is reliant on one major economy.
Political risks — over the last five years, the Company has put in place robust processes to deal with additional export :
, control and national security regulations arising out of its inclusion on the so called ‘Entity List’ by the USA
Administration in 2020. This means that the Company is now in a robust position to respond to changes in
international political situations.
Conflict in Ukraine risk — Since the start of the conflict, the Company has been compliant with all applicable laws in
relation to the conflict in Ukraine, in particular in relation to sanctions. The Company is vigilant in checking its
supplier and customer lists making sure that there are no business relationships with any Russian entities or
individuals. The Company has identified that the conflict and applicable laws will not have an impact upon the .
Company’s operations or its supply chain.
Finanéial risks — the principal rule of financial risk management at the Company is to ensure the appropriate cash
balance to manage the Company’s liquidity as well as financial assets and liabilities, and to control financial risk
exposure in a manner consistent with underlying business risk and financial policy. The Company does not undertake
any borrowing activities.
Future developments and business environment
The purpose of the Company is to develop leading edge technology solutions for the next generation of products and
applications and the Company continues to undertake research and development to this effect. Disruptive technologies
such as artificial intelligence are both an opportunity as well as a challenge in that they affect the business of the
Huawei Group and its customers. In response the Company shall adjust its operations in the forthcoming year when
and where that is necessary.
The directors acknowledge their responsibility for the Company’s systems of internal control and risk management :
and for monitoring their effectiveness. The purpose of these systems is to manage, rather than eliminate, the risk of
failure to achieve business objectives, to provide reasonable assurance as to the quality of management information
and to maintain proper control over the income, expenditure, assets and liabilities of the Company. .
No system of control can, however, provide absolute assurance against material misstatement or loss. Accordingly,
the directors have regard to what controls, in their judgment, are appropriate for the Company’s business relative to
the costs and benefits of implementing specific. controls.

Engaging with the Company’s stakeholders
A critical aspect of working constructively with the Company’s five key stakeholder groups is the engagement which
takes place to understand material issues of interest, and set out below are details of the engagement mechanisms that
exist within the Company, which ultimately support the Board’s understanding of relevant stakeholder views:
Internal customers ,
Proactive and continual engagement with our customers, through all project stages ensures high quality of services
and solutions provided. Maintaining long term relationships with our customers at management level allows
identification of customers’ needs to ensure that the Company’s services and solutions remain relevant, advanced and
reliable. Regular engagement with telecom operators and their customers allows the Company to gather the
requirement from the end-user market, thereby further developing our offering. Outputs of engagement are reported
and reviewed by the management team (Research Centre Directors) to support the right business decision for the
Company’s future investment plans and development of operational management processes.
Employees
The Company engages with its employees at all sites to understand areas of importance in respect of the working
environment, career development and Company culture. To gather the views from employees, the Company has built
an open-door policy, with direct access to Research Centre Directors and HR Business Partners at each local site as
well as direct engagement with the CEO. This way employees’ opinions and voices can be heard and the Company’s
culture can be understood and followed efficiently. The Company also proactively engages with employees through
staff briefings which include ‘no holds barred’ Q&A sessions.
Suppliers and Contractors
Constructive engagement with suppliers and contractors sets fair expectations on delivery and safety performance and
ensures good outcomes for local supply chains and the environment. The Company’s supplier relationship
management system (SRM) monitors qualification, selection, performance and development of suppliers that have a
critical influence on the growth of the Company. SRM has the ultimate aim of enhancing documentation filing
normalisation, decision-making transparency, and performance evaluation and strengthening relationships, and
provides quality management interface, which are a forum for dialogue with the supply chain on other topics, including
finance, quality management system, corporate social responsibility, safety, cyber security, logistics and business
capability. This work is led by the Company’s procurement team, in order to build a secure, reliable, and healthy
industry chain.
The Company began to disclose its payment mechanisms, processes, and efficiency on the Government website every
six months in accordance with the regulations and this information is publicly available. The Company pays attention
to the timeliness of payments to suppliers and carries out semi-annual reviews on payment statistics.
Board Members ,
The Company has a Board of statutory directors. The CEO has direct access to, and support from, the Company
directors and, if necessary, creating efficient communications in respect of key corporate risk management decisions.
Government and Regulators
The Company’s interest in establishing and maintaining excellent relationships with the UK government is driven by
the Company’s need to collaborate with its external stakeholders in the UK, including government agencies &
regulators, universities, UK companies, as well as prospective employees. The Company therefore has a strict
governance practice, with corporate risk management having ultimate control over operational management.
