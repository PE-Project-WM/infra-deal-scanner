Introduction
oe The directors present their Strategic Report on the group for the year ended 31 December 2025. :
Business review ,
The principal activity of the company is a holding company of the group's two main subsidiaries and trading
companies, Datum Datacentres Limited and Teledata UK Limited.
The principal activity of trading companies in the group is to provide colocation services as a Platform for Digital
Transformation to a mix of Tier 1 Enterprise and Service Providers.
With its strong and resilient business model underpinned by a robust client base, the group performed strongly
during the period exceeding growth targets in 2025 and started a second Datacentre in Manchester. Growth
continues to be impressive from the existing client and partner bases, a trend we expect to continue.
On a run rate basis the group exited 2025 at a quarterly run rate EBITDA performance of £3.9m (2024: £4.1m).
We expect the metric to progress in 2026.
With an established position as a premium provider at a competitive price point Datum is well placed to continue
to grow its client list of FTSE 250 and Fortune 500 clients.
With UBS Asset Management now as an investor, The Datum Group now has a new long-term investor that
provides certainty to our clients & business partners and will support our growth ambitions.
The group has drawn down a further £5m of shareholder loans to complete the funding for further expansion of
the Farnborough site, £4.5m has been invested as at the Balance Sheet date as construction in progress.
The group had cash reserves of £6.4m at 31 December 2025 (2024: £13.0m). :
The group's strategy is to maintain its strong organic growth, whilst also working with strategic partners to
introduce new clients and to broaden the services delivered to existing clients and to complement this growth
with appropriate M&A. :
The prevailing economic climate is uncertain, but the group believes it can continue to grow strongly. .
Principal risks and uncertainties , ‘
The management of the business and the execution of the group's strategy are subject to a number of risks;
these risks are identified and mitigated where appropriate. The key business risk affecting the group relates to
competition. In order to mitigate this risk, the group continues to expand its offerings and increase the value-add
provided to clients. The business continues to have a sound backlog of future contract income and has a long
track record of consistent revenue and profit growth coupled with cash generation. The group trades solely in the
UK with some supply chain items being sourced from the EU, therefore the recent US trade tariffs increases and
the conflict in the Middle-East are expected to have limited effects.

uo Going concern

The directors have a reasonable expectation, consistent with their forecasting procedures and the repayments
due on the various loans within the group, that the group have adequate resources to continue in operational
existence for the foreseeable future. At year end the group had more than £6m of cash and a further £10m of
approved shareholder loan notes to draw which is more than adequate for the FY26 development plans. On 29
April 2026 the group secured a £15m debt package from HSBC for further expansion, currently £4.7m has been
drawn. Most of the debt is shareholder debt and is not repayable until September 2031. The directors have also
considered the uncertainly of the current ongoing macroeconomic environment e.g. rising costs and are satisfied

. that the robust nature of the business model including long term contracts minimises any risk. The group trades
solely in the UK with some supply chain items.
Financial Risk Management Objectives and Policies .
In addition to its loan arrangements, finance leases and shareholder loans the group uses a number of different .
instruments including cash, trade debtors and trade creditors, which arise directly from its operating activities to
provide working capital for the group's operations.
Given the size and structure of the group the directors have not delegated the responsibility of managing
financial risk management to a subcommittee of the board. The polices agreed by the board are implemented by
the group's finance function. The directors’ view is that the main risk to the business is interest rates risk, credit
tisk and liquidity risk. : .
Interest Rate Risk
The group has £5.8m of bank loans and finance leases, where for the majority the interest rate is fixed for the
term of the loans. Due to the high percentage of loans on a fixed rate, the perceived risk for existing borrowing is
low, as has sufficient cash reserves to meet these obligations for at least 12 months after the signing of the
financial statements. :
Credit Risk
The group's principle financial assets are cash and trade debtors. In order to manage credit risk that directors set
a policy of monitor exposure by the size of the debt and the clients payment history. Exposure levels are reported
on and reviewed by senior management on a monthly basis in conjunction with the debt ageing and payment
history. .
Liquidity Risk
The group seeks to manage liquidity risk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable needs.
The cash position is reviewed on a daily basis and cashflow forecasts are updated weekly.
Matters covered in the Group Strategic Report
The directors have included details on future developments within the Strategic Report, as assessed by the
directors, in accordance with section 414C (11) of the Companies Act 2006.
Subsequent events ,
After the Balance Sheet date, the group refinanced repaying the £4.4m Barclays debt and taking a facility with
HSBC for £15m that can be drawn down. when required. At the date of signing £4.6m of the £15m had been
drawn. A further £7.1m of finance leases were also taken on 24th March 2026.
