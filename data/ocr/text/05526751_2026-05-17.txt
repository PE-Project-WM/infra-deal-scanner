STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
Introduction
The directors present their strategic report together with the audited financial statements for the year ended 31
December 2025.
Business review
Sagacity Solutions Limited (‘Sagacity’ or the ‘company’) is a leading data intelligence provider to UK consumer
facing enterprises, unlocking value across the whole customer lifecycle, from prospecting and acquisition to
billing and retention. Sagacity supports its customers’ teams, leveraging deep sector experience to rapidly deliver
best in class return on investment (ROI). Sagacity’s solutions are sector agnostic with a well-diversified customer
base of over 350 customers. Customers served include Sky, The AA, Tesco, British Gas, Southern Water,
Staysure, Octopus Energy and Kwik-Fit.
Sagacity saw continued success from its sales initiatives, securing a record number of multi-year contracts
awarded in 2025. This led to significant year on year growth and record revenues. These multi-year contracts
have strengthened the business’ committed revenue with similar levels of expected revenue growth in 2026. The -
business has continued to focus on further developing relationships with existing customers, as exhibited by
maintaining strong customer feedback, with the latest NPS score of +82 and 70% of the revenue being
: generated from repeatable engagements.
The business has continued to invest significantly in people and technology to preserve its strong market
position. Investments have been made in developing both solutions and better data alongside a first of its kind
. ~ Data Intelligence Platform for the full suite of Sagacity solutions, launched for Beta customers in Q4 2025.
: Sagacity expect this will enhance how its customers interact and execute the solutions purchased and also allow :
customers to visualise ROI before investing. Sagacity expect significant revenues to be generated from the new
: platform on an ongoing basis driving better cross sell opportunities and providing more value to the end
customer. Sagacity continues to invest in its people, bringing new skill sets and developing existing talent, with
continued low levels of churn in 2025.
The accounts show turnover for the year of £28,266,123 (2024 - £21,620,138) and profit before tax for the year
of £6,406,594 (2024 - £3,042,039).
Principal risks and uncertainties
Sagacity's biggest risk is the market competitive environment and our clients’ in house teams. This is mitigated
to a large extent as Sagacity provides specialised software and consultants along with unique proprietary data
sets which customers cannot replicate in house and Sagacity's software is provided to customers as a service
with support on an ongoing basis. The offering is further differentiated through the delivery of the Data
Intelligence Platform.
Financial key performance indicators ("KPls'} :
The directors use a variety of performance measures in order to monitor and manage the business effectively.
The main KPls for the company are turnover, gross profit and EBITDA as these are the drivers of profitability.
The directors are satisfied with the performance of the business against these KPIs. There are no non financial
KPIs. ,

Financial risk management
The activities of the business expose it to several financial risks. Management of these risks is outlined below:
Credit risk
The company's credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade debtors. Credit risk is managed by weekly aged
debtor meetings, sending monthly client statements of accounts and one to one chasers with customers who
become overdue.
Price risk. .
The company is marginally exposed to movement in foreign exchange. Sagacity currently doesn't have any
customers who pay in non-Sterling.
Liquidity and cash flow risk
The company monitors cash flow as part of its day-to-day control procedures. The board considers cash flow
projections on a monthly basis and ensures that appropriate facilities are available to be drawn upon as
necessary. Sagacity maintains a healthy level of cash by working on 30-day terms with customers.
Data loss and risk of cyber attack .
- The company is susceptible to the risk associated with data loss or cyber attack. The company sets out to :

. mitigate these risks through regular threat analysis, internal and external audits, vulnerability scanning and
regularly monitoring legislation changes in the UK.
Future developments
Sagacity's aim is to continually grow and achieve the business plan through both organic and inorganic activity.
Sagacity has a business development team who use account based management. Sagacity are also present at

. marketing events, trade shows and have good Search Engine Optimisation (SEO) capability. The revenue is
expected to grow year on year and continue at a similar trajectory in the following year.
Research and development
Sagacity continues to enhance, improve and build new solutions in order to help with the growth of the company
and to help Sagacity stand out in the competitive market environment.
People .
Sagacity continues to invest in people and aims to attract the best talent across all teams with different skills and
knowledge throughout different levels to contribute to the growth of the company. Employee numbers have

. remained relatively consistent with the prior year. Sagacity is expecting the number of employees to grow, but

: not proportionally to revenue growth expected in future years, given the data solutions offered which are not :
resource dependent. . .

Going concern :
The directors are continually reviewing their forecasts and believe that the going concern basis continues to be
appropriate for the company. The directors have prepared forecasts covering a period of at least 12 months from

operational existence for the foreseeable future.
The company has a very high level of customer retention, a well diversified customer portfolio with a growing
number of customers.

2.4 Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the

consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes.
Turnover is recognised by reference to the nature of the service provided as follows:
Licence fees and delivery of support and maintenance is recognised based on a straight-line basis
over the period to which they relate when the terms of the contract support straight-line recognition.
Provision and processing of data is recognised at a rate per record in the period in which the service
is provided.
Batch data arrangements that include an on-going update service are apportioned to reflect the level
of service provided across each delivery to the customer.
Professional services contracts containing separate performance obligations (milestones) are
recognised as the relevant milestones are achieved.
Risk and reward contracts are based on an appropriate assessment of the expected reward share on
the individual project.

2.5 Foreign currency translation
Functional and presentation currency
: The Company's functional and presentational currency is GBP.

Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency using the spot exchange

2.7 Research and development
In the research phase of an internal project it is not possible to demonstrate that the project will

project if and only if certain specific criteria are met in order to demonstrate the asset will generate
probable future economic benefits and that its cost can be reliably measured. The capitalised
development costs are subsequently amortised on a straight-line basis over their useful economic
lives, which range from 3 to 5 years.
If it is not possible to distinguish between the research phase and the development phase of an
internal project, the expenditure is treated as if it were all incurred in the research phase only.

Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial. The Company's cash and cash
equivalents, trade and most other debtors due with the operating cycle fall into this category of
financia! instruments.
Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference
between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the
asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the
estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The
impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of
the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the
impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying
amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in the profit or loss.
