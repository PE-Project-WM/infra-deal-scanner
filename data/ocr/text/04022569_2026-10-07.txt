The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025. ;
Review of business
During the year, the Company has continued to fulfil its obligations in respect of commercial contracts.
The Company saw a decrease in revenue this year to £40,224k (2024: £46,351k), primarily due to the loss of a
significant client contract during the year. Operating profit increased to £3.2m (2024: £2.5m) reflecting effective
cost management and operational efficiencies. Administrative expenses were lower year-on-year, consistent with
the scale-down of activities following the contract loss, which contributed to the improved profitability.
The balance sheet remains strong with a good cash balance at the end of the year £20,176k (2024: £12,945k).
During the year, the Company received a dividend of £2.1m (2024: £5.5m) from its subsidiary, Conduent
Business Services Canada Inc. .
During the year ended December 2024, Conduent Inc. completed an internal restructuring transaction pursuant
to which Conduent Export LLC transferred 35% of its shareholding in Conduent Netherlands Holding BV (“CNH
BV’) to Conduent Business Process Solutions Limited in exchange for cash resulting in Conduent Business
Process Solutions Limited owning a 35% “hook stock interest” in CNH BV, which is the Conduent group's top
non-US holding company. The transaction was funded by way of a capita! contribution from a higher tier US
parent company down the chain of legal entities. The value of the capital contribution is £177m.
We engaged third parties to review this under multiple accounting authorities and the onward accounting
implications which Conduent has accepted and applied.
Owning a 35% interest in CNH BV would entitle Conduent Business Process Solutions Ltd to share in the future
eamings of shared services centres and other foreign businesses beneath CNH BV via dividend distributions up-
the-chain to, and then ultimately by, CNH BV. Conduent Business Process Solutions Ltd could then use such
dividend distributions to fund (as well as potentially expand) its business operations conducted in the UK and
globally.
In accordance with Section 136 of the UK Companies Act 2006, a body corporate that is a subsidiary is generally
prohibited from being a member of its UK holding company. However, the Directors obtained advice that
confirmed this this prohibition applies only to companies formed and registered under the Companies Act 2006.

. As Conduent Netherlands Holding B.V. is a Dutch entity and not a company as defined under the Act, the
restriction does not apply in this case.
The directors are satisfied with the results achieved for the year. Furthermore, the directors expect the Company
to maintain the healthy gross and operating profits in the future.

. Principal risks and uncertainties

With over 90% of our revenue eamed from third parties we take commercial risks inherent in any contract, such
as pricing, credit, liquidity and cash flow risk.

It is noted that the Company bears local risks in relation to employee management, quality control, contract
performance, facilities, equipment management and similar matters. The risks are mitigated by ensuring that the
continued support and resources of the group are made available to the Company. The Company is not reliant on
any one individual in fulfilling its contractual obligations.

Financial risk management

The Company operates systems and controls to mitigate any adverse effects across the range of risks that it
faces. The nature of the majority of the Company's business is such that it has no significant exposure to price
risks, extemal credit risk or interest rate risks. The company is exposed to currency risk from billing in foreign .
currency (EUR and USD). Fluctuations in exchange rates between these currencies and the company's
functional currency can lead to unpredictable variations in revenue and expenses. This exposure can impact the .
company's financial performance, potentially leading to reduced profit margins or even losses if adverse currency
movements occur. The risk is managed through hedging.

Key performance indicators .
The Company uses two key performance indicators to measure and monitor performance. These are operating
profit margin percentage and turnover. Key performance indicators are calculated and reviewed at divisional and
consolidated levels. All indicators are calculated under UK Generally Accepted Accounting Principles ("GAAP").
Operating profit margin percentage (taken to be the ratio of operating profit/turnover in percentage) has
increased from 5% in 2024 to 8% in 2025. For 2026 the margin percentage is expected to remain at a similar
level.

Turnover has decreased from £46 million in 2024 to £40 million, due to the loss of a client contract at the end of
2024, the activity ramped down in the first half of 2025. _

Going concern :

The business generated an operating profit of £3,244k in 2025 (2024: £2,480k). From 2026 we anticipate
improved margins from restructuring activities taking place in 2025. A cashflow forecast exercise was carried out
for the period up to September 2027. The directors believe that the Company is well placed to manage its
business risks successfully despite the current challenging economic environment. Accordingly, the directors
continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements.

. We made comprehensive projections and factored in severe but plausible scenarios to ensure robust planning
and preparedness. This approach allows us to anticipate potential challenges and develop effective strategies to
mitigate risks, ensuring resilience and adaptability in the face of uncertainty.

The Company is a private company limited by shares and is incorporated and domiciled in the UK. The address
of its registered office is C(O Rayner Essex Tavistock House South, Tavistock Square, London, England, WC1H

Principal activities
. The principai activities of the Company include business process outsourcing, human resources information
services and finance and accounting processing.
Financial Results .
The company achieved an operating profit in the year of 3,244k (2024: 2,480k).
The net assets in the company are strong at £249m (2024: £258m). The company has a wide spread of risk .
across the balance sheet and is not exposed to a single customer or supplier to achieve its results.
The company remained profitable at the operating activities level in both 2024 and 2025, demonstrating
: resilience despite the loss of a major client contract. Management remains confident in the strength of the
existing customer base and the ongoing renewal of current contracts, which is expected to support sustained
profitability going forward. In addition, restructuring activities undertaken at the beginning of the year have
streamlined operations and are anticipated to deliver improved margins from 2026 onwards.
While the company has reported losses due to impairments of investments in 2025, it is important to note that it
was an exceptional circumstance. The value of the investments held by the company has decreased following an
impairment assessment carried out of its investment portfolio, considering the impact of the announced disposals
and the resulting restructuring of the investments, together with the performance and future prospects of the
remaining underlying businesses. As part of the assessment, the Company considered the expected future
economic benefits and income streams associated with both the investments impacted by the disposals and the
: remaining investment portfolio.
The assessment identified a reduction in the expected future income from certain investments, together with the
impact of the disposals on the value and future income-generating capacity of the relevant investments. As a
result of the overall impairment assessment, the Company has recognised an impairment charge of £15 million at
the reporting date, which has been recorded against the carrying values of the Company's investments.
There was no impairment for the year ended 31 December 2024. The impairments represent a non-recurring
event, and the underlying business fundamentals remain strong.
During the year the Company did not pay a dividend (2024: £Nil).

Impairment of tangible and intangible assets
At each reporting end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible
assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss.
\f.any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the
extent of the impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an
individual asset, the company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which
the asset belongs. : .
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in
. use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate

for which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
. If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying

Trade and other receivables
Trade and other receivables are amounts due from customers for services performed in the ordinary
course of business. Loans receivable are measured initially at fair value, net of transaction costs, and
are measured subsequently at amortised cost using the effective interest method, less any impairment.
Trade receivables, loans and other receivables that have fixed or determinable payments that are not
quoted in an active market are classified as ‘debt instruments’. Debt instruments are measured at
' amortised cost using the effective interest method, less any impairment.
Interest is recognised by applying the effective interest rate, except for short-term receivables when the
recognition of interest would be immaterial. The effective interest method is a method of calculating the
amortised cost of a debt instrument and of allocating the interest income over the relevant period. The
effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash receipts through the
expected life of the debt instrument to the net carrying amount on initial recognition.
The company applies the IFRS 9 simplified approach to measuring expected credit losses which uses a ,

receivables are a reasonable approximation of the loss rates for the accrued income. The assessment
