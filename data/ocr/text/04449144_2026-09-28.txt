Strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025
The directors present the Strategic report of Airplus International Limited (“the company”) for the year ended 31
December 2025.
Review of business
The results for the year are set out in the income statement on page 10. The result for the company shows a
profit before income tax of £7.2 million for the year (2024: £0.9 million).
The UK business is split almost equally across the three primary products that are offered in the UK: company
account, corporate card and virtual card. The customers base is also well balanced with large strategic,
multinational as well as SME clients. This diversification ensures a stable and robust client base on which organic

"growth and new business is added on a daily basis. The client retention rate is strong due to the service level
and specialist services that we can provide.
We continue to foster new partnerships in new tangents of the business to minimise the risk to the business
should an existing partnership fail or stagnate and have a desire to increasingly source new business directly to
mitigate the reliance on existing/new partners
A large part of the overall business is linked to travel and expenditure. An economic downturn, geopolitical
conditions, along with a negative or uncertain political climate, poses significant risks to the travel industry. Such
conditions can reduce consumer spending, lower discretionary income, and deter travel, leading to decreased
demand for travel-related products and services. Consequently, these factors could materially and adversely
affect our business.
There are no significant events that have been identified which remain to be adjusted in the financial statements
and supporting notes.
Key Performance Indicators : :
The company’s directors monitor the performance of the business, with revenue being the company’s key
performance indicator. The company achieved revenue of £25.2 million in 2025 (2024: £24.0 million).
Principal risks and uncertainties
Commercial risk
The primary commercial risk results from a highly competitive market and potential losses of key customers to
competitors. The company actively monitors client performance and maintains its client relationships, provides
customer services and reacts to customer demands to mitigate the loss risk.
The directors’ consideration of the appropriateness of the going concer basis in preparing the financial
statements is set out in note 1 to the financial statements. The directors have not identified any material
uncertainty relating to going concern since sufficient funding is available and is robust in a stressed scenario.

Financial risk management
The company’s financial risk strategy is determined on a group wide basis by the company’s ultimate parent
company Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken AB. The company's operations expose it to a variety of financial risks
that primarily include the effects of credit, interest rate and foreign exchange risk. The company does not use
derivative financial instruments and as such no hedge accounting is applied. Price risk, liquidity risk and cash
flow risk are not significant to the company and have therefore not been separately discussed.
Credit risk
The company’s credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade debtors. The company establishes credit limits with
each customer which are approved by management and the client. The company’s exposure is monitored on a
daily basis using automated credit blocks. The company transfers its funds to a group affiliate at the end of each
business day, creating an intercompany loan repayable on demand. .
Interest rate risk
There is no high interest rate risk, because during the course of the year the company had interest-bearing assets
and liabilities which comprise intercompany balances, all of which earned interest at a variable rate. Loan interest
is payable at a rate of 3-month GBP SONIA plus a margin.
Foreign exchange risk
The immediate parent company is based in Germany and uses the Euro as a functional and transactional
currency, exposing the company to exchange risk. The immediate parent company uses currency risk
management policies to reduce the impact in the company.
