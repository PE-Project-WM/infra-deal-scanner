Strategic Report
The Board of Directors of Colt Group Holdings Limited (the ‘Company’) present their Strategic Report on the Group (‘Colt), for the
year ended 31 December 2025.
Overview
Colt is a London-headquartered global digital infrastructure company founded in 1992, specialising in high-bandwidth, software-
defined networking (SDN), voice solutions as well as hyperscale and enterprise data centre services. Colt’s ultimate parent company
is SHM Lightning Investors LLC, with the business having been acquired in 2015 as part of an investment involving Fidelity
Investments. It operates a vast fibre network connecting over 30,000 buildings in Europe, the Middle East and Africa, Asia, and North
America, serving businesses with secure, on-demand, and Al-powered connectivity. Colt’s strategy centres around its infrastructure
powering the global Al economy and its commitment to making connections simple and effortless, creating extraordinary outcomes
for its customers.
Strategy
As a global digital infrastructure company for more than 30 years, Colt empowers businesses to succeed by connecting them to the
platforms, infrastructure, and ecosystems that make extraordinary outcomes possible. Guided by a deep commitment to customer
experience, firm values, and a people-first culture, Colt enables organisations to thrive in an ever-evolving digital world.
With its vision to be the digital infrastructure company that the world’s leading businesses choose to connect with, Colt is guided by
its dedication to customer innovation, its values and its responsibility to its customers, partners, people and the planet.
Business review and principal activities
Colt is structured along two key lines of business: Colt Core (CORE’, including Network and Voice) and Data Centre Services (‘DCS’).
This structure focuses on Colt’s assets, providing end-to-end ownership and responsibility for revenue, profits and cash returns with
visibility of performance and delivery.
Since the acquisition of the Lumen EMEA business in 2023, Colt has undergone significant transformation as it immediately began
integrating the two organisations. LUMEN EMEA was the EMEA business of Lumen Technologies Inc - NYSE (LUMEN US) which
was acquired by Colt in 2023 and forms part of its CORE business. In 2025, the Group continued to make progress in unifying
operations, enhancing customer experience, and strengthening Colt’s position in the market. Colt has substantially expanded its
scale and capabilities, reflecting the impact of the enlarged business. Integration activities will continue into 2026, with a continuous
focus on migrating customers onto the Colt systems stack and realising the full operational and commercial benefits of the combined
organisation.
On 2 September 2025, the Group completed the divestment of eight of its nine Lumen EMEA data centres, classified as held for sale
last year, enabling us to focus on our strategic imperatives of driving growth and optimising our portfolio.
Colt's EBITDA(AL)' reduced by €99.1m from €464.9m in 2024 to €365.8m in 2025. This decrease was primarily driven by the
reduction in revenue and other income of €111.6m, a result of the challenging macro-economic and geopolitical climate, and cyber
incident experienced in Q3 2025. Our cost base showed improvements year on year, a reflection of our integration and transformation
programmes, and Colt’s ability to manage and contain operational costs.
Exceptional items increased during the year by €398.1m fram €98.8m in 2024 to €496.9m in 2025. This increase is primarily due to
impairment charges of €610.3m recognised in relation to the CORE and DCS cash-generating units. See Note 12. This impact was
partially offset by a gain on disposal of €234.7m arising from the divestment of the Lumen EMEA data centres, supporting the Group’s
ongoing portfolio optimisation. Further details are provided in Note 8 Exceptional items.
The group’s net assets are €2,844.3m (2024: €3,550.4m), with the year-on-year movement largely driven by impairment charges
totalling €610.3m across both CORE and DCS CGuUs. The balance sheet remains resilient underpinned by cash inflows from the
completed divestment of data centres.

Cyber incident on COLT and its impact
Colt suffered a Cyber incident in August 2025, which impacted all business support systems, further requiring manual processes to
be implemented for critical activities. While CORE network infrastructure was reported to be unaffected, internal business support
systems, Colt Online, and customer portals faced outages. Colt contained the issue, and our core network infrastructure remained
untouched. DCS data centres were not affected by the incident.
Colt took action to remove the threat actor from our environment, and proactively took some systems offline, leading to disruption to
some elements of our services. Colt brought in specialist third-party investigation, forensic and cyber support and have implemented
enhanced containment and security measures to further harden our systems as well as help prevent similar incidents in the future.
The company took systems offline as a precaution and worked to restore services. Coit implemented additional controls and
completed the restoration of the majority its systems in December 2025. Financial reporting systems were not impacted by the
activities of the threat actor and no further threat actor activity was identified within Colt's network environment since the restoration
of systems.
Colt proactively notified the relevant regulators and authorities and will continue to work closely with law enforcement agencies. The
cyber incident caused temporary disruption in our business in the second half of 2025, impacting overall financial performance for
the year and commercial momentum.
Financial performance and position
Total Revenue
Total revenue for 2025 dropped by €94.3m (4.3%) from €2,191.6m in 2024 to €2,097.3m in 2025.
Network revenue decreased by €73.2m (4.1%) from €1,799.9m in 2024 to €1,726.7m in 2025. The decline reflects challenging
macroeconomic and geopolitical conditions.
Voice revenue dropped by €20.4m (8.7%) from €233.7m in 2024 to €213.3m in 2025, primarily driven by broader decline in demand
for voice services across Europe, with the impact from 2024 continuing into 2025.
Data Centre Services revenue remained broadly stable, decreasing by €0.7m (0.4%) from €158.0m in 2024 to €157.3m in 2025.

Gross profit dropped by €64.4m (11.9%) from €540.9m in 2024 to €476.5m in 2025 largely caused by the reduction in revenue.
Net Operating Expenses
Net operating expenses increased by €626.9m (89.5%) from €700.4m in 2024 to €1,327.3m in 2025, primarily driven by impairment
charges of €610.3m.
Operating Loss
Operating loss after exceptional items increased by €691.3m from €159.5m loss in 2024 to €850.8m loss in 2025 largely driven by
the factors noted above.
EBITDA(AL)
Colt's EBITDA(AL) reduced by €99.1m from €464.9m in 2024 to €365.8m in 2025. This decrease was primarily driven by the reduction
in revenue and other income of €111.6m, a result of the challenging macro-economic and geopolitical climate, and cyber incident
experienced in Q3 2025. Our cost base showed improvements year on year, a reflection of our integration and transformation
Programmes, and Colt’s ability to manage and contain operational costs.
Exceptional items
In 2025 exceptional costs increased by €398.1m from €98.8m in 2024 to €496.9m in 2025. The movement is largely driven by higher

€21.0m in relation to amounts arising from customer disputes. These increases were partly offset by a gain on disposal of €234.7m,
from the disposal of data centres previously classified as held for sale. Refer to Note 8 Exceptional items for further detail.
Finance income, finance costs & non-operating expenses/income
Net other expenses before exceptional items were €6.3m (2024: €129.9m). The movement is largely driven by a swing from a foreign

the year on the USD denominated Revolving Credit Facility (RCF) facilities. Finance costs also decreased from €87.3m in 2024 to
€64.4m in 2025, associated with the financing arrangements noted above.

Colt’s risk management programme
Colt’s enterprise risk management programme seeks to drive effective identification and management of risk across the Group. Colt
CORE and Colt DCS maintain two separate Audit Committees, supporting teams and governance structures to enable oversight of
the two areas. The Head of Risk (Colt CORE) andthe Head of Governance, Risk and Compliance (Colt DCS) including their
supporting teams are responsible for their respective risk management frameworks.
The CORE and OCS Audit Committees received reporting and review the results of the respective risk management processes on
behalf of the Board at least twice a year and report relevant matters to the Board. Any combined Group disclosures are reported
through the CORE Audit Committee. Colt’s Internal Audit team reports to the respect Audit Committee chairs and provides
independent assurance across both CORE and DCS on selected areas in terms of the design and operating effectiveness of controls
against a risk-based plan, which is subject to Audit Committee approval.
In addition to its established governance structures, CORE has launched a cross-functional Risk Committee, bringing together
executives and senior leaders from across the business to strengthen oversight and accountability for top enterprise risks.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Macro-economic and geopolitical
Pricing in the telecoms market continues to decline on a per Gigabit-per-second basis, particularly for Optical and Wave products
across European markets, in line with long established industry trends. This pricing dynamic is, to a large degree, offset by sustained
growth in customer bandwidth requirements, resulting in broadly stable average revenue per user over time.
One specific area of heightened competitive intensity is long distance Optical Wave services between major data centres. In this
segment, several providers are present in largely the same datacentre locations, offering highly standardised products. This has
driven fierce price competition and extended capital payback periods. This position is partially compensated for by Colts existing and
extensive connectivity to enterprise buildings where there is a lower level of competition.
Geopolitical uncertainty remained elevated throughout 2025. US trade policy, including the introduction of broad-based tariffs
affecting global supply chains for technology and network equipment, has increased cost and lead-time pressure on certain
components relevant to Colt's infrastructure programmes. The associated uncertainty has also dampened business investment
confidence, with some customers delaying digital transformation decisions and negatively affecting Colt’s sales pipeline. tn parallel,
demand for data centre sites and capacity continues to increase, driven by Al and data intensive workloads, against a backdrop of
growing constraints on power availability and grid connectivity in key markets.
The vulnerability of critical communications infrastructure was brought into sharp focus during 2024 and 2025 by anumber of subsea
cable incidents in the Baltic Sea. European authorities and NATO raised concerns about deliberate sabotage, prompting dedicated
maritime operations in response. These incidents underscore the strategic importance of route diversity and resilience, reflected in
Colt's continued investment. Colt continues to engage with government and industry forums to monitor nation-state and geopolitical
threats to critical infrastructure.
Change, integration and transformation
Colt CORE continues to execute on asignificant change agenda. The ongoing integration of the Lumen EMEA
business remains central to realising the full strategic and financial benefits of the acquisition, including the harmonisation
of technology stacks and networks.
Colt CORE and Colt DCS are jointly delivering a major transformation programme to separate their systems and services, enabling
DCS to focus on its own processes to support growth ambitions, security requirements, and a standalone business operating model.
In parallel, CORE is accelerating its cost transformation through simplification and automation of supporting processes and tools to
remain competitive. The pace and volume of change require careful management to not create significant disruption, change fatigue
and capacity constraints. Delivery on these change programmes is required for ongoing efficiency improvement, maintenance of
customer experience, ability to reduce cost to serve, to ensure sustainable margins and growth.
The cyber incident experienced in 2025 resulted in incident-related response and recovery activities that required reprioritisation of
elements of the transformation and integration roadmap, creating schedule pressure on certain programmes. Targeted mitigations
are in place to protect revised critical milestones and managed disruption risk to customers during migration activities. Colt manages
this through detailed cutover planning, testing and contingency procedures, with oversight from the Executive Leadership Team
supported by sub-governance structures.

Sales performance and competition

The telecoms sector is characterised by ongoing substitution from legacy services to newer technologies, including migration from

IP VPN to SDWAN, Ethernet and Internet access, and from legacy voice services to Cloud Voice solutions. This transition is

industrywide and requires sustained investment in new product capabilities and incremental sales effort to migrate customers, often

with the objective of preserving revenues rather than driving near-term growth.

A central element of CORE'’s strategy is the progressive investment and development of Network as a Service (NaaS) capability to

manage risk around a competitive offering and sustainable efficiency. In the short term, this is primarily focused on driving internal

efficiencies through increased digitalisation and automation of commercial and operational processes, reducing reliance on manual

interactions and improving cost-to-serve and creating a more streamlined buying experience. Over the mid-term, Naa& is intended

to simplify the customer experience by enabling greater self-service, assisted serve and real-time interaction.

The cyber incident experienced in 2025 has introduced new reputational risk that may impact customer confidence and retention in

the near to medium term, with knock-on risks to sales pipeline and renewal rates. The treatment of cyber risk is set out in the Cyber

Security section below.

CORE operates a structured sales governance methodology, the Go Process, to ensure deals are bid on the right commercial and

strategic terms across all markets and customer segments. A key element of CORE's strategy is to develop partnerships with other

major players in the connectivity world to create economies of scale and deliver seamless experiences for partners and customers.

Failure to deliver expected returns from these strategic partnerships could jeopardise Colt's growth ambitions. To manage this, CORE

has developed specific capabilities through its partner hub, providing a structured approach to partnership development across the

full lifecycle.

DCS applies a robust Sales Governance framework across all sales opportunities to ensure effective oversight, risk management

and strategic alignment throughout the deal lifecycle. The framework defines the internal functions that contribute to each opportunity

and establishes a clear governance pathway through which opportunities are qualified as they progress.

Sales opportunities are reviewed through a structured series of governance forums, including the Deal Team (comprising members

of the Senior Leadership Team), the Executive Team (including the Chief Sales Officer, Chief Commercial Officer and Chief Real

Estate Officer), the Investment Board, and ultimately the Board, as per the defined Delegation of Authority levels.

The framework provides a defined approach to managing opportunities in accordance with their risk profile, complexity and capacity

requirements, including instances where new capacity development is required. It also sets out clear roles and responsibilities to

support auditable and disciplined decision-making, transparency and accountability across the organisation.

Service interruption

Network and data centre availability and security are paramount to Colt's customers. A failure in the operation of Colt's key systems,

data centre operations or network infrastructure, including its transatlantic subsea cables, could cause a failure of service to

customers and negatively impact Colt’s brand and reputation. The breadth of this risk was demonstrated across several events during

2024 and 2025. Extreme weather conditions, including flooding in southern Europe impacted Colt infrastructure with services

migrated to alternate sites and a full assessment completed across other potentially vulnerable locations.

The national power outage affecting Spain and parts of Portugal in 2025, affected customer services across the region, though all

Colt owned and operated facilities remained operational with zero customer impact. Subsea cable cuts in the Middle East resulted in

impact to Colt's IP backbone and certain customer services, with additional diversely routed capacity subsequently put in place to

increase resilience. As noted in the Macro-economic and Geopolitical section above, sector-wide subsea cable incidents in the Baltic

Sea during 2024 and 2025 further validated Colt's ongoing investment and approach to maintaining route diversity.

To manage the risks of large-scale disruption and outages, Colt has designed a resilient network architecture and uses dedicated

teams to monitor, manage and maintain its infrastructure across both CORE and DCS. Colt operates a Business Continuity

Programme certified to ISO 22301 standards where relevant. Colt continues to leverage the growth of its backbone network

infrastructure to increase route diversity and resilience, including 24x7 monitoring of connectivity infrastructure, multiple active cable

routes and established marine maintenance arrangements for repair in the event of an incident.

Cyber security
Cyber threats continue to evolve rapidly. In 2025, there was a marked increase in the sophistication and frequency of attacks targeting
critical infrastructure and network service providers. Heightened geopolitical tensions and nation-state activity, the exploitation of
zero-day vulnerabilities, the rise of Al-enabled attacks (including more sophisticated phishing and business email compromise), and
the increased targeting of supply chains and technology vendors all contributed to a significantly elevated threat environment
throughout the year.
In 2025, Colt experienced a cyber incident that disrupted certain back-office systems; however, there was no impact to customer-
facing networks. Following the incident, Colt implemented additional preventive and detective controls, with a particular focus on
strengthening perimeter security, secure remote access, enhancing identity and privileged access management security logging and
monitoring capabilities.
Looking ahead, the threat landscape continues to intensify. For CORE, obligations under EU NIS2, the UK Telecommunications

Auditors’ responsibilities for the audit of the financial statements :

Our objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements as a whole are free from

material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue .an auditors’ report that includes our opinion.

, Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance, but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with

ISAs (UK) will always detect a material misstatement when it exists. Misstatements can arise from fraud or error and

, are considered material if, individually or in the aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence the.
economic decisions of users taken on the basis of these financial statements.

Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures in

: line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect material misstatements in respect of irregularities, including
fraud. The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud, is detailed below.

Based on our understanding of the group and industry, we identified that the principal risks of non-compliance with .

laws and regulations related to cyber security and data protection regulations and telecommunication industry specific

. legislation and regulations which could result in penalties for non-compliance or limitation or withdrawal of. Colt Group

Holdings Limited's authorisations to operate across territories, and we considered the extent to which non-compliance

might have a material effect on the financial statements. We also considered those laws and regulations thathavea_

direct impact on the financial statements such as taxation legislation relevant to the group and the company and the

Companies Act 2006. We evaluated management's incentives and opportunities for fraudulent manipulation of the

financial statements (including the risk of override of controls), and determined that the principal risks were related © :

to posting inappropriate journal entries to manipulate financial results and potential management bias in accounting ~
. estimates and judgements. Audit procedures performed by the engagement team included:
