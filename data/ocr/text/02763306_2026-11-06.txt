INTRODUCTION ‘
The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31st January 2026 ("FY26”).
The Company (“JHG’) notes the requirement for the board to take into account the factors set out in section 172 of
the Companies Act 2006 when completing their strategic report. This report takes into account that requirement.
The Shamrock Topco Group comprises of the parent company and 10 wholly owned subsidiaries.
The range of products and specialist services offered by the subsidiaries covers telecommunications infrastructure
installation, power infrastructure installation, associated engineering services, civil engineering, highways
diversionary works and traffic management services.
BUSINESS REVIEW
In line with our primary duty under section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 to “promote the success of the company”
the directors have developed a strategy that is clear and fully aligned with the members wishes and requirements
for the company over the next three years.
Strategic Update :
Through FY26 John Henry Group continue to deliver extensive digital infrastructure services across Tier 1 Telecoms
asset owners, whilst further derisking exposure to the Altnet new build programmes across the UK.
The offering of a strategic mix of new fibre services, with national network maintenance and network assurance
services, alongside an excellent reputation for customer service, was key in securing the award of over £150m of
Fibre Upgrade works through the “Hyperion” procurement project for VMO2 stretching beyond 2030.
Foreseeing the downturn in revenues from build activity has been a key factor in the successful strategy of increasing
wallet share with VMO2 through extension into electrification activity on street-scene assets and infrastructure across
the VMO2 southern regions.
The customer centric approach now extends to specific fibre and engineering led services across other key asset
owners and pursuing digital infrastructure projects across the Datacentre market into FY27.
There is a significant increase in spend and activity across the National Grid estate in readiness for the Energy
Transition. This has been mirrored by increased revenues within our Overhead Fibre maintenance projects for
Vodafone/National Grid to support their national control room network. This volume is expected to continue following
the path of the significant £10bn investment through the “Great Grid Upgrade”, however it is expected that the true
revenue uptick will start to come through in Q4 FY27 as projects move from design into delivery.
Our Avanti Traffic Management business continues to grow its external revenues with a very bright outlook for future
growth. Pivoting from 20% to 80% external revenues whilst delivering excellent safety and customer service mainly
across the East Anglia region. The business has undertaken a detailed review of its operating model to ensure our
growth is led by industry leading safety and assurance across the network, in preparation for further growth into
adjacent regions.
Meanwhile, Highways infrastructure activities are increasing in investment across frameworks such as the LCRR
framework covering over circa £1bn of spend across 644km of roads; much of which is within our geography.

Diversification
Whilst the Directors remain committed that our Digital Infrastructure and complex Civil Engineering are core to our
forward strategy, we are also focused on the opportunity for growth in Renewable Power and Power Infrastructure
across DNO and Grid upgrades over the coming years.
The appointment of sector leading business development expertise alongside a laser focused approach to work
winning has seen our business development pipeline expand to over £1bn of viable project opportunities. JHG has
successfully gained accreditation as an Independent Connections Provider and is now delivering civils and
electrification and connection works across the renewables sector, specifically Solar and Battery Storage schemes
and network reinforcement. This strategy will continue to see JHG expanding capability into the ED3 regulatory
cycle and the large programmes required to upgrade DNO and Transmission assets, again within. the addressable
geographies in the East and South East of England.
JHG celebrated its 40" Year in FY26 and is well placed to continue expansion of its specialist services into adjacent
sectors, bringing decades of Civils expertise into the Power and Water sectors where Civils capability and capacity
is highly sought.
Going Forward
Once again, the business plan for the year to 318 January 2027 aims to improve the quality of the revenues and
margins, maintain a name for Excellence, reduce overheads as a percentage of revenues, increase the Earnings
Before Interest, Taxation, Depreciation and Amortisation (“EBITDA’”), and deliver high levels of cash generation.
Cash generation and conversion of profits into cash has, historically, been very strong and in the eight years between
FY17 to FY26 the Group generated net cash from Operations of £51m.
The Company continues to believe that any Tier 1 provider of scale with repeat revenues, multi- disciplinary
offerings, a strong pipeline of opportunities, enduring profitable growth, good cash generation and a strong
management team, will continue to attract interest from investors and trade parties alike.
The Company is a cash generative, Tier 1 provider of scale to the Telecoms industry with all those attributes on
show in the financial year to 31 January 2026 and operates in markets with healthy growth prospects for many
years to come.
Review of FY26
In the year to 31st January 2026 the Group delivered full year revenues of £45.3m, gross profit of £11.1m (24%) and
an operating profit of £3.9m.
After a difficult financial performance in FY25, in line with most operators in the market, FY26 has been a good
year with the ‘core continuing’ activities within the business performing very strongly with a significant improvement
in gross margin being generated through these ‘core continuing activities’ as well as margin improvement being
generated by close out of low margin build contracts.
A strong focus on working capital and cash collection, together with profits for the year turning to cash, meant that
the Group generated £6.2m of cash in the year, demonstrating the continued strength in this working capital
management across the business.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
All new opportunities are, and will continue to be, assessed by a New Work Approval Process (NWAP), a key part
of our internal governance that assesses risk. The NWAP includes a sign off by at least four directors including the
approval of one Group non-executive director, thereby strengthening the chances of managing risks whilst achieving
profitable growth from the opportunities that the Company chooses to pursue.
This control process is extremely important to the business as we progress our diversification strategy into ‘Power’
and ‘EV charging’ markets to ensure that all new opportunities meet the requirements of the longer-term plan for the
Group
Accounting Controls :
The business operates a variety of accounting and control systems and as part of the ongoing growth of the business
these are regularly reviewed by the Directors to ensure their applicability to the growth in business volumes.
Information Technology
The company implemented a new works management system in FY26 and this has now been successfully rolled
out across the Group improving operational controls and eliminating the risks associated with legacy IT infrastructure.
Competition
The Company believes that its strong, embedded and longstanding relationships with clients are not easily replicated
by competitors, but the business still remains wary of competition and competitive risk.
The Company continues to encounter competition from long-standing competitors, regional players and new
entrants to the market and we expect this to continue in the future. The Directors regularly monitor performance to
ensure that any new competitive threats are being addressed, and to ensure that positive relationships with key
accounts and customers are being maintained.
The Directors remain convinced that improving the quality of delivery, and the health and safety standards, is the
: best way to stay ahead of the competition and satisfy the demands of its customers
Financial Risk
The key areas of financial risks are primarily linked to the control of debtors and the management of WIP to levels
that are commensurate with the levels of activity in the business, along with control of the operational and financial
delivery of individual projects in line with expectations.
The Company ensures the credit worthiness of its customers to minimise the bad debt risk and there have been no
material concerns on this point during the year. The levels of WIP are regularly reviewed and analysed by the
Directors with focus directed at maintaining optimum levels for the business.
For large scale project work additional controls have been implemented to track performance at a more granular
level and on a more frequent basis to ensure corrective action can be taken to mitigate issues as they arise.

The directors recognise that the long-term success of the company is dependent on the sustainability of its business
model and its management of risk. The directors accept that decisions made by the board will have a direct impact
on the viability of the company over differing time periods. Accordingly, all strategic decisions are made in close
association with the members and with regular and controlled reporting procedures in place. Planning, preparation,
improved systems, and continuous improvement in controls across the board are features of the business
performance in the year to 31 January 2026.
Preparation for the monthly board meetings is thorough with considered analysis and reports from each division and
responsible director. The financial analysis is comprehensive and the focus on key strategic metrics such as margin
and cash is both detailed and consistent. KPls are measured and available across Health and Safety, Finance,
Commercial, Operations, and Support functions and board subject papers are considered at each meeting. All such
Papers and reports are supported by appendices, with granular detail providing ample opportunity to assess and
highlight areas of risk on an ongoing basis.
Annual business plans result from detailed and comprehensive input from all areas of the business. Using bottom
up preparation and a top-down review process, input is challenged and considered to ensure that strategic ambition
and day to day practicalities are blended to deliver well thought through and thoroughly tested business plans. The
company also produces 3-year strategic plans consistent with delivering “Success” through a long-term increase in
value.
The directors recognise and accept that, whilst the general duties specified in the Companies Act 2006 are owed by
the directors to the Company, they do not owe any such corresponding general duties to stakeholders such as
employees, suppliers, customers, the wider community or to the environment. Having said that, they fully accept that
they must pay due regard to these stakeholder’s interests when making strategic decisions and formulating the
annual business plan. Accordingly, in practice, the directors consider carefully the different elements highlighted in
the Act but see these interests as being secondary to their duty to “Promote the success of the company” through
improving shareholder value.
The following section details some practical examples of compliance with the Act and is provided as evidence as to
how the Board uses and applies the regulations and guidelines in action.
In line with their primary duty under section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 the directors developed a strategy that
was clear and fully aligned with the wishes of the members and furthermore consistent with the requirements of the
company over the next three years.
The business plan for the year to 31 January 2026 aimed to improve the quality of the revenues and margins;
maintain a name for excellence; reduce admin expenses as a percentage of revenues; increase the EBITDA; and
deliver high cash generation.
This strategy, originally designed back in 2018, was well thought through and paid particular attention to the likely
consequences of such a plan on the long-term health of the company. At that time, Management took advantage of
the evolving market dynamics and decided to change strategic direction in order to pivot from being Civils Heavy to
being Fibre Cabling Led; from being willing to accept lower margins to commanding higher margin work; and to
requiring an improvement in margins across any workstreams that the business kept.
However, market demand Tier 1 Telecoms infrastructure services slowed during the calendar year 2023 and 2025,
primarily due to a significant reduction in Altnet activity. Penetration is low across the industry and competition from
both BT and VM has been significant. This, together with the dramatic rise in the cost of money, meant that some
funders slowed the pace of investment.

There is presently a significant requirement to upgrade the electrical grid capacity for the UK but not enough suppliers

EARLY ADOPTION OF AMENDMENTS TO FRS 102
The directors have elected to early adopt the amendments to FRS 102 issued as part of the Financial Reporting
Council's Periodic Review 2024, which are mandatory for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January
2026.
The principal amendments adopted by the Company relate to Section 20 Leases and Section 23 Revenue from
Contracts with Customers.
Under the revised leasing requirements, the Company recognises right-of-use assets and corresponding lease
liabilities for leases that were previously accounted for as operating leases, subject to the exemptions available
under the standard. Under the revised revenue recognition requirements, revenue is recognised using a control-
based model based on the identification of performance obligations and the transfer of contro! of goods and
services to customers.
The adoption of the amended revenue recognition requirements has not resulted in a material change to the timing
or measurement of revenue recognised by the Company. The principal impact of early adoption relates to the
recognition of right-of-use assets and lease liabilities under the revised leasing requirements. .

2026. The principal amendments adopted by the Company relate to Section 20 Leases and Section 23 Revenue
from Contracts with Customers. In applying this approach, comparative information has not been restated.
On initial application, the Company recognised a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a corresponding lease liability in
respect of its lease arrangements.
Revenue
The revised Section 23 introduces a five-step model for revenue recognition, with revenue recognised as
performance obligations are satisfied through the transfer of control of goods or services to customers. The
Company has reviewed its existing revenue streams and contractual arrangements against the requirements of the
revised Section 23 and concluded that the application of the revised requirements does not result in a change to
the timing or amount of revenue recognised by the Company. Accordingly, there was no impact on retained
earnings at the date of initial application or on the Company's reported revenue or profit for the year.
Leases
Under the previous version of FRS 102 Section 20, leases were classified by the Company as either finance
leases or operating leases. Under the revised Section 20, the Company recognises a lease liability and
corresponding right-of-use asset for leases, subject to the applicable recognition exemptions.
Prior to transition, the Company had no leases classified as finance leases, with its vehicle and property leases
previously accounted for as operating leases. The Company has applied the practical expedient not to reassess
whether a contract is, or contains, a lease at the date of initial application.
On transition, right-of-use assets of £4,867,470 were recognised, comprising £3,432,898 in respect of vehicle
leases and £1,434,572 in respect of property leases. The corresponding lease liabilities were measured at the
present value of the remaining lease payments, discounted using the Company's applicable borrowing rate.
In determining the initial carrying value of the right-of-use assets, the Company considered prepaid and accrued
lease payments and restoration obligations, including the existing dilapidations provision. The impact of these
adjustments was considered immaterial and, accordingly, no adjustment was made to the right-of-use assets in
respect of these amounts.
There was no adjustment to opening retained earnings as a result of the transition to the revised Section 20.
