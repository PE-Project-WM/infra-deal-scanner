Strategic report
The Directors present their strategic report for Airwave Solutions Limited (Airwave) for the year ended 31 December
2025.
Principal activities and business review
The principal activity of the Company is to develop and provide mobile information and communications technology
to Public Safety Organisations.
Airwave was incorporated in 2000 having successfully won a Home Office contract to design, build and operate a
secure communications network for Police forces across England and Wales. The Airwave network has since been
expanded to include all three blue light services and many more non-blue light Public Safety Organisations across
Great Britain.
The Home Office commissioned the Airwave network as a Private Finance Initiative (“PFI”), whereby a private
contractor develops or builds an asset up-front using their own funding, before being paid by the government for
the use of that service over the long-term. Since incorporation in 2000, the Company has invested £1.9 billion in
building, expanding and developing the Airwave network as of 31 December 2025, which is based around the
established and open technology standard of Terrestrial Trunked Radio (‘TETRA’). .
The Company is focused on providing excellent service to all customers and expanding its services to existing and
new customers. Central to our proposition is ensuring we provide a stable, resilient and efficiently run network to
our customers.
Turnover was £292.3m for the year ended 31 December 2025 compared to £318.7m for the year ended 31
December 2024. Gross profit for the year was £137.0m (2024: £160.1m), representing a gross profit margin of
46.9% (2024: 50.2%). The reduction in both tumover and gross profit reflected the termination of revenue streams
related to ambulance bundle two control room services and certain software products.
Operating profit was £41.6m for the year ended 31 December 2025 compared to £19.2m for the year ended 31
December 2024. This increase in profitability reflects cost savings achieved within cost of sales and administrative
expenses and a lower £18.3m (2024: £52.8m) non-cash exceptional fixed asset impairment charge, which more
than offset the reduction in turnover noted above. The lower impairment charge reflects a partial impairment of
tangible fixed assets in 2025, whereas in 2024 there was a full impairment.
Profit after interest and taxation was £42.6m for the year ended 31 December 2025 compared to £38.2m for the
year ended 31 December 2024.
Net assets at 31 December 2025 were £52.4m compared to £10.2m at 31 December 2024, reflecting the operating
profit achieved in the year.
Operating review
The Company continues to meet the demanding service levels required by its public safety customers and has
delivered 99.89% network availability during the financial year. Airwave continues to offer its users a secure and
dedicated service which is uncompromised by the requirements of commercial users and, in addition, a resilient
service which ensures users can access the network in circumstances where commercial networks can struggle in
respect of both capacity and coverage.
Airwave also continues to develop mobile data solutions and products that aim to create significant efficiencies
allowing greater utilisation of our customers’ front line resources and allowing them to secure savings in their back
office activities.
The Directors have reviewed the operating performance and development of the business. This performance is in
line with expectations including the impact of adopting the CMA remedies order with effect from 1 August 2023.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of the Airwave business and the execution of its strategy are subject to a number of risks and
uncertainties. The key risks facing the business are set out below.
Operational risk management, policies and exposure
Network outage risk
There is a risk of an Airwave network outage due to events outside of the Company's control. A serious outage
would have a major impact on customers’ performance and, with the nature of the emergency service customer
base, repercussions could be critical. This could result in severe damage to the Company, its brand and reputation
and has therefore been mitigated by increasing network security and resilience and putting in place robust
operational procedures to enable the service to be maintained during such incidents.
Cybersecurity risk .
There is a risk of a network outage or information theft as a result of cybercrime which could impact on company
performance or reputation. This has been mitigated by having in place suitably skilled information security support
teams, real-time monitoring and detection of possible system compromises, firewalls, security controls and identity
: validation checks.
Contract risk
In March 2024, the Home Office issued a notice to Airwave to extend the Company's key blue light core contracts
to a national shutdown target date of 31 December 2029. The Company continues to work with its customers to
ensure mission critical communications are maintained up to this date.

Health risk
Concerns about the perceived health risks of TETRA radio technology could have a negative impact on business
and tarnish the Airwave brand. Airwave takes the issue of health and safety extremely seriously. The Company is
very aware of its responsibilities to ensure that staff, customers and the public are not put at risk, which is why
Airwave is committed to rigorous compliance with strict safety guidelines.
Financial risk management objectives, policies and exposure
The Company's operations expose it to a variety of financial risks that include the effects of changes in credit risk,
liquidity risk and cash flow interest rate risk. The Company's overall risk management programme seeks to
minimise potential adverse effects as noted below.
Price risk
The Company is also subject to the impact on its contracts by changes to Government spending patterns based
on the economic cycle and increasing pressure on pricing from customer organisations. The Company manages
this risk through the development of new products and services that support the Government's efficiency and
spending agenda, The Company's revenues are secured under long term contracts with pricing, including any
impact from the CMA intervention, linked to the rate of inflation, which offsets any potential impact from inflation
and rising input prices.

Credit risk
The Company's principal credit risks are attributable to cash and cash equivalents and trade receivables. Cash
and cash equivalent credit risk is monitored on an overall basis through the consideration of credit limits and the
long term credit ratings of the respective institutions where funds are held. The Company has limited exposure to
trade receivable credit risk as the majority of its customers are public safety organisations backed by government
funding.
Liquidity risk
The Company could be exposed to liquidity risk if its cash flows are not sufficient for it to meet its short-term debt .
obligations. The Company has no external debt and is able to redeem group funding loans on a monthly basis in
order to meet short term cash flow obligations.
Exchange rate risk
Management monitors exchange rate risk but as yet there is no current significant foreign currency exchange :
exposure. This is because more than 99% of assets and liabilities are denominated in Pounds Sterling (GBP).
Interest rate risk
The Company is not reliant on interest income and currently bears no significant external funding costs.
Capital risk
The Company has no external borrowings. The Company’s objective when managing capital is to safeguard the
Company’s ability to continue as a going concern, providing mission critical services to the UK’s public safety
organisations whilst providing returns to shareholders and benefits to other stakeholders and to maintain an optimal
capital structure to reduce the cost of capital.
Competition and Markets Authority.
in 2021, the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) announced that it had opened a market investigation into
the supply of public safety mobile radio services in the UK.
In 2023, the CMA imposed a legal order on the Company that implemented a prospective price control on Airwave.
After the Competition Appeal Tribunal (CAT) dismissed the Company's appeal of the CMA's final decision, the
Company appealed the CAT’s judgment to the UK Court of Appeal.
On 30 January 2025, the Court of Appeal denied the Company's application for permission to appeal the CAT's
judgment.
Since 1 August 2023, revenue under the Airwave contract has been, and will continue to be, recognised in
. accordance with the prospective price control.

The Directors have considered the impact of the price control on the carrying value of the Company's fixed assets
and going concern assessment as at 31 December 2025. Consequently, the Company has booked a further £18,3
million (2024: £52.8 million) asset impairment charge in the year to 31 December 2025 (see note 10) to reduce the
carrying value of the. Airwave network asset to its recoverable amount. The Directors’ going concern assessment
is considered within the Directors’ Report.

In December 2024, the CAT received an application to begin proposed collective proceedings against Motorola
Solutions entities including the Company, as well as Motorola Solutions UK Limited, a related group undertaking,
and Motorola Solutions, Inc, the ultimate parent company. The proposed class representative is funded by the UK

Competition Act 1998. In September 2025, the CAT certified the proposed collective proceedings on an “opt out"
basis. In December 2025, Airwave Solutions Limited filed its defence, and the Class Representative filed her reply
in January 2026. in March 2026, the CAT listed the trial for 6-8 weeks commencing in October 2027. The CAT
ordered a split trial into two phases with a potential second hearing to be held in 2028 to address the question of
“pass on" defence if the Class Representative is successful in the first trial (October / November 2027). Airwave
Solutions Limited Directors are complying with alt legal requests. .
The Directors are aware that there is a risk of a material economic outflow, to the group, shoutd the proceedings
continue and result in the claim being upheld. The claim is at an early stage with expert evidence and discovery yet .
to be completed, there are unknowns around the number of claimants, the size of their claims and likelihood of any
potential outflow. Therefore, at this stage the Directors cannot reasonably estimate any potential liability and have
made no provision in the accounts. Motorola Solutions Inc. has confirmed that it will provide financial support to
Airwave Solutions Limited and Motorola Solutions UK Limited should the claim be upheld.

Going concern
In assessing the Company's ability to continue to operate as a going concern, the Directors have considered the
cash position of the Company, the forecast profits or losses from existing operations including the impact of
prospective price control within the CMA remedies order and, after making enquiries, have reasonable expectation
that the Company has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future.
Accordingly, they continue to adapt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing these financial statements.
In making this assessment, the Directors have sought and obtained a letter of support from the ultimate parent

. company, Motorola Solutions Inc. The ultimate parent company has confirmed support, should it be needed, for at
least fifteen months from the date of approval of these financial statements, to ensure the Company can meet its
liabilities as they fall due. The Directors have considered the ultimate parent company’s financial performance, its
cash, current assets and available borrowing facilities in making their assessment of the ultimate parent company's
support.
As disclosed above in the Strategic Report, in December 2024, the CAT received an’ application to
begin proposed collective proceedings against Motorola Solutions entities includingthe Company, as well as
Motorola Solutions UK Limited, a related group undertaking, and Motorola Solutions, Inc, the ultimate parent
company. As the CAT listed the trial for 6-8 weeks commencing in October 2027, with a potential second hearing
to be held in 2028, the Directors believe that the proceedings will not be settled within the going concern period.
The Directors consider that the financial resources available to the Company, together with the ultimate parent
company support, will be sufficient for the Company to meet its operational needs for the foreseeable future or at
least twelve months from the date of approval of these financial statements. Accordingly, the going concern basis
has been adopted in preparing these financial statements.

Action taken during the year
We are committed to responsible energy management and will practice energy efficiency throughout our
organisation, wherever it is cast effective. We recognise that climate change is one of the most serious
environmental challenges currently threatening the global community and the role that organisations such as
Airwave has to play in reducing greenhouse gas emissions.
Airwave has seen an overall reduction in total location based CO2e emissions between 2024 and 2025. This is
largely driven by a reduction in the Scope 2 grid electricity conversion factor for 2025 versus 2024, although there
is a reduction in overall consumption. There has been an overall increase in the market based total tCO2e for 2025
versus 2024 as a result of not having green energy supplied for much of the portfolio in 2025. The temporary
purchase of non-renewable energy was a strategic cost-management decision following the implementation of the
CMA remedies order in 2023. To address this, the Company has approved the transition of the portfolio back to
renewable energy sources in the 2026 procurement cycle. There was an increase in Scope 1 and 2 travel emissions
in 2025 versus 2024, this is driven by a combination of increased mileage as well as better quality data being made
available for reporting in 2025 compared to previous years. To address this, the Company continues to work closely
with our fleet management company to provide low carbon options for company cars and operations vehicles, and
we have already seen an increase in the uptake of electric vehicles within the fleet and also via EV car schemes
for personal vehicles in 2025.
In addition, Airwave have established quarterly energy/carbon steering group meetings throughout 2025 to support
the delivery of its Carbon Management Action Plan. Airwave has also continued with its programme of identifying
and removing old and redundant equipment from its switching centres, which has resulted in a significant reduction
in power consumption at those locations and subsequently less demand for cooling.
The Company has continued to support homeworking, both before, during and after the Covid pandemic, to reduce
travel costs and reliance on real estate through the use of IT and communications tools, and encourages the
reduction of face-to-face meetings with clients and suppliers. Airwave has assessed and implemented a
programme to address all of the recommendations identified in the recent ESOS report. All the higher consuming
areas identified have been completed or are progressing well.
The Company has published its carbon reduction plan alongside an intention to track towards achieving net zero
emissions by 2050, recognising that the Company's key blue light core contracts currently have a national shutdown
target date of 31 December 2029. The plan can be viewed on the ESG pages at motorolasolutions.com.

Company will continue to derive value from these assets beyond current contractual timescales, currently
assumed to be until December 2031. Additionally, the length of time taken by the decommissioning project
itself is estimated, alongside a view on any potential partial network operation in this time and any site or
rental costs that will be incurred across the decommissioning period. It is also reflective of the known
number of sites involved and industry resource available. Management currently estimates that
decommissioning cash outflows will predominantly crystallise in the 12 months following the final network
shutdown date.
