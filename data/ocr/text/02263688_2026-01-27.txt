Andrew Wireless Systems UK Limited
Directors' Report for the Year Ended 31 December 2024

The directors present their report for the year ended 31 December 2024.

Principal activity

The company has continued to distribute electrical components in the year. In addition, during the year the

company provided project management and engineering services related to the installation of equipment

required for wireless telecommunications infrastructure.

Directors of the company

The directors who held office during the year were as follows:

A More (ceased 31 January 2025)

J Murphy - Company secretary and director (ceased 31 January 2025)

The following directors were appointed after the year end:

L.E D'Amico (appointed 31 January 2025)

C.A Lampo (appointed 31 January 2025) .

Results and dividends

_ The loss for the year after taxation amounted to £27,520,123 (2023 profit £1,368,786). The directors do not

recommend a final dividend.

Review of the business and future developments

Revenue for the 12 month period to 31 December 2024 has increased 21% to £3,857k compared to year ending

31 December 2023 (£3,194k). This revenue came as a result of several large projects in the transport sector. We

would expect business in 2025 to be slightly lower than 2024 as many companies slow down their capital

spending. This is a general trend in the telecom market as a whole and not specific to the Company. In the main

the UK market remains dominated by a few neutral host companies who deliver large scale projects into the

Enterprise, Transport, Retail; Hospitality and Stadia markets. This trend is expected to drive increased

competition for future projects, which may impact future margins. The gross margin remains consistent but can

vary somewhat due to product mix.

Further to the plans announced in 2021 by the Company’s owner to spin off Home Networks business segment

as an independent group, on 9 January 2024 the group closed the transaction to sell Home Networks business

segment to Vantiva SA. In preparation for the Home Networks business sale to Vantiva, on 5 December 2023

the company purchased Home Networks licenses from affiliated companies at fair value of £30.1m against a

loan note payable held in US Dollars, which increased the foreign currency exchange rate risk for the company.

During 2024 the Company's plans changed and the assumptions used for the valuation of the intangibles. The
. company received £2.2m ($2.835m) in October 2024 in licence income. The intangibles were then sold for

£230k ($284k) in January 2025. The parent company has made a capital contribution of £24.6m in January

2025.

Financial instruments

Technological risk, credit risk, liquidity risk and cash flow risk

The company is competing in a technologically intensive industry. It is important that we remain competitive in

this field and so invest appropriate resources in this area.

The company's credit risks are covered by a process of credit checks and an experienced accounts receivable

department.

Liquidity and cash flow risk are managed by the group treasury function.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The market is becoming very cost centric with Networks under scrutiny from shareholders for their spend and is

dominated by a few host companies. The company has the support of experienced staff by CommScope Group

who monitor the markets staying up-to-date with technological trends and who are able to offer solutions to

these key customers.

Going concern

The company distributes electrical components and providing project management and engineering services

related to the installation of equipment required for wireless telecommunications infrastructure.

Based on its forecasts to 31 January 2027, the Company expects to remain profitable on an annual basis. The
. Company is a part of centralised treasury structure which includes Andrew Wireless UK Limited, Andrew

Design & Integration UK Limited and the UK Branch of Andrew Technologies UK (incorporated in

Switzerland ), together called as the ‘UK Group’. Therefore, the Company is dependent on the activities of the

UK group, given the centralised treasury structure whereby cash and cashflow is managed on a UK group-wide

basis.

Consequently, the Company has obtained a cross guarantee letter of support between the UK group that

confirms that they will provide financial support to each other to assist in meeting its liabilities to the extent that

funding is not otherwise available to the Company to meet such liabilities for the period from the date of

approval of the financial statements until 31 January 2027 , ‘the going concern period’.

The Directors have assessed the level of financial support available, taking into account the UK groups available

liquidity, consisting of $4.6m cash in hand at the end of November 2025 and its financial plans. The

intercompany loans in the company are contained within the UK group therefore is not expected to affect the

going concern position of the company due to the existence of cross guarantee letter of support. The Directors
. have reviewed the UK Group’s budget and medium-term financial plan. The UK Group has modelled a range of

scenarios with the base forecast being on in which, over the 12 months ending 31 January 2027, the sales grow

in line with the budget. In addition, the Directors have considered reasonably possible downside scenarios,

including adjustments to the base forecast, a period of significantly lower sales, profitability and cash flows.

From this assessment, the Directors are satisfied that even in the reasonably possible downside scenarios, the

UK Group have sufficient liquidity throughout the going concern period, unti] 31 January 2027.

Intangible assets and impairment of intangible assets

Intangible assets that are acquired by the company, which have finite useful lives, are measured at cost less

accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. The useful lives of identifiable intangible assets

are determined after considering the specific facts and circumstances related to each intangible asset. Factors

considered when determining useful lives include the contractual term of any agreement related to the asset, the

historical performance of the asset, the company’s long-term strategy for using the asset, any laws or other local
- regulations which could impact the useful life of the asset, and other economic factors, including competition

and specific market conditions. Intangible assets that are deemed to have definite lives are amortised, primarily

on a straight-line basis, over their useful lives, generally ranging from 1 to 10 years. Where there are indicators

of impairment of individual intangible assets, the company performs impairment tests based on a value in use

calculation. The value in use calculation is based on a discounted cash flow model. The cash flows are derived

from the budget for the next four years and based on contracted terms related to royalties. The recoverable

amount is most sensitive to the discount rate used for the discounted cash flow model as well as the expected

future cash flows and the growth rate used for extrapolation purposes.

Trade debtors

Trade debtors are amounts due from customers for merchandise sold or services performed in the ordinary

course of business.

Trade debtors are recognised initially at the transaction price. They are subsequently measured at amortised cost

using the effective interest method, less provision for impairment. A provision for the impairment of trade

debtors is established when there is objective evidence that the company will not be able to collect all amounts

due according to the original terms of the receivables.

Stocks

Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell. Cost is

determined using the first-in, first-out (FIFO) method. :

The cost of finished goods and work in progress comprises direct materials and, where applicable, direct labour

costs and those overheads that have been incurred in bringing the inventories to their present location and

condition. At each reporting date, stocks are assessed for impairment. If stocks are impaired, the carrying

amount is reduced to its selling price less costs to complete and sell; the impairment loss is recognised

immediately in profit or loss.

Trade creditors

Trade creditors are obligations to pay for goods or services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of

business from suppliers. Accounts payable are classified as current liabilities if the company does not have an

unconditional right, at the end of the reporting’ period, to defer settlement of the creditor for at least twelve

months after the reporting date. If there is an unconditional right to defer settlement for at least twelve months

after the reporting date, they are presented as non-current liabilities.
