Strategic Report for the Year Ended 31 March 2025
The directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 March 2025.
This Strategic Report has been prepared in accordance with the requirements of Section 414 of the Companies
Act 2006. Its purpose is to inform shareholders and help them assess how the directors have performed their duty
to promote the success of Neos Networks Limited and its subsidiaries.
The Strategic and Business Performance Overview sets out the main trends and factors underlying the
development and performance of Neos Networks Limited (the "Company") and its subsidiaries (together the
"Group") during the year ended 31 March 2025, as well as those matters which are likely to affect its future
development and performance. "Neos Networks" is the trading name for the Group.
Business review
Fair review of the business
The Group loss for the year after tax amounted to £70.7m (2024: £83.0m).
The Group has improved sales, focusing on recurring revenue across its core telecoms installation and
maintenance contracts business.
The Group made an operating loss in the year; however significant savings were made within administrative
expenses in relation to items such as network efficiencies and operating losses were improved. The group is on a
5- year plan for profitability improvement.
During the year, four new shareholder loan notes were issued totalling £34.0m. In the prior year, four shareholder
loan notes were issued totalling £40.0m, as well as two ordinary share issues totalling £20.0m.
The consolidated statement of financial position has net assets of £41.7m as at 31 March 2025 (2024: £112.4m).
The business continued to focus on delivering well for our customers and continued to provide market leading
customer service as measured by our monthly transactional Net Promoter Score (NPS) which has been maintained
at an average level higher than our 60% target.
Neos Networks is proud to maintain its position for ethical leadership in the industry and was pleased to progress
our Net Zero 2030 plan. We can confirm that we had no employee safety incidents and have maintained our ISO
27001 accreditation with no nonconformances.

Section 172 Statement
In accordance with section 172 of the Companies Act 2006, each of the directors acts in the way that he or she
considers, in good faith, would most likely promote the success of the Company for the benefits of its members
as a whole. Our directors take the necessary steps to understand the needs and priorities of each stakeholder group
and do this through different methods, including by direct engagement and via Board and committee meetings,
covering:
a) the likely consequences of any decision in the long term
We continuously invest in our people, assets, processes, and systems to ensure the continuity and longevity of the
business and all decisions taken by the Board have this overarching objective in mind.
b) the interests of the Company's employees
Our talented and committed workforce are critical to our success and without their expertise we could not deliver
on our ambitions. We are continually improving how we communicate and collaborate with our people through a
mix of communications channels including our intranet, targeted emails, face to face and virtual meetings, and
our two-way communications tool, Viva Engage. Feedback is encouraged and welcomed through regular
engagement surveys, employee forums, and business updates. The Company is actively working to ensure we
build and maintain a diverse and inclusive workforce, where everyone feels a sense of belonging, can thrive in
their career, feel comfortable to be themselves, and add value to the business. We have three key values which
underpin and guide us in how we lead, manage, and operate every day. 1) We find solutions 2) We do what’s right
3) We care.
c) the need to foster the Company's business relationships with suppliers, customers, and other key stakeholders
We use tier one technology partners and work with a high-quality supply chain to create a highly resilient network
which attracts and retains customers and builds long term value. We target technology partners with a strong
innovative, product roadmap and embed national security issues in our selection process. We choose construction
and delivery partners using a range of criteria, including safety, quality, productivity, and sustainability. We also
require our supply chain to adhere to our Supplier Charter.
We are a business focused on serving our customers via a national, high capacity, highly resilient network for
both direct and indirect customers; and have specialist teams engaging with and supporting the needs of multiple
sectors, including businesses, mobile operators, wholesalers, resellers, system integrators and the public sector.
As well as suppliers and customers, we seek to build strong relationships with other key stakeholders, including
trade associations and Ofcom, our industry regulator.
d) the impact of the Company's operations on the community and the environment
We actively encourage our people to engage with local charities and communities around our major places of
work. On construction projects, we select supply chain partners who employ and operate locally.
We recognise our environmental responsibilities and are committed to reducing our environmental impact and
continually improving our environmental performance as a fundamental part of our strategy and operating
methods. Ultimately, our infrastructure helps the UK achieve its climate targets. Aligned to this is our commitment
to Net Zero by 2030.
e) the need of the Company to maintain a reputation for high standards of business conduct
We seek to protect the reputation of the business and to maintain high standards of business conduct in everything
we do. Our audit compliance and risk committee which meets several times a year, considers a range of reporting,
management, control, and governance issues, providing assurance to the Board on the way in which the business
conducts itself. We have policies and associated training to maintain these high standards, on topics including
conflicts of interest, confidentiality, anti-bribery, modern slavery, whistleblowing, and our expectations of
conduct in the workplace.
f) the need to act fairly as between members of the Company
Both shareholders are active owners of other UK infrastructure businesses and nominate members to the Board
who are actively involved in the major decision making of the business.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors acknowledge that they have a responsibility for the Company's systems of internal control and risk
management and for monitoring their effectiveness. The purposes of these systems are to manage, rather than
eliminate, the risk of failure to achieve business objectives, to provide reasonable assurance as to the quality of
management information and to maintain proper control over the income, expenditure, assets and liabilities of the
Company.
No system of control can, however, provide absolute assurance against material misstatement or loss.
Accordingly, the directors have regard to what controls, in their judgement, are appropriate to the Company’s
business and to the relative costs and benefits of implementing specific controls.
The main financial risks that the Company could face have been considered by the directors. These include:
+ failure to grow the business;
+ delay in the Transformation and Separation Programme;
+ failure to maintain sufficient and satisfactory levels of network performance;
+ risk of cyber attack and data loss.
To mitigate these risks there has been continued focus this year to ensure that optimum levels of capacity are
delivered and maintained to avoid network congestion to customers. The Company also reviews and improves the
level and quality of customer service to mitigate the risk of customer experience falling short of what is expected.
There has been continued focus on improving the Company’s processes and controls around supplier selection to
reduce the likelihood and potential impact of business interruption due to supplier failure. Reviews are carried out
to mitigate the risk of confidential data loss and continuous improvements implemented to systems to prevent and
detect incidents.

Directors of the Group
The directors who served during the year are as listed on page 1. In accordance with the Articles of Association
of the Company the directors are not required to retire by rotation.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the Company and Group is the provision of telecommunication services to wholesale and
retail customers throughout the UK.
Neos Networks Limited is 50% owned by SSE Plc and 50% owned by Bowmore UK 3 Limited. Bowmore UK 3
Limited is part of Infracapital Partners.
Neos Networks provides high-capacity resilient connectivity and data centre services to large businesses,
government organisations and wholesale partners. Its reputation for project delivery and high availability is based
on the effective management of a UK wide fibre optic network. The network offers access to 15 Neos Network
owned regional co-location facilities, 525 unbundled BT exchanges and 61 Commercial Data Centres. Neos
Networks manages SSE’s internal telephony and data networks infrastructure, digital microwave network and
Private Mobile Radio (PMR) network.

IFRS 16
IFRS 16 ‘Leases’ addresses the definition, recognition and measurement of leases. Operating leases are accounted
for as right-of-use assets and a lease liability discounted at the Company’s incremental borrowing rate. The asset
is depreciated over its useful economic life, while lease payments are apportioned between a repayment of the
lease liability and a finance charge.
The Company’s incremental borrowing rate (IBR) is the rate at which a similar borrowing could be obtained from
an independent creditor under comparable terms and conditions. The weighted average IBR applied to the
Company’s lease liabilities recognised at the balance sheet date 31 March 2025 was 6.43% (2024: 5.84%). :
The Company assesses at contract inception whether a contract is, or contains a lease. That is, ifa contract conveys
the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration. Leases are
recognised as right-of-use assets and corresponding lease liabilities at the date at which assets are available for
use by the Company.
Lease liabilities are measured at an amount equal to the present value of the expected future payments for the
underlying right-of-use assets during the lease term. The lease payments are discounted using the incremental
borrowing rate at the lease commencement date if the implicit rate in the lease is not readily determinable.
Each lease payment is allocated between a repayment of the liability and a finance cost. Lease liabilities are
subsequently measured using the effective interest method. The carrying amount of the liability is remeasured to
reflect reassessments and lease modifications.
In line with IFRS 16, Lease liabilities have been reassessed where the cost or term of the lease has changed in the
period. The corresponding adjustment is reflected in the right-of-use asset, or profit and loss and disclosed as a
separate line item - “Modifications to lease terms”.
Right of use assets are measured initially at cost comprising:
+ the amount of the initial measurement of the lease liability
* any lease payments less lease incentives at or before the lease commencement date
* any initial direct costs; and
+ restoration costs
Subsequently, the right-of-use assets are measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and any accumulated
impairment losses, and adjusted for remeasurements of the lease liability due to reassessments or modifications.
Where onerous leases are identified, the assets are impaired as appropriate. The right-of-use assets are depreciated
over the lease term on a straight-line basis.
Payments associated with all short-term leases and leases of low value are recognised on a straight-line basis as
an operating expense in the income statement. Short-term leases are leases with a lease term of 12 months or less.
Low value assets comprise assets < £4,000 based on the fair value of the asset as new.
The Company leases various land & buildings, telecommunications network infrastructure, computer equipment
and vehicles. The lease term varies dependant on the nature of the underlying asset, begin at the commencement
date, and are typically made for fixed periods of 12 months to 25 years, but may have extension options.

Tax
Taxation for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Taxation is recognised in the income statement unless
it relates to items recognised directly in equity, in which case it is recognised in other comprehensive income.
Current tax is the expected tax payable on the taxable income for the year, using tax rates enacted or substantively
enacted at the balance sheet date, and any adjustment to tax payable in respect of previous years.
Deferred tax is calculated using the balance sheet liability method, providing for temporary differences between
the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities for financial reporting purposes and the amounts used for taxation
purposes. The amount of deferred tax provided is based on the expected manner of realisation or settlement of the
carrying amount of assets and liabilities, using tax rates enacted or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date.
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset where there is a legally enforceable right of offset within the same tax
authority and where the Group intends to either settle them on a net basis, or to realise the asset and settle the
liability simultaneously. A deferred tax asset is recognised only to the extent that it is probable that future taxable
profits will be available against which the asset can be utilised. Deferred tax assets are reduced to the extent that
it is no longer probable that the related tax benefit will be realised.
