STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2025
introduction
The Directors have prepared the following Strategic Report for the year ended 31 March 2025.
Pelion IOT Limited ("Company") is a wholly owned subsidiary of Pelion Holdings Limited. Pelion loT is a leading
Internet of Things (“loT”) Connectivity Service Provider in the United Kingdom. Pelion loT provides cellular
connectivity, an loT Connectivity Management Platform, network infrastructure and security services which
delivers a robust, scalable, and reliable global connectivity offering for enterprises.
Pelion loT provides connectivity services to over 500 enterprise and original equipment manufacturer (“OEM”)
customers that have deployed connected devices in over 190 countries to date. Pelion loT’s unique offering of
connectivity services combines a broad suite of wholesale loT connectivity agreements from Mobile Network
Operators, seamlessly integrated into our platform and suite of network and security services to provide
customers with an effortless solution to deploy and manage fleets of connected devices anywhere, anytime
across the globe. .
The Company has focused on the Connectivity Management business since the acquisition in October 2022 and
has taken the necessary steps to right-size the operating costs while driving continued revenue growth. Post year .
end, the Company has continued to improve its profitability and is projected to maintain this performance for the
foreseeable future.
Business review
Evaluating the underlying financial performance of the business, the Company's turnover saw a year-over-year
increase of 20.6% to £14.8 million (2024 — £12.2 million). Total cost of sales for the year ended 31 March 2025
were £6.5 million (2024 - £6.7 million), yielding a gross margin of 55.9% (2024 — 45.5%) on associated turnover.
The ‘improvement in gross margin reflects sustainable efficiencies delivered through a structurally lower cost
base, rather than any one-off benefits.
Total operating costs for the year ended 31 March 2025 were £10 million (2024 — £10.5 million). Average
employee numbers for the year ended 31 March 2025 remained in line with the prior year at 65 (2024 — 64).
The Company has made a loss for the year ended 31 March 2025 of £0.8 million (2024 — £4.5 million),
representing an improvement of £3.7 million year-on-year, driven by revenue growth, strong gross margin
performance and the impact of the business rightsizing and efficiency initiatives.
The Company had total cash deposits as at 31 March 2025 of £0.8 million (2024 — £0.2 million) and total net
assets of £7.7 million (2024 — £8.4 million). The Parent Company, Pelion Holdings Limited, held cash reserves at
31 March 2025 of £1.6 million (2024 — £3.5 million), with the sole purpose of providing working capital for the
Company.
Market and business strategy
The global loT connectivity market is large and growing attractively across multiple major market segments and
regions. This market has consistently exhibited double-digit annual growth with many analysts predicting that this
growth will continue into the foreseeable future.
This growth is driven by the continued digital transformation, adoption of Al, adoption of edge computing, growth
in network technology access, including 5G, Low Power Wide Area Networks (LPWAN), and SIM technology,
coupled with the emergence of new and more sophisticated use cases.

Market and business strategy (continued)
In a fast-paced market, Pelion loT continues to delight customers with continued investment in our Connectivity
Management Platform (Pelion Portal) as well as the launch of new connectivity offerings, notably Consumer
eSIM for loT and Pelion Plans. The former offers businesses more ways to deliver Pelion connectivity to a wider
range of eSIM-only devices utilising SGP.22 connectivity standards, demonstrating the strength and variety of
our eSIM offering as businesses seek managed connectivity for less traditional loT devices.
The launch of Pelion Plans demonstrates our ability to offer more commercial flexibility to businesses whilst
offering enriched value-added services across technology, security and support. Pelion loT will continue to invest
in providing an industry-leading loT Connectivity Platform and Infrastructure to securely connect and manage
connected devices at scale for enterprise and OEM customers in the United Kingdom, European and North
American markets that want to deploy locally or globally.
Fundamental to the scalable ‘success of Pelion loT is the ability for Pelion loT to continue its development of its
market-leading loT Connectivity Management platform (Pelion Portal) which seamlessly delivers global loT
connectivity, and resilient infrastructure services (Pelion APN) that enable the business to maintain a low cost of
acquisition and cost to serve its customers today and in the future.
Pelion loT continues to ensure that its products and services are built to provide a high degree of flexibility that
allows it to have a horizontal offering capable of serving the demanding technical and commercial needs of
different growth verticals, including energy, transportation and logistics, industrial, retail and healthcare.

Economic impact of global events
UK businesses are currently facing many uncertainties, such as environmental sustainability and geopolitical
events such as the Russian invasion of Ukraine and the conflict in the Middle East. These uncertainties have
contributed to an environment where there exists a range of issues and risks, including inflation, rising interest
rates, labour shortages, disrupted supply chains and new ways of working.
The Directors have carried out an assessment of the potential impact of these uncertainties on the business,
including the impact of mitigation measures, and have concluded that these are non-adjusting events with the
greatest impact on the business expected to be from the economic ripple effect on the global economy. The
, Directors have taken account of these potential impacts in their going concern assessment.

2.7 Research and development
All ongoing research expenditure is expensed in the period in which it is incurred. Where a product is
technically feasible, production and sale are intended, a market exists, expenditure can be measured
reliably, and sufficient resources are available to complete the project, development costs are
capitalised and amortised on a straight-line basis over the estimated useful life of the respective
product. The Company believes its current process for developing products is essentially completed
concurrently with the establishment of technological feasibility, which is evidenced by a working
model. Accordingly, development costs incurred after the establishment of technological feasibility
have not been significant and, therefore, no costs have been capitalised to date.
Where no internally-generated intangible asset can be recognised, development expenditure is
recognised as an expense in the period in which it is incurred. Any collaborative agreement whereby
a third-party agrees to partially fund the Company's research and development is recognised over the
period of the agreement as a credit within research and development expenses.
2.8 Government grants
The Company accounts for government grants under the accrual! model. Government grants are
recognised when the Company has reasonable assurance that it will comply with the conditions
attaching to the grant and that the grant will be received.
Grants relating to revenue are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income on a
systematic basis over the periods in which the Company recognises the related eligible costs for
which the grant is intended to compensate.
2.9 Finance costs
Finance costs are charged to the Statement of Comprehensive Income over the term of the debt

Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include trade and other receivables, cash and bank balances, are
initially measured at their transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried
at their amortised cost using the effective interest method, less any provision for impairment, unless
the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the
present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.
Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial. The Company's cash and cash
equivalents, trade and most other receivables due with the operating cycle fall into this category of
financial instruments.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting date.
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the
estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The
impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of
the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the
impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying
amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.
Financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in
the assets of the Company after the deduction of all its liabilities.
Basic financial liabilities, which include trade and other payables, bank loans and other loans are
initially measured at their transaction price after transaction costs. When this constitutes a financing
transaction, whereby the debt instrument is measured at the present value of the future receipts
discounted at a market rate of interest. Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is
immaterial.
Debt instruments are subsequently carried at their amortised cost using the effective interest rate
method.
Trade payables are obligations to pay for goods and services that have been acquired in the ordinary
course of business from suppliers. Trade payables are classified as current liabilities if the payment is
due within one year. If not, they represent non-current liabilities. Trade payables are initially
recognised at their transaction price and subsequently are measured at amortised cost using the
effective interest method. Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial.
