GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 SEPTEMBER 2025

Introduction
The group strategic report for the year ended 30 September 2025 is set out below.
Business review
The group financial performance improved over the year with a 6% increase in revenue to £4.7m (2024: £4.4m)
and although still loss making, this reduced by 90% year on year to just over £0.2m (2024: £2.5m) due to
efficiencies in cost reduction. The group continues to invest in research and development. Net assets reduced by
just 4% to £5.4m (2024: £5.7m). A good cash position and liquidity was maintained throughout the year and cash
closed at £1.6m (2024: £1.2m), a 30% increase on September 2024.
The group continues to be adequately capitalised with a small amount of debt incurred in previous years for
capital expenditure on manufacturing equipment.
The sales pipeline is active with large opportunities currently being processed for technology refresh and further
installations into our main existing customer base inside and outside of the UK. These orders were received in
December 2025 and processed in March and May 2026.
Effort has been made to improve the quality of our marketing by sending a clearer picture of our products by
means of a new website and more concise collateral.
Future developments
The group's partnership in Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA) alongside A. A. Turki Group (ATCO) is progressing
and we have now received government accreditation enabling product sales. We jointly exhibited our first sales
event in December 2025. We will be looking for new distribution channels in US and Europe as a matter of
urgency. The board is of the opinion that the outlook for the company is one of stability and growth and that
performance will continue to improve and that, driven by existing customer demand, its sales and profitability for
the year ended 30 September 2026 will exceed that of the year ended 30 September 2025.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Overview
The group uses financial instruments, other than derivatives, comprising cash and other liquid resources and
various other items such as trade debtors and creditors that arise directly from its operations. The main purpose
of these financial instruments is to raise finance for the group's operations. The directors review and agree
policies for managing each of the risks as summarised below:
Cost inflation risk
The group is exposed to the risk of increasing costs, including salaries, materials and overheads, which could
impact profitability. The directors monitor costs closely and review pricing and operational efficiencies to help
mitigate the effects of inflationary pressures.
Credit risk
Credit risk represents the risk of financial loss being caused if a counter party fails to discharge an obligation. All
financial assets of the group, except cash in hand, are subject to credit risk. In order to manage credit risk,
where appropriate, credit limits are set for customers based upon a combination of payment history and third
party credit references. Credit limits are reviewed on a regular basis.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk represents the risk that the group shall encounter difficulties in meeting obligations associated with
financial liabilities. The group seeks to manage risk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet
foreseeable needs and to invest cash assets safely and profitably. Primarily this is achieved through cash
generated from operations. Short term flexibility is achieved by overdraft facilities.
Political climate
Political risk represents risk faced by the group that political decisions, events, or conditions will significantly
affect the profitability of the group. Political policies, outside of the group’s control can impact the market which
directly impacts the trading performance of the business. The directors consider the group has sufficient
resources and prompt reporting mechanisms to identify such that they can act upon political changes as
required. ;
Disruption in supply chain
Supply chain risk represents the risk that the company cannot furnish sales orders in a timely manner, which
could affect revenue and cash flow. To mitigate this, the company ensures that low value or long lead time stock
is held on site, has multiple sources for procurement wherever possible and our in house manufacturing facility
enables the company to produce hardware as required.
: Fall in demand
As with most businesses, the group is at risk of a fall in demand for its products and services, especially due to
the fast moving, high tech market. In order to mitigate this, the company is constantly reviewing its products and
customer base to ensure the new technology the company is developing is required in today's markets.

Qualifying third party indemnity provisions
Throughout the period from 1 October 2019 to the date of this report, an indemnity has been in force under

their duties to the company or as a result of things done by them as directors on behalf of the company.
Going concern
In assessing whether the financial statements for the group and company should be prepared on the going
concern basis, the directors have considered the outlook of the group and company, including future operating
profits, cash flows and facilities available to the group and company.
Despite the low revenue in the current financial year, future cashflows and forecasts based on anticipated
revenue, along with the reduction in overheads, confirm that the group and company will remain cash positive.
Further information on this is given in note 2.4 to these financial statements. The directors are satisfied that the
company wilt have sufficient funds to repay its liabilities as they fall due and adequate resources to continue in
operational existence for the foreseeable future. Consequently, the financial statements of the group and
company are prepared on the going concern basis.
Research and development
The group invests in activities which qualify as Research and Development expenditure resulting in a tax credit in
the year recognised against the expenditure to which it relates. Total expenditure in the year amounted to £2.0m
(2024: £2.3m), with a research and development tax credit arising of £257k (2024: £362k).

Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference

estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The
impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present vaiue of
the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the
impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying
amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in the profit or loss.
