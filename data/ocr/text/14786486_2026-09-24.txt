Strategic report
The Directors present their strategic report of RETN Networks Ltd. and its subsidiaries (‘the Company’
and together ‘the Group’) for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Business review
The Group is a leading independent Eurasian provider of IP Transit and high-bandwidth communication
services with unique network assets operating an extensive network connecting more than 45 territories
throughout three continents. Total fiber network exceeds 42,400+ km with deep metro network in selected
cities. The Group network has presence in 420+ data centres and commercial buildings across the world
providing a broad product offering of data transmission services such as IP Transit, Capacity, Dedicated
Internet Access, VPN, Cloud Connectivity, Ethernet and DDoS mitigation. :

: The Group provides comprehensive data connectivity solutions to leading global telecoms companies, blue
chip enterprises and the world’s most renowned content companies and is driven by technological
excellence in long-haul network design and service provisioning.

Many of the Group’s fiber routes and points of presence, especially in Central Eastern Europe (“CEE”) and
Central Asia, are unique and they secure direct access to high growth, often under-served markets. RETN’s
AS9002 (Autonomous System) was ranked 11th globally (as of April 2026) by customer cone size
(asrank.caida.org) and along with the other networks utilising Group fiber infrastructure, forms a critical
part of the global internet backbone.
The Group serves international and local carriers, and corporate customers with connectivity and related
services in Europe, Caucasus, Central Asia and Southeast Asia. The Group’s unique network routes,
industry leading service delivery, strong technical expertise, and competitive pricing, supplied with more
_ flexible commercial arrangements than larger players, makes the Group an attractive partner for many
European, American and Asian carriers, the world’s largest Hyperscalers, content companies and
enterprises.
In 2026 the Group continued to focus upon improving customer services, delivery and network specialisms
in order to continue its strong customer retention successes, whilst driving growth in both established and
new markets, as well as preparing the way for the company’s future strategic developments. Around 36%
of customers have been with the Group for more than 5 years.
Geographic network expansion continued throughout 2025-26, as RETN doubled network capacity in
Southeast Asia, upgraded its presence in the USA, introduced new routes in Turkey and the Middle East,
and added a new long-haul DWDM route across the Balkans, the Baltics and Norther Europe to improve
the resilience of the Group's global backbone across Europe. RETN invested in IP network developments
in Italy, Spain and Greece, increasing access to local markets and enhancing local enterprises’ access to
global connectivity. New optical paths, such as Sofia — Bulgaria/Turkey Border and Bucharest — Chisinau,
help increase cross-regional redundancy and further the Group's commitment to providing diverse, low-
latency routes for the telecommunications sector and other critical industries. In the UK, the Group
expanded its backbone network with additional routes to London, increasing reliability and adding further
redundancy for connectivity between the UK, Europe and the US.
In a world shaped by rising geopolitical tensions, environmental challenges, and rapid technological
change, the resilience of global network infrastructure is more critical than ever. Our ‘Building the Network
of Tomorrow’ message to the industry underscored the urgent need for investment in robust infrastructure
and the development of diversified, resilient routes. RETN urged the industry to prioritise resilience and
customer-focused solutions to safeguard the future of global connectivity.

Environmental Considerations:
The Group has invested heavily in the latest generation of networking technologies to try and reduce power
consumption (kWh/Mb) across the network and is establishing KPIs to monitor and therefore measure
improvements over time. Older equipment is then either reused in lower data transfer sites where its power
impact will be minimal, to reduce waste, or recycled appropriately to ensure minimal environmental impact.
The Group has sought to maximise the use of international video meetings over in person meetings where
possible, to minimise the environmental impact from travel. Car use, (whether company, employee or
private hire) has always been dissuaded across the Group and in 2024 the company started replacing its
very limited fleet of company vehicles with electric/hybrid equivalents. The Group has further updated its
travel policy to prioritise train travel above air travel on shorter journeys within Europe.
The Group continues to strengthen its commitment to environmental responsibility through initiatives

: aimed at reducing the environmental impact of its logistics and supply chain activities. In 2024, the Group
joined DHL’s GoGreen programme, which supports lower-carbon logistics through initiatives such as the
use of sustainable aviation fuel (SAF), carbon-efficient transport routes and more sustainable last-mile
delivery solutions.
Building on these efforts, in 2026 the Group entered into a partnership with Hellmann Worldwide Logistics
to further enhance the sustainability and efficiency of its global logistics operations. The collaboration is
intended to optimise equipment transportation, improve supply chain efficiency and reduce the
environmental impact associated with the movement of network infrastructure and other operational assets.
Participation in these initiatives supports the Group's objective of reducing the carbon footprint of its
shipments, improving transparency through emissions reporting and embedding sustainability
considerations into its day-to-day operations. Together, these partnerships contribute to the Group's wider
environmental strategy and its long-term commitment to supporting the transition to a lower-carbon
economy. :
In 2026, the Group achieved certification to ISO 14001:2015, awarded by the British Assessment Bureau,
demonstrating that its environmental management system meets internationally recognised standards. The
certification reflects the Group's commitment to responsible environmental management, the continual
improvement of its environmental performance and the integration of sustainable practices into its
operations. . ,

RETN is 18027001:2022 certified and has well established formal procedures for Information Security

Foreign currency exchange risk
The Group operates internationally and is exposed to foreign currency exchange risk.
The Group reports in euro (EUR) but generates revenues and incurs costs also in other functional currencies,
like USD, UAH, PLN, KZT, GBP and others. Consequently, the Group is exposed to both translation risk,
arising from the consolidation of foreign operations into EUR, and transactional foreign exchange risk,
where depreciation of certain currencies could adversely affect revenues, profitability and cash flows.
These exposures relate mainly to revenues, operating expenses, receivables and payables denominated in
foreign currencies. The Group monitors foreign currency risk on an ongoing basis and seeks to mitigate
potential impacts where possible, including through natural hedging by matching income and costs.
Compliance with laws and regulation .
The Group operates in a complex legal and regulatory environment. As a result, there is a risk that failure

. to comply with applicable laws, regulations, and industry standards may lead to significant legal, financial,
and reputational damage. This includes risks related to regulatory changes, litigation, data protection,

. environmental regulations, sector-specific legislation and international sanctions.
The Group takes a proactive approach to managing compliance risk and implements continuous monitoring
of regulatory developments across all jurisdictions it operates. In addition, the Group fosters a culture of
compliance, ethical behaviour, and accountability at all levels. This commitment ensures that the Group
remains vigilant and responsive to potential risks and maintains the high standards of governance.
The Group has a rigorous proactive sanctions compliance policy in place, confirming compliance of
customers, suppliers and other partners both at an onboarding stage and at regular intervals after
onboarding. This process checks for compliance with the sanctions of, the United Kingdom, the European
Union, and the United States of America. Further the Group’s operating policies are developed with the
guidance of leading legal specialists in international sanctions. .
Going Concern
The Group operates across the UK, Europe and Asia and therefore is exposed to the impact of various laws
and regulations, including sanctions regulations. The Group is continuously monitoring its compliance with
laws and regulations, which include sanctions from the governments, where the Group operates to ensure
the Group remains in compliance. This is done in conjunction with their legal advisors who offer them
support and advice.
Management continues to monitor all their options available and has run a range of scenarios of the financial
results to quantify the adverse impact on its cash flows. This exercise included the preparation of cashflow
forecasts under different scenarios. These scenarios indicate that the Group will continue in operational
existence for the foreseeable future and for at least twelve months from the approval of these financial :
: statements.
Therefore, the Directors believe that the Group is well placed to manage its business risks successfully,
despite the current uncertain economic and political outlook and therefore continue to adopt the going
concern basis of accounting in preparing the annual financial statements.

Principal activities :
RETN Networks Ltd. (“RETN Networks” or (‘the Company’ and together ’the Group’) was established on
7 April 2023 in England, UK as a company owned by shareholders whose liability is limited and was set
up under the law of England and Wales. The registered office of RETN Networks Ltd. is 5 Greenwich View
Place, London, England, E14 9NN, United Kingdom.
RETN Networks Ltd. is an international network service provider offering a range of services across its
own fiber-optic network to large enterprises, media content providers, carriers and internet service
providers. The Group has over 42,400+ km of lit fiber on its pan-European network which connects 45+
: countries across Europe, Asia and North America, with a heavy presence in the highly attractive markets
of Eastern Europe, Central Asia and Caucasus. The Group’s backbone network (which:is connected directly
/ to all major carrier-neutral Data Centres) carries a large proportion of international traffic in the regions it
passes through. The Dense Wavelength Division Multiplexing (“DWDM”) based network is designed in a
multiple ring-architecture offering several layers of redundancy. The network’s unique geographical
footprint provides more than 97.8 Tbps of capacity over its four primary routes between the West and Asia.
For the most part, the network is either fully owned by the Group or secured through long-term leases
(typically 10 — 15 years each).
The Group also operates its terrestrial award-winning network route — TRANSKZ — connecting the Group’s
pan-European network with China and Hong Kong. The TRANSKZ route allows for the transport of high-
bandwidth traffic between Europe and Asia, linking three of the world’s biggest financial centres in
London, Frankfurt and Hong Kong.
The Group also provides further value-added services such as DDoS Mitigation and security products,
remote Internet Exchange, dedicated private connections to major cloud service providers and colocation
services, deploying and housing for equipment, servers and data storage at Group points of presence
(“PoPs”) and Group data centres.

Financial assets — Assessment whether contractual cash flows are solely payments of principal and

For the purposes of this assessment, ‘principal’ is defined as the fair value of the financial asset on initial
recognition. ‘Interest’ is defined as consideration for the time value of money and for the credit risk
associated with the principal amount outstanding during a particular period of time and for other basic
lending risks and costs (e.g. liquidity risk and administrative costs), as well as a profit margin.
In assessing whether the contractual cash flows are solely payments of principal and interest, the Group
‘ considers the contractual terms of the instrument. This includes assessing whether the financial asset

contains a contractual term that could change the timing or amount of contractual cash flows such that it
would not meet'this condition. In making this assessment, the Group considers:

- contingent events that would change the amount or timing of cash flows;

- terms that may adjust the contractual coupon rate, including variable-rate features;

- prepayment and extension features; and

- terms that limit the Group’s claim to cash flows from specified assets (e.g. non-recourse features).
A prepayment feature is consistent with the solely payments of principal and interest criterion if the
prepayment amount substantially represents unpaid amounts of principal and interest on the principal
amount outstanding, which may include reasonable additional compensation for early termination of the
contract. Additionally, for a financial asset acquired at a discount or premium to its contractual paramount,
a feature that permits or requires prepayment at an amount that substantially represents the contractual
paramount plus accrued (but unpaid) contractual interest (which may also include reasonable additional
compensation for early termination) is treated as consistent with this criterion if the fair value of the
prepayment feature is insignificant at initial recognition.

3.9 Impairment
Financial assets
The Group assesses at each reporting date whether there is any objective evidence that a financial asset or
a group of financial assets is impaired. A financial asset or group of financial assets is deemed to be
impaired when there is objective evidence of impairment as a result of one or more events.
In accordance with the requirements of IFRS 9, an asset impairment model for recognising impairment
allowances is compiled based on expected credit losses (“ECLs”), and not just credit losses incurred. The
Group estimates loss allowance in an amount equal to the lifetime ECL because receivables are mostly
short-term. :
Lifetime ECLs are ECLs that result from all possible default events over the expected life of a financial
instrument.
The maximum period considered when estimating ECLs is the maximum contractual period over which the
Group is exposed to credit risk.
The Group has elected to use the simplified expected credit loss model when applying IFRS 9.
Measurement of ECLs
ECLs are a probability-weighted estimate of credit losses. Credit losses are measured as the present value
of all cash shortfalls (i.e. the difference between the cash flows due to the entity in accordance with the
contract and the cash flows that the Group expects to receive).
Non-financial assets
The carrying amounts of the Group’s non-financial assets, other than inventories and deferred tax assets
are reviewed at each reporting date to determine whether there is any indication of impairment. If any such ;
indication exists, then the asset’s recoverable amount is estimated.
The recoverable amount of an asset or cash-generating unit (CGU) is the greater of its value in use and its
fair value less costs to sell. In assessing value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their
; present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of
money and the risks specific to the asset. For the purpose of impairment testing, assets that cannot be tested
individually are grouped together into the smallest group of assets that generates cash inflows from
: continuing use that are largely independent of the cash inflows of other assets or groups of assets (the “cash-
generating unit”). The goodwill acquired in a business combination, for the purpose of impairment testing,
is allocated to cash-generating units, or (“CGU”).
An impairment loss is recognised if the carrying amount of an asset or its CGU exceeds its estimated
recoverable amount. Impairment losses are recognised in profit or loss. Impairment losses recognised in
respect of CGUs are allocated first to reduce the carrying amount of any goodwill allocated to the units,
and then to reduce the carrying amounts of the other assets in the unit (group of units) on a pro rata basis.
