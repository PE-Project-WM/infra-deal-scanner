GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2025
Introduction
The directors present their strategic report together with the audited financial statements for the year ended 31
March 2025.
Principal Activity
Our company's purpose is to help create a sustainable future by enabling households to manage energy
consumption and cost as well as carbon-footprint. This is achieved with claud connected systems for monitoring
and controlling energy generation, consumption and storage in residential buildings. The company designs and
manufactures products which provide visibility for consumers and offer Home Energy Management (HEMS) data
collection and device management. Usually installed alongside smart meters, the collection and analysis of
energy data is core to all of these systems which are designed to optimise usage efficiency and significantly
contribute to the flexible, low carbon electricity grids required to meet net zero targets.
Business Review
Market Environment
Energy prices have softened over the year and the UK price cap has remained relatively stable with 65% of
consumers on a standard variable tariff. This may have moderated consumer activity around energy saving
though more recently there has been some upward pressure on the cap.
However, the major dynamic is the government push to achieve near 100% smart meter deployment in the UK
by 2030, a date that is also the government target for net zero emissions from grid supplied electricity. With
current smart meter deployment sitting at around 67% of homes and approximately 9% of those operating in
non-smart mode, there is a considerable pressure on Energy Retailers to get to full smart meter coverage and to
fix the meters that aren’t working reliably. In respect of the latter challenge, a major new initiative has been
launched to permit smart meter billing data to be collected via the consumers’ internet connection over
broadband. Data in this configuration will be passed via the In-Home Display or another device capable of
connecting to the meter and will be able to operate even if there is no mobile or network coverage to the home.
geo has adapted its products to enable this functionality to be incorporated ahead of deployment in 2026.
In addition to the initial deployment of smart meters as part of the roll out and the fixing activity for non-
communicating meters, there is now also the replacement of the original SMETS1 meters which are installed in
11m UK homes and which are reaching the end of their service contract life. Other major market activities
include the upgrading of smart meter comms hubs to 4G standards. This will involve site visits to almost all
homes with 3G comms hubs as the 2G/3G mobile networks are decommissioned between now and 2033. This
represents an opportunity for upgrading other elements of in-home equipment to enable management of
electrical loads for Demand Side Response (DSR) services.
: These trends suggest that there will be solid demand for geo’s products from Retailers looking forward.
Smart Home Systems
The SeeZero system developed by geo is moving into the large-scale deployment phase and is the cornerstone
of many services which will continue the growth of recurring revenues for the company. In the near term it is
particularly valuable for helping Retailers deal with the communication issues to smart meters. The major longer
term value will be the ability to switch electrical devices in the home in response to time of use tariffs and DSR
commands, both to address the balancing needs of the grid and the trading-positions of energy Retailers.

It is notable that every country in the world is facing a similar challenge with the uptake of renewable generation
and electrification of transport and in some countries also with heating. geo has focussed its efforts on the UK
market to date yet is receiving continuous interest from energy companies and partners overseas for potential
deployments of SeeZero as it is seen as one of the most advanced and most cost-effective systems of its type
on the market. .
Future Developments

With the core SeeZero system now developed geo is focusing on the development of additional services that run
on the platform which will grow the recurring revenue line. These are being prioritised with the help of customers
such that they support the introduction of new tariffs and are interoperable with DSR platforms in a way that
facilitates both event-driven demand reduction or automated tariff activated optimisation, helping consumers
save money charging vehicles and running heat pumps in particular. A key activity has been heavy involvement
in standards development, notably the ‘matter’ standard for home automation and the OpenADR standard for .
interfacing with demand response services.

The range of services that will add value to customers is extensive and the pipeline that will generate growth in
recurring revenues for several years to come.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties ’ ;
Energy Suppliers Financial Stability

The risk of energy Supplier failure is monitored continuously. geo's major customers are mostly well financed
and capital adequacy requirements of Ofgem mean their financial positions are under constant review. Some
opportunities for business lie with the newer start up energy retailers and where the risk of default is higher. The
level of exposure is kept low as a consequence.

Supply Chain Disruption

Supply chains have been robust over the accounting period. Long term planning and forward commitment to
component suppliers have ensured reliable deliveries to customers. Price pressure remains upward on some
silicon supplies but most components are relatively stable.

Geopolitics

Geopolitical tensions remain a concern, particularly the potential for trade wars to disrupt supply chains. To date,
tariffs imposed by the US and other countries retaliatory tariffs have not directly impacted geo and are not
expected to do so. The threat of an escalation in hostilities either in Ukraine or the Middle East remain a concern
and are highly unpredictable.

Exchange Rates

The company procures its products and some of its services in US dollars. The company has a hedging strategy
designed to limit exposure to key exchange rate movements and sales are made in multiple currencies to
minimise currency conversions.

Interest Rates

Interest charges over the period have fallen as debt levels have been reduced. Repayments of the CBILS loans
taken out during the pandemic are being paid down on schedule.

Other Risks :
The board continually reviews the business’ exposure to price, credit, liquidity and other financial risks and is
satisfied that these are adequately addressed.

Financial key performance indicators
Sales during the year were steady at £20.7m, reflecting similar demand to the prior year. Margins improved to
30% as a result of cost reductions and favourable currency movements. Administrative expenses reduced by
25% as the cost reductions initiated in the prior year fed through on a full year basis. Although a difficult statistic
to track due to increases and decreases in inventory in the supply chain, market share in the UK increased
during the year.
ESG
The environmental agenda has always been core to geo and to demonstrate this the company monitors
sustainability goals via the Ecovadis service achieving a high score for the fifth year running. The company
continues to be a signatory to the UN Global Compact, the principles of which are embedded in geo's policies
and procedures.

The consolidated financial statements present the results of the Company and its own subsidiaries

2.13 Stocks
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost and net realisable value after making due allowances for
obsolete and slow-moving stocks. Cost is based on the first-in first-out principle and includes
expenditure incurred in acquiring the stocks, production or conversion costs and other costs in
bringing them to their existing location and condition. Net realisable value is based on estimated
selling price, less further costs expected to be in the year and contributions paid are shown as other
accruals or prepayments in the balance sheet.
At each balance sheet date, stocks are assessed for impairment. If stock is impaired, the carrying
amount is reduced to its selling price less costs to complete and sell. The impairment loss is
recognised immediately in the consolidated profit or loss account.

2.14 Debtors
Short-term debtors are measured at transaction price, less any impairment. Loans receivable are
measured initially at fair value, net of transaction costs, and are measured subsequently at amortised
cost using the effective interest method, less any impairment.

2.15 Cash and cash equivalents
Cash is represented by cash in hand and deposits with financial institutions repayable without penalty
on notice of not more than 24 hours. Cash equivalents are highly liquid investments that mature in no
more than three months from the date of acquisition and that are readily convertible to known
amounts of cash with insignificant risk of change in value.

2.16 Creditors
Short-term creditors are measured at the transaction price. Other financial liabilities, including bank
loans, are measured initially at fair value, net of transaction costs, and are measured subsequently at

. amortised cost using the effective interest method.

2.17 Provisions for liabilities :
Provisions are made where an event has taken place that gives the group a legal or constructive
obligation that probably requires settlement by a transfer of economic benefit, and a reliable estimate
can be made of the amount of the obligation. .
Provisions are charged as an expense to the consolidated profit and loss account in the year that the
group becomes aware of the obligation, and are measured at the best estimate at the balance sheet
date of the expenditure required to settle the obligation, taking into account relevant risks and
uncertainties.
When payments are eventually made, they are charged to the provision carried in the balance sheet.

2.22 Impairment of assets:
Assets, other than those measured at fair value, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each
balance sheet date. If there is objective evidence of impairment, an impairment loss is recognised in
the consolidated profit and loss account as described below:
_ Financial assets
For financial assets carried at amortised cost, the amount of an impairment is the difference between
the asset's carrying amount and the present value of estimated future cash flows, discounted at the
financial asset's original effective interest rate. For financial assets carried at cost less impairment,
the impairment loss is the difference between the asset's carrying amount and the best estimate of
the amount that would be received for the asset if it were to be sold at the reporting date. Where
indicators exist for a decrease in impairment loss, and the decrease can be related objectively to an
event occurring after the impairment loss, and the decrease can be related objectively to an event
occurring after the impairment was recognised, the prior impairment loss is tested to determine
reversal. An impairment loss is reversed on an individual impaired financial asset to the extent that
the revised recoverable value does not lead to a revised carrying amount higher than the carrying
value had no impairment been recognised.
Non -financial assets
An asset is impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that
occurred after initial recognition, the estimated recoverable value of the asset has been reduced. The
recoverable amount of an asset is the higher of its fair value less costs to sell and its value in use.
Where indicators exist for a decrease in impairment loss previously recognised for assets other than
goodwill, the prior impairment loss is rested to determine reversal. An impairment loss in reversed on
an individual impaired asset to the extent that the revised recoverable-value does not lead to a
revised carrying amount higher than the carrying value had no impairment been recognised.

. Any deferred tax balances are reversed if and when all conditions for retaining associated tax
