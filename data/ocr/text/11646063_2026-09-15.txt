Strategic Report
for the year ended 31 December 2025
The Directors present the Strategic Report of Vocalink International Limited (the "Company" or "VIL") for the year ended 31
December 2025.
Principal activities .
The Company provides a range of Real-Time Payment ("RTP") services and software applications across international markets.
The Company leverages assets and expertise from Mastercard to bring innovation to the RTP market and develop and enhance its
products and services.
VIL is part of the Mastercard family, one of the most widely recognised and respected technology companies in the world. The
shared vision is to power economies and empower people building an inclusive and sustainable economy where everyone prospers
and making transactions secure, simple, smart and more resilient.
Business review :
The Company's long-term vision is to deliver world-class, international real-time payments with overlay services that foster digital
growth, accelerate financial inclusion and drive seamless adoption. With the rapid advancement of RTPs and growing global demand
and adoption, our strategy outlines our objectives to solidify our position as a market leader in international RTPs, while harnessing
innovation to provide customers with products and services that meet their evolving needs and expectations.
2025 was a year of deliberate progress - strengthening our foundations and actively preparing the business for sustainable growth.
Key areas of focus were:
e increasing customer satisfaction by delivering on our commitments at pace;
e achieving continued increases in operating income and revenue growth;
e strengthening service excellence and operational resilience; and
e driving a strong culture and leadership, underpinned by robust governance practices.
Focus and investment in operational excellence and enhancing risk management and resilience continued throughout 2025,
reflecting that as transaction volumes scale into higher value and higher risk use cases, growth increasingly depends on whether
systems deliver confidence and intelligence, not just immediacy.
In 2025, the global momentum of RTPs continued and the Company continued to see strong transaction revenue growth with a 19%
increase in transaction volumes, processing over 30.5 billion transactions in 2025. Transaction volume growth, in addition to
customer contractual changes effected during the year, resulted in a 37% increase in transaction processing revenue and drove
increases of 8% in licence and maintenance revenue. .
Internal revenue increased by 15% during the year, due to steady growth and increasing scale in the Company's subsidiary operations
which recorded a 226% increase in customer transaction volumes in 2025. In addition, the Company received dividend income of
£3.0m.
During the year, revenue increased to £102.7m (2024: £85.3m). Staff and other operating costs reduced by 30% from prior year due
to the effectiveness of multiple cost efficiency initiatives. The Company now owns key intellectual assets which resulted in the
cessation of licence fees during the year (2024: £39. 9m), however the associated assets transferred resulted in D&A costs increasing
to £58.8m (2024: £12.4m). Therefore, the Company's loss in the current year reduced to £78.0m (2024: £107.4m). The Company
continues to be loss making due to its commitment to undertake substantial investment in payments innovation and to enhance the
resilience and security of the services it delivers. The Company continues to strive to improve future profitability. During the year
its growth strategy was refreshed for the next phase of expansion into new markets and reviews are progressing on existing customer
contracts to support revenue and margin growth, in addition to a stronger focus on agile cost management. The Company's net asset
position is £249.5m (2024: £297.8m).
The Company maintained its consistent international operating footprint in six markets, delivering a combination of managed
services and software sales. The global evolution and growth of RTPs continues to be driven by the acceleration of the electronic
payments share and associated cash displacement, offering consumers and businesses more choice to transact in ways that meet .
their needs. RTPs have expanded from person-to-person ("P2P") transactions to person-to-merchant ("P2M") payments, driven by
government mandates, mobile phone usage, and QR codes. The Company continues to evolve its core products to remain in step
with technological innovation, regulatory changes and consumer demand, while leveraging existing assets and expertise from
Mastercard to bring enhancements and innovation to the international payments market.

Business review (continued)
The Company continues to support customers by enhancing its products and services and by strengthening operational performance.
Diverse solutions have enabled widespread adoption of RTPs, making them accessible to a broad range of consumers and merchants.
The approach to implementing and delivering growth and additional value through RTPs differs considerably across markets,
influenced by factors such as consumer needs, regulatory influence and market maturity. Each implementation requires incremental
work to adapt to local needs and the customer solution, growing the value proposition by linking infrastructure services with other
drivers of customer value. These drivers include making payments more secure, resilient and more intelligent, as well as supporting
new payment use cases. The Company continues to strengthen its portfolio by driving improved commercial outcomes for the
Company's customers.
Future outlook
Global adoption of RTP systems is entering a second wave of growth, with greater global adoption and an increasing need for real-
time solutions. The landscape is becoming increasingly competitive and the value proposition to customers is evolving as their
needs and expectations change, requiring innovation and adaptation.
Leveraging the combined strengths of global expertise and local market insights in partnership with the broader RTP and
Mastercard teams, VIL is strengthening customer value propositions to accelerate tangible commercial growth in international
markets through innovative use-cases, resilient layered architecture and enhancing convenience. This will ensure that the
Company remains at the forefront of innovative payments solutions and delivers measurable results for our stakeholders.
Growth in the adoption of RTP products and services is not abating, with the value of transactions processed using RTP technology
predicted to continue to substantially grow. Understanding the evolution of markets, what technologies and stakeholders have
supported them, and anticipating future developments will assist the Company and Mastercard maximise the full potential.
The Company's RTP products and services will expand to cover increasingly more diverse use cases, including
business-to-business ("B2B") and business-to-consumer ("B2C") payment flows while deepening P2P and P2M use cases already
enabled via existing payment instruments from banks, digital wallets and alternative payment methods ("APMs"), offering
consumers and businesses fast, secure and convenient payment options. These innovative solutions not only support financial
inclusion but will also drive growth in e-commerce and mobile commerce by simplifying online and in-app purchases.
A consistent and user-friendly payment experience is essential for driving adoption and ongoing usage and the Company will
continue to invest in enhancing and strengthening the products and services it provides as well as the resilience of the underpinning
infrastructure. Additionally, increased transactions and new participants create more fraud opportunities and the Company will
continue to invest in its Financial Crime Solutions to prioritise fraud risk management.
Financing and investment
The Company is supported by capital investment from its sole shareholder and immediate parent company Vocalink Holdings
Limited ("VHL"), which is part of the Mastercard Group. On 1 April 2025, VHL invested an additional £30.0m (2024: £75.0m) in
the Company in the form of ordinary share capital. The Company continues to pursue a programme of investment in existing
products and new product development, as well as growing the Company's customer base and international footprint. The investment
in the development of assets is disclosed in the notes to the financial statements. The Company's ultimate parent, Mastercard
Incorporated, is committed to continuing to invest in RTP and the associated infrastructure of the Vocalink Group.
The Directors have considered the letter of support received from Mastercard International Incorporated ("MCI"), an intermediary
parent of the Company. This letter of support provides proportionate financial support for a period of at least 12 months from the
date of signing these financial statements.
Key performance indicators

VIL Risk Management Framework
The Directors have overall responsibility for risk management and are supported in their oversight duties by the Real Time Payments
International ("RTPI") Executive Risk Committee ("ERC"). VIL operates a Three Lines of Defence model which establishes a clear
organisational structure and allocation of responsibilities for risk management. The First Line takes and manages risk, the Second
Line provides guidance and assurance, and the Third Line (Internal Audit) provides independent opinions on the effectiveness of
risk management. Risk management processes are in line with industry and regulatory standards and are executed with collaboration
and challenge across the Three Lines of Defence.
Risk Operating Model and Governance
The Directors have overall responsibility for risk management and are supported in their oversight duties by the RTPI ERC which
oversees risk management and the effective operation of the RMF. The ERC operates in line with delegated authorities and defined
terms of reference.
Internal audit
Internal audit is accountable for providing independent and objective assurance and advisory activities designed to add value and
improve Mastercard's operations and control environment. It supports the Company to accomplish its objectives by bringing a
systematic and disciplined approach to evaluating and improving the design and operating effectiveness of governance, risk
management, and internal controls. Internal Audit adheres to the Institute of Internal Auditors standards.

Strategic risk
VIL continues to make a number of significant investments to deliver payment services for a range of international customers. VIL's
strategy is to continue growing existing customer relationships by providing services that drive innovation, ensure reliability, and
deliver safe and secure payments. Maintaining excellent and resilient services and high business standards is key to managing VIL's
strategic risks and securing new business opportunities.
VIL continues to invest significantly to develop and enhance its applications, services, core infrastructure and internal ways of
working, as well as partnering with Mastercard to leverage payments innovation.
VIL also relies on people to deliver and maintain its technology and manage strategic risks, and therefore capabilities, culture and
well-being remain a key focus for the Executive team.
Information security risk
Cyber Security remains a prevalent and fast-evolving threat across many industries and particularly within financial and payment
services. Global inter-connectivity and technological advancement are adding to the complexity of cyber security risk and enabling
more advanced Al-enabled methods of attack to be employed by perpetrators.
VIL continues to work closely with its customers, governments and the wider payments industry to understand and evaluate the
nature of these threats, enabling the Executive and Directors to steer strategic and tactical risk decisions in line with the constantly
evolving threat landscape. VIL is committed to the continued enhancement of cyber resilience and will ensure that it has the
capabilities to resist and counter such threats through mature and robust layered defences, system monitoring and traffic analysis,
and close alignment with industry and international security standards. Ongoing enhancements of Mastercard's threat intelligence
capabilities continue to keep pace with the evolving and dynamic cyber threat landscape.
Operational resilience risk (including technology and third-party risk management)
Resilience and recovery capabilities are assured through active control management, external certifications and standards
assessments, rigorous disaster recovery testing and scenario testing exercises. Ongoing investment in a multi-layered, secure IT
infrastructure designed to safeguard client data and maintain uninterrupted access to critical systems, tools and people enables VIL
to uphold its operational resilience and service availability obligations.
Operational Resilience remains a core strategic priority for VIL. Over the past year, RTPI has continued to strengthen and mature
capabilities. This has included the investment in a Technology & Operations department that sets out objectives focusing on
sustaining and embedding operational resilience controls whilst aligning to the overarching organisational strategy and business
imperatives. Key areas of focus include improved service resilience, increased infrastructure and network headroom, strengthened
incident management, monitoring and governance processes. Improvements aim to better control core services and improve
scalability for future volume growth.
VIL utilises Vocalink Limited ("VLL"), a fellow subsidiary of VHL, and Mastercard capabilities to manage the bulk of its core
infrastructure and relies on partners for selected activities. VIL's suppliers are managed via a Supplier Management Framework and
Outsourcing and Third-Party Risk Management Policy. Operational governance forums are in place for key and critical suppliers,
enabling VIL to manage any risks within its supply chain.
VIL remains committed to sustained operational capability and reliability, thereby safeguarding business continuity and maintaining
confidence in the broader financial ecosystem. VIL continues to conduct more sophisticated testing, including with other industry
participants. : .
Regulatory risk . :
VIL provides services within multiple regulated environments across the world and takes an active role in understanding the
regulatory !andscape and associated risks. An increasing number of activities and services that VIL provides directly or indirectly
fall within the regulatory perimeter. Failure to meet regulatory expectations and comply with relevant requirements would erode
confidencé in VIL and impact on its operations. VIL continues to see requests to amend commercial agreements for its customers to
reflect their own regulatory obligations.
VIL manages its regulatory risk by adopting a risk-based approach, tailored to risk tolerance and proximity to potential disruptions.
Regulatory, legal and compliance teams support Mastercard and its subsidiaries and are responsible for monitoring and assessing
regulatory developments and for reporting regularly to the Executive. The Vocalink Group and Mastercard regularly engage in
regulatory and industry events and roundtables to remain informed about all aspects of the broader payments ecosystem.

The Company recognises loss allowances for expected credit losses ("ECL") on financial assets measured at amortised cost, and

Lifetime ECLs are the ECLs that result from all possible default events over the expected life of a financial instrument.

The maximum period considered when estimating ECLs is the maximum contractual period over which the Company is exposed to
credit risk.

1.13 Provisions
A provision is recognised in the Statement of Financial Position when the Company has a present legal or constructive obligation
as a result of a past event, that can be reliably measured and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to
settle the obligation. Provisions are determined by discounting the expected future cash flows at a pre-tax rate that reflects risks
specific to the liability.
Onerous contract provisions are recognised on the date on which the contract is identified as onerous. The provision represents the
excess of the unavoidable costs of meeting the contractual obligations over the expected economic benefits arising from the contract.
The unavoidable costs reflect the lowest net cost of exiting the contract, which is measured as the lower of the cost of fulfilling a
contract and any compensation or penalties arising from failure to fulfil it. The cost of fulfilling a contract comprises the costs that
relate directly to the contract, including both incremental costs and an allocation of other directly attributable costs.
A contingent liability is a possible obligation whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of an
uncertain future event or a present obligation that is uncertain as regards the amount. Where there is possibility of an outflow of
economic benefits, the Company discloses an estimate of the financial effect and any uncertainties relating to it unless the probability
of the liability arising is considered to be remote. No contingent liabilities have been identified for 2025.

Leases
At the inception of a contract, the Company assesses whether a contract is, or contains, a lease. A contract is, or contains, a lease
if the contract conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.
The Company's leases generally relate to property or office equipment. Lease terms are negotiated on an individual basis and do not
impose any covenants. The lease term is determined by the non-cancellable period of the lease, together with; optional renewable
periods if the Company is reasonably certain to extend; and periods after an optional termination date if the Company is reasonably
certain not to terminate early.
As a Lessee ;
The Company recognises a right of use asset and a lease liability at the lease commencement date. The right of use asset is initially
measured at cost, which comprises the initial amount of the lease liability adjusted for any lease payments made at or before the
: commencement date, plus any initial direct costs incurred and an estimate of costs to dismantle and remove the underlying asset or
to restore the underlying asset or the site on which it is located, less any lease incentives received.
The right-of-use asset is subsequently depreciated using the straight-line method from the commencement date to the end of the
lease term, unless the lease transfers ownership of the underlying asset to the Company by the end of the lease term or the cost of
the right-of-use asset reflects that the Company will exercise a purchase option. In that case the right-of-use asset will be depreciated
over the useful life of the underlying asset, which is determined on the same basis as those of property and equipment. In addition,
the right-of-use asset is periodically reduced by impairment losses, if any, and adjusted for certain remeasurements of the lease
liability.
The lease liability is initially measured at the present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the commencement date,
discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, if that rate cannot be readily determined, the Company's incremental
borrowing rate. Generally, the Company uses its incremental borrowing rate as the discount rate.
Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability comprise the following:
e fixed payments, including in-substance fixed payments; ‘
e variable lease payments that depend on an index or a rate, initially measured using the index or rate as at the commencement
date;
| amounts expected to be payable under a residual value guarantee; _
e the exercise price under a purchase option that the Company is reasonably certain to exercise;
e — lease payments in an optional renewal period if the Company is reasonably certain to exercise an extension option; and
e penalties for early termination of a lease unless the Company is reasonably certain not to terminate early.
