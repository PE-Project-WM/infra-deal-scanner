FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

Introduction

The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2025. The Company's long-term

strategy focuses on enabling clients to modernize and scale their IT infrastructure through comprehensive Hybrid

IT services, with a strong emphasis on cloud migration, infrastructure consolidation, and managed services. The

strategy includes: supporting clients’ growth ambitions by delivering scalable, flexible, and resilient cloud solutions,

reducing clients’ total cost of ownership and operational costs, allowing reinvestment into innovation and improved

service delivery, enhancing security and resilience, enabling clients to focus on higher-value strategic projects,

acting as a strategic partner to align technology transformation with clients’ ambitious business strategies,

ensuring agility and readiness for future growth and market changes.

Principal! activities :

The Company's principal activity focuses on providing comprehensive Hybrid IT services and governance,

covering everything from cloud to mainframe environments. The company primarily operates within the United

Kingdom, supporting clients through digital transformation and ensuring robust, secure, and efficient IT operations.

Business review

Running IT operations well is complex and requires expertise and experience and Ensono is trusted by some of

the world’s most successful companies given its record in delivering solutions customised to each client's IT

environments, allowing companies to focus on their core business. Clients can rely on Ensono to develop, deliver,

and maintain their ideal managed IT solution.

Ensono provides this efficiently and effectively, taking the pain of IT operations away from clients through

mainframe, infrastructure, and public cloud offerings. Ensono’s Consulting and Advisory practice provides

business transformation capabilities, ranging from technology innovation reviews to solution design and

transformational project delivery.

In 2025, the Company began an initiative to further simplify the legal entity structure by novating customer and

supplier contracts from Ensono Digital, it’s directly owned, subsidiary to the Company. As of the end of the year,

all employees and supplier contracts were moved to the Company. Nearly all client contracts were novated by the

end of the year, with one completed in 2026.

Total turnover for 2025 grew to £88.1m, up from £76.9m in 2024, representing a 15% year-over-year increase.

This growth was driven by organic expansion across mainframe, advisory and consulting services, alongside

accelerated growth in public cloud, largely attributable to the novation of Ensono Digital contracts.

The Company is supported by the strong financial position of its immediate parent, Ensono Intermediate HoldCo,

Inc. Ensono Intermediate HoldCo, Inc. is committed to growing the Company's turnover and profitability and

believes the most successful way to achieve this is by continuing to deliver excellent service to current clients and

by onboarding new clients while having long-lasting relationships with key suppliers. Based on recent financial

results, the Company has taken several actions to improve its financial profile. The company’s parent undertaking,

Ensono Intermediate HoldCo, Inc. has provided confirmation that it will provide financial support to the company,

if required, for a period of twelve months from the date of approval of the accounts.

In 2025, the Company entered a consultation process with employees and reduced the number of roles within the

company. The consultation process concluded during the year at a cost of £1,320,000 affecting a total of 43

employees. The change is expected to result in a reduction in staff costs of approximately £3,401,000 per year.

Turnover
Total turnover for 2025 reached £88.1m, compared to £76.9m in 2024, representing an absolute increase of
£11.3m and a year-on-year growth rate of 15%. This performance reflects a strong commercial trajectory
underpinned by organic service expansion and contract novation activity.
Gross Profit ,
Gross profit increased to £14.5m in 2025 from £12.8m in 2024, an improvement of £1.6m (13%). The gross profit
margin contracted marginally by 1 percentage point, declining from 17% to 16%, indicating that whilst absolute
profitability improved, cost growth slightly outpaced revenue growth at the gross level.
Adjusted EBITDA :
Adjusted EBITDA improved significantly, narrowing from a loss of £1.2m in 2024 to a loss of £0.3m in 2025, in
total £0.98m improvement (79%). The Adjusted EBITDA margin correspondingly improved by 2 percentage points,
moving from -2% to 0%, signaling that the business is approaching operational breakeven.
Operating Loss
The operating loss reduced substantially from £26.4m in 2024 to £9.2m in 2025, a reduction of £17.2m (65%).
The operating loss margin improved by 24 percentage points, from -34% to -10%, demonstrating meaningful
progress toward operational sustainability (note 5):

Staff Turnover
Staff turnover measures attrition through the ordinary course of business, which increased by 14% to 26% in 2025
(2024: 12%). The increase was primarily attributable to redundancies implemented as part of the company
restructuring programme.
Excluding the impact of redundancies, underlying staff turnover remained stable at 11%, consistent with the prior
year (2024: 10%). This underlying stability is a positive indicator of the business's ability to retain talent and reflects
ongoing efforts to foster employee engagement and satisfaction.
Business environment and strategy
Ensono operates within the enterprise and internet-facing application infrastructure management market a defined
subset of the broader IT services operations segment which continues to demonstrate sustained maturity and
expansion. The prevailing shift from in-house IT management to third-party outsourcing is expected to persist
across mid-to-large enterprises for the foreseeable future. In response.
Ensono's strategy is centred on sustaining strong organic growth through the cultivation of strategic partnerships,
both to acquire new clients and to deepen and broaden the scope of services delivered to its existing client base.
Research and development ,
The Company continues to invest in extending its products and services whilst delivering continuous
improvements to those services. Continued emphasis was placed on the implementation of Ensono Envision
globally. The Company continues to invest in enhancements to Envision Advisor. Envision Advisor was developed
and introduced to provide a new level of service to clients and was enhanced with additional machine learning Al
capability in 2024 known as Ensono Predictive Engine that predicts potential service outages proactively so that they
can be prevented from ever occurring. It provides continuous and proactive recommendations to clients, to ensure

: that clients’ public cloud and traditional infrastructure environments are optimised for cost, performance, and security
as they continually evolve. Additionally, the Ensono Cloud Platform, a scalable, demand-based IT infrastructure
continues to be developed and enhanced to provide a flexible, cost-efficient private cloud platform for clients. :
Costs capitalised during the year for development of software amounted to £1.9m (2024: £2.4m).
Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of the business and the execution of the Company's strategy are subject to several risks; these
tisks are identified and mitigated where appropriate. The key business risks affecting the Company relate to
competition and evolving technologies requested by clients. To mitigate this risk Ensono continues to expand its
offerings and increase the value-add provided to clients and carefully monitors client satisfaction to help
understand clients’ expectations and whether their needs are being met.
Technological Environment
Operating within a technical industry presents risks due to advances in products and services. The success of the
Company depends on our ability to continue to deploy services and solutions that meet the technological
requirements of our clients. The Directors of the Company and the wider Ensono Group continue to encourage
investment in the development of our Envision intellectual property which provides broad visibility and deep insights
into client's infrastructure, from mainframe to cloud, allowing Ensono to proactively respond to adverse events in
client infrastructure and manage our client's IT estates in real-time.
For information on financial risks please refer to the Directors’ Report.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The Company uses a variety of financial instruments including cash, borrowings, equity investments and various
items, such as trade debtors and trade creditors, that arise directly from its operations. The main purpose of these
financial instruments is to provide working capital for the Company's operations.
The Directors are of the view that the main risks arising from the Company's financial instruments are interest rate
tisk, liquidity risk and credit risk. The Directors set and review policies for managing each of these risks and they
are summarised below. These policies have remained unchanged from previous years.
Interest rate risk
The Company has no external interest-bearing loans. It finances its operations through intercompany loans which
have a variable rate of interest. The agreements for these intercompany allow for deferral of interest payments
without penalty.
Liquidity risk
The Company seeks to manage financial risk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable needs
and to invest cash assets safely and profitably. Cash flows are monitored on a weekly basis. The Company is
supported by its parent company Ensono Intermediate HoldCo, Inc. which has provided a letter of support to the
Company confirming it has significant liquid assets to support the Company if required. The Company is also
entitled to payment on demand of intercompany receivables due from the securitisation of trade debtors (note 17)
of £10.7m (2024 - £14m) at 31 December 2025.
Credit risk
The primary risk associated with trade debtors relates to the non-payment of customer invoices. The Company
retains and manages this risk internally through the securitisation of trade debtors (note 17), which is structured on
a non-recourse basis, ensuring the risk remains within the Company. The securitised amount is adjusted for any
credit notes subsequently issued by the Company. In this arrangement an intercompany special purpose entity
(“SPE”) purchases the Company's trade debtors and unbilled revenue at fair market value discounted based on
prime rates published in the money market section of The Wall Street Journal in a non-recourse arrangement. The
Company retains responsibility for collections from clients and in exchange receives immediate entitlement to
payment from the SPE for the cumulative trade debtors and unbilled revenue.

Methodologies
The company has taken guidance from the ‘UK Government Environmental Reporting Guidelines’ (March 2019)
and the ‘Greenhouse gas reporting: conversion factors 2025’ documents for calculating its carbon emissions.
Gas and electricity data from our data centres and offices have been obtained directly from our landlords. Diesel

Energy Efficiency Action
During the year, the company has implemented the ISO 14001 standard as it continues its commitment to
sustainable practices and dedication to reducing its carbon footprint, with the aim of identifying areas of
improvement, measure the environment impact and implement strategies to achieve environmental objectives.

the company’s data centre footprint, where appropriate, and encouraging teams to take advantage of a flexible

2.6 Share based payments
Equity settled share-based payments are measured at fair value of the equity instruments at the date of grant
and the expense recorded in the Statement of Comprehensive Income over the vesting period, with the
corresponding entry in retained earnings. The fair value has been determined by an external valuer using the
Monte Carlo Simulation model which considers market conditions and factors such as exit date probabilities,
transaction costs, and the impact of the equity capital structure in deriving a value for the RIAs.

Non-market vesting conditions are taken into account by adjusting the number of equity instruments expected
to vest at each Statement of Financial Position date so that, ultimately, the cumulative amount recognised over
the vesting period is based on the number of options that eventually vest.

The value of the share options is reassessed at each Statement of Financial Position date and takes into
account leavers, performance based vesting conditions and time-based vesting conditions. Performance based
conditions are estimated based on the future performance of the company. Time based conditions are
calculated based on the time elapsed from the grant date, to the reporting date and on estimations of the date
of the future triggering event.

Where the terms of an equity-settled award are modified or a new award is designated as replacing a cancelled
or settled award, the cost based on the original award terms continues to be recognised over the original vesting
period. In addition, an expense is recognised over the remainder of the new vesting period for the incremental
fair value of any modification, based on the difference between the fair value of the original award and the fair
value of the modified award, both as measured on the date of the modification. No reduction is recognised if
this difference is negative.

: Where an equity-settled award is cancelled, it is treated as an acceleration of vesting, and any cost not yet
recognised in the statement of comprehensive income for the award is expensed immediately. Any
compensation paid up to the fair value of the award at the cancellation or settlement date is deducted from
equity, with any excess over fair value expensed in the statement of comprehensive income.

2.13 Financial instruments

The Company has chosen to adopt Sections 11 and 12 of FRS 102 in respect of financial instruments.

The Company only enters into basic financial instrument transactions that result in the recognition of

financial assets and liabilities like trade and other debtors and creditors, loans from banks and other third

parties, loans to related parties and investments in ordinary shares.

Debt instruments (other than those wholly repayable or receivable within one year), including loans and

other accounts receivable and payable, are initially measured at present value of the future cash flows and

subsequently at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Debt instruments that are payable or

receivable within one year, typically trade debtors and creditors, are measured, initially and subsequently, at

the undiscounted amount of the cash or other consideration expected to be paid or received. However, if the

arrangements of a short-term instrument constitute a financing transaction, like the payment of a trade debt

deferred beyond normal business terms or in case of an out-right short-term loan that is not at market rate,

the financial asset or liability is measured, initially at the present value of future cash flows discounted at a

market rate of interest for a similar debt instrument and subsequently at amortised cost.

Financial assets that are measured at cost and amortised cost are assessed at the end of each Statement

of Financial Position period for objective evidence of impairment. If objective evidence of impairment is

found, an impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

For financial assets measured at amortised cost, the impairment loss is measured as the difference between

an asset's carrying amount and the present value of estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original

effective interest rate. If a financial asset has a variable interest rate, the discount rate for measuring any

impairment loss is the current effective interest rate determined under the contract.

For financial assets measured at cost less impairment, the impairment loss is measured as the difference

between an asset's carrying amount and best estimate of the recoverable amount, which is an approximation

of the amount that the Company would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the Statement of Financial

Position date.

Financial assets and fiabilities are offset and the net amount reported in the Statement of Financial Position

when there is an enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a

net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
