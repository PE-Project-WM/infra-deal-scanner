Growth strategy

As the key UK operating unit in the Proactis Group (the Group), Proactis Limited aims to deliver the Group’s

strategy as appropriate in the UK.

. Overview ;

The Group's long-term strategy is to build an international business focused on delivering best value to its

customers through the digital transformation of their spend management systems and processes with the

application of the Group’s software technology and provision of its expert services.

The Group’s strategy to address what the Board believes to be a large and rapidly-growing market for

business spend management software solutions can be illustrated as follows:

e Providing a leading modular, end-to-end, cloud-based SaaS solution focused on meeting the needs of
mid-market, services-led Private, Public and Not for Profit organisations, which the Board believes is
the most rapidly-growing and underserved segment of the market;

e Accelerate momentum in new business across all the territories the Group operates in, primarily being
the UK, the USA, France, DACH, and the Benelux regions;

e Maximise the opportunity within the Group’s current customer base of +1,000 clients through focusing
on cross and up-sell of the Group’s modular solution set, as well as continuing to provide leading
support and investing in the product base to continue high levels of retention; and

e Promote a positive workplace culture to make Proactis an employer of choice, to help the Group
attract and retain leading talent in all functional areas of the business, and to act as a responsible
corporate citizen.

This strategy is designed to deliver a strong financial proposition of profitable, cash generative, organic

growth with a high level of visibility illustrated by its annual recurring revenue (“ARR”) across both buyer

and supplier paid products.

The Group aims to drive organic growth into its business spend management solutions by retaining existing

and winning new customers through continually improving its best-in-class spend management solutions

with high service levels and excellent user support as well as a focused approach to the upselling of the

Group’s extensive range of solutions, creating even broader and deeper customer relationships.

The Group is focussed on the Proactis modular solutions suite. This platform combines our key strengths

and heritage as an expert provider within our chosen markets, providing a powerful Source-to-Pay suite of

modules specifically designed and proven for mid-market organisations. The flexibility of scaling modules
as organisations mature or adopting the full suite as part of more strategic transformation, delivers
customers short, medium and long-term benefits with proven outcomes.

During the period, the Board have formed an overall ESG strategy which includes targets by each area.

Initial actions have included the Group joining the UN Global Compact, and setting a Net Zero agenda.

Other actions are being worked through.

Solutions and market performance review
Business Spend Management (Buyer) solutions
The group provides spend management solutions to customers that enable those customers to drive cost
reduction, increase efficiency and greater compliance and control. The modular nature of the end-to-end
platform, from supply chain management through to invoice approval enables customers to partner with
Proactis, on a journey to driving best value Spend Management and process excellence.
Supplier solutions
Efficient and effective supply chain interaction with buyers is critical and Proactis provides a range of
solutions enabling suppliers to trade and grow with buyers. From SME suppliers looking for more buyer
opportunities to grow their business, through to promoting the goods and services they provide buyers via
“amazon style" promotion in a Marketplace, to electronically driving orders and invoices between buyers
and suppliers. The Proactis supplier solutions support suppliers as consequential to the buyer activities, in
addition to providing direct growth opportunities.
Supplier subscription revenues for the period were £5.3m (12 months to 31 January 2024 - £5.9m and 18
months for FY23: £9.0m ). Revenue arising from the Group’s networking technology was £2.0m (12 months
to 31 January 2024 - £2.5m and 18 months for FY23: £3.8m). The supplier side of the business represents
an underpenetrated opportunity to align with the mid-market buyer proposition, enabling further
differentiation within the buyer proposition. This in turn will bring more value to our Supplier customers
and grow this revenue stream
Markets
The Group continues to sell its solutions to customers operating across over 46 countries, supported by
our direct presence in UK, US, DACH, NL, France, Philippines, New Zealand and also through our partner
network. The Group offers true multi-company, multi-currency and multi-language capabilities and this
remains an essential differentiator to existing customers for their international operations and to potential
customers as the Group increases its presence in public and serviced orientated sectors worldwide.

The Group competes on various levels, both tactically with the positioning of individual modules across the
Source-to-Pay suite (land and expand strategy) and more strategically at a transformational level with the
sale of the whole suite of modules as a platform; competitors are therefore varied across local vendors,
Enterprise Resource Planning (“ERP”) vendors and international procurement vendors and this mix makes
for an extremely competitive environment.
The Group's go-to-market strategy is based on a targeted and efficient deployment of its marketing and
sales resource within each market segment it operates in. Within those segments, the Group seeks to
maximise its return by selecting service industry verticals where its solutions has delivered proven
outcomes with a proven customer base and where a clear rationale can be outlined for the selection of
Proactis.
The “land and expand” modular and “end-to-end” platform messages, alongside the tight integration
narrative (integration strength ranging from strategic ERP partnerships to proven footprint of customers)
enable differentiation in sales scenarios and positions the Group as a value led solution against more
“feature-function” led vendors who are more horizontal in market approach. This modular platform
approach enables optimum levels of flexibility as a value proposition and is particularly compelling for
service based, mid-sized commercial and public sector organisations, both of which the Group is focused
on across all of its business segments.
One further such rationale is providing solutions that have excellent references through strong delivery
and Account Management where prospects can then speak with existing customers in the same or
associated industries. A further, proven component of differentiation is strategic partnerships, close
alignment with "Office of the CFO" strategic solutions such as Finance/ERP system vendors, where
Proactis is a key complimentary solution and extends the reach and competitiveness of the Finance/ERP
provider, enabling those providers to present best-in-class Spend Management and ERP as a unified
proposition, drives a low-risk, high value proposition to customers where the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) is
highly synergistic.

2.19 Leases

Following the transition to IFRS 16, the Company considers whether a contract is, or contains, a

lease. A lease is defined as ‘a contract, or part of a contract, that conveys the right to use an

asset (the underlying asset) for a period of time in exchange for consideration’.

To apply this definition the Company assesses whether the contract meets three key

evaluations which are whether:

e the contract contains an identified asset, which is either explicitly identified in the contract
or implicitly specified by being identified at the time the asset is made available to the
Company;

e the Company has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from the
identified asset throughout the period of use, considering its rights within the defined scope
of the contract; and :

« the Company has the right to direct the use of the identified asset throughout the period of
use. The Company assesses whether it has the right to direct ‘how and for what purpose’ the
asset is used throughout the period of use.

At lease commencement date, the Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability

on the balance sheet. The right-of use asset is measured at cost, which is made up of the initial

measurement of the lease liability, any initial direct costs incurred by the Company and any
lease payments made in advance of the lease commencement date (net of any incentives

received). ,

The Company depreciates the right-of-use assets on a straight-line basis from the lease

commencement date to the earlier of the end of the useful life of the right-of-use asset or the

end of the lease term. The Company also assesses the right-of-use asset for impairment when
such indicators exist. :

At the commencement date, the Company measures the lease liability at the present value of

the lease payments unpaid at that date, discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease if

that rate is readily available or alternatively, the Company's incremental borrowing rate.

Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability are made up of fixed

payments (including in-substance fixed), amounts expected to be payable under a residual

value guarantee and payments arising from options reasonably certain to be exercised.

Maintenance payments which vary are not included within the measurement of the initial lease

liability; these are instead recognised in the Income Statement in line with their usage.
