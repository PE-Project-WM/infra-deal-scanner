The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025
PRINCIPAL ACTIVITY
The principal activity of the company is the provision of unified cloud communications, contact centre solutions and managed IT services.
REVIEW OF THE BUSINESS
During the financial year 2025, 4Net Technologies Ltd continued to deliver customer experience and digital transformation solutions
underpinned by secure networking, cloud and cybersecurity services to private and public sector organisations across the UK and beyond.
Trading in the year reflected continued rebalancing of the public and private sector revenue mix and a further shift in the composition of pipeline
and new business, with a greater proportion of new business opportunities derived from CX-led consultancy, public cloud and cross-sell
opportunities linked to acquired capabilities, rather than legacy solutions. This shift reflects the Company’s ongoing portfolio evolution and
increasing demand for secure, scalable and outcomes-focused services.
The Company continued to develop its CX Consulting model, supporting customers with diagnostics and adoption alongside technology
delivery. This approach supported carly delivery of artificial intelligence use cases and, during the year, the Company launched its IntellAIgent
agentic solution and invested in capability growth through senior hires to strengthen delivery governance and methods.
Partner-led routes to market also continued to develop. The Company established initial partnerships with business process outsourcers (BPOs)
and expanded its relationship with other longer standing BPO customers. These relationships are intended to support access to additional end
customers and repeatable delivery opportunities in markets with significant outsourced contact centre operations.
The Company was named NICE UK Partner of the Year in July 2024 and subsequently confirmed as a Platinum partner. The Company also won
Gold at the ECCSA Awards for its work supporting vulnerable customers, including work delivered with the Alzheimer’s Society, reflecting
growing demand for compliant and inclusive customer journeys in regulated environments. During the year the Company achieved an EcoVadis
Silver Medal, improving its score by 15 points year-on-year to 72/100, supporting continued differentiation in both public and private sector
tender processes.
During the financial year 2025, 4Net Technologies Ltd continued to strengthen its position as a leading provider of cloud communications,
contact centre solutions, unified collaboration, and managed IT services for both private enterprises and public sector organisations across the
UK. Performance during the year was underpinned by strategic investment, an expanded service portfolio, and increasing demand for secure,
cloud-native and flexible technology solutions.
4Net enhanced its technical and service capabilities, particularly in cloud infrastructure, cybersecurity, and unified communications. The
acquisition of specialist expertise and the development of its Agile Cloud and collaboration platforms enabled the delivery of more integrated,
secure solutions tailored to customers’ evolving needs. The company also responded to growing demand for digitally enabled, flexible working
environments and for remote or hybrid operating models. Investments in secure cloud communications, Contact Centre as a Service (CCaaS),
and advanced collaboration tools helped clients adapt to changing working patterns. Innovation in customer experience, automation, and secure
infrastructure remained a core focus.
Financial Performance:
Revenue for the year ended June 2025 was £47.4m, representing a 8.1% increase on the prior year (£43.8m). Operating profit rose to £4.0m, up
28.3% from £3.1m, while adjusted EBITDA increased by 36.2% to £5.2m (2024: £4.0m), reflecting strong growth in contracted recurring
revenue.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

4Net Technologies Ltd continues to implement its transformation strategy, investing in technology, data and process improvement to enhance

customer experience, operational efficiency and scalability. The Company is also increasing internal adoption of Al and automation tools to

The Company is progressing a data lake programme to improve reporting and analytics and to support increased use of AI internally and for

customers. An early use case, automating service reporting, was delivered in June 2025, with further phases planned to extend data sources and

EMPLOYEES AND DIVERSITY

The Company recognises that its people are fundamental to the delivery of high-quality services and sustainable long-term performance. The

Company seeks to maintain an inclusive working environment through established policies and practices covering equal opportunities, dignity at

work and non-discrimination, supported by recruitment processes intended to attract and retain diverse talent. Employees are supported through
. structured performance and development reviews, role-based learning pathways and professional accreditation, alongside wellbeing initiatives

including access to support resources and trained mental health first aiders. During the year, the Company continued to strengthen the

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES

The directors regularly assess the key business risks of the company, which are considered to be:

Increased Competition

The markets in which the Company operates are competitive and continue to evolve, including through consolidation and new entrants. The

Company competes with large national IT services providers and global systems integrators, as well as specialist UK providers focused on cloud

communications, contact centre and CX platforms, managed networks and cybersecurity services. Competitive pressure may impact pricing,

sales cycle length and the availability and cost of delivery resources. The Company mitigates this risk by maintaining a broad and actively

governed portfolio, strengthening partner and accreditation-led capability, and focusing on service quality, customer retention and repeatable

delivery. The Company also monitors market developments and competitor positioning through regular review by the executive team and the

Board.

Technological Change

The Company’s markets are characterised by rapid technological development, changing vendor strategies and evolving customer requirements,

including increasing adoption of automation and artificial intelligence. This risk is mitigated through CTO-led portfolio governance, structured

roadmaps, architecture standards and close engagement with strategic suppliers. The Company invests in continuous skills development and

certification, and maintains a structured approach to evaluating, piloting and scaling new capabilities so that customer solutions remain current

and supported.

Key Resources

The Company's performance depends on recruiting, developing and retaining employees of an appropriate calibre, including key personne! .

within the executive team and senior delivery and sales leadership. Loss of key individuals or constraints in specialist skill areas could affect the

Company’s ability to win and deliver contracts. The Company mitigates this risk through competitive reward and incentive arrangements,

including employee shareholdings, structured training and professional development, succession planning and active engagement and wellbeing

initiatives,

Regulatory Change :
The Company operates in markets where regulatory requirements can change, including obligations relating to cybersecurity, vulnerability
management, data protection and operational resilience, as well as sector-specific requirements in regulated industries. The Company mitigates
this risk by monitoring regulatory developments, maintaining appropriate governance, policies and controls, and ensuring delivery practices and
supplier standards remain compliant. Where regulations increase the need for assurance and resilience, the Company’s compliance, cyber and
risk management capabilities also create additional market opportunity as customers seek support to meet these requirements.
Data Protection and Back-up
. The Company holds and processes confidential information and relies on the availability and integrity of its systems and data. A security

incident, service disruption or failure to comply with applicable data protection requirements could result in reputational damage, operational
disruption and financial loss. The Company mitigates this risk through established information security policies and controls, identity and access
management, monitoring and patching processes, staff awareness training, incident response procedures, and business continuity and disaster
recovery arrangements (including appropriate back-up and recovery testing). The Company also applies these controls and operating disciplines ©
in the delivery of managed security and resilience services to customers.
Credit risk
At any one time, the Company has outstanding debts due from customers. The failure of a customer to honour its debts, or a deterioration in
overdue balances, could have a material effect on cash flow and results. The Company mitigates this risk through active credit control, regular
review of customer limits and payment terms, and monitoring and periodic credit assessment of the largest exposures. The Company’s customer
base is diversified and a number of its larger customers operate in the public sector and critical national infrastructure, which reduces exposure to
insolvency risk. .
Liquidity and cash flow
The Company ensures that it has the financial wherewithal to be able to meet any changes in tumover, without placing undue pressure on the
Company’s cash resources. Cash resources at the year-end were in excess of £4,1m. The company monitors cash flow and working capital
requirements on an ongoing basis and maintains appropriate forecasting and reporting controls. :
SECTION 172(1) STATEMENT
Under section 172 of the Companies Act 2006, the directors have a duty to promote the success of the Company and the wider Group for the
benefit of the members as a whole. This includes having due regard to the broad range of stakeholders of the Company, such as its workforce,

The Company’s Code of Conduct defines general principles for ethical behaviour; it applies to all employees of the Company, all its subsidiaries

The directors meet regularly to discuss latest trading performance. Ad-hoc meetings are also held as required for specific purposes such as the
approval of annual accounts, or the approval of acquisitions.
The directors behave responsibly towards stakeholders and aim to promote the long-term success of the Company, by considering certain
stakeholder groups as noted below as being fundamental to this objective.
Investors
The Board is committed'to openly engaging with our investors, as we recognise the importance of a continuing effective dialogue, whether with
private investors or employee shareholders. It is important to us that investors understand our strategy and objectives, so these are explained
clearly, feedback is received and any issues or questions raised properly considered.
Culture and Values
The Board recognises the importance of an appropriate corporate culture. The Company’s values and behaviours are framed through “The
FourNet Way”, emphasising customer focus, working together, a can-do attitude, growth and leadership. These behaviours are embedded
through recruitment and onboarding, performance and development reviews, and recognition activities such as the 4Net Heroes awards.

3. ACCOUNTING POLICIES
The principal accounting policies applied in the preparation of these financial statements are set out below. These policies have been
consistently applied to all the years presented, unless otherwise stated.
a) Basis of preparation ,
The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis, under the historical cost convention, as modified by the recognition
of certain assets and liabilities measured at fair value.
The financial statements contain information about 4Net Technologies Limited as a single entity and do not contain consolidated financial
information as the parent of a group. The company is exempt under Section 400 of the Companies Act 2006 from the requirements to
prepare consolidated financial statements as it and its subsidiary undertaking are included by full consolidation in the consolidated financial
statements of its ultimate parent, Project Eaton Topco Limited, 3 Scholar Green Road, Stretford, Manchester, England, M32 OTR.
The preparation of financial statements requires the use of certain critical accounting estimates. It also requires management to exercise its
judgement in the process of applying the company accounting policies. The areas involving a higher degree of judgement or complexity, or
areas where assumptions and estimates are significant to the financial statements are disclosed in Note 4.
b) Going concern
The company meets its day to day working capital requirements through its bank balances. The nature of the company’s managed services
are such that payment is ordinarily required in advance of services being delivered and matched with terms received from suppliers. This
enables the company to manage working capital fluctuations between customer and supplier terms. A working capital requirement may
arise where a project installation progresses over a period of months and is invoiced on completion. In some circumstances the company
will agree payments against deliverable milestones.
At 30 June 2025 the company reported bank balances of £4.1m (2024: £2.8m) and has no borrowings other than intercompany liabilities of
£1.7m (2024: £1.2m). The company has received letters of support confirming the group companies, to whom sums are owed, do not intend
to seek repayment for a period of at least 12 months.
The company has forward visibility of revenues and costs generated by its contracted recurring revenue base. The company’s forecasts and
projections, taking into account a severe but plausible change in trading performance, show that the company should be able to operate
within the level of its current bank balances. After making enquiries, the directors have a reasonable expectation that the company has
adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. The company therefore continues to adopt the going
concem basis in preparing its financial statements.

(ii) Transactions and balances
Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency at the rate of exchange at the date of the transaction. Assets
and liabilities in foreign currency are translated into the functional currency at the rates of exchange ruling at the balance sheet date.
Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the translation at period-end exchange rates
of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are recognised in the profit and loss account.
(e) Revenue recognition
Revenue is measured at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable and represents the amount receivable for goods supplied or
services rendered, net of returns, discounts and rebates allowed by the company, value added tax and other sales taxes.
The company recognises revenue when (a) the significant risks and rewards of ownership have been transferred to the customer, (b) the
company retains no continuing involvement or control over the goods, (c) the amount of revenue can be measured reliably, (d) it is probable
that future economic benefits will flow to the company and (e) when the specific criteria to each of the company’s revenues has been met:
@ Projects
The company provides professional services including equipment delivery services to customers. Revenue relating to equipment is
recognised on delivery. Professional services fees associated with project installation are recognised as the project progresses by
reference to consumed hours as a proportion of total expected hours for the project. Project delivery costs are recognised as incurred
either as internal staff costs or 3" party professional services costs.
di) Managed & Network Services
The company provides Managed Services for delivery of its cloud platform, secure infrastructure and network services to customers.
These revenue streams are contracted over multiple years, with revenue recognition in equal instalments over the duration of the
contract as the service is delivered. This includes Network Services managed on behalf of customers comprising fixed and variable
charges for telecommunications lines and calls both cloud based and on premise.
(f) Exceptional items
The company classifies certain one-off charges or credits that have a material impact on the company’s financial results as “exceptional
items’. These are disclosed separately to provide further understanding of the financial performance of the company. The main categories of
exceptional items are:
(0) Transaction related — these are costs relating to potential acquisitions which have not proceeded
(ii) Restructuring and legal costs — relate to the costs associated with business restructuring and any associated legal cost “
_ iii) Onerous contracts — when there is no future economic benefit from a contract, a provision is made in respect of any future
committed costs which cannot be avoided or recovered.
(g) Employee benefits
The company provides a range of benefits to employees, including periodic bonus arrangements, paid holiday and defined contribution
pension plans. The company also provides a number of options to employees under salary sacrifice arrangements.
(0) Short term benefits .
Short term benefits are recognised as an expense in the period in which the service is rendered and if over multiple periods, accrued to
: ensure the full liability is accounted for by the end of the period to which service relates.
(ii) Defined contribution pension plans
The company operates a defined contribution plan to which all employees are auto-enrolled, The company pays fixed contributions
into the scheme which is managed by an FCA regulated third party. The assets of the plan are held separately from the company in
independently administered funds. Once contributions are paid the company has no further payment obligations. Contributions are
recognised as an expense in the period to which they relate, with amounts not paid shown in other creditors on the balance sheet.
(iii) Sales commissions
Sales staff can earn commission by reference to their performance in securing sales bookings. Where the commission relates to new
customers, the commission is pre-paid in the balance sheet and recognised as an expense over the contract duration.

(b) Taxation
Taxation for the year comprises current and deferred tax recognised in the reporting period. Tax is recognised in the Income Statement,
except to the extent that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity.
Current or deferred taxation assets and liabilities are not discounted.
@ Current tax
Current tax is the amount of tax payable in respect of the taxable profit for the year or prior years. Current tax is recognised at the
amount of tax payable using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.
(ii) Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance sheet date. Timing
differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from those in which they are
recognised in financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted
by the year end and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.
Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered
against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
@ Intangible assets other than goodwill ,
Intangible assets are recognised at cost and are subsequently measured at cost less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment
losses. Amortisation is calculated, using the straight-line method, to allocate the depreciable amount of the assets to their residual values
over their estimated useful lives as follows:
Software development 3 years
Amortisation is included in ‘administrative expenses’ in the profit and loss.account. The assets are reviewed for impairment if there is an
indication the carrying value may be impaired. .
Costs associated with maintaining computer software are recognised as an expense as incurred. Development costs that are directly :
attributable to the design, testing and implementation of identifiable software products or platforms controlled by the company are
recognised as intangible assets when the following criteria are met:
- It is technically feasible to complete the software so that it can be available for use.
- Management intends to complete the software product or platform and use it or sell it
- There is an ability to use or sell the software
- It can be demonstrated how the software will generate probable future economic benefits
- Adequate resources to complete the development are available
- The expenditure can be reliably measured
Other development expenditure not making these criteria is expensed as incurred.

Tangible fixed assets are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses. Cost includes the original
purchase price and costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to its working condition for its intended use.

Tangible assets are derecognised on disposal when no further economic benefits are expected. On disposal the difference between the

(0) Financial instruments
The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 'Basic Financial Instruments' of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments.
Financial instruments are recognised when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument.
Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is a legally enforceable

simultaneously.
Basic financial assets, which include work in progress, trade debtors, other debtors, amounts due from group undertakings and cash and
bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using
the effective interest method. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity
instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.
Basic financial liabilities, including trade creditors and other creditors, that are classified as debt, are initially recognised at transaction price
unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the debt instrument is measured at the present value of the future
payments discounted at a market rate of interest.
Financial liabilities are derecognised when, and only when, the company's contractual obligations are discharged, cancelled, or they expire.
4. CRITICAL ACCOUNTING JUDGEMENTS AND KEY SOURCES OF ESTIMATION UNCERTAINTY
In the application of the company's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions about the
carrying amount of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are
based on historical experience and other factors that are considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the period
in which the estimate is revised where the revision affects only that period, or in the period of the revision and future periods where the
revision affects both current and future periods.
The estimates and assumptions which have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying amount of assets and liabilities
are as follows:
Intangible Assets
The directors make a judgement in capitalising software development costs that future economic benefits will accrue from the development
expenditure. These projects are subject to an overarching ongoing review which would be expected to identify if future economic benefits
would not be generated or would cease to be of benefit, leading to the need for an impairment review.
Project Delivery
The directors make an estimate as to the total amount of professional services time which will be consumed on projects. The directors’
estimate is updated on a regular basis and is used to evaluate the proportion of project revenue to recognise as a delivery project progresses.
The estimate is subject to uncertainty with regard to circumstances, which will be customer specific, that may occur during the project
resulting in more professional services time required to complete the project than estimated. As project activity progresses the outcome is
expected to be become more certain.
