STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024
. The directors present the Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
’ Fair review of the business
Network Merchants Limited is the UK trading entity within the Network Merchants Inc. (“NMI”) group, which is
headed by NMI Topco (incorporated in the USA). The combined group is a leader in omnichannel payments
technology, joining both card-present acumen and card-not-present expertise. Together, we are able to deliver a
unified payment solution to ISOs, ISVs and VARs servicing merchants in omnichannel environments.
The NMI group has been at the forefront of the technical innovations enabling and driving this migration of
payment methods and is well placed to power the next era of payments as merchants and consumers look for a
simplified, secure, and holistic card payment solution in an increasingly complex environment.
The upgrades to the gateway infrastructure have continued. The combination of industry-mandated increasing
levels of security combined with cost reducing the infrastructure using the latest virtualisation techniques has .-
provided Network Merchants Limited with one of the most technically advanced gateways in the world. This will
allow us to at least triple transaction volumes with minimal or no requirement to upgrade our infrastructure.
Transaction volumes rose from 788 million in 2023 to 876 million in 2024 and the greater transaction volume in
the year facilitated an increase in revenue from £19.0m to £20.6m, a rise of 9%. This translated into an increase
in profit for the financial year due to good cost management control with cost of sales and admin expenses both
falling year on year, amongst other items.
The cash position has remained strong over the year as a result of good cash collection processes translating
the profit to cash inflows and the group has transferred excess cash to other entities in the NMI group to ensure
optimal resource allocation. The strong performance of the group and the aforementioned changes in working
capital resulted in cash moving from £7.2m to £5.1m while overall net assets rose from £10.4m to £16.5m.

Risks and uncertainties
The principal risks within the company relate to credit risk, business continuity and current economic conditions.
The directors recognise this and wherever possible a payment in advance model is adopted. Where customers
are post billed this is monitored and services may be suspended where payment is overdue. This encourages
payment within terms.
The board recognises that a major outage on its processing payment platform could disrupt income flow and
continuity of service to customers. To mitigate this risk the company operates four processing nodes. One is
sited in the UK, one in the Netherlands and two in North America with each node replicating the data from all the
other nodes in real time. Each location has independent power services and fully diverse communications
routings. Any one node has sufficient capacity to absorb the traffic from the other three nodes.
The current UK and wider global economic environments are such that customers and their merchants and end
consumers are suffering from rising costs which could impact the volume of transactions processed or the
revenue that the company is able to make from these transactions. The directors note that a significant portion of
the company's revenue is recurring, and the company can leverage its gateway’s reliability in new and existing
relationships to ensure customer retention and new customer growth continues despite challenging market
conditions.
We acknowledge the uncertainties inherent in forecasting future sales growth, and the risk remains that such
growth targets are not achieved, which could impact on the company’s ability to meet its future obligations and
could impact the carrying value of some of the company’s assets. This risk is managed by the regular review and
update of sales forecasts and managing the company’s cost base required to service its revenue streams.

Financial risk management
In order to finance the company’s operations, the company utilises financial instruments that arise from its

. operations such as trade debtors and creditors, alongside cash and intercompany debtors and creditors. These
financial instruments expose the company to financial risks, of which the significant risks are described in more
detail below. The directors review and agree policies for managing these risks, which remain unchanged from :
previous years. The directors note that due to the nature of the company’s financial instruments it is not

. significantly exposed to price risk. .
Credit risk ;
The company is paid in advance where possible but there are a number of customers that pay in arrears. The
company also has significant intercompany debtors and the combination of these two leaves it exposed to credit
tisk. To mitigate this risk, customers must pass credit checks before services are provided and failure to pay
within agreed terms is a breach of contract which can result in the cessation of services crucial to the customer's
operations.
Credit risk related to intercompany balances is actively monitored by the directors. Credit risk associated with
cash is minimal due to the high credit ratings of the institutions used by the company. :
Currency risk . :
The company undertakes a variety of transactions in overseas currencies and seeks to mitigate the financial risk
of such transactions by operating bank accounts in these overseas currencies. However, the company remains
partly exposed to adverse exchange rate fluctuations.
Cash flow risk
The directors prepare detailed cash flow forecasts which indicate the future expected incomings and outgoings
of cash, and that any potential shortfalls are identified in advance with appropriate steps taken to ensure that
suitable facilities are agreed ahead of when they are forecast to be required in order for the company to continue
its operations.

_ 2.3 Going concern :
Given the inter-dependencies of all entities within the Creditcall Holdings Limited group (‘group’), the
directors consider their assessment of going concern on the group’s forecast cash flows to assess
going concern for the group, parent company and all subsidiaries. Management considers the
group’s cash headroom and transfers funds between group companies as is required to meet
obligations as they fall due, and has formal support letters in place with subsidiaries to support their
own going concern assumptions.
In order to assess the going concern assumption, the directors have considered detailed trading and
cash flow projections, taking into account the impact of current macro-economic factors such as
inflation, the cost of living crisis and the potential impact of US tariffs, and the uncertainty this
presents in drawing their conclusions.
The group has remained resilient over recent years with revenues growing and the business was not
notably affected by increased inflation and the cost of living crisis due to the variety of operational
costs incurred. The group has continued to grow revenue and be cash generating throughout the
year and beyond also showing a profit for the year.
The group has the full financial support of the wider group headed by its ultimate parent and has
received a formal confirmation of this support by NMI Intermediate Inc., an intermediate parent within
the wider group, being the highest level that consolidated financial statements are prepared and
audited. The formal confirmation of support confirms its intentions to provide continued financial
support to the group so that it can meet its liabilities as they fall due for a period of at least 12 months
from the date of approval of these financial statements, and that intercompany balances won't be
recalled unless the group is able to afford such repayments.
The directors are confident that the steps that they have taken to mitigate associated risk will ensure
that the group is well positioned to continue to trade throughout the current uncertain economic
environment. They have therefore concluded that there is no material uncertainty about the ability of
the group to continue as a going concern and that it remains appropriate to prepare the financial
statements on a going concern basis.
Furthermore, based on the above, and given the formal support letters in place to subsidiaries within
the group, the directors have also concluded that there is no material uncertainty about the ability of
the company to continue as a going concern and that it remains appropriate to prepare the financial
statements on a going concern basis.

Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include trade and other debtors, cash and bank balances, are initially
measured at their transaction price (adjusted for transaction costs except in the initial measurement
of financial assets that are subsequently measured at fair value through profit and loss) and are
subsequently carried at their amortised cost using the effective interest method, less any provision for
impairment, unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is
. measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.
Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial. The company's cash and cash
equivalents, trade and most other debtors due within the operating cycle fall into this category of
financial instruments.
Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference
between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the
asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the
estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The
impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of
the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the
impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying
amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in the profit or loss.
Financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in
the assets of the company after the deduction of all its liabilities.
Basic financial liabilities, which include trade and other creditors, bank loans and other loans are
. initially measured at their transaction price (adjusting for transaction costs except in the initial
measurement of financial liabilities that are subsequently measured at fair value through profit and
loss). When this constitutes a financing transaction, whereby the debt instrument is measured at the
present value of the future payments discounted at a market rate of interest, discounting is omitted
where the effect of discounting is immaterial.
Debt instruments are subsequently carried at their amortised cost using the effective interest rate
method.
Trade creditors are obligations to pay for goods and services that have been acquired in the ordinary
course of business from suppliers. Trade creditors are classified as current liabilities if the payment is
due within one year. If not, they represent non-current liabilities. Trade creditors .are initially
: recognised at their transaction price and subsequently are measured at amortised cost using the
effective interest method. Discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial.
