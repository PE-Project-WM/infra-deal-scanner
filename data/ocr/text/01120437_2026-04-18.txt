Introduction

The directors present their Strategic Report for the 52 week period ended 27 September 2024.

Business review

The Company designs, engineers, constructs and acts as Project Manager and Consultant principally in the nuclear and

conventional power generation industries together with defence, process and environmental engineering sectors.

The loss after taxation for the period amounts to £222k (2023 profit: £8,772k). The loss in the year is due to onerous

contracts booked in the year. The Company paid a dividend during the period of £25k to its new immediate parent

undertaking Energy, Security and Technology UK Limited (2023: £20m paid to former owning parent Jacobs UK Limited).

Jacobs Group (“Jacobs”) completed a strategic restructuring this year, involving the spin-off and merger of its Critical Mission

Solutions (CMS) and Cyber & Intelligence government services businesses with Amentum. This resulted in the creation of a

new independent, publicly traded company.

The Company undertook a range of internal group restructuring transactions during the year, including the change in the

ultimate parent ownership of the Company which are detailed in the relevant notes and disclosures in the financial

statements.

The main transaction in the internal group restructuring involving the Company was the acquisition by the Company of

Jacobs U.K. Limited's CMS business, certain other transactions have been deemed immaterial and thus are not individually

disclosed within the financial statements.

The Company continued to build on its relationships with key customers and pursue a partnership approach to working. The

Company built on its strong and established resource base to further develop its capabilities and product offering and

continued to focus on the key strategic areas of reactor support, new build and clean up. Turnover for the period was strong

at £554,399k (2023: £303,675k) and included six months in respect of the business activities transferred from Jacobs UK

Ltd.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The main operational risks of the business include health and safety and failure to meet client expectations. The :

management of health and safety risk includes the use of a Health and Safety Manual, risk assessments and internal audits.

The other operational risks are managed by ensuring all project managers are suitably qualified and experienced to perform

their current role plus all significant contracts undergo regular reviews.

The Company's commercial risks include profitable delivery of technically complex projects and tower than forecast

work volumes due to volatility of public and private sector spending programmes. All potential new businesses undergo

both a comprehensive tender review and profit study before being tendered for.

Other risks include compliance with laws and regulations and potential changes in the regulatory environment.

Financial risk management

The Company's operations expose it to financial risks. These are primarily foreign currency transaction risks and credit

risk.

Foreign currency translation risks

A proportion of the Company's trading income is denominated in the local currency of the business operations, which

provides a natural hedge against the currency of its cost base.

Credit risk

The Company is exposed to credit risk in relation to customers. The credit risk associated with customers is considered as

part of the tender review process and is addressed initially via contract payment terms, and, where appropriate,

payment security. Credit control practices are applied thereafter during the project execution phase. A right to interest and

suspension is normally sought in all contracts. ,

Corporate Governance Arrangements
The Company is a large private company for the purposes of the Companies (Miscellaneous Reporting) Regulations 2018
and is therefore required to disclose its corporate governance arrangements for the 52 week period ending 27 September
2024. The Company has adopted the Wates Corporate Governance Principles for large private companies (“the Wates
Principles”) as its corporate governance framework.
The Company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Amentum Holdings, Inc, a company listed on the New York Stock Exchange,
and as such is subject to the group governance framework established by its ultimate parent. The Board considers that this
group framework is consistent with the spirit and intent of the Wates Principles and supports the long-term sustainable
success of the Company.
The Board has reviewed the application of the Wates Principles during the year and considers that the Company has
applied the principles in a manner appropriate to its size, ownership structure and role within the wider group. The key
aspects of how the principles have been applied are summarised below.
Board Composition, Responsibilities and Remuneration
The Board comprises 3 directors, all of whom are senior leaders within the Group with relevant experience. As a wholly
owned subsidiary, the Company does not have independent non-executive directors. The Board considers its composition
appropriate for the Company's current scale and complexity and has access to independent challenge through Group .
governance, assurance and external advisers where required.
The responsibilities of the Board are clearly defined and include oversight of strategy, risk, internal controls, financial
reporting and compliance. Directors are aware of their statutory duties under the Companies Act 2006 and are supported
by Group policies, defegated authorities and reporting frameworks.
The Company does not operate a standalone remuneration committee. Directors’ and senior management remuneration is
determined in accordance with Group remuneration policies, which are designed to align reward with performance, risk
management and long-term value creation.

Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting (SECR) ‘
Introduction ‘ :
The below statement holds Amentum Clean Energy Limited (previously Jacobs Clean Energy Limited) annual energy
consumption, associated relevant greenhouse gas emissions, and additional related information, as required under the
Companies (Director’s Report) and Limited Liability Partnerships (Energy and Carbon Report) Regulations 2018.
Methodology
The method applied to the calculations of Greenhouse Gas emissions is the ‘GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and
Reporting Standard’. An ‘operational control’ boundary has been applied. Carbon conversion factors have been taken from
‘UK Government GHG Conversion Factors for Company Reporting — 2024’. Emissions are reported as COze. Electricity
emissions have been reported as both ‘location’ and ‘market-based’. .
We continue to ensure that all electricity that we are responsible for procuring is supplied from low carbon sources. Similarly,
all gas supplies for which we are responsible have been procured on carbon neutral contracts. The ongoing certification of
our Energy Management System to ISO 50001 continues to drive energy reduction savings throughout the UK.
Energy Use and Greenhouse Gas Emissions
The table below shows the total annual UK energy use, apportioned to Amentum Clean Energy Ltd based on the percentage
of its total UK headcount. Energy use is associated with the consumption of; electricity, natural gas and other fuels
combusted on-site, and fuel consumed for relevant business transport purposes, for the period 1°* October 2023 — 30%
September 2024, and comparison with 2023. FY24 figures were taken from Amentum’s Global Carbon Inventory (formerly
Jacobs Global Carbon Inventory).
The significant increase in energy emissions between FY23 and FY24 is due to a change in methodology used to collate and
apportion emissions to each legal entity. Prior to FY24, all UK geographical emissions were combined and apportioned to
each legal entity by headcount percentage. Both the emissions and headcount held Jacobs and data now presented as
Amentum Clean Energy Ltd data.
For FY24, all activity data points were appropriately assigned to either Jacobs or Amentum (legacy Jacobs) to determine UK
geographical emissions for both. Only Amentum UK (legacy Jacobs) geographical emissions were apportioned to Amentum
legal entities (legacy Jacobs) using headcount percentage. Both emissions & headcount contain Amentum (legacy Jacobs)
data only.
Comparatively, Amentum (legacy Jacobs) has a much bigger fleet, number of labs and engineering workshops, more sites,
more gas intensive sites and more business travel on average than Jacobs, thus, more emissions. Additionally, Jacobs UK
Limited had the highest headcount percentage which masked the emissions apportioned to the other legal entities prior to
FY24.

1.4 Revenue

Under IFRS 15 multiple service contracts where the Company is responsible for providing a single deliverable
are treated as a single performance obligation for the purposes of revenue recognition. Typically, the
performance obligation is satisfied upon completion of service when the Company is contracted to perform
both engineering and construction of a project.
In general, the Company recognises revenues using the percentage-of-completion method, based primarily on
contract costs incurred to date compared to total estimated contract costs. Estimated contract costs include
the Company’s latest estimates using judgements with respect to labour hours, costs, materials, and
subcontract costs. The percentage-of-completion method is the most representative depiction of the
Company's performance as it directly measures the value of services transferred to the client. The Company
provides for contract losses in their entirety in the period they become known without regard to the percentage
of completion.
Estimated standalone selling prices are allocated to services in line with the policy for transaction prices. The
transaction price does include an allocation of amounts of variable consideration. The transaction price is
allocated to the single performance obligation within the contract based on agreed budget with the customer.
Revenue is then recognised over time using the percentage-of-completion method
The amounts recoverable on contracts included in debtors represent the costs incurred plus
recognised profits, less provision for recognised losses and progress billings. Progress billings that
have not been settled by customers (including retentions related to contracts in progress) are
included in debtors where they are stated after allowance for any doubtful debts.
Payments on account in excess of the amounts recoverable under contracts are included in
creditors and represent payments on account received from customers in excess of the amounts
recoverable on contracts and advances. Advances are amounts received by the customer before the
related work is performed.

1.6 Leases
The Company as a lessee.
The Company assesses whether a contract is or contains a lease, at inception of a contract. The
Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a corresponding lease liability with respect to all lease
agreements in which it is the lessee, except for short-term leases (defined as leases with a lease

The lease liability is initially measured at the present value of the lease payments that are not paid at
the commencement date, discounted by using the rate implicit in the lease. If this rate cannot be
readily determined, the Company uses its incremental borrowing rate.
Lease payments included in the measurement of the lease liability comprise:
¢ fixed lease payments (including in-substance fixed payments), less any lease incentives.
The lease liability is included in ‘Creditors’ on the Balance Sheet.
The lease liability is subsequently measured by increasing the carrying amount to reflect interest on
the lease liability (using the effective interest method) and by reducing the carrying amount to reflect
the lease payments made.
The Company remeasures the lease liability (and makes a corresponding adjustment to the related
right-of-use asset) whenever:
e the lease terms have changed or there is a change in the assessment of exercise of a purchase
option, in which case the lease liability is remeasured by discounting the revised discount rate.
e the lease payments change due to changes in an index or rate or a change in expected payment under
a guaranteed residual value, in which cases the lease liability is remeasured
by discounting the revised lease payments using the initial discount rate (unless the lease
payments change is due to a change in a floating interest rate, in which case a revised discount rate
is used).

‘ 1.15 Provisions for liabilities :
Provisions are recognised when the Company has a present obligation (legal or constructive) as a
result of a past event; it is probable that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will
be réquired to settle the obligation; and a reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the
obligation.
Onerous contracts
If the Group has a contract that is onerous, the present obligation under the contract is recognised and
measured as a provision. However, before a separate provision for an onerous contract is established, the
Group recognises any impairment loss that has occurred on assets dedicated to that contract.
An onerous contract is a contract under which the unavoidable costs (i.e., the costs that the Group cannot avoid
because it has the contract) of meeting the obligations under the contract exceed the economic benefits
expected to be received under it. The unavoidable costs under a contract reflect the least net cost of exiting
from the contract, which is the lower of the cost of fulfilling it and any compensation or penalties arising from
failure to fulfil it. The cost of fulfilling a contract comprises the costs that relate directly to the contract {i-e.,
both incremental costs and an allocation of costs directly related to contract activities).

2. Judgments in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
The Directors believe the most significant areas of judgement and estimation arise from:
Revenue recognition and long term contracts .
A significant amount of the Company's activities is undertaken via long-term contracts. Management
bases its judgements of contract costs and revenues on the latest available information, which includes
detailed contract valuations. In many cases the results reflect the expected outcome of long-term
contractual obligations which span more than one reporting year. Contract costs and revenues are
. affected by a variety of uncertainties that depend on the outcome of future events and often need to be
revised as events unfold and uncertainties are resolved. The estimates of contract costs and revenues
are updated regularly and significant changes are highlighted through established internal review
procedures. In particular, the internal reviews focus on the timing and recognition of incentive payments
and the age and recoverability of any unagreed income from variations to the contract scope or claims.
The impact of the changes in accounting estimates is then reflected in the ongoing results.
Principally, there are two types of long-term contracts being cost reimbursable contracts and fixed price
contracts. Due to the nature of these contracts the significant estimates tend to arise on fixed price
contracts rather than cost reimbursable contracts.
