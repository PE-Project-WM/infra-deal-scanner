The Directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.
Principal activities and review of the business

u The principal activity of Boldyn Networks Global Limited (the "Parent Company", the "Parent" or the
"Company") and its subsidiaries (collectively, "Boldyn Networks", “Boldyn Group" or the "Group") for
the year ended 30 June 2025 is developing, constructing, and providing telecommunication services via
neutral host digital infrastructure solutions including small cells and towers, distributed antenna systems
(WAS), private and public Wi-Fi, fibre and mobile private networks. Our primary customers include
mobile network operators (MNOs), transit authorities, governmental authorities, venue owners, higher
education institutions, and other customers in the United Kingdom (UK), United States of America (USA),
Ireland, Italy, Hong Kong, Finland, Spain, France and Germany.
As at 30 June 2025, the ultimate parent and controlling party of Boldyn Networks was Canada Pension
Plan Investment Board ("CPPIB"), which owned 86.8860% of the Non-Director Voting Shares. PIP7
(AABA) Ltd owned 10.0784% of the Non-Director Voting Shares is managed by Alberta Investment
Management Corporation (AIMCo), and Pioneer Communications GP Inc owned 3.0356% of the Non-
Director Voting Shares. The owners of the Non-Director Voting Shares are collectively referred to as the
"Shareholders".
Significant transactions noted during the year were as follows:

* On 9 July 2024, Boldyn Networks PNE Holdings UK Limited entered into a £185 million
financing agreement for 7 years. The financing agreement consists of a £135 million term loan
and £50 million revolving credit facility.

¢ On 30 October 2024, Infrastructure Wireless Italiane S.p.A. purchased 52.08% of Boldyn
Networks Smart City Roma S.p.A.

* On 28 January 2025, the Boldyn Networks Holdings US Finance LLC entered into new financing
agreements consisting of Senior Secured Credit Facilities and Fixed Rate Notes of approximately
$1.2 billion with a maturity range from three to seven years. A portion of the proceeds from the
new financing agreements were used to repay existing credit facilities, which were also
terminated.

* On 31 January 2025, the Group purchased 100% of the share capital of Smart Mobile Labs AG ©
("SML"), a Germany based telecommunications infrastructure provider. SML offers end-to-end
individualised private 5G network services, including development, implementation and
operation, ensuring high-quality wireless transmission for video, voice, data and audio-visual
applications. : :

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
The Directors consider the principal risks and uncertainties for the Group to be as follows:

* Market environment
Changes to the economic conditions of our customers and the markets in which the Group operates may
impact our business. The Group regularly monitors industry and customer developments. The Group also
continuously engages with its customers to understand their needs and constraints.

+ Technology
The Group is a provider of neutral host digital infrastructure. New or emerging technology including
artificial intelligence (AI) could have an impact on the demand from our customers and the types of digital
infrastructure required. The Group monitors trends in technology including AI, and the impact on our
products and services.

+ Key network build programs
The Group has commenced significant network build programs and therefore potential failure to complete
these programs within the agreed timeframes or budgets poses financial and reputational risk to the Group.
The Group mitigates this risk by ensuring projects are appropriately planned, resourced and coordinated
with external parties and key stakeholders, supported by the Major Capital Projects Committee, which
provides executive oversight over key projects. The status of major projects including budgets, is reviewed
regularly and the Group shares lessons between projects.

¢ Cyber threats and information security
Cyber threats and information security breaches could lead to theft, loss, misappropriation of critical assets

: and loss of confidential information which could significantly impact the Group's business operations.

Additionally, this could result in financial losses and reputational damage. The Group mitigates these risks
through programs and governance processes including alignment to security frameworks such as
1S027001, which was in place for the majority of the Group, regular audits and assessments, vulnerability
and resiliency management, security training and awareness campaigns,

¢ Group transformation
The recent formation of Boldyn Networks, including bringing together six portfolio companies, recent
acquisitions and establishment of a new head office in the UK over the past 36 months, represents a
significant amount of change for Boldyn Networks. The number of activities and degree of change could
impact the business resulting in issues in performance, processes and systems. The Group manages this
transformation by ensuring project plans are established and in place, they are supported with the
appropriate internal and external expertise and resources, and processes and systems are established for the
current and future needs of the business.

* People
Our people are critical to the success of our business. Changes or issues in our culture, engagement, skills,
capabilities, organisation structure, or diversity of employees could impact the performance of the business
and our reputation. The Group manages this risk by measuring employee engagement, taking action on
feedback received, establishment of the Global Inclusion, Diversity, Equity & Action (IDEA) Council to
ensure Boldyn Networks is developing a strategy, tracking our progress, championing initiatives for an
inclusive and diverse workforce, and having talent development programs and plans.

° Health and safety
Harm to our employees, contractors and/or the public because of our actions and operations can impact our
reputation. The Group has health and safety management systems in place including policies, safety plans,
incident reporting and regular monitoring.

« Governance and compliance
Non-compliance with laws and regulations may result in financial penalties, have a negative impact on our
ability to operate effectively and cause reputational damage. The Group manages this risk by having
compliance policies in place such as an employee code of conduct, financial escalation authority policy, a
whistleblower policy and hotline, gift reporting, and an Ethical Conduct and Integrity (anti-bribery and
corruption) policy. A global fraud risk register has been established that supports compliance to the
Economic Crime and Corporate Transparent Act 2023, which came into effect on the Ist of September
2025.

Section 172 Statement
The Directors are aware of their responsibilities under Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 and ensure
that their responsibilities under Section 172 are fulfilled in the course of their work. The Directors of
Boldyn Networks consider, both individually and together, that they have acted in the way they consider,
in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the Group for the benefit of its members as a
whole, having regard to the stakeholders and matters set out in Section 172(1)(a-f) of the Act, in the
decisions taken in the period to 30 June 2025.
Section 172 requires that “a director of a company must act in the way he considers, in good faith, would
be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, and in
doing so have regard (amongst other matters) to:

(a) the likely consequences of any decision in the long term;

(b) the interests of the company's employees;

(c) the need to foster the company's business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;

(d) the impact of the company's operations on the community and the environment;

{e) the desirability of the company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business

conduct; and

(f) the need to act fairly as between members of the company”.
Our Section 172 statement summarises how the Board has factored these considerations into our decision-
making.
Key decisions are taken by the Directors either in Directors’ meetings or through written resolution. In
promoting the success of the Group for the benefit of its shareholders, Directors take into consideration the
likely consequences of any decision in the long-term, and the interests of various stakeholders. The
competing needs of the various stakeholders of Boldyn Networks are monitored and reviewed by
management, at monthly Board-level meetings and at quarterly Audit and Risk Committee meetings.
The Directors have identified the key stakeholders of the Group and these are outlined further below.

Boldyn Networks is a subsidiary of Canada Pension Plan Investment Board (CPPIB). The Directors have

Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting (SECR) :
The following disclosure is for the purpose of compliance with the Streamlined Energy and Carbon
Reporting (SECR) regulations. The information presented has been prepared in compliance with the SECR
Framework, as implemented in the Companies Regulations 2018, and does not include data relating to
additional voluntary reporting. The Group has taken the exemption available for excluding overseas
subsidiaries.
The Group’s operations that are based in the UK used a total of 6,993,003 kWh (2024: 2,131,745 kWh) of
energy, generating carbon emissions of 1,333 tCO2e (2024: 444 tCO2e) during the year. The Key
Performance Indicator of emissions per full time equivalent employee for the year was 4.68 tCO2e / FTE
(2024: 1.75 tCO2e / FTE).
The data has been prepared using the GHG Reporting Protocol — Corporate Standard methodology, taking
best available data and estimates where required. The reporting meets the minimum requirements for
SECR, and no voluntary emission sources are stated.
The electricity consumption is based on actual readings for all occupied sites except for our Sunderland
office which has been estimated based on the office space leased floor area. The landiord at this site does
not have tenant metering fitted and, therefore, actual consumption data is not available.
It is also a requirement under SECR to state any energy efficiency actions that were undertaken in the
reporting period. During the year, the following actions were taken to promote energy efficiency:

+ Review of and progression on recommendations from a UK-dedicated Scope 3 emission pilot
survey, which included onboarding a holistic carbon accountancy tool for emission tracking and
reporting globally;

+ Reporting on Scope | and 2 emissions from operational sites and offices to monitor and identify
opportunities of improvement;

+ Colocation of base station hotels in data centres where the operator uses certified renewable
energy supplies and using lower impact fuels for self-built base station hotel back-up generators,
e.g. hydro-treated vegetable oil;

* Working with key subcontractors who have a clear commitment to carbon reduction, minimising
waste from construction and aiming to work with subcontractors who have ISO14001 certified
management systems when possible;

+ Promoting hybrid working to reduce employee commuting and office space requirement and use
of energy-efficient office facilities; and

* Continuation of the Group's collaboration with a sustainability consultant to advance the
development of a UK-focused carbon reduction strategy.

1.4.2 Business combinations and goodwill
Business combinations are accounted for using the acquisition method as at the acquisition date, which is

The cost of an acquisition is measured as the aggregate of the consideration transferred, which is measured

Any contingent consideration to be transferred by the acquirer will be recognised at fair value at the

settlement is accounted for within equity. Contingent consideration classified as an asset or liability that is
a financial instrument is measured at fair value with the changes in fair value recognised in the
consolidated statement of profit or loss in accordance with the Group’s accounting policy on financial
liabilities.
Goodwill is measured at the acquisition date as the fair value of consideration transferred, plus non-
controlling interests and the fair value of any previously held equity interests less the net recognised
amount (which is generally fair value) of the identifiable assets and liabilities assumed. Goodwill is
subject to an annual review for impairment (or more frequently if necessary) in accordance with the
Group’s accounting policies.
Goodwill is from the acquisition date, allocated to each of the Group’s cash-generating units (CGUs) that
are expected to benefit from the acquisition, irrespective of whether other assets or liabilities of the
acquiree are assigned to those units. Where goodwill has been allocated to a CGU and part of the operation
within that unit is disposed of, the goodwill associated with the disposed operation is included in the
carrying amount of the operation when determining the gain or loss on disposal. Goodwill disposed in
these circumstances is measured based on the relative values of the disposed operation and the portion of
the CGU retained.
Non-controlling interests are valued based on the proportion of net assets of the acquired company at the
date of acquisition. Acquisition-related costs are expensed as incurred and are included within other
expenses in the consolidated statement of profit and loss.
Changes in ownership that do not result in a change of control are accounted for as equity transactions and
therefore do not have any impact on goodwill. The difference between consideration and the non-
controlling share of net assets acquired is recognised within equity.
Common control transactions are accounted for by the Group using the pooling of interest methods which

* The assets and liabilities of the combining parties are reflected at their carrying amounts. No

* No new goodwill is recognised as a result of a common control transaction, only pre-existing
goodwill of the combining entities as at the date of acquisition is recognised.

1.4.4 Fair value measurement
The Group measures financial instruments such as derivatives, at fair value.
Fair value is the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly
transaction between market participants at the measurement date. The fair value measurement is based on
the presumption that the transaction to sell the asset or transfer the liability takes place either:
+ Inthe principal market for the asset or liability; or
+ In the absence of a principal market, in the most advantageous market for the asset or liability.
All assets and liabilities for which fair value is measured or disclosed in the consolidated financial
statements are categorised within the fair value hierarchy, described as follows, based on the lowest level
input that is significant to the fair value measurement as a whole:
+ Level 1: quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical assets or liabilities.
+ Level 2: inputs other than quoted prices included in Level | that are observable for the asset or
liability, either directly (i.e. as prices) or indirectly (i.e. derived from prices).
+ Level 3: inputs for the asset or liability. that are not based on observable market data
(unobservable inputs).
For the purpose of fair value disclosures, the Group has determined classes of assets and liabilities on the
basis of the nature, characteristics and risks of the asset or liability and the level of the fair value hierarchy,
as explained above.
If the inputs used to measure the fair value of an asset or a liability fall into different levels of the fair
value hierarchy, then the fair value measurement is categorised in its entirety in the same level of the fair
value hierarchy as the lowest level input that is significant to the entire measurement.

Intangible assets with indefinite useful lives are not amortised, but are tested for impairment annually,
either individually or at the cash-generating unit level. The assessment of indefinite life is reviewed
annually to determine whether the indefinite life continues to be supportable. If not, the change in useful
life from indefinite to finite is made on a prospective basis.
