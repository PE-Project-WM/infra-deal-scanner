Strategic Report
For the 15-month period ended 31 March 2025
The directors present their strategic report on UK Broadband Limited ("UKB” or the “Company”) for the 15-month period
ended 31 March 2025. An extended accounting reference 15-month period is presented to the new accounting
reference date. Subsequent periods will end on the same day and month in future years. The amounts presented are
therefore not entirely comparable with those of the previous.
a) Review of business and future developments
The Company is an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of CK Hutchison Holdings Limited (“CKHH”), a limited liability
company incorporated in the Cayman Islands, registered and listed in Hong Kong.
Trading overview
The Company trades under the name “Three Broadband”, rebranded from “Relish” in April 2019. Its principal activities
are the provision of wireless broadband internet access services in the United Kingdom (the “UK”), the provision of
consultancy services in relation to designing, implementing and operating private wireless broadband networks, and
provision of wireless solutions including design and equipment supply. In addition, the Company holds spectrum
licenses and operates network infrastructure on behalf of Hutchison 3G UK Limited (“Three”), the Company's immediate
parent undertaking, with associated spectrum license fees and network operating costs being recharged on a usage
basis. This represents the Company's primary economic activity by value.
The net liabilities of the Company were £3.7 million (2023: £2.9 million). The operating profit in the 15-month period was
£0.2 million (2023: operating loss of £6.2 million). The Company made a profit in the 15-month period mainly because
of an increase in revenue and decrease in administrative expenses.

. The Company had an operating profit of £0.2 million for the 15-month period ended 31 March 2025, compared to £6.2m
loss in the prior year to 31 December 2023. Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (“EBITDA”) was
a profit of £2.0 million for the 15-month period (2023: £3.5 million loss), the increase in EBITDA being attributable to a
decrease in administrative expenses and an increase in revenue. Depreciation decreased resulting from fewer
depreciable assets being held in the 15-month period. Revenue has increased in the 15-month period to £2.1 million
(2023: £0.6 million), being 269% higher than in 2023. This was principally due to an increase in customer contract value
resulting from a contract renewal during the 15-month period.

The Company's operations are transitioning following changes in the customer base, including the conclusion of certain
customer arrangements. The Company will continue to deliver its contracted support for Three using existing UKB
assets and vendors and will fulfil any remaining customer obligations under existing agreements.
The Company and Three have an intercompany arrangement where Three is compensating the Company
predominantly for use of the Company’s 3.4Ghz and 3.6Ghz spectrum holding and active network assets.
As at 31 May 2025, CK Hutchison Group Telecom Holdings Limited (‘*CKHGTH’), the Company's intermediate holding
entity, and Vodafone Group Plc (“Vodafone”) successfully completed the transaction to merge their UK
telecommunications operations (the “Transaction’). The newly combined entity, VodafoneThree Holdings Limited
(‘VodafoneThree”) is jointly owned, with Vodafone International Operations Limited, owning 51% and Brilliant Design
(BVI) Limited 49%. This strategic combination is expected to materially enhance the Company’s financial performance
through improved customer experience and operational efficiencies. Additionally, binding commitments on capital
investment and market price protections, as agreed with regulatory authorities, are anticipated to support long-term
value creation.

b) Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of the business and the execution of the Company's strategy are subject to a number of business
risks. The Company faces risk of network failure which could lead to loss of customers. The risk is mitigated by the
enhancement of network resilience and constant monitoring of network performance. The Company also faces market
competition which could lead to reduced market penetration and price reduction. The Company manages this risk by
adopting a customer focused approach in branding, marketing and customer service activities.
The Company has a series of projects underway within the organisation to ensure that it meets the obligations of the
Telecoms Security Act as set out in the Act and the Code of Practice. The program spans activity from 2023 through to
2028 when the final compliance deadline is set. Funding for any necessary activity is assessed and planned over the
lifetime of the program, with budget requests made/to be made for identified activity planned in each financial period.
The Designated Vendor Direction for Huawei came into legal effect on 13 October 2022. In summary, compliance
requirements started from 14 April 2023 with subsequent dates phased for different Huawei components. Relevant
requirements for Three and the Company:

e All equipment is compliant with the Government Risk Mitigation Strategy from 14 April 2023.

e All Huawei equipment is under support arrangements from 14 April 2023.

* Cap on the use of Huawei equipment of 35% in PCPs’ 5G access networks, achieved by 31 July 2023.

e Cap of 35% of forecasted network traffic passing through Huawei equipment in PCPs’ 5G access networks, to

be measured over a year starting from 31 July 2023, achieved by early June 2024.
« No use of Huawei equipment in PCPs’ Core Network, achieved by 31 December 2023. ;
e No use of Huawei equipment in high data rate intra-core and inter-operator transmission equipment, to be
achieved by 31 December 2025.

« No Huawei equipment in 5G network, to be achieved by 31 December 2027.
Three and the Company have met compliance obligations that have passed and have plans to meet compliance
obligations set out above.
The Company has engaged a second supplier, Ericsson, in respect of its 5G network to ensure full compliance with the
restrictions. The contract with Ericsson is an umbrella contract enabling accommodations for any future scope of work.
The agreement with Huawei has already been amended in December 2020 to comply with these regulations. The
contract with Ericsson is designed to coexist with the amended Huawei contract, if required to. The Company does not
consider any of its other suppliers to pose a similar concern.
In 2022, the UK government imposed sanctions on dealing with certain entities and individuals connected to Russia.
The Company continues to be in compliance with these regulations. Critical supplier relationships continue to be
managed by Three while the Company's broadband offering has no material impact of these sanctions.

c) Financial risk management
During the 15-month period, the Company was funded by Three and CKHGTH, the Company’s intermediate holding
entity. Funding is used for the Company's operations and acquisitions of fixed assets. Such funding is non-interest
bearing and it is repayable on demand. CKHGTH and Vodafone will continue to provide financial support for at least 12
months following the approval of these financial statements. ‘
During the periods ended 31 December 2023 and 31 March 2025, the Company has not entered into any derivative
transactions. It is also the Company's policy not to invest liquidity in financial products, including hedge funds or similar
vehicles, with significant underlying leverage or derivative exposure.
Price risk and currency risk
The Company is primarily exposed to price and currency risk on the purchase of capital assets as well as on the amounts
owed to group undertakings. Intercompany funding in foreign currency is not subject to forward exchange contracts and
therefore is not a risk exposure.
Credit risk
Financial instruments which potentially subject the Company to concentration of credit risk with a specific counterparty
consist principally of cash and debtors. Management believes the concentration of credit risk associated with the
Company's cash is mitigated by the fact that these amounts are placed in, what management believes, to be high quality
financial institutions.
The Company is exposed to its customers defaulting on the payment of their debts. The Company mitigates this risk by
credit assessment. Concentration of credit risk with respect to trade debtors is considered limited.
Liquidity risk
The Company has obtained financing from Three in order to meet its funding requirements. Ultimately, CKHGTH and
Vodafone will continue to provide financial support for at least 12 months following the approval of these financial
statements.
d) Statement by the Directors in performance of their statutory duties in accordance with Section 172 of the

Companies Act 2006
Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 requires a director of a company to act in the way he or she considers, in good
faith, would most likely promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members. In doing this, Section 172
requires directors to have regard to, amongst other matters:

e the likely consequences of any decisions in the long-term;

e the interests of the company’s employees;

e the need to foster the company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;

e the impact of the company’s operations on the community and environment;

e the desirability of the company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct; and

e the need to act fairly as between members of the company.
In discharging our Section 172(1) duties we have regard to the factors set out above. In addition, we also have regard
to other factors which we consider relevant to the decision being made. Those factors for example include the interests
and views of members of the Group. By considering the Company's purpose, vision and values together with its strategic
priorities and having a process in place for decision making, we aim to make sure that our decisions are consistent.
We delegate authority for day-to-day management of the Company to executives and then engage management in
setting, approving and overseeing execution of the business strategy and related policies. Board meetings are held
periodically where the directors consider the Company's activities and make decisions.
For reporting to the directors, the Company's results and governance are incorporated in Three, immediate parent
entity's results. Three has implemented the Wates Codes of Corporate Governance for Large Private Companies.
The business goal is to monetise our spectrum assets through provision of service to Three for their customers. This
allows Three to continue to enhance 5G experience for their customers by extending the coverage area and enhancing
the network. We will also continue to work with our Enterprise customers (as defined below) to enhance the service we
are delivering to them and what advantages that can bring for CKHH.

2. Critical accounting estimates and judgements
The preparation of financial statements in conformity with FRS 101 requires the use of certain critical accounting
estimates. It also often requires the use of judgements to select specific accounting methods and policies from several
acceptable alternatives. Furthermore, significant estimates and assumptions concerning the future may be required in
selecting and applying those methods and policies in the financial statements. The Company bases its estimates and
judgements on historical experience and various other assumptions that it believes are reasonable under the
circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates and judgements under different assumptions or
conditions.
The following is a review of the more significant assumptions, estimates and judgements as well as the accounting
policies and methods used in the preparation of the financial statements.
: 2.1 Critical accounting estimates and assumptions

(a) Depreciation and amortisation ‘
Depreciation and amortisation of operating assets constitutes a substantial operating cost for the Company. The cost
of tangible and intangible assets (excluding telecommunication licenses) is charged as depreciation or amortisation
expense over the estimated useful lives of the respective assets using the straight-line method. The Company
periodically reviews changes in technology and industry conditions, asset retirement activity and residual values to
determine adjustments to estimated remaining useful lives and depreciation rates.
Actual economic lives may differ from estimated useful lives. Periodic reviews could result in a change in depreciable
lives and therefore depreciation expense in future periods.
The annual depreciation and amortisation charge is sensitive to changes in the estimated useful economic lives and
residual values of the assets. The useful economic lives and residual values are reassessed annually. They are
amended when necessary to reflect current estimates, based on technological advancement, future investments,
economic utilisation and the physical condition of the assets. If the average useful life of tangible and intangible assets
were to increase by 1 year from 12.1 years to 13.1 years (2023: 9.9 to 10.9 years), the depreciation and amortisation
charge for the 15-month period would be lower by £0.1 million (2023: £0.2 million) and the net book value would be
higher by the same amount.
(b) Carrying value of long lived assets :
Assets that have an indefinite useful life are tested for impairment annually and when there is indication that they may
be impaired. Assets that are subject to depreciation and amortisation are reviewed for impairment to determine whether
there is any indication that the carrying value of these assets may not be recoverable and have suffered an impairment

: loss. if any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of
the impairment loss, if any.

. The fair value of the Spectrum has been determined using the average price provided by Ofcom, indexed to March
2025. The cost of disposal is not considered to be material and so no allowances have been made for disposal costs.
Any impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income. In the current period, there is no
impairment loss in relation to Spectrum.

(c) Taxation
The recognition of deferred tax assets, particularly in respect of tax losses, is based upon whether it is probable that
there will be sufficient and suitable taxable profits in the relevant legal entity or tax group against which to utilise the
assets in the future. Changes in the assumptions which underpin the Company's forecasts could have an impact on the
amount of future taxable profits and could have a significant impact on the period over which the deferred tax assets
would be recovered. We have based vur judgement to recognise deferred tax on the amounts that Can be recovered in
the immediate future and any surplus is left unrecognised.

(d) Intercompany recharge to Hutchison 3G UK Limited
The Company holds spectrum licences and operates network infrastructure on behalf of Hutchison 3G UK Limited
("Three"). Under the intercompany recharge arrangement, the Company recovers spectrum licence fees, network traffic
costs, site utilities and other related operating expenses from Three. The recharge is determined using an estimated
cost per unit of traffic, which reflects the underlying network and site costs incurred by the Company and is applied to
traffic volumes attributable to Three.
The determination of the cost per unit of traffic involves significant judgement, including the identification of relevant
costs to be included in the recharge and the allocation of those costs to traffic volumes. During the 15-month period

"ended 31 March 2025, as new and more reliable information became available regarding the nature and quantum of
network and site-related costs, management refined its estimate of the allocation of those costs to intercompany traffic
volumes. This refinement constitutes a change in accounting estimate and has accordingly been applied prospectively
from the current period. Whilst the Company's underlying cost base includes, among other items, utilities costs, the
refinement relates solely to the measurement and allocation of costs to traffic volumes and is therefore distinct from the
prior period restatement described in Note 1(r), which addresses the timing of recognition of utilities accruals within the
Company's own financial statements.
The recharge is sensitive to changes in the estimated cost per unit of traffic. If the estimated cost per unit were to
increase or decrease by 30% the annual recharge for the 15-month period would increase or decrease by approximately
£1.9 million, with a corresponding impact on administrative expenses and amounts recoverable from group
undertakings.
(e) Utilities accrual
The Company incurs utilities costs in connection with the operation of its network sites. Due to the timing and frequency
of invoicing by utilities providers, a proportion of these costs are estimated and recognised as an accrual at the reporting
date. The accrual represents management's best estimate of the utilities costs incurred but not yet invoiced as at 31
March 2025.
In forming its estimate, management has considered the range of invoices received from utilities providers during and
subsequent to the reporting period. Based on this review, management has determined that an estimated cost of £5,000
per 4G site and £8,000 per 5G site represents a reasonable expectation of the outstanding utilities costs, reflecting a
middie ground within the range of amounts indicated by invoices received.
The utilities accrual is sensitive to changes in the estimated cost per site. If the estimated cost per site were to increase
or decrease by 20%, reflecting a reasonably possible range of outcomes based upon management's judgement, the
accrual recognised at the reporting date would increase or decrease by approximately £1 million, with a corresponding
impact on administrative expenses.
2.2 Critical judgements in applying the Company’s accounting
(a) Useful economic life of telecommunications licences
The assessment of whether licences have an indefinite or finite life is subject to significant management estimate,
requiring evaluation of factors such as the legal, regulatory, and technologica! environment, expectations of renewal at
minimal cost, and the absence of foreseeable limits on the licence period. Following the Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006
(Directions to Ofcom) and related statutory instruments, the original fixed expiry dates for certain spectrum licences
were removed, such that those licences now continue on a perpetual basis subject to comply with licence conditions
and on payment of annual spectrum fees. Based on this assessment during the reporting 15-month period, spectrum
licences were determined to have indefinite lives due to their perpetual nature, subject to an annual fee (see Note 10).
The carrying value of assets assessed as having indefinite lives is £9.7 million (2023: £9.7 million).

: (a) Useful economic life of telecommunications licences (continued)
Although these licences have no fixed contractual end date and are therefore treated as having an indefinite useful life,
they remain subject to Ofcom's statutory powers, including revocation or variation of the licences in defined
circumstances. Any such revocation would typically follow a regulatory consultation process with the licence holder and,
where imposed, is expected to involve a multi-year notice period before the licence ceases, reflecting the long-term
nature of the underlying spectrum rights.
In forming its view as at 31 March 2025, the Company has considered these rights alongside its expectations that,
provided licence conditions continue to be met, and annual licence fees are paid, the spectrum will continue to be held
and renewed for the foreseeable future to support the ongoing operation ef its mobile network.
The initial cost of these licences is capitalised and reviewed annually for impairment (see Note 1). The annual fee is
recognised as an expense as incurred. This assessment is reviewed periodically considering any changes in regulation,
technology, network strategy, and market conditions, which could result in the licences being assessed as having a
finite useful life and hence being amortised in future periods.
As part of management's review of useful life of spectrum, the annual licence fee is also considered. Due to spectrum
licences being concluded to have an indefinite life related to annual licence fees to comply with the terms of the licence
as recognised as expense as incurred, Accordingly, any advance payment of license fee covering multiple years and
related to capitalised spectrum licences are treated as long term prepayments (see note 10) and expensed in line with
their expected pattern of benefit.
(b) Determination of lease term for right-of-use asset .
The Company has assessed the lease terms along the IFRS 16 guidance. Enforceable extension or termination options
have been included in the lease term when reasonably certain to be or not to be exercised, respectively, considering all
relevant facts and circumstances that create an economic incentive to exercise an option. For rolling leases, where the
lessee and the lessor each had the right to terminate the lease without permission from the other party, an analysis has
been made of whether there was more than an insignificant penalty for termination, in order to determine the lease
length.
(c) Gain on Spectrum Licence
In October 2022, Ofcom issued a statement regarding ‘Aligning license terms in the 3.4-3.8 GHz band’ with the purpose
of aligning the terms of UKB's 5G licenses with other licenses in the same band. This resulted in the annual license fee
being valued and charged at an updated market rate, reflecting the most recent spectrum auctions (held in 2018 and
2021). Ofcom also required UKB to now prepay the license fee for this band, for an initial 20 year term, which reflects
higher amounts paid by Three, the immediate parent entity of the Company, since 2018. The payment made was
recorded as a prepayment at the point of transaction and is subsequently being released over the licence fee term.
