Introduction
The directors present the strategic report and financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025.

The corporate parent measures performance not only through traditional financial indicators but also by considering the
company’s wider impact and governance. Key Performance Indicators include:
. Operating profit, turnover, and cashflow, Strength of the order book
. Safety performance and compliance with accreditations
. ESG and CSR contributions
. Standards of professional conduct and ethical behaviour
This holistic approach ensures that the business maintains resilience in both financial and non-financial terms, while
reinforcing our reputation as a trusted partner in the delivery of critical infrastructure.
Financial Performance :
The business delivered a strong trading performance during the year. Gross profit increased to £11.6m, representing a
gross margin of 12.2%, while operating profit is reported at a satisfactory/modest at £1.9m.
Our core sectors of Energy, Defence, Water & Process continued to perform strongly, generating sustainable ievels of
turnover, profit, and cash. Clients across these sectors remain committed to investing in the upgrade, protection, and
maintenance of critical assets through asset management programmes, infrastructure protection works, and capital delivery
frameworks. The breadth of our in-house, multidisciplinary capabilities has enabled us to successfully support project teams
and deliver a wide range of complex engineering solutions for our clients.
Return on sales remained in line with forecasts at 2%, while return on capital employed (ROCE) increased to 11.5%,
reflecting the benefits of continued investment in the Group’s core assets. Liquidity remained stable at 1.56, providing
assurance that the business maintains sufficient assets to meet its obligations and liabilities. Working capital stood at a
healthy £12.6m, and existing working capital facilities remain adequate to support both current operations and future
growth.
Throughout the year, our Business Management Systems team continued to refine and enhance processes and procedures
to support the effective delivery of projects across the business. The Board remains satisfied with the diversity of our project
portfolio and client base, with secured work in hand exceeding £107M at the year end.
Outlook
The board are confident that the strong performance has provided a foundation on which the business can continue to grow
and succeed. The business is committed to continuous investment into resources and capabilities including human
resource, the business management system, Information Technology, Environment Social Governance responsibilities.
This success and subsequent investment provide the business with opportunity to attract skilled and competent employees
to the business to deliver projects of high quality. This is also reflected in the ability to secure appropriate tendering
opportunities and the award of projects in targeted sectors and work scopes. The secured works (160M) is predicated on a
balanced portfolio of frameworks contracts derived from compliance and regulatory requirements for the upgrade and
maintenance of critical assets and bespoke capital projects.

Human Resources
The skills and competencies of our employees underpin the business. The board of directors identify that a highly
motivated, engaged, and skilled workforce is fundamental to gaining competitive advantage and differentiation. Our
investment in our people is unwavering as we implement training plans, matrices and programme for the growth of our
employee's compliance, skills, competencies and professional development. Our graduate and apprenticeship programmes
combine engineering technical training with a leadership skills programme delivering exceptional engineering talent. These
programmes raise the profile of the Company’s engineering capacity and professional status within the marketplace in order
to maintain its position and ensure robustness and sustainability going forward. The programme has produced accreditation
in the Institute of Civil Engineers (ICE), Institute of Mechanical Engineers (IMechE), Institute of Engineering and Technology
(IET), Royal Institute of Chartered Surveyors or Charted Institute of Management Accounts (CIMA), HNC/HND and NVQ
qualifications. The business has continued to invest significantly in the apprenticeship programme whereby engineering
disciplines and other support roles such as individuals in the finance, procurement and human resources department can
be developed and upskilled accordingly.
The Company has established a Career Development Organisation to provide structure, consistency, and focus to the
career progression of employees across the business. This framework supports development pathways across academic,
vocational, engineering-led, and shared services disciplines, ensuring that all employees have access to clear and
meaningful progression opportunities.
The Career Development Organisation also formally recognises key milestones in employees’ professional journeys,
reinforcing achievement, engagement, and retention. This structured approach further strengthens the Company’s
commitment to developing internal tatent and supporting longterm career growth across all areas of the business.
c Our Values, Our Way

Our core values of Care, Respect, Trust, and Sustainability continue to be embedded throughout the business and guide
the way we work with clients, colleagues, suppliers, and communities.
These values foster an inclusive working environment, support the development of long-term relationships, and underpin
our commitment to sustainability and social responsibility. They ensure that business decisions consider not only
commercial performance but also the wider impact on society and the environment. Employees across the organisation are
encouraged to embrace these values through a series of workshops and engagement initiatives designed to reinforce the
behaviours expected in every aspect of our operation.
Management Systems .
The Company's Business Management Systems play a critical role in supporting the effective delivery of projects,
maintaining compliance, and ensuring operational consistency across the business. The systems are subject to regular
review and enhancement to ensure they remain fit for purpose and responsive to evolving business requirements,
regulatory obligations, and market conditions.
Throughout the year, continued investment was made in strengthening governance arrangements, enhancing operational
procedures, refining business standards, and developing the Company's proprietary project management systems,
including the recently launched Trant Portal. These initiatives have further improved control, accountability, and efficiency
across project delivery activities. The Board considers the Trant Portal to be a key enabler of accessibility and operational
effectiveness, providing a robust framework that supports the delivery of safe, high-quality, and commercially successful
projects while ensuring appropriate oversight and assurance across the organisation. As the business continues to evolve,
ongoing investment in management systems will remain a priority to ensure they remain accessible, user-friendly, and
capable of supporting a dynamic workforce and the future growth of the Company.

Going Concern
The Board of Directors is satisfied that the adoption of the going concern basis remains appropriate. The business has
delivered a resilient performance during the year, underpinned by stable turnover, a satisfactory operating profit, and a
strong return on capital employed.
The Company continues to maintain healthy levels of cash reserves, liquidity, and working capital, providing a solid financial
platform to support ongoing operations and future growth. Detailed cash flow forecasts covering the next twelve months,
together with sensitivity analysis, indicate that the business is well positioned to withstand potential fluctuations in trading
performance. in addition, access to an established working capital facility provides further financial flexibility and support
should it be required.
The Board also takes confidence from the strength of the Company's order book, which is supported by a significant
pipeline of tenders at various stages of evaluation and negotiation. Continued success in securing new strategic clients
further enhances the quality and diversity of future revenue streams, providing confidence in the Company's prospects and
long-term sustainability.
Health and Safety
The group recognises that it the responsibility of the entire workforce to perform the company’s health and safety processes
and managements systems in providing a safe and secure place of work for our employees, clients, visitors, members of
the public. Our commitment to the excellent health and safety performance across the company is unwavering and remains
an imperative key performance indicator.
The business has continued the commitment and investment into the development of integrated management system and
related accreditation to the ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 to ensure robust operating standards, practices, and
policies. The systems have been methodically developed based on the outputs of audits, observations, and data analytics
to ensure they are tailored to the operations of this business. Our commitment has continued to be recognised from clients
and governing institutions such as the British Standard Institutions and ROSPA for performance in health and safety. We
are also cognisant that we must remain vigilant and proactive in maintaining this level of safety and protecting all
stakeholders of the business. The board is pleased to report that current Accident Frequency Rate is 0, based on 2.1M
hours worked.
Environmental, Social & Governance (ESG)
Engaging with the community
The business has a longstanding history in positively contributing to the local communities in the areas of operations. We
promote STEM interest (science, technology, engineering, and maths) by deploying our engineers to visit local schools and
colleges presenting on the various facets of engineering and technology within the business. Our STEM ambassadors have
received positive feedback on the impact of the various visits, with attendees often expressing interest in pursuing careers
in the engineering industry. We also contribute to local communities by donating time, skills and services to refurbish or
upgrade local community spaces. Our employees regularly volunteer for “litter pick” days.
Economic
The business’s Goodwill Trust charity has financially supported charitable causes in the local community, frequently
promoted by the causes or endeavours of our employees. The funds donated have allowed the charities to upgrade their
assets, infrastructure or undertake research and development. Internationally, we support WaterAid to fund water-giving
projects in poor communities abroad.
Environmental
The directors acknowledge the increasing importance in identifying and evaluating the impact of the company's operations
the environment. The business continues to develop and implement our Carbon Reduction Plan (CRP) outlining our
commitment to achieving net zero emissions. This encompasses steps to mitigate our direct emissions in addition to
engaging with our supply chain to understand, measure and develop meaningful emission reduction targets. The
implementation of our CRP and Energy Management System and data gathering identifies means of improving our energy
performance and the use of energy more efficiently will ultimately reduce our carbon emissions. We have an extensive
breadth of initiatives in progress, for example, the business has introduced electric vehicles into the fleet, installed charging

Principal Risks and Uncertainties

The Board recognises the inherent risks associated with operating in complex and regulated markets.

Recruitment_and Retention of Staff: The ability to attract, develop, and retain skilled people is critical to our continued :

success. The market for experienced engineers and technical specialists is highly competitive, presenting a key risk. To

mitigate this, the company has reviewed and adjusted pay structures to remain competitive with market conditions, ensuring

fairness and transparency across the workforce. We have also introduced structured appraisal processes to support career

development, provide clarity on progression opportunities, and strengthen employee engagement. These initiatives,

alongside our graduate and apprenticeship programmes, leadership development, and EDI commitments, form a

comprehensive strategy to build a sustainable and motivated workforce.

Cybersecurity: The business is exposed to the risk of cyber security threats, which could result in disruption to operations,

loss of data, or reputational damage. This risk is mitigated through robust IT controls, secure system architecture, ongoing

monitoring, and regular staff awareness training. The Company also maintains appropriate cyber security standards and

continues to invest in system resilience to protect against evolving threats.

Credit Risk: The Company is exposed to credit risk arising from the potential failure of customers to meet their contractual

payment obligations. This risk is mitigated through robust financial due diligence undertaken prior to engagement, alongside

the structuring of commercial arrangements to appropriately manage exposure. In addition, the majority of the Company's

client base comprises established blue-chip organisations, which further reduces credit risk.

Health and Safety. Health and safety remain a key operational risk for the business, given the nature of the environments in

which it operates. This risk is mitigated through robust processes, procedures, and safe systems of work, supported by

clear operational controls and governance. The Company continues to invest in behavioural safety programmes and visual

standards initiatives to reinforce safe working practices and further strengthen its safety culture.

Secured Work: The level and continuity of secured work represents a key business risk, with potential implications for

revenue visibility and future workload planning. This risk is mitigated through the Company’s strategic focus on securing

positions on long-term framework agreements and maintaining strong, long-standing relationships with established clients.

These approaches provide a more stable pipeline of work and enhance future revenue certainty.

Methodology and process changes from previous year
*The Government Department for Energy Security & Net Zero (DESNZ) GHG Conversion Factors for Company Reporting,
2025, were used to calculate emissions.
‘The market-based emissions from electricity consumption of business offices have been calculated and reported alongside
location-based emissions, to demonstrate the use of tariffs that contain renewable energy supply. Certificates are on file to
demonstrate the mix of renewable energy used.
*Segregation of company car mileage currently only separates vehicles into hybrid, electric, diesel or petrol groups.
Consequently, the carbon costs attributed to hybrid vehicles is done so on the assumption of a ‘worse case’ self-charging
hybrid. Improvements in recording in the upcoming financial years will further improve this analysis in the future and provide
a clearer, more realistic assessment of the data.
“As we have been improving our data collection methods and increasing transparency of our emissions data this provides
ever improving confidence in our results. Similarly works are underway with our IT development teams to maximise the
benefits of Al, using this technology to minimise the chance of human error in reporting of pertinent data.

Energy efficiency and carbon reduction measures taken in the reporting year

«In line with the published TEL Net Zero Action Plan, the company is in the process of transitioning the car fleet from
diesel/petrol to hybrid and electric. Only 8 diesel cars remain in the 92-strong fleet, with the rest having been replaced by
hybrid and electric vehicles. This change of vehicles has been a significant factor in the reduction in company fleet fuel use
this year. This will further improve as the company has, in 2025, made the decision to only purchase ‘Ultra Low Emission’
vehicles from this point and is ahead of schedule in removing the remaining diesel/petrol vehicles from the fleet.

‘Works commenced at TEL Head Office in 2025 to refurbish and renovate the office buildings. Within the scope of these
works includes plans to improve office heating/cooling systems, water efficiency and reducing our reliance on gas within the
buildings. Whilst the carbon reduction effect of these changes is not yet seen, these will materialise in the coming years.
-Trant didn’t use any gas cannisters in 2025 equating to a 100% reduction. This is primarily due to changing requirements
on individual sites and a change in business direction, meaning that welding is no longer a significant activity within the .
business. :

“The solar panels at Head Office continue to generate good levels of power, particularly given the optimal weather in the
summer/autumn of 2025. TEL is now looking into battery storage options or selling back to the grid surplus energy produced
by the solar panels in future years.

- _. _, ‘Diesel generator alternatives are being trialled on key projects in order to assess suitability, cost and energy savings as an =
alternative to diesel generators when mains power connection is not possible. To date fuel savings have been recorded as
high as 75% compared to traditional diesel generators of similar power and output.
+A rise in site fuel was recorded over the 2025 reporting year however this was in conjunction with a 9% increase in works in
the ‘Energy’ sector of the business which primarily comprises large scale civil/groundworks requiring a large amount of
diesel-powered heavy machinery.

Summary
Whilst carbon savings have not been as significant this year compared to last year (when compared against the intensity
metric) this can be attributed to the fact that most major carbon saving measures have already been implemented into the
business in previous years.
In addition, much of our site-based carbon costs (e.g. fuel) are dependent on the works that are instructed and therefore fall
outside of TEL’s control. This will continue to vary year-on-year based on the requirements of our clients, their scope of
works, the properties that we work on and, the location of these properties.
TEL continues to strive for improvement of our scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions throughout our day-to-day works, and we hope
that a number of the current fuel and carbon reduction trials we are currently undertaking will offer company-wide carbon
savings in the future.

2.5 Turnover
Turnover in the statement of income and retained earnings arises from increases in certified valuations for work
performed on contracts, excluding VAT in the year.
Turnover on term contracts is recognised upon completion of each contractual element of the contract.
Construction contracts
Where the outcome of a construction contract can be estimated reliably, revenue and costs are recognised by
reference to the stage of completion of the contract activity at the reporting end date. Variations in contract
work, claims and incentive payments are included to the extent that the amount can be measured reliably and
its receipt is considered probable.
Profit on long term contracts is taken as the work is carried out if the financial outcome can be assessed with
reasonable certainty.
When it is probable that the total contract costs will exceed total contract turnover, the total expected loss is
tecognised as an expense immediately.
Where the outcome of a construction contract cannot be estimated reliably, contract costs are recognised as
expenses in the period in which they are incurred and contract revenue is recognised to the extent of contract
costs incurred where it is probable that they will be recoverable.
Short term contracts
Where the outcome of a construction contract is not able to be estimated reliably, no profit is recognised until
the contract is complete. Where it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract turnover, the
expected loss is recognised as an expense immediately.
2.6 Research and development
In the research phase of an internal project it is not possible to demonstrate that the project will generate future

that its cost can be reliably measured. The capitalised development costs are subsequently amortised on a
straight-line basis over their useful economic lives, which range from 3 to 6 years.
If it is not possible to distinguish between the research phase and the development phase of an internal project,
the expenditure is treated as if it were all incurred in the research phase only.

2. Accounting policies (continued)
2.23 Financial instruments (continued)
Basic financial liabilities .
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets
of the Company after the deduction of all its liabilities.
Basic financial liabilities, which include trade and other creditors, bank loans and other loans are initially
measured at their transaction price (adjusting for transaction costs except in the initial measurement of financial
liabilities that are subsequently measured at fair value through profit and loss). When this constitutes a
financing transaction, whereby the debt instrument is measured at the present value of the future payments
discounted at a market rate of interest, discounting is omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial.
Debt instruments are subsequently carried at their amortised cost using the effective interest rate method.
Trade creditors are obligations to pay for goods and services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of
business from suppliers. Trade creditors are classified as current liabilities if the payment is due within one
year. If not, they represent non-current liabilities. Trade creditors are initially recognised at their transaction
price and subsequently are measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Discounting is
omitted where the effect of discounting is immaterial.
Derecognition of financial instruments
Derecognition of financial assets :
Financial assets are derecognised when their contractual rights to future cash flows expire, or are settled, or
when the Company transfers the asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another
party. If significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained after the transfer to another party, then the
Company will continue to recognise the value of the portion of the risks and rewards retained.
Derecognition of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities are derecognised when the Company's contractual obligations expire or are discharged or
cancelled.

3. Judgements in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In the application of the company's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates
and assumptions about the carrying amount of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other sources.
The estimates and associated assumptions are based on historical experience and other factors that are considered
to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are
recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised where the revision affects only that period, or in the period
of the revision and future periods where the revision affects both current and future periods.
Critical judgements
The following judgements (apart from those involving estimates) which have the most significant effect on amounts
recognised in the financial statements are as follows:
Construction contracts and percentage completion
Revenue and profits are recognised according to stage of completion of a contract based on the expected final
outturn. As a contract can run for a number of years this is a judgement area. On major construction contracts, it is
assessed, based on past experience, that their outcome cannot be estimated reliably during the early stages of the
contract, but that costs incurred will be recoverable. In this instance, the group will recognise revenue equal to the
total costs incurred and expected to be recovered on the contract. Once the outcome can be estimated reliably, the
soe - stage of completion is measured by the proportion of contract costs incurred for work performed to date compared to
the estimated total contract costs.
Onerous contracts
Managements estimate the total losses incurred on the contract based on their best estimate of costs to be incurred
and income to be received. This is a significant judgement by management as there could be delays in the contract
or additional costs incurred that could impact these losses recognised on the contracts.
Key sources of estimation uncertainty
The estimates and assumptions which have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying
amount of assets and liabilities are as follows:
Provisions
The remedial provision of £2,296,591 (2024: £2,085,732) is based on management's best estimate of costs to be
incurred during the maintenance period. Management's estimate is based on historic cost trends as a percentage of
overall costs once each project reaches a stage where the remaining maintenance-period costs can be reliably
estimated.
Overhead allocation
Management estimate overhead costs allocated to contracts to be in line with historic overheads actually incurred.
Recoverability of intercompany receivable balances
Estimates are made relating to the recoverability of receivable balances to reflect unrecoverable amounts based on
historic data and the directors' knowledge. The directors have assessed that no provision is necessary against the
intercompany balances at the reporting date.
