1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES
The principal accounting policies are summarised below and are in line with FRS 102. They have all been
applied consistently throughout the current year as well as in the preceding year. ~
General information :
Econet Wireless Limited is a private Company limited by shares, incorporated in the United Kingdom under the
Companies Act, and registered in England and Wales. Details of its registered office is provided on Page 1. :
Basis of accounting
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention and in accordance with
Financial Reporting Standard 102 (FRS 102) issued by the Financial Reporting Council.
The functional currency of the Company is considered to be US Dollar (USD) because that is the currency of the
primary economic environment in which the Company's group operates. Accordingly, the set of financial

. statements have been prepared in US dollars.
The disclosure requirements of section 1A of FRS 102 have been applied.
Basis of preparation other than going concern
The Directors do not believe that the Company serves any further strategic purpose to the Econet Group. As
disclosed in note 10 of the accounts, the Company is subject to an ongoing HMRC tax enquiry, but following
resolution of the enquiry the company intends to cease operations and be liquidated. Accordingly these
accounts are prepared on a basis other than going concern. This basis includes, where applicable, writing the
Company's assets down to net realisable value. No provision has been made for the future costs of liquidating
: the Company as such costs were not committed to at the reporting date.
Financial instruments
Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised when the Company becomes a party to the contractual
provisions of the instrument. ‘
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual
arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets
of the Company after deducting all of its liabilities. .
Financial assets
Financial assets include balances due from group companies, and are measured at transaction price less
impairment.
Financial liabilities
Basic financial liabilities include other creditors, accrued expenses and tax liabilities. Financial liabilities
classified as payable within one year are measured at transaction price. In the current and prior financial year,
there are no financial liabilities classified as payable in more than one year.

2 CRITICAL ACCOUNTING JUDGEMENTS AND KEY SOURCES OF ESTIMATIONS
In the application of the Company’s accounting policies, which are described in note 1, the Directors are
required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions about the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities
that are not readily apparent from other sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are based on -
historical experience and other factors that are considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these
estimates.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting
estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised if the revision affects only that period,
or in the period of the revision and future periods if the revision affects both current and future periods.
Provision for Tax Charge
In 2019, HM Revenue & Customs ("HMRC") issued a discovery determination under paragraph 41(2) of
Schedule 18 to the Finance Act 1998 regarding the historical treatment of a loan receivable from the
: Company's parent, Econet Global Limited. HMRC has challenged whether the loan's terms were consistent with
the arm's length principle. The determination concerns the Company's corporation tax returns for the financial
years ended 28 February 2014 through 28 February 2019.
The Company has formally appealed the discovery determination and continues to engage with HMRC. The
Directors, having obtained appropriate professional advice, believe that the Company's tax treatment is
supportable and that the appeal has merit. However, given the inherent uncertainty of the matter and in
accordance with the requirements of the applicable accounting standards, the Directors have concluded that it
is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the matter.
Based on the information currently available, the Directors have recognised a provision of US$2.8 million
(2025: US$2.8 million), representing their best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation as
at the reporting date. . .
The Directors also consider that the ultimate outcome remains uncertain and that the final settlement could
differ from the amount provided. Based on current assessments, the potential financial exposure ranges from
no additional liability to approximately US$12.8 million (2025: US$12.8 million). The matter will be resolved
upon conclusion of the ongoing discussions and appeals with HMRC, and any resulting adjustment will be
recognised in the period when the uncertainty is resolved.

Impairment of Intercompany Loan
As disclosed in note 8 of the accounts, there is a long-term loan of $11,219,818. This loan has historically been
subject to material impairment provisions due to concerns regarding the going concern position.
The group’s latest audited accounts for the year ended 28th February 2025 disclosed Cash and Cash
. Equivalents of $165m and the group remains capable of discharging the impaired loan balance in full out of :
cash reserves. Nevertheless, the group’s Statement of Financial Position at 28th February 2025 reflect a
negative equity position of $168m and net current liabilities of $829m. The net current liabilities position is
principally a result of bank borrowings of< 1 year $677m falling due within 12 months.
As is disclosed in the group accounts, since 28th February 2025 the directors have initiated several funding
plans to secure both business expansion and working capital, and to refinance bank facilities falling due within
12 months. There remains material uncertainty with regards to the ability of the Group to secure funds from
external parties through Group fund raising. Nevertheless it should be noted that the group audit report for
the year ended 28th February 2025 includes a disclaimer of opinion as regards the going concern assessment.
The recoverability of the long-term loan balance is subject to material estimation uncertainty depending on the
going concern assertion at group level.There are a range of potential outcomes, ranging from the
intercompany balance being fully irrecoverable to the balance being discharged in full. Having reviewed the
position, including ongoing efforts to improve solvency at group level, the directors believe it is reasonable that
the long-term loan balance should be carried at a level sufficient to allow it to discharge its declared liabilities.
