STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
Business review

. The Arrow Communications Holdings Limited (ARO) group (the “Group") continued to operate as a market-leading Cloud
Services provider in 2025, navigating a challenging trading environment with resilience and disciplined operational focus.
The Group delivered revenue of £99.1 million and gross profit of £46.8 million, with recurring revenue representing 77% of
total revenue for the year (FY24: 77%), reflecting the continued importance of predictable, high-quality income streams. As
the main trading entity of the ARO Group, Arrow Business Communications Limited plays a pivotal role in delivering these
results and was the driving force behind these key performance indicators.
Despite softer market conditions and delays in certain project-led activity, particularly where customer decisions moved into
2026, the Company maintained a strong financial performance. Adjusted EBITDA, a core financial metric increased by 10%
to £12.5m (2024: £11.4m) reflecting the strength of the Company's recurring revenue base and its continued focus on
operational efficiency.
The Group continued to realise synergies from prior acquisitions. This strategic consolidation enhances scalability,
streamlines operations, and elevates both customer and employee experiences. ARO’s focus on refining its internal
structure and platforms has positioned the business for its next growth phase, ensuring long-term competitiveness.
In summary, 2025 continues a trend of stable EBITDA coupled with disciplined operational management. Strong cash
generation and operating cost control allowed the Group to create the strong foundation pillars for the next stage of our
growth journey. The Group remains focused on improving pipeline conversion, sustaining recurring revenue quality and
positioning the business for future growth.
Going concern
As of August 2026, the Group has exchanged contracts to merge with TalkTalk Business Direct (TTB). Once completed,
this transaction will result in a significant change to the capital structure of the group, including a new credit package to
support the business in the medium to long term. This includes the introduction of a new senior debt facility, a new super
senior revolving credit facility and new PIK junior and shareholder loans.
As a result, the combined group will secure a robust long-term capital structure and a markedly stronger liquidity position
than either company could achieve on its own today. The merger is expected to enhance future trading opportunities
through a broader product and service offering across the combined customer base. In addition, the Directors anticipate
operational efficiencies and cost synergies arising from the integration of the two businesses, which are expected to support
profitability and strengthen margins over the medium to long term.
Subsequent events
As of August 2026, the Group has exchanged contracts to merge with TalkTalk Business Direct (TTB), one of the UK's
leading B2B telecoms and managed service providers. The transaction will create one of the UK's largest independent
providers of connectivity and managed technology services, with approximately 650 employees, annual revenues of around
£200 million and a combined customer base of over 70,000 organisations.
The transaction has received government clearance under the National Security and Investment Act (NSIA). The merger will
result in a change in the capital structure of ARO Group, with the ultimate parent company of the new Group being
Sherwood Group Topco Limited. All current debt provider loans held by the Group will be repaid, to be replaced by a new
credit package.
The Directors have reviewed the impact of this announcement and concluded that it represents a non-adjusting event
occurring after the reporting date. Accordingly, no adjustments have been made to the amounts recognised in these
financial statements. At the date of approval of the financial statements, the Directors are unable to reliably quantify the
financial impact of the proposed merger on the future operations, assets, liabilities or results of the company.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The key business risks and uncertainties affecting the Group have the potential to be impacted in the longer-term by trends
in other markets and the wider economic environment. In order to mitigate the risk of a downturn in one market having a
significant impact on the Group, management try to ensure that all of the trading activities in the Group have market
diversification where possible. The Group's customer base is highly diversified across a wide range of sectors, both private :
. and public. Whilst the customer base is predominantly located in the UK an element of the supply chain for our hardware is
globally located and therefore presents procurement risks for timeliness of delivery (represents 9% of Gross Profit in 2025).
Such risks are proactively managed and minimized where at all possible.
Pricing continues to be a risk to the Group, both in terms of regulatory changes and competition. This risk is mitigated by
offering new services that offer additional value and by cross selling products supplied via the various acquisitions. The
growth strategies provide greater scale and therefore buying economy opportunities.
Key performance indicators
: The Company operates a comprehensive weekly and monthly KPI reporting regime to assess the on-going performance of
the business. The scope of the KPis extends to sales and gross profit measures within the existing customer base and new
customers. In addition to optimising profitability, non-financial metrics monitor employee engagement and satisfaction,
customer satisfaction and operational delivery.

Directors’ statement of compliance with duty to promote the success of the Company
The directors have complied with the requirements of $172 of the Companies Act 2006.
The overarching Values and Commitment strategy within ARO is to develop a long term,-sustainable business that delivers
value for ail its stakeholders including employees, clients, suppliers, business partners, and the wider community. By
managing the business responsibly, the directors intend to support the creation of a financially stable organisation and
* deliver value for all stakeholders.
The ARO Group is owned by its management team and employees, together with investment partner, MML. As noted
elsewhere in this report, all employees are regularly consulted with and fully understand the strategic direction in both the
short and longer-term, as well as the current trading performance of the business. This ownership model and consultative
approach fosters a strong culture and high levels of employee satisfaction and retention, whilst also ensuring that all
employees are treated fairly and consistently. There is a dedicated Employee Engagement team within the business that
focuses on continually improving this key area within the business.
Customer relationships are the heartbeat of the business with customer retention being a key performance indicator.
Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) and monthly Net Promoter Score surveys provide valuable feedback on customer
satisfaction and engagement within ARO and achieving high levels of service excellence is a core element of the
Company’s philosophy. Many experienced customer account managers are employed to ensure customers are well
supported by strong levels of service excellence can be provided.
As a business that uses global suppliers, the directors fully acknowledge a duty to trade responsibly. ARO has an OFCOM
approved Code of Practice, is a member of the approved dispute resolutions scheme OTELO and holds network
accreditations with several of the industry’s leading network operators. Business is conducted in line with ARO’s Code of
Conduct and several other internal policies and procedures that are designed to ensure the Company maintains the highest
reputation possible for standards of conduct. The Company has a dedicated commercial team with responsibility for
maintaining regular engagement with suppliers and ensuring that they are kept well abreast of the Company's performance
and strategic objectives in both the short and longer-term.
The Company has a Corporate Social Responsibility Policy to ensure that the interests of all stakeholders, including those
based in the wider community, are acknowledged, and protected. Arrow is committed to identifying, managing, and
minimising the environmental impact of business operations and it maintains an Environmental Policy to manage this and
ensure compliance with all applicable environmental legislation and to strive to use pollution prevention and environmental
best practices in all areas. With regards to impact on the community, Arrow engages with the local communities in which it
works, is committed to making a positive social and economic impact and understanding and managing any negative
impacts of its business operations. In addition, the Company seeks to make a positive social contribution through the
services we provide to clients. Programmes are in place to support employee volunteering and fundraising as well as local
community and charity support.

2.3 Going concern
The operating losses for the year arise as a result of the amortisation of goodwill as set out in note 13, directly
as a result of the acquisition programme of the group of which it is a part of. As of August 2026, the Group has
exchanged contracts to merge with TalkTalk Business Direct (TTB). Once completed, this transaction will result
in a significant change to the capital structure of the group, including a new credit package to support the
business in the medium to long term. This includes the introduction of a new senior debt facility, a new super
senior revolving credit facility and new PIK junior and shareholder loans.
As a result, the combined group will secure a robust long-term capital structure and a markedly stronger
liquidity position than either company could achieve on its own today. The merger is expected to enhance
future trading opportunities through a broader product and service offering across the combined customer
base. In addition, the Directors anticipate operational efficiencies and cost synergies arising from the
integration of the two businesses, which are expected to support profitability and strengthen margins over the
medium to long term.
As such the directors consider it appropriate that the financial statements are prepared on the going concern
basis.

2.5 Revenue
Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the Company and
the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the consideration received or
receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales taxes.
Revenue from one off sale of goods is recognised when the risks and rewards of ownership have been passed
: to the customer, being once they have been delivered.
Where revenue from services is part of a fixed length contract, the revenue is spread evenly across the length
of the contract, along with any associated costs which have been incurred in securing the contract.
2.6 Operating leases: the Company as lessee
Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is representative of the time pattern of the lessee's
benefit from the use of the leased asset. .

Interest income is recognised in profit or loss using the effective interest method. .

2.10 Intangible assets
Goodwill ,
Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and the
acquirer's interest in the fair value of its identifiable assets and liabilities of the acquiree at the date of
acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost less accumulated amortisation and
accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a straight-line basis to the Statement of
Comprehensive Income over its useful economic life.
Other intangible assets
Intangible assets are initially recognised at cost. After recognition, under the cost model, intangible assets are
measured at cost less any accumulated amortisation and any accumulated impairment losses.
