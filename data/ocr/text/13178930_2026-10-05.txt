Strategic report .
The directors present their strategic report on the Group for the year ended 31 December 2025.
The Group consists of the parent Sonovate International Holdings Limited (‘the Company’) and its wholly owned
subsidiaries Sonovate Limited, Sonovate US LLC, two dormant entities Sonovate Business Services Limited and
Sonovate US Servicing LLC (‘the Subsidiaries”) and a Special Purpose Vehicle (“SPV”) set up for funding purposes
(see below).
The Group is an innovative financial services technology company that predominantly supports recruitment agencies
and HR Technology businesses in the contingent labour market. Since 2015, the Group provides innovative
technology solutions and highly valued financial products to its customers.
Review of the business
The Group continues to invest considerable resources into the development of software, including AI, which enables
its customers, including recruitment agencies and other providers of contractors and contingent workers, to leverage
the back-office workflows provided by Sonovate, including processing contracts, invoices, payments, and the
provision of working capital funding with the objective of becoming the supplier of choice for such services. The
Group provides significant liquidity into UK small businesses and is an enabler of UK small business growth.
Its technology allows it to offer its services via APIs in a “Lending as a Service” type product. During the year, the
Group continued to enhance the new platform to enable projected growth and to provide existing and new customers
with exceptional solutions and experience.
The audited consolidated financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025 are set out on pages 15 to 34. The
Group continued to evolve and grow its business in the UK. The Operating loss for the financial year to 31 December
2025 was £5,332,177 (2024: loss of £7,879, 138). The statutory loss after tax for the year was £9,331,940 (2024: loss
£10,108, 146). These losses are a result of continued investment into software development and personnel. At the
balance sheet date, the Group’s total net liabilities reduced to £2,023,492 (2024: net liabilities £7,691,552) as a result
of the equity raise and losses during the year.
Over the course of the year ended 31 December 2025, the Group processed and purchased £1,318,918,658 of invoices
(2024: £1,258,841,922) an increase of 4.8% (2024: 5.6%) over the previous year. Cumulatively to 31 December 2025
the Group has provided over £7bn of funding to businesses.
Funding and liquidity
The Group has made significant investment to expand the sources of funding leveraging a private securitisation facility
with an Irish special purpose vehicle (“SPV”), Sonovate Trade Receivables Designated Activity Company. This is a
single purpose entity established for the securitisation facility and is considered “bankruptcy-remote” as its sole
purpose is to administer the securitisation of the company’s receivables. The Company’s subsidiaries securitise certain
receivables assets via the sale of these assets to the SPV, which in tur issues asset-backed securities to investors.
During the year, the Group progressed a long term refinancing of the facility, that closed in February 2026, to ensure
the long-term stability of funding and liquidity for a further 3-year tenor.
As at 31 December 2025, as a result of the securitisation and the loan syndication the Group is funded by the following
facilities:

Lloyds Bank.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Group is subject to principal risks and uncertainties as detailed below:
Continued access to funding and liquidity
In order to purchase invoices, mainly supporting the liquidity of UK small business, the Company requires access to
a committed funding line either through equity or debt capital. This is managed by the group and as detailed above
the Senior Financing Facility, the private securitisation facility of £150m of senior notes held by a large European
Bank, a £40m mezzanine notes facility issued to a UK asset manager which are both now held in a special purpose
vehicle ‘Sonovate Trade Receivables Designated Activity Company’, and the £30m revolving loan facility were all
extended subsequent to the year end.
Credit risk
The Group is exposed to the risk that debtors do not settle debts in a complete and/or timely manner. The Group has
in place credit risk models to ensure that lending levels are appropriate for the credit risk profile of the customer and
debtor. The vast majority of funding is credit insured which significantly mitigates the risk of non-collection. This
risk is monitored closely in terms of the delinquency of the invoices funded and the proportion of debt that is uninsured.
The Group has recorded a bad debt charge in the year ended 31 December 2025 of £1,230,367 (2024. £1,363,077)
against debtors that are unlikely to be recoverable; for debts that have not been written off at 31 December 2025
attempts are continuing to recover the sums due.
Financial Crime risk
The Group is exposed to a number of financial crime risks, with the most significant exposure to fraud risk. The most
likely fraud risk to the Group is the financing of fake invoices. The Group mitigates this risk through a proprietary
software which uses a combination of public and non-public data sources to assess the potential risk for fraudulent
invoices on a continuous basis.
Operational risk
Operational Risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems or from
external events. As a result of these risks, the Group may have a negative impact to reputation with its stakeholders,
face fines or damages and loss of future business. The Group mitigates this risk through regular internal review
processes and the maintenance of an internal risk register.
Competition
The provision of finance into the temporary worker sector is a competitive marketplace. While the Group remains
significantly ahead in terms of its technology, the entrance of multiple and / or well backed competitors with
significant financial resources and a focus on technology could result in more competitive pricing and / or a reduced
rate of market share capture. The market is monitored regularly.
Key performance indicators
The directors manage the Group’s operations holistically and monitor the ongoing performance of the business
through several measures including:
© preparation of and monitoring of weekly and monthly management information including detailed
management accounts and variance analysis;
e — regular review of the portfolio performance including delinquencies, debtor days, debt turn and credit quality
including credit insurance;
e monitoring of new business volumes, organic growth of existing customers, net revenue retention, client
churn, cost of customer acquisition, cost to service customers and the lifetime value of customers; and
e — gathering and responding to customer feedback.

Going concern (continued)
As a provider of financing to customers, the funding requirements of the business are a key management activity as it
is required to grow, and therefore be renewed, on a continual basis as the business scales. The group and its
management are therefore focused on continuing to monitor, forecast and discuss with lenders and investors, both
existing and potential. Both the latest senior and junior debt facilities are aligned to the growth plans of the business
and have been agreed in accordance with the terms being more favourable compared to the previous facilities and
therefore, allowing the business to continue to scale.
The directors have prepared detailed cash flow forecasts for the Group, for a period of at least 12 months from the
date of approval of these financial statements, the going concern period, which indicate that, considering a severe but
plausible downside scenario including a reduction in volume of invoices funded by 30% for 12 months and cost
mitigations adopted, including headcount freezes, the Group and Company will have sufficient funds to meet their
obligations as they fall due for that period.
All existing debt facilities have been renegotiated and extended until February 2029 to ensure the business has the
right sized debt portfolio to facilitate the next stage of growth. On this basis, the Directors are confident based on the
ongoing dialogue that there is sufficient interest amongst lenders to roll the transaction over for another term as their
intention is to grow the receivables book with Sonovate in the coming years.
The financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis and do not include any adjustments that would result
from the basis of preparation being inappropriate.
Post balance sheet events
In February 2026 the Group successfully completed the extension of its private warehouse transaction, bringing in a
new Senior noteholder and maintaining the Class B Noteholder. In addition, the maturity of the Parent Company’s
on-balance sheet revolving facility of £30 million with BCI Capital was also extended to February 2029.
Financial risk management
The Group’s activities expose it to a variety of financial risks: credit risk, liquidity risk and market risk (including
interest rate risk and currency risk).
Interest rate risk
The Group’s exposure to interest rate risk is minimal. While the Group’s financing is linked to underlying interest rate
benchmarks, it has the ability to reprice and adjust its income based on a number of factors including movement in
underlying interest rates.
Liquidity and capital risk
The Group manages liquidity and capital risk through its access to various sources of debt and equity funding. During
the year the Group worked on refinancing the SPV and the revolving loan to February 2029.
The Group maintains a cash burn forecast to enable strategic decision making and the potential to launching their
Series C equity fundraising, which will enable the next stage of growth.
Currency risk
The Group transacts in USD, EUR, CHF, CAD, DKK, SEK, NOK and AUD although the vast majority of business
is in GBP. In cases where the Group funds customers in GBP, USD or EUR (Matched Currencies), the SPV is funded
in matching currencies which mitigates any potential currency risk. For the Non-matched Currencies, the Group draws
against the SPV’s GBP note and deals with the currency exposure by reserving a generous amount in cash to mitigate
this risk. However, with the debt tum standing at 40 days, the likely deviation in currency pairs in that term is small.
