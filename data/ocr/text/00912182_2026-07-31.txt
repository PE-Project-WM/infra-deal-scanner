Principal activities and business review
We are a leading provider of mission-critical communication infrastructure, devices, accessories, software and services. Our
products and services help government, public safety, and commercial customers improve their operations through increased
effectiveness, efficiency, and safety of their mobile workforces.
Tumover increased to £166.8m for 2025 from £151.7m in 2024. :
Operating profit decreased from £10.5m in 2024 to £6.0m in 2025. Profit before tax decreased from £16.1m in 2024 to £14.3m
in 2025. Operating activity and margins vary from year to year depending on pricing and performance on individual large
contracts, as well as the impact of the defined benefit pension scheme, share-based payments (within other operating income and
expenditure) and transfer pricing (within cost of sales). The Company’s performance is in line with expectations and the Directors
have no concerns regarding the long-term performance or position of the Company.
Net assets of the Company were £244.9m at 31 December 2025 (2024: £210.5m).
The Company primarily operated in the following business during the year:
Government
The Government business includes sales of public safety mission-critical communications systems, commercial two-way radio
systems and devices, software and services.
Channel sales
The channel business sells to third-party customers through various distributors.
The Company had five overseas branches at 31 December 2025, in Pakistan, United Arab Emirates, Ireland, Qatar and Norway,
which provide sales and marketing support, or direct technical support on local contracts.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Global economic conditions :
Some of our customers require substantial financing, including public financing or government grants, in order to fund their
operations and make purchases from us. The inability of these customers to obtain sufficient credit or other funds to finance
purchases of our products and/or meet their payment obligations to us could have a negative impact on our financial results. In
addition, if global economic conditions result in insolvencies for our customers, it will negatively impact our financial results.
We make sales in certain regions through third-party dealers, distributors and retailers. Although many of these third parties have
significant operations and maintain access to available credit, others are smaller and more likely to be impacted by significant
decreases in available credit that may result during economic downturns. If credit pressures or other financial difficulties result
in insolvency for important third parties and we are unable to succcssfully transition eiid-customers to purchase our products from
other third parties or from us directly, it may cause, and in some cases has caused, a negative impact on our financial results.
The Company maintains strong relationships with each of its key customers and has established credit control parameters.
Appropriate credit terms are agreed with all customers and these are closely managed. In addition, the group maintains credit
insurance for certain contracts. In the event of a customer failing to pay amounts due or considered at risk of failing to pay, the
cost of such bad debt is passed to group companies in accordance with transfer pricing arrangements.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
We have significant pension obligations within a defined benefit scheme. The funding position of our pension plans is affected
by the performance of the financial markets, particularly the equity and debt markets, and the interest rates used to calculate our
pension obligations for funding and expense purposes. Annual pension contributions are calculated based upon our pension
funding status, interest rates, and other factors. If the financial markets perform poorly, we could be and have been required to
make additional large contributions. The equity markets can be volatile, and therefore our estimate of future contribution
requirements can change significantly in relatively short periods of time. Similarly, changes in interest rates can affect our
contribution requirements. In a low interest rate environment, the likelihood of higher contributions in the future increases.
Competition
The markets for our products are characterised by rapidly changing technologies and evolving industry standards. We face intense
competition in these markets and new products are expensive to develop and bring to market. Our success depends, in substantial
part, on the timely and successful introduction of new products and upgrades of current products to comply with emerging industry
standards and to address competing technological and product developments carried out by our competitors. The research and
development of new, technologically-advanced products is a complex and uncertain process requiring high levels of innovation
and investment within the Motorola Solutions group, as well as the accurate anticipation of technology and market trends. Many
of our products and systems are complex and there may be delays within Motorola Solutions group in their development and
introduction of new products or technologies in the future. We may focus our resources on technologies that do not become widely
accepted or are not commercially viable. In addition, our products may contain defects or errors that are detected only after
deployment. If our products are not competitive or do not work properly, our business will suffer.
It may be difficult for us to recruit and retain the types of highly-skilled employees that are necessary to remain competitive.
Competition for key technical personnel in high-technology industries is intense. We believe that our future success depends in
large part on our continued ability to hire, assimilate, retain and leverage the skills of qualified engineers and other highly-skilled
personnel.
Regulation
We are subject to a wide range of product regulatory and safety, consumer, competition, worker safety and environmental laws.
Compliance with existing or future laws could subject us to future costs or liabilities, constrict our ability to sell, expand or acquire
facilities, and generally impact our financial performance.
Long-term contracts
We are exposed to risks under large multi-year system contracts that may negatively impact our business. We enter into large
multi-year system contracts with large customers. This exposes us to risks, including: (i) the technological risks of such contracts
when they involve new technology, and (ii) financial risks under these contracts, including the estimates inherent in projecting
costs associated with large contracts and the related impact on operating results. Financial risks arising from such contracts are
mitigated through Transfer Pricing arrangements with fellow group entities.
Technology
We rely on complex information technology systems and networks to operate our business. Any significant system or network
disruption could have a material adverse impact on our operations, sales and operating results. We rely on the efficient and
uninterrupted operation of complex information technology systems and networks, some of which are within Motorola Solutions
and some are outsourced. All information technology systems are potentially vulnerable to damage or interruption from a variety
of sources including, but not limited to, computer viruses, security breach, energy blackouts, natural disasters, terrorism, war and
telecommunication failures. Security systems and controls are placed within our systems to reduce the possibility of unauthorised
external access, and training is given to all staff so that they can recognise and prevent attempts by unauthorised parties to breach
. those security protocols. Critical data is backed up and held securely so that can be restored, if necessary, following a system
failure.
Foreign currency
Foreign currency hedges are held at an international level by Motorola Solutions, Inc. not at individual company level. Motorola
Solutions UK Limited is therefore exposed to exchange rate currency fluctuations which could lead to potential losses in the future.

Revenue Recognition
The Company’s material revenue streams are the result of a wide range of activities, from the delivery of stand-alone equipment to
custom design and installation over a period of time to bundled sales of equipment, software and services. The following specific revenue
recognition policies are followed:,
Products and Equipment - For product and equipment sales, revenue recognition generally occurs when products or equipment have been
shipped, risk of loss has transferred to the customer, objective evidence exists that customer acceptance provisions have been met, no
significant obligations remain and allowances for discounts, price protection, returns and customer incentives can be reliably estimated.
Recorded revenues are reduced by these allowances. The Company bases its estimates on historical experience taking into consideration
the type of product sold, the type of customer, and the type of transaction specific in each arrangement.
Long-Term Contracts (including contracts meeting definition of construction contracts) - For long-term contracts that involve
customisation of the Company’s equipment or software, the Company generally recognises revenue using the percentage of completion
method based on the percentage of costs incurred to date compared to the total estimated costs to complete the contract. Revenue for
such contracts is stated at cost appropriate to their stage of completion plus attributable profits, less amounts recognised in previous years.
The amount of profit attributable to the stage of completion of a long-term contract is recognised when the outcome of the contract can
be foreseen with reasonable certainty. In certain instances, when revenues or costs associated with long-term contracts cannot be reliably
estimated or the contract involves unproven technologies or other inherent hazards, revenues and costs are deferred until the project is
complete and customer acceptance is obtained. When current estimates of total contract revenue and contract costs indicate a contract
loss, the loss is recognised in the period it becomes evident.
Services - Revenue for services (including software maintenance, technical support and unspecified upgrades) is generally recognised
tateably over the contract term as services are performed.
Software and Licences - Revenue from licences is recognised at the inception of the arrangement, presuming all other relevant revenue .
recognition criteria are met.

16 Contingent assets and liabilities
GMP Underpin
The defined benefit pension scheme includes a ‘GMP underpin’ whereby members who were contracted out of SERPS (the State
Earnings-Related Pension Scheme) between 17 May 1990 and 6 April 1997 are guaranteed to be reimbursed from the Pension Scheme
to the extent that the value of the pension assets which were invested separately during the contracted out period are insufficient to
provide for the Guaranteed Minimum Pension (GMP). The data held by the pension administrators are incomplete in respect of these
members including the fund data, and the calculation of the liability at 31 December 2025 cannot be accurately assessed. From the
limited data available, the potential liability at 31 December 2025 is estimated to be between £0.3m and £2.6m (2024: £0.4m to £2.6m).
The best estimate is calculated as £0.7m (2024: £0.9m).
However, there is uncertainty whether any liability will actually arise at the time of each member's retirement, due to the impact of future
conditions, including market values of pension assets and changes in actuarial assumptions. Where pension assets held outside the
scheme are sufficient to meet the GMP no refund is due to the pension scheme.
On the basis that the GMP underpin liability is uncertain and not capable of being accurately estimated due to the lack of data available,
the directors have therefore not made any provision within the pension scheme liabilities for the GMP underpin and have instead disclosed
a contingent liability. .
Virgin Media case
In June 2023, in the case of Virgin Media vs NTL Pension Trustees IT Limited, the High Court judged that amendments made to the
Virgin Media scheme were invalid because they were not accompanied by the correct actuarial confirmation. On 25 July 2024, the Court :
of Appeal upheld the June 2023 High Court decision. The Court's decision could have wider implications, affecting schemes that were
contracted-out on a salary related basis and made amendments between April 1997 and April 2016.
The UK govemment introduced legislation in May 2026 to allow affected pension schemes to retrospectively obtain the necessary
actuarial confirmations.
The trustees continue to monitor potential implications and do not believe there to be any additional liabilities requiring quantification
within the financial statements.

Class action proceedings
In December 2024, the Competition Appeal Tribunal (CAT) received an application to begin proposed collective proceedings against
Motorola Solutions entities including the Company, as well as Airwave Solutions Limited, a related group undertaking, and Motorola
Solutions, Inc, the ultimate parent company. The proposed class representative is funded by the UK Home Office and alleges that
“Motorola occupied a dominant position on the relevant market and has charged excessive and unfair prices for Airwave Services" in the
period January 2020 to July 2023, in contravention of the Competition Act 1998. In September 2025, the CAT certified the proposed
collective proceedings on an "opt out" basis. In December 2025, Airwave Solutions Limited filed its defence, and the Class
Representative filed her reply in January 2026. In March 2026, the CAT listed the trial for 6-8 weeks commencing in October 2027. The
CAT ordered a split trial into two phases with a potential second hearing to be held in 2028 to address the question of "pass on" defence
if the Class Representative is successful in the first trial (October / November 2027). Motorola Solutions UK Limited directors are
complying with all legal requests.
The Directors are aware that there is a risk of a material economic outflow, to the group, should the proceedings continue and result in
the claim being upheld. The claim is at an early stage with expert evidence and discovery yet to be completed, there are unknowns around
the number of claimants, the size of their claims and likelihood of any potential outflow. Therefore, at this stage, the Directors cannot
reasonably estimate any potential liability and have made no provision in the accounts. Motorola Solutions Inc. has confirmed that it
will provide financial support to Motorola Solutions UK Limited and Airwave Solutions Limited should the claim be upheld.
