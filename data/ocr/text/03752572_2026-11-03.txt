4.2 Revenue
The Company recognised revenue using the five-step model contained within IFRS 15. The five steps are
as follows:
. Identify the contract(s) with a customer
. Identify the separate performance obligations in the contract
. Determine the transaction price
. Allocate the transaction price to the separate performance obligations
: Recognise revenue when the entity satisfies the performance obligation.

. The Company does not expect to have any contracts where the period between the transfer of the
promised goods or services to the customer and payment by the customer exceeds one year. As a
consequence, the Company does not adjust any of the transaction prices for the time value of money.
Sale of hardware
The company sells a range of meters. Revenue is recognised when control of the products has
transferred which is when the products are delivered to the customer. Delivery occurs when the products
are shipped to the specific location, the risks of obsolescence and loss have been transferred to the
customer, and either the customer has accepted the products in accordance with the sales contract, the
acceptance provisions have lapsed, or the company has objective evidence that all criteria for acceptance
have been satisfied. Performance obligation is delivery of the hardware which is fulfilled when the risk and
reward passes over to the customer. Freight charges are charged separately. They are not shown as
revenue but as a reduction of costs as the freight charges are classified as a cost reimbursement.

The company sells prepaid unit meters and revenue is recognised in full when the performance obligation

is fulfilled which is the delivery of the meters.

BMS projects are project sales in which the company installs lighting in show rooms. This work can take

between three and four months. The customer normally pays after the completion of the project. Revenue

is also recognised with the completion of the work. For IFRS 15 purposes, revenue is recognised as each

separate performance obligation in the contract is fulfilled.

Delivery of electric and gas meters is a straight sale business. Revenue is recognised when hardware is

dispatched which is the performance obligation.

Service revenue

a) Data collection

Contracts for data service and collection have a term of between 2 to 5 years. Reading service can be

provided daily, weekly, monthly or quarterly. Revenue is recognised when reading is performed which is

deemed to be the performance obligation. In line with group accounting policy, this service is comparable
: to the energy data management and web portal service in other countries and as this service is provided

continuously, revenue is recognised over time.

With Minute View the customer views the energy usage in consistent intervals on heat, water, electricity

and humidity which is provided by a portal. In line with the group accounting policy, this service is

comparable to the energy data management and web portal service in other countries and as this service

is provided continuously, the revenue is recognised over time.

b) Heat statements
Heat statements are provided to property owners on a quarterly or annual basis and the statements are
used by the customer to claim governmental incentives. The provision of the statement is deemed to be

c) Pre-paid unit (PPU) service :
The service consists of maintenance of the installed hardware and the recharge of the deposit of the
- customer. Related fees:are the PPU service fee and the PPU change of tenancy fee. The PPU service fee
is an annual fee but revenue is recognised monthly. The change of tenancy fee is for move in/move out of
tenants and is normally charged when it occurs. As this fee is very similar to the move in/move out fee in
other countries, the accounting treatment is that it is treated as a separate performance obligation and
revenue is recognised at one point of time (when the service is performed/is invoiced). PPU
commissioning is an additional service which is treated as a separate performance obligation and revenue
is recognised when the commissioning is done.
d) Non-haif hourly electricity and data collection
NHH and data collection is a service in which the company provides data monthly to the customer. The
customer is invoiced one month later but revenue is accrued in the month in which the data is read and
the performance obligation in the contract fulfilled. ;
. e) EMS support and call out .
EMS support is a stand-by service. The fee is invoiced annually but revenue is recognised monthly. The
performance obligation is the stand-by over the year and revenue recognition is over time.
f) Energy management report
Energy management report is very similar to the energy data management. The company takes the
collected reading data and provides the customer with a report. The report can be provided monthly,
quarterly or yearly. The provision of the report is deemed to be the performance obligation and therefore ~
revenue is recognised when the report is provided.
, g) Energy procurement
The procurement of energy service is where the company acts as an agent. The company brings its
customer and an energy supplier in contact. If they close an energy supply agreement, the company
receives a commission based on the energy consumption. The performance obligation is fulfilled when the
customer closes a contract with the energy supplier. Therefore, revenue needs to be recognised at that
point in time. As the consideration is variable, revenue needs to be estimated. Management estimates this
using expected data usages based on past events and current usage lock ins. The revenue is adjusted
when a better estimation can be made.

Water audit is a service the company offers to identify water usage anomalies due to over consumption or

a refund.

Audit electricity and gas is an energy consulting service which is provided on a monthly basis when the
: , performance obligations are fulfilled. Revenue from this service is recognised every month.

(ii) The Company as a lessee

4.10 Impairment of tangible and intangible assets other than goodwill
At the end of each reporting period, the Company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and
intangible assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an
impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to
determine the extent of the impairment loss (if any). When it is not possible to estimate the recoverable
amount of an individual asset, the Company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit
to which the asset belongs. When a reasonable and consistent basis of allocation can be identified,
corporate assets are also allocated to individual cash-generating units, or otherwise they are allocated to
the smallest group of cash-generating units for which a reasonable and consistent allocation basis can be
identified.

. Intangible assets with indefinite useful lives and intangible assets not yet available for use are tested for
impairment at least annually, and whenever there is an indication that the asset may be impaired.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use. In assessing value ;

_in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate ~
that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for
which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.

\f the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying
amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount.

An impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a

: revalued amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease (see note 4.8).

When an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the asset (or a cash generating
unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but so that the increased carrying
amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined had no impairment loss
been recognised for the asset (or cash-generating unit) in prior years. A reversal of an impairment loss is
recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in
which case the reversal of the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation increase (see note 4.8).

Lifetime ECL represents the expected credit losses that will result from all possible default events over the
