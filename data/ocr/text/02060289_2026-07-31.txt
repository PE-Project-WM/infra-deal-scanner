Strategic Report

The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025.

Principal activities

Teleperformance Limited provides services to many of the most respected brands in the UK and across the globe by

providing customer care, technical support, customer acquisition, digital solutions, analytics, back-office and other

specialized services to ensure consistently positive customer interactions. Within our contact centres and cloud
campus network of highly skilled employees, Teleperformance interacts with clients’ customers through a wide
variety of channels including voice, e-mail, SMS, web-chat and social media. The company strategy of transitioning
from a customer service provider to a digitally-integrated customer and business support provider is now advanced
with ’non-voice’ revenues climbing steeply each year.

The Company is a subsidiary of the largest outsourced omnichannel customer experience management service

organisation in the world, Teleperformance S.E. Group.

Review of business & future developments

Business focus

In 2025, the primary focus of the Directors has been to continue to deliver excellent service to our wide portfolio of

clients while keeping our employees engaged and delivering a portfolio of business improvement projects. 2025 has

also been a particularly successful year for gaining new clients and business growth which, as part of our strategy,
has also required focus and investment to ensure success

The priorities of the Directors throughout 2025 are summarised in the following statements:

e Ensuring our operating model evolves to remain a market leader — this includes deploying several technology
advancements — several using AI capalities to support our clients service and cost optimisation. It also includes
ensuring our delivery operations can seamlessly operate as either WAH (work at home — which of course
increased substantially post pandemic), increased hybrid working and also increased in-site working in our
network of operational centres, several of which we have made major investment into in 2023-2025 and will
continue this into 2026

¢ Delivering market leading collecgue engagement practises and culture to drive significant levels of employee
engagement and continued focus on people strategies relating to diversity, well-being and reward. In 2025
Teleperformance Limited was re-accredited as a ‘Great Place to Work’ (large organisations) and as a “Best
Place to Work for Women’ (super-large organisations)

e Enhancing our transformation capabilities with key investment in people and digital technology to provide
advanced and innovative solutions for our clients ,

e Continuing focus on driving growth of both revenues and profitability

¢ Continuing to apply strong governance throughout the business and ensuring relentless focus on Risk &
Compliance on behalf of our clients

In addition to the priorities set out above, we also continued to support increasing levels of offshore transition with

our South Africa business (which remains a 100% owned subsidiary of Teleperformance Limited). Following

material growth in the last 3 years, sales in South Africa continued to grow at levels way in advance of the market.

The majority of contracts in 2025 remain Teleperformance existing UK clients who want to benefit from the

efficiencies and the similar levels of service our Teleperformance teams in South A frica can deliver. In addition, we

also see more companies moving their UK in-house operation directly to Teleperformance South Africa as a result
of those same benefits and trust we can deliver these types of transitions seamlessly.

Business focus (continued)
The business continues to focus on its core business offering high quality, strategic solutions on behalf of major
public and private sector clients. It is firmly established as one of the largest outsourced customer management
providers in the UK market. The Company is frequently nominated for and wins various industry specific awards
and has an excellent reputation in the industry. The Company is fully committed to delivering the best practice in
standards, policies, security and procedures of the wider global Teleperformance S.E. Group.
Business risks and uncertainties
The Directors continue to monitor a range of external risks and uncertainties that could affect the Company’s
operations and market environment. These include global macro-economic conditions and ongoing geopolitical
tensions, including recent developments in the Middle East.
While such geopolitical events, including the conflict involving Iran, may contribute to broader economic volatility
and uncertainty, they have not had, and are not expected to have, a direct operational or financial impact on
Teleperformance Limited’s UK operations. The Directors will continue to assess developments closely as part of the
Company’s ongoing risk management framework.
Capital investment
The Group’s capital investment programme during the year formed part of its long-term strategy to support
sustainable growth, operational resilience and service quality. Investment decisions were aligned to the Board’s
assessment of current performance, anticipated future requirements and the efficient deployment of capital.
In the UK, capital expenditure continued to focus on maintaining and enhancing the Group’s operational
infrastructure and technology platform. Investment in facilities was targeted at providing safe, efficient and
adaptable working environments across the UK network, supporting both office-based and work-at-home delivery
models. During 2025, the Group opened a new operational hub in Manchester, replacing an existing facility within
the city. The new site increases capacity and includes dedicated space to support collaboration with clients.
The Group also continued to invest in technology and digital capability to support workforce productivity,
information security and service continuity. During the year, more than 2,000 personal computers were deployed
across the UK business as part of the ongoing refresh of end-user technology, incorporating systems and
applications developed both internally and by external providers.
Total capital expenditure in the UK during the year amounted to £2.6 million.
In addition to UK investment, Teleperformance Limited supported capital investment across its wholly owned
subsidiaries in line with the Group’s strategy to diversify delivery locations and enhance operational resilience. In
South Africa, this included a £5.2 million commitment to expand existing facilities in Cape Town and to establish a
new operational centre in Gqeberha, increasing capacity to support existing and new client programmes. Investment
also continued in TP Ireland, reflecting the growth of current operations and the addition of a new major client
during the year, with further expansion expected during 2026.

Security
The global Teleperformance S.E. Group recognise the ongoing risks pertaining to data protection, fraud and physical
security and continues to maintain an enhanced level of ontrol through the “Global Essential Compliance and
Security Policies” (GECSP) programme. Teleperformance Limited and its subsidiaries follow the same GECSP

Financial results
At a total reported level, 2025 turnover was £385.6m, an increase of £108.7m (+39.3%) versus 2024 (£276.9m).
Revenue continues to be derived primarily from UK based clients. The increase in the ‘core’ year on year revenues
was attributable to a number of factors, primarily:
e Revenues from existing client volume growth across a number of sectors as we work more closely with clients
to expand their services and capabilities
e Revenues from new business wins secured in 2024 and early 2025
¢ Strong growth in both government and financial services in which there were multiple contract wins and
renewals in 2024 and 2025. :
The business delivered an Operating profit in 2025 was £28.7m (7.5%), compared with £21.7m (7.8%) in 2024, an

Other
The business has significant fixed assets, the value of which it is confident of realising through future trading
activity. In addition, the company has recovered its trade debtor and accrued income balances since the year end so
no impairment of any amounts is anticipated. The business will continue to invest to support ongoing activities.
Management uses a range of Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to monitor the performance of the business and to
support effective decision-making.
The principal financial KPIs include revenue, gross margin, and operating profit, which are consistent with the

performance and profitability trends.
In addition, management monitors non-financial KPIs to assess operational effectiveness and workforce
sustainability. A key non-financial KPI is the average number of employees, which was 10,797 in 2025 (2024:

delivery capability, and cost efficiency.
Continued significant investment in the IT infrastructure and security protection environment will ensure quality and
resilience of the service provision. Investment and operating strategies will be continually reviewed and amended to
match client needs.

Going concern
Accounting standards (in particular LAS 1.25 and 1.26) require the Directors to make an assessment of an entity's
ability to continue as a going concern, taking into account all available information about the future, which is at
least, but is not limited to, twelve months from the end of the reporting period. Management’s going concern
assessment must be updated to consider all relevant information available up to the date the financial statements are
authorised for issue.
These financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. The Company’s available cash position
remains strong, with £1.4m in cash at bank, and a cash pool receivable of £46.4m held centrally by group (see note
below), providing total available liquidity of £47.8m as at 31 December 2025.

Consequently, the Directors are confident that the Company will have sufficient funds to continue to meet its
liabilities as they fall due for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the financial statements and therefore

Corporate Governance code
As a subsidiary of Teleperformance S.E. Group, Teleperformance Limited falls under the group corporate
governance structure. Teleperformance S.E. Group adheres to the corporate governance code of listed companies of
the AFEP and the MEDEF, and sets out in its public results and on the corporate website its governance framework.
The corporate governance framework of Teleperformance S.E. Group is structured as follows:
« The Board of Directors (in the form of a Conseil d’administration). It sets the strategic guidelines for
Teleperformance’s activities and oversees their implementation. The Board considers all matters regarding the
operations of the Group and, through its deliberations, settles any issues arising. It is composed of a majority of
independent members
« The Executive Management. The governance structure set up in March 2026 is based on a Chairman and
Chief Executive Officer, an expanded Management Committee in terms of skills, experiences and expertise
including the Executive Committee. The combination of the functions of Chairman of the Board and
Chief Executive Officer the Group to assert a more transparent, rectilinear and, above all, flexible management
organization structure in order to encourage the acceleration of the strategic decision-making process and
decision-making circuits so that decisions can be implemented more quickly to meet the Group’s current and
future challenges
«The Executive Committee. The Executive Committee is responsible for the Group’s operational management.
It meets at least twice per month. It implements the strategic orientations, ensures the coherence of the actions
. undertaken by all of the subsidiaries and discusses the major operational initiatives necessary to the
development of the Group and to its performance
In the performance of its missions and duties, the Board is assisted by two specialised Committees: the
Remuneration & Appointments Committee and the Audit & Compliance Committee.
The works performed by the Committees, which report on their work after each of their meetings, assist the Board
"of Directors in its discussions and decision making. The Board Committees work on assignments entrusted to them
by the Board. They actively prepare their works and inform the Board of all points which appear to raise an issue or
require a decision, thus facilitating its deliberations. They also provide any advice and recommendation to the Board
as falls within their remit, but have no power of decision, subject to the decisions that the Audit & Compliance
Committee may adopt pursuant to applicable legal and regulatory provisions, under the responsibility of the Board.
The Remuneration & Appointments Committee issues opinions and recommendations regarding: ©
e the determination of the remuneration policy and of the benefits granted to executive officers, including
determining the variable portion by assessing the definition of the rules for setting this variable portion and the
annual application of these rules;
¢ the overall policy for granting performance shares, together with the conditions attached to the final vesting of
these shares;
e the global amount and rules of allocation of directors’ fees;
¢ — the succession plans; and :
© the candidates for membership of the Board of Directors, their status as independents, and annual review of
such quality in accordance with the, criteria defined by the AFEP-MEDEF code and/or the renewal of terms of
office of directors

Going concern approach

Management’s going concern assessment was based on consideration of the following different factors:

e Variable forecasts based on key client volumes, particularly those clients whose business is expected to grow
further and clients whose business could contract due to inflationary pressures and economic outlook (such as
retail clients)

e Variable forecasts based on key client volumes, where volumes are expected to move offshore to enable clients
to deliver large cost savings (whether to Teleperformance Limited 100% owner subsidiary in South Africa or to
other Teleperformance operations in countries such as Ghana, Kenya and Nigeria)

« Retention of key clients, particularly within the public sector

e Clients with a preference for office based working for Teleperformance staff, whilst others continue to
preference to prefer home based working, particularly with the new and constantly developing home working
processes and policies that have been rolled out since the pandemic

e Different assumptions regarding reductions in capital expenditure, other investments and overheads, partly due
to the work at home model, meant that a new and evolving approach to the Company’s estate, property, has
been taken post pandemic and this has continued for 2025 and 2026 planning

The view of the Directors following this assessment was that the Company is in a strong position and will have

sufficient funds to continue to meet its liabilities as they fall due for at least 12 months from the date of approval of

the financial statements. The Company has a healthy balance sheet, no non-group debt, a strong sales pipeline, and
