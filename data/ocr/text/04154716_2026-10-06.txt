STRATEGIC REPORT

The directors present their Strategic Report for Talkmobile Limited (the ‘Company’ for the year ended 31
March 2026.

General information

References to ‘the Group’ throughout this report and financial staternents refer to Vodafone Group Plc and.
its subsidiaries and associated undertakings. References to ‘VodafoneThree’ throughout this report and
financial statements refer to VodafoneThree Holdings Limited and its subsidiaries. References to ‘Vodafone ~
UK’ throughout this report and financial statements refer to Vodafone Limited and its subsidiaries.

Review of the business and future developments

The principal activity of the Company is the provision of telecommunications services in the UK as a mobile
virtual network operator. The Company continues to serve the existing Pay Monthly base that has access to
VodafoneThree's network in the UK. Acquisition of new customers continued throughout the financial year.
The directors do not expect any significant changes to the principal activity of the Company in the
foreseeable future.

Key performance indicators for the financial year are customer base (number) and in-bundle Average
Revenue per User (ARPU’). The average customer base increased to 737,323 (2025: 582,608).

In-bundle ARPU increased 1.4% to £7.12 per month (2025: €7.02) due to changes in the mix of tariffs sold
during the year.

Total revenue increased by 27.6% to £73,305,000 for the year ended 31 March 2026 (2025: £57,437,000).
The growth in revenue was driven by the gains in average customer base, with continued demand for
Talkmobile products in a value centric market, along with the year-on-year increase in ARPU.

An operating profit of £55,235,000 (2025: £41,749,000) was generated in the year, an increase of 32.3%.
The increase in operating profit was primarily as a result of the 26.6% increase in the customer base during
the year and a slight increase in overall ARPU.

The objective for the year ahead is to maintain customer service for theexisting pay monthly base and
continue acquiring new contract customers.

The directors recommended a final dividend of £215,000,000 to the Company's immediate parent company
(2025: €nil). Further details are provided in note 11.

Financial position and liquidity

The Statement of Financial Position on page 16 of the financial statements shows the Company's financial
position at the end of the year. .

Net current assets increased from the prior year by 22.2%, with cash generated in the business being :
deposited under central treasury arrangements with other companies in the Group.
The major source of liquidity for the Company comes from cash generated from trading activities. The Group
treasury function provides a centralised treasury service to the Company, including managing its cash flow
risks and follows a framework of policies and guidelines authorised and reviewed annually by the Group's
management.

Principal risks and uncertainties

VodafoneThree, created by the merger of Vodafone UK and Three UK, brings significant strategic

opportunities alongside potential risks associated with integration and scale. The Company, as part of

Vodafone UK, has maintained a consistent approach to risk management during this period of change,

supported by its Risk Management Framework and dedicated governance arrangements, including the

VodafoneThree Board Audit and Risk Committee and Executive Risk and Compliance Committee.

VodafoneThree has identified the following principal risks which, due to their potential impact on

customers, operations, financial performance and reputation, could affect the delivery of our strategy.

These risks are monitored throughout the year, with in depth reviews presented to the relevant oversight :

committees. The principal risks, together with the compensating controls and mitigations to support

effective operations within appetite, are set out below.

a) Cyber threat .
VodafoneThree processes and stores significant volumes of customer and commercial data across its
Business and Consumer operations. A major cyber incident could result in data loss, service disruption,
and damage to customer trust. The merger has increased the scale and complexity of the technology
and data environment, creating additional cyber integration risks that are being actively managed

, through enhanced security controls and governance, continuous vulnerability scanning, security
testing, and threat intelligence alerts.

b) Technology resilience and service availability
VodafoneThree relies on the resilience and availability of its IT systems to support critical business
processes and deliver services to customers. A significant system outage could lead to service
interruption, reduced customer satisfaction and loss of revenue. The integration of two large
technology estates increases complexity, system interdependencies and operational change activity.
These risks are being managed through structured integration planning, resilience testing and the
progressive adoption of common technology standards and controls.

c) Network resilience and delivery :

. VodafoneThree is dependent on the resilience and ongoing development of its network to deliver
services to customers and meet regulatory expectations. A prolonged network outage or delay to our
network delivery plan could result in loss of customer and regulator confidence. Network integration
activities increase delivery and operational complexity, with governance and resilience controls in place
to maintain service stability throughout. This risk is managed through life cycle management of
network assets, capacity monitoring and forecasting, operational monitoring, resilience testing, and
strong delivery governance.

Managing risks and uncertainties is an integral part of successfully executing our strategic objectives and —

delivering long term success following the merger. Risks are not static and as the internal and external

environment changes, some risks may increase or diminish while new risks may emerge. We continue to
monitor current macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty and any potential impact to VodafoneThree.

As part of the risk management process, several additional risks with varied likelihood, potential impact and

priority are managed on an ongoing basis; the risks listed above are those considered the highest priority.

Section 172(1) statement
The Company's long-term success is central to the Directors' discussions and decision making. In
accordance with section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 each of the directors acts in the way that they
consider, in good faith, would most likely promote the success of the Company for the benefit of its
members as a whole. In doing so, section 172 requires the directors to have regard, amongst other matters,
to the:
a) likely consequences of any decisions in the long-term;
b) need to foster the Company's business relationships with suppliers, customers and other key
stakeholders;
c) impact of the Company's operations on the community and the environment;
d) need to protect and maintain the Company's reputation for high standards of business conduct; and
e) need to act fairly as between members of the Company.
Stakeholder engagement
The Company takes stakeholder engagement seriously because it appreciates the fundamental need to
build a holistic view of its business to promote a strategy which takes account of the broader operating
environment. Directors benefit from improved insight into the needs of our stakeholders, provoking
discussion of the potential risks and opportunities for our business in satisfying those needs and
understanding the potential impact of decisions on affected stakeholders. Better insight and a diversity of
perspectives lead to more productive and balanced discussions among the Board of Directors on complex
issues and as a result, decisions are well considered.
The Company's Board of Directors is committed to engaging with stakeholders directly wherever possible.
Provided below is an overview on how the Company has had regard to section 172 matters during the year.
Further details on our engagement with stakeholders including the way we engaged and responded as a
Group can be found in the financial statements of Vodafone Group Plc for the year ended 31 March 2026
on its website wwwvodafone.com.
Suppliers
During the year, the Company operated within the Group under a global supply chain management .
function. Vodafone UK is helped by circa 850 suppliers who partner with it. These range from start-ups and
small businesses to large multinational companies. Our suppliers provide us with the products, services and
the devices we need to deliver our strategy and connect our customers.
The Group engaged with its supply chain through regular collaborative performance reviews, safety forums,
events, conferences, and site visits. Purpose criteria relating to planet, diversity and safety are included in
tenders and the Group conducts supplier audits and assessments. Key topics from this engagement
included driving health and safety standards, net-zero supply chains and supplier/product innovation.
Vodafone UK hosted an annual supplier safety forum, where the Group worked together with industry peers
and suppliers through the Joint Alliance for Corporate Social Responsibility GAC). This collaboration
included taking corrective actions for human rights within the supply chain. Additionally, the Group
continued with its environmentally linked supply chain finance programme, which offers benefits to
suppliers in the UK.

regardless of their confidence with technology. .

Net zero
In 2020, Vodafone Group set a series of ambitious objectives and established greenhouse gas emissions
reduction targets which were approved by the Science Based Targets initiative (‘SBT’) in line with reductions
tequired to keep warming to 1.5°C. For Vodafone UK, we expedited our target to achieve net zero emissions
across our operations to 2027. Vodafone is committed to achieving net zero emissions status across its
complete value chain by 2040.
Vodafone UK has been powered by 100% renewable electricity since July 2021, and we have already cut
carbon emissions from our operations by 88% (scope 1 and 2 market-based emissions vs 2019/20
baseline). This is being delivered through a major rationalisation and efficiency programme, moving to
electric vehicles, and powering our business and network with renewables.

e Conducted an external and internal feasibility assessment of maintaining the Company's existing net

zero targets;

1.4 Revenue recognition
When the Company enters into an agreement with a customer, goods and services deliverable under the
contract are identified as separate performance obligations (‘Obligations’) to the extent that the customer
can benefit from the goods or services on their own and that the separate goods and services are considered
distinct from other goods and services in the agreement. Where individual goods and services don't meet
the criteria to be identified as separate Obligations they are aggregated with other goods and/or services in
the agreement until a separate Obligation is identified.

The Company determines the transaction price to which it expects to be entitled in return for providing the

promised Obligations to the customer based on the committed contractual amounts, net of sales taxes and

discounts.

The transaction price is allocated between the identified Obligations according to the relative standalone

selling prices of the obligations. The standalone selling price of each Obligation deliverable in the contract

is determined according to the prices that the Company would achieve by selling the same goods and/or
services included in the obligation to a similar customer on a standalone basis; where standalone selling
prices are not directly observable, estimation techniques are used maximising the use of external inputs.

Revenue is recognised when goods and services are delivered to customers and payment remains probable.

Revenue for device sales to end customers is generally recognised when the device is delivered to the end

customer. Revenue for the provision of services, such as mobile airtime, is recognised when the Company

provides the related service during the agreed service period. /

different from those in which they are included in the Company financial statements. Deferred tax assets
are recognised to the extent that it is regarded as more likely than not that they will be recovered. Deferred
tax assets and liabilities are not discounted.
