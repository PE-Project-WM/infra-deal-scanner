The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
REVIEW OF BUSINESS
Thunes is a group of companies dedicated to the seamless facilitation of cross-border transfers and
payments. Thunes global network has grown to reach new markets and multiple payment options in more
than 128 countries, 500 network partners and 80 currencies. As an infrastructure provider, our network
connects mobile wallet operators, corporates, merchants, money transfer operators and banks. As the
network grows, it drives an exponential increase in payments flow which leads to cost reductions and
improved efficiency.
The company considers revenue to be a key performance indicator in assessing the company’s financial
performance and growth. For the year ended 31 December 2024, the Company has achieved $78,060,116
(2023: $55,267,001). The Company reported a profit for the year of $2,957,441 (2023: loss of $2,654,226).
The improved financial performance in the current year reflects the continued expansion of the Company's
network and customer base. In the prior year, the Company reported a loss, mainly due to a higher operating
cost base relative to its level of revenue.
As at 31 December 2024 the company had total assets of $234,196,270 (2023: $194,215,826), total equity
of $10,532,386 (2023: $133,481) and total liabilities of $223,663,884 (2023: $194,082,345).
PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES :
The company is a financial institution regulated by Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) in the United Kingdom.
The operational, financial, compliance and business risks are continuing to be judiciously managed by the
. company.
The company has a risk-mitigating strategy in place to address the operational, compliance and financial
risks. The company also has a well-defined on-boarding process including end-to-end testing and has robust
Processes and procedures in place to resolve any exceptions. Business risks arising from market forces are
constantly reviewed and addressed by the company's management and team.
FUTURE PLANS
In the next 3 years, the company will continue to focus on emerging market coverage and build a leading
global network. The company is continuing to enhance its capabilities to serve the global economy in
compliance, technologies, and operations. We believe that our capabilities that provide efficient and
simplified process, same day transaction and competitive transparent foreign exchange rate would address
the many pain points that business payments are experiencing today.
The company will continue to leverage the growth from our existing business. The forecasted revenue growth
is a function of increasing transaction volume with existing customers through new corridors and securing
contracts with new customers.

17. FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS Financial risk management Overview
The company has exposure to the following risks arising from financial instruments:
- liquidity risk
- market risk
- currency risk
- interest rate risk
Financial instruments are not used for speculative activity and complex financial instruments are not
entered into by the Company. This note presents information about the company's exposure to each
of the above risks, the company’s objectives, policies, and processes for measuring and managing
risk.
Risk management framework
Risk management is integral to the business of the company. The company has a system of controls
in place to create an acceptable balance between the cost of risks occurring and the cost of managing
the risks.
The management continually monitors the company's risk management process to ensure that an
appropriate balance between risk and control is achieved.
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the company will encounter difficulty in meeting the obligations associated
with its financial liabilities that are settled by delivering cash or another financial asset. The company's
approach to managing liquidity is to ensure, as far as possible, that it will always have sufficient
liquidity to meet its liabilities when due, under both normal and stressed conditions, without incurring
unacceptable losses or risking damage to the company's reputation.
Typically the company ensures that it has sufficient cash on demand to meet expected operational

. expenses for a period of 60 days, including the servicing of financial obligations; this excludes the
potential impact of extreme circumstances that cannot reasonably be predicted, such as natural
disasters.
