The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Review of the business
The company is an established Critical |T managed service provider focused on both public sector and enterprise
customers. Services provided range from basic co-location through to complex integrated managed services and
cloud solutions. The company provides its customers with a flexible range of services and choice around
-technology, finance, cloud platforms and location. Most customers need a trusted partner, who can deliver a fully
managed hybrid service, tailored to their individual business requirements, locating technology on-premise and in -
: one of our eco-friendly data centres.

The company is part of the US-based Thrive group. The Thrive group is a leader in the delivery of managed Next
Generation IT services particularly in the areas of Cloud computing and Cyber Security solutions.
The company continued to focus on building more !T Services revenue within both its established customer base
and new customers. Provision of IT services was 88% (2024 — 84%) of total company revenue with the balance
being the resale of IT equipment and licences.
The skills and expertise of the company’s staff continue to be recognised in the marketplace. The company has
continued investing in staff training to acquire and retain the necessary vendor accreditations. The company
continued to be certified under BS 10012:2017 (Data Protection), 15027001 (Information Security Management)
and 1SO9001 (Quality Management). Information security remains of utmost importance to the company and the
company holds the Cyber Security Services 3 accreditation which allows the company to tender more widely for the
provision of IT services to UK public sector clients.
The company analyses performance in certain key performance indicators. Turnover reduced by £0.6m or 2%.
Resale of equipment and licences reduced by £1.5m whereas sale of IT services continued to increase to £29.2m.
Gross profit margins reduced from 39% to 34% reflecting a higher level of sale of lower margin subscriptions and a
reduction in the level of salary costs recharged to the company’s parent in the US.
Company EBITDA for the year was a profit of £135k compared to a profit of £3,323k for the previous year. This
reflected the lower sales and gross profit margin noted above, restructuring costs and a reduction in the level of
salary costs recharged to the company’s parent in the US.
Trade debtors reduced from £3,419k at 31 December 2024 to £1,891k at 31 December 2025 reflecting lower
invoicing in December 2025 reflecting the ending of support for a customer at the end of December 2025. Deferred
income reduced from £3,249k at 31 December 2024 to £1,411k at 31 December 2025 similarly reflecting the ending
of support for the customer in December 2025.
The net assets of the company reduced from £8,065k (as restated - refer to note 3) at 31 December 2024 to
£7,733k at 31 December 2025. The movement reflected the loss for the year of £332k. :

Promoting the success of the company
The directors of the company have a legal responsibility under section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 to act in a
way that they consider would most likely promote the company’s success for the benefit of its members as a whole
and to have regard to the long-term effect of their decisions on the company and its stakeholders.
The company was established in 1992 and has built a reputation as a trusted partner in the design, installation and
management of its customers’ critical IT infrastructure. It has over 34 years of experience and know-how making it
one of the longest-established IT businesses in the UK. :
The company is very much a people-business. The directors recognise that the most valuable asset of the company ;
is its employees and prioritises employee engagement and investment. As an Investor in People, the company is
proud that some employees have been with the company since its establishment. The directors are proud of the
ways in which the company has provided employment, training and financial reward to its employees over many
years.
The company has always focused on building long-term, trusting relationships with its customers. Some have been
working with the company for over 30 years. The company adopts a consultancy led approach to solutions which :
delivers significant value to its customers. The skills and experience of the employees has allowed the company to
continuously improve quality and service.
- The company represents, and is highly regarded by, key IT vendors including Cisco and Microsoft. It has been a
Gold Partner of Cisco for many years. As with its approach to employees and customers, the company focusses on
: building strong working relationships with suppliers as they are integral to the provision of excellent customer
service and solutions.
The company is located in London, Luton and Nottinghamshire. Whilst the company serves customers across the
UK, it also works with a number of loca! organisations including charities and schools. It is an important employer in
each location and, together with its staff, it has supported a number of local charities. The directors recognise the
company’s environmental responsibilities and are focused on improving the efficiency of energy consumption.
The directors are committed to the highest standards of corporate behaviour. All employees are required to read
and understand the company’s Code of Conduct which sets out the expectations of behaviour in the many aspects
of business. The company also requires all employees to undertake regular training in areas such as preventing
discrimination and harassment, anti-bribery and anti-corruption.
Principal risks and uncertainties 7
The major risks to the business identified by the directors include:
a) Foreign exchange risk
. Whilst the company purchases products and services predominantly in UK pounds, the underlying cost prices of
most of the products and services that are resold to customers are denominated in US dollars whilst selling prices
are generally denominated in UK pounds. The company minimises this foreign exchange risk by only buying to
customer order and voiding customer quotations if the exchange rate moves by more than 2%.
b) Product development risk :
Fast and constantly changing customer requirements, along with increased technological innovation contribute to
the risks and challenges associated with developing new electronic interconnect products.
c) Recruitment and retention of key employees
The ability of the business to recruit and retain talented employees is a critical part of the culture of the business.
Loss of key employees or an inability to attract employees with the required skill sets could have an impact on the
company’s operations.
: d) Economy and competition risk
The level of economic activity in the IT Services industry will impact the performance of the business and the
company’s products are subject to competition from peers, some of which have significant resources.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The company’s main financial instruments comprise cash and various trading items such as trade debtors and
creditors. The main purpose of these instruments is to provide finance for the company’s operations.
There are limited risks arising to the company as a result of these instruments and the directors agree policies for
the management of these risks, which are summarised below. The policies have remained unchanged from the
previous period:
a) Credit risk
The company seeks to minimise any credit risk by dealing only with established or financially sound clients. It also
establishes clear contractual relationships and identifies any credit issues in a timely manner. The directors continue
to monitor credit risk across the business and the level of business failures both in the UK and internationally.
Detailed credit checks are always performed on all new customers where the exposure for non-payment is
considered material. Alternatively, for new customers, the company will aim to agree an advance payment or
deposit when the customer places an order.
b) Liquidity risk ,
The company seeks to minimise financial risk by ensuring that appropriate liquidity is available to meet foreseeable
requirements.

Accounting convention
These financial statements have been prepared in compliance with United Kingdom Accounting Standards,
including Financial Reporting Standard 102, “The Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and
Republic of Ireland” ("FRS 102”) and the requirements of the Companies Act 2006, including the provisions of
the Large and Medium-sized Companies and Groups (Accounts and Reports) Regulations 2008.
* The financial statements are prepared in sterling, which is the functional! currency of the company. Monetary
amounts in these financial statements are rounded to the nearest £.
The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis, under the historical cost convention.
. The principal accounting policies adopted are set out below. .
Exemptions for qualifying entities under FRS 102 .
This company is a qualifying entity for the purposes of FRS 102, being a member of a group where the parent
of that group prepares publicly available consolidated financial statements, including this company, which are
intended to give a true and fair view of the assets, liabilities, financial position and profit or loss of the group.
The company has therefore taken advantage of exemptions from the following disclosure requirements:

. Acquisition of trade and assets
: On 1 January 2024, the company acquired the business and assets of Edge TG Limited and Custard
Technical Services Limited, companies under the common control of the group headed by the company's
immediate parent undertaking, Thrive UK Holdings Limited. Both the trade and asset acquisitions met the
definition of a group reconstruction under FRS 102 and satisfied the criteria for the application of merger
accounting. Accordingly, the company applied the hybrid accounting method to account for these acquisitions
in the prior year.
Under the hybrid accounting method, the acquired assets and liabilities were recognised at their existing book
values, as reported in the selling companies immediately prior to the acquisition. No adjustments were made
to reflect fair values at the acquisition date, nor were any new identifiable assets or liabilities recognised that
would otherwise arise under the acquisition accounting method. As a result, no goodwill was recognised on
acquisition. The only adjustments made were to align accounting policies between the entities. In accordance
with generally accepted accounting practice under FRS 102, the results of the acquired businesses were
recognised in the company’s financial statements prospectively from the date of acquisition.

Turnover
Turnover is recognised at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable for goods and services
provided in the normal course of business, and is shown net of VAT and other sales related taxes. The fair :
value of consideration takes into account trade discounts, settlement discounts and volume rebates.
The company recognises revenue when the significant risks and rewards of ownership have been transferred
to the buyer, the company retains no control over the goods/services, the amount of revenue can be
measured reliably and it is probable that future economic benefits will flow to the company.
Revenue is recognised based on the following principles:
Cloud, co-location and managed services are recognised based on the services provided in the month where
the quantum of such services is variable or straight line over the contract term where the contract gives a right
to continual access to the services.
Third party support services are recognised at the start of the period invoiced as the company has no ongoing
obligations once the third party is engaged.
Sales of hardware / software and professional services are recognised on a principal basis and on delivery.
Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets are initially measured at cost and subsequently measured at cost, net of depreciation
and any impairment losses. Cost includes the original purchase price, costs directly attributable to bringing
the asset to its working condition for its intended use, dismantling and restoration costs. :
Depreciation is provided on all tangible fixed assets, other than freehold land, at rates calculated to write off ,
the cost of each asset to its estimated residual value on a straight line basis over its expected useful life, as

, follows:

Freehold land and buildings are held under the cost model and are carried at their cost at the date of
purchase less any subsequent accumulated depreciation and subsequent accumulated material impairment
losses.
The gain or loss arising on the disposal of an asset is determined as the difference between the sale
proceeds and the carrying value of the asset, and is credited or charged to profit or loss.
Impairment of fixed assets :
At each reporting period end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible assets to

indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the :
impairment loss (if any). Impairment losses are recognised in profit and loss.

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12

Financial instruments are recognised when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the

Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when

Basic financial assets :

impairment of financial assets

Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or

future discounted payments through the expected life of the loan. For all other financial liabilities amortised
cost is transaction price less amounts settled.
Derecognition of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities are derecognised when the company’s contractual obligations expire or are discharged or
cancelled.

Taxation :
The tax expense represents the sum of the current tax expense and deferred tax expense. Current tax assets
are recognised when tax paid exceeds the tax payable. :
Current and deferred tax is charged. or credited to profit or loss, except when it relates to items charged or
credited to other comprehensive income or equity, when the tax follows the transaction or event it relates to
and is also charged or credited to other comprehensive income, or equity.
Current tax assets and current tax liabilities and deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities are offset, if
and only if, there is a legally enforceable right to set off the amounts and the entity intends either to settle on
the net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Current tax is based on taxable profit for the year. Current tax assets and liabilities are measured using tax
rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date.
Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply to the period when the asset is realised
or the liability is settled based on tax rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting
date.
Deferred tax liabilities are recognised in respect of all timing differences that exist at the reporting date. Timing
differences are differences between taxable profits and total comprehensive income that arise from the
inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in different periods from their recognition in the
financial statements. Deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered by the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
Employee benefits
The costs of short-term employee benefits are recognised as a liability and an expense. The cost of any
unused holiday entitlement is recognised in the period in which the employee's services are received.
Termination benefits are recognised immediately as an expense when the company is demonstrably
committed to terminate the employment of an employee or to provide termination benefits.
Retirement benefits
For defined contribution schemes the amount charged to profit or loss is the contributions payable in the year.
Differences between contributions payable in the year and contributions actually paid are shown as either
accruals or prepayments.
Leases
Leases are classified as finance leases whenever the terms of the lease transfer substantially all the risks and
rewards of ownership to the lessees. All other leases are classified as operating leases.
Assets held under finance leases are recognised as assets at the lower of the assets’ fair value at the date of
inception and the present value of the minimum lease payments. The related liability is included in the
statement of financial position as a finance lease obligation. Lease payments are treated as consisting of ,
capital and interest elements. The interest is charged to profit or loss so as to produce a constant periodic
rate of interest on the remaining balance of the liability.

. Rentals payable under operating leases, including any lease incentives received, are charged to profit or loss
on a Straight line basis over the term of the relevant lease except where another more systematic basis is
more representative of the time pattern in which economic benefits from the leases asset are consumed.

3 Prior period adjustment
In the prior year, on 1 January 2024, the company acquired the business and assets of Edge TG Limited, a
company under common control of the group headed by the company’s immediate parent undertaking. The
: Asset Purchase Agreement between the company and Edge TG Limited stated that the Purchase Price was
equal to the aggregate book value of the values attributed to the assets less the value of the assumed
liabilities. The Purchase Price was satisfied via the issue of a Promissory Note by the company to Edge TG
Limited. The principal amount of the debt under the Promissory Note was equal to the Purchase Price set out
in the Asset Purchase Agreement, and interest was calculated daily from 1 January 2024. In the prior year, on
30 April 2024, a Deed of Release was entered into between the company and Edge TG Limited, under which
Edge TG Limited agreed to discharge and release the company from its obligations and debt in respect of the
Promissory Note. .
Management have concluded that the aggregate book value of the values attributed to the assets less the
value of the assumed liabilities, in respect of the business and assets acquired from Edge TG Limited was -
overstated by £1,473,304. The overstatement was in respect of an intra-group debt due from the wider US
group recorded as part of the book value of the assets acquired. Management have concluded that the
principal amount of the debt recorded under the Promissory Note was therefore overstated by the same
amount. Management have concluded that the interest payable under the Promissory Note (prior to the Deed
of Release) was also overstated by £22,160 as a result of the overstatement of the principal, and that the
capital contribution (recognised when the Deed of Release was entered into) was overstated by £1,495,464
(the amount of the overstatement of principal and interest under the Promissory Note).
Management considers the effect of the errors related to previous periods to be material and, in accordance
with FRS 102- Section 10 ‘Accounting policies, estimates, and errors’,'a prior period adjustment has been
recognised to correct the accounting retrospectively.
