Strategic Report
for the year ended 31 December 2025
The Directors present the Strategic Report of Vocalink Holdings Limited (the "Company") for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activities
The principal activity of the Company is that of a holding company. It is the parent company of Vocalink Limited ("VLL"), Vocalink
International Limited ("VIL") and certain other entities which are dormant; these entities and their subsidiaries constitute the
"Vocatink Group".
Review of business and future outlook
The Company's ultimate parent undertaking and controlling party is Mastercard Incorporated ("Mastercard"). The Mastercard
strategy is to grow and protect its core payments business, diversify into new customers and geographies, and to build new
capabilities through a combination of organic and inorganic strategic initiatives to which the Company is fully aligned. The shared
mission is to power economies and empower people worldwide delivering a wide range of payment choices and making transactions
: secure, simple, smart and accessible.
In the year ended 31 December 2025, the Company incurred a loss of £17.0m (2024: £41.3m), primarily due to finance costs on
intercompany loans of £66.7m (2024: £57.8m). At 31 December 2025, the Company had net liabilities of £281.1m (2024:
£264./m). During the year, the Company made additional investment in VIL of £30.0m (2024: £75.0m) and received distributions
in kind of £141.2m (2024: £Nil) from IPCO 2012 Limited ("IPCO") comprising of amounts due from group undertakings. IPCO
had ceased trading in November 2024. During the year, there was a reversal of impairment on the Company’s investment in VLL
of £32.9m (2024: £Nil) following the extension of certain revenue contracts. The carrying value of the investments at the year ended
31 December 2025 was £967.7m (2024: £1,046.0m).
As a subsidiary of Mastercard, the Company is aligned to Mastercard's strategy of growing its core payments business, diversifying
into new customers and geographies and building new capabilities. Protecting and enhancing Mastercard's investment in the
Vocalink Group's UK and International business remains key to this strategy. VLL, which focuses on the UK market, returned to
profitability in 2025 and extended several key service contracts to ensure sustainable growth from January 2026. VIL's offering of
Real Time Payments (RTP) to the international market continues to grow, requiring continued investment in payments innovation,
enhancing its proposition and strengthening the security and resilience of its services to support this growth. As a result, VIL posted
an operating loss in 2025, though as a result of cost efficiency measures, the loss reduced compared to the prior year.
With the value of transactions processed using RTP technology predicted to continue to substantially grow, understanding the
evolution of markets, what technologies and stakeholders have supported them, and anticipating future developments will assist the .
Vocalink Group and Mastercard to maximise the full potential.
Financing and Investment
The Company is supported by capital investments from an intermediary Mastercard company, Mastercard Payment Gateway
Services Limited ("MPGS").
Tn 2025, the Company entered into an additional intercompany loan arrangement with MPGS of £19.0m repayable by 1 December
2025. In December 2025, MPGS approved an extension of the maturity date of seven intercompany loan arrangements of
£562.2m from 1 December 2025 to 1 December 2026. Loans from group undertakings which are due in 2026 amount to £813.2m
as at 31 December 2025. Please refer to Note 8 of the financial statements for more information on these loans.
The Directors have considered the letter of support received from Mastercard International Incorporated ("MCI"), an intermediary
parent of the Company. This letter of support provides proportionate financial support for a period of at least 12 months from the
date of signing these financial statements.
Key performance indicators ("KPIs")
As the Company's principal activity is that of a holding company, the Directors are of the opinion that there are no meaningful
financial or non-financial KPIs to understand the development, performance and position of the Company as part of the group
headed up by Mastercard; its ultimate parent company. The Directors review the carrying value of the investments and consider
their recoverability. Any impairment or reversal of impairment would be reflected in these financial statements, see Note 6 for more
information on these investments. . :

Risk Management Framework
Effective risk management is fundamental to the sustainable success of Mastercard and is an integral part of the ongoing
management of its operations. Mastercard has adopted an Enterprise Risk Management Framework (“ERMF"), applicable to
Mastercard and its subsidiaries, to ensure a consistent approach to identifying, assessing, measuring, managing and reporting on
risks and on emerging risks in the broader ecosystem which have the potential to disrupt operations and impact customers. The
ERMF also sets out the guiding principles and boundaries to establish, communicate and report on risk management and to help
foster a risk-aware culture, drive growth and innovation and support decision-making.
Internal audit
Internal Audit is accountable for providing independent and objective assurance and advisory activities designed to add value and
improve Mastercard’s operations and control environment. It supports the Company to accomplish its objectives by bringing a
systematic and disciplined approach to evaluating and improving the design and operating effectiveness of governance, risk
management, and internal controls. Interna! Audit adheres to the Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA) standards.
Principal Risks
The key risks that could impact the Company’s medium-term performance remain broadly consistent with those disclosed in the
Company’s Annual Report and Financial Statements for the year ended 31 December 2024. No material changes to the Company's
principal risks have been identified during the 2025 financial year. The Company's principal focus is on managing and enhancing
the performance of its investments, with key risk factors expected to influence outcomes outlined below.
Strategic Risk
The Company's key strategic risks relate to the broader Vocalink Group, specifically the Company's active trading subsidiaries,
VLL and VIL. Maintaining excellent and resilient services and high business standards is key to managing these strategic risks and
securing new business opportunities to maximise the Company's investments.
Regulatory Risk
The Company's subsidiary VLL is subject to the supervision of the Bank of England and also falls within the regulatory perimeter
of the Payment Systems Regulator (PSR), the UK's economic regulator for payment systems industry. As announced by the UK
government in March 2025, the PSR will be largely consolidated into the Financial Conduct Authority over the coming years.
Regulatory risk is managed through a risk-based approach, that is aligned to risk appetite and informed by proximity to potential
disruptions. Regulatory legal and compliance teams support Mastercard and its subsidiaries and are responsible for monitoring and
assessing regulatory developments and for reporting regularly to management. The Vocalink Group and Mastercard maintain open
and transparent engagement with regulators and actively participate in regulatory and industry forums, events, and roundtables to
remain informed of developments across the wider global payments ecosystem.
Financial Risk :
The key financial risk that the Company is exposed to is liquidity risk which arises on its loans due to related parties and credit risks
which arises on amounts due from related parties. However, these risks are considered limited as the underlying financial instruments
are with companies within the group headed by Mastercard Incorporated, the Company's ultimate parent company. As noted
previously, the Company received an extension on seven intercompany loan arrangements of £562.2m from 1 December 2025 to |
December 2026.
As a holding company, the Company is also subject to the risk that the cost of its investments in subsidiaries may not be fully
recoverable depending on performance, market conditions and other risks. The Company does not have any material exposure to
other financial risks, including foreign exchange risk.
Reputational Risk
As a Mastercard company, the Company acknowledges the importance of managing its reputation and brand effectively.
Maintaining a positive industry perception is crucial for its operations, as any negative publicity or litigation potentially could impact
current and future business opportunities.

17 Investments in subsidiaries
Investments in subsidiaries held as non-current assets are stated at cost less any provision for impairment. The need for any
impairment is assessed by comparing the carrying value of the investment with the recoverable amount, which is defined as the
higher of fair value less costs of disposal or value in use, see note 6.
Management has determined the recoverable amount of the investment by assessing the fair value less costs of disposal. The fair
value less cost of disposal is the price that would be received to dispose an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction
between market participants at the measurement date under current market conditions, less incremental costs directly attributable to
the disposal of an asset or cash generating unit ("CGU"), excluding finance costs and income tax expense. The valuation has been
determined using the income approach and is considered to be level 3 in the fair value hierarchy due to unobservable inputs used in
the valuation. Key assumptions used to determine the fair value less costs of disposal as disclosed in note 1.11.
If the carrying amount of an investment exceeds its recoverable amount, an impairment is immediately recorded.
If the recoverable amount of a previously impaired investment exceeds its carrying value and the increase is due to a change in the
estimates used to determine the recoverable amount since the last impairment loss was recognised, impairment is reversed only to
the extent that the revised carrying value does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined had no impairment
loss been recognised for the investment in prior years.
