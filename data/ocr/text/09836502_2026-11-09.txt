STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2025.
BUSINESS REVIEW
In line with group strategy, Workiva UK Limited grew during 2025, with turnover increasing to £41.7
million (2024: £33.7 million) as it added customers and expanded its footprint in the United Kingdom.
Growth was supported by increased investment in its employees, with the average number rising to 196
; employees (2024: 153). . ;
Administrative expenses for the year were £40.4 million (2024: £32.1 million), the increase resulting
from higher staff costs and foreign exchange losses. Profit for the year was £1.3 million (2024: £1.6
, million) and net assets at the year end were £6.0 million (2024: £4.7 million).
. GOING CONCERN
Workiva Inc., the Company’s ultimate parent, has confirmed in writing that it will provide financial
support to the Company, to enable it to meet its liabilities as they fall due, for at least 12 months from the
date of approval of these financial statements. Workiva Inc. has also confirmed that it has the resources to
provide this support. On this basis, the directors consider the going concern basis of preparation to be
appropriate.
KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS
There are no additional KPIs considered by management other than those already discussed within the
business review section.
PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The board considers the matters described below to be the principal risks and uncertainties the business
faces.
The Company’s activities expose it to a variety of financial risks: foreign currency risk, credit risk,
liquidity risk and interest rate risk. The Company’s overall risk management programme focuses on the
unpredictability of financial markets and seeks to minimise potential adverse effects on the Company’s
. , financial performance.
(a) Foreign currency risk
The Company is exposed to foreign currency risk on transactions and monetary balances
denominated in currencies other than sterling. The Company recognised a net foreign
exchange loss of £431,668 in the year (2024: gain of £254,889).
The Company does not have a foreign currency hedging policy. The Company monitors
its foreign currency exposure closely and will consider hedging significant foreign
currency exposure should the need arise.
(b) Credit risk ;
The Company’s credit risk arises from amounts owed by group undertakings (principally
: Workiva Inc.), trade receivables and cash at bank and in hand. The Company does not
obtain collateral.

1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES (CONTINUED)
Functional and presentational currency
The financial statements are presented in pounds sterling, which is the Company's functional currency.
Amounts in these financial statements are rounded to the nearest £1, except where otherwise stated.
Taxation ,
Taxation comprises current and deferred tax expense. Tax is recognised in profit or loss, except to the
extent that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity. Current and
, deferred tax are not discounted.

Current tax is the amount of tax payable on the taxable profit for the year, using tax rates and laws
enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date.
Deferred tax is recognised on timing differences at the reporting date, using the tax rates expected to
apply when the differences reverse. Deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable
they will be recovered against future taxable profits.
Foreign currencies
Transactions in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rate ruling on the date of the
transaction. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are retranslated at the rates
tuling at the reporting date. Exchange gains and losses are recognised in profit or loss.
Operating leases

: Rentals under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the lease term.
Any lease incentives received are recognised as a reduction of the lease expense over the lease term.
Share-based payments
The Company participates in a share-based payment arrangement operated by Workiva Inc., under which
stock options and awards are granted to employees of Workiva Inc. and its subsidiaries, including the
Company. The Company has elected to recognise and measure its share-based payment expense on the
basis of a reasonable allocation of the group expense recognised in the consolidated financial statements
of Workiva Inc. The directors consider that using the element of the group charge relating to unvested
options and stock awards granted to the Company's employees is a reasonable basis for allocating the
expense. .
The expense is recognised in profit or loss with a corresponding credit to equity as a capital contribution.
Amounts recharged by Workiva Inc. in respect of these awards are recognised as a deduction from equity.
Turnover
Turnover comprises subscription and support revenue, professional services revenue, and revenue from
services provided to Workiva Inc. under an International Services Agreement for marketing, support

1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES (CONTINUED)
Subscription and support revenue
Revenue is recognised on a straight-line basis over the contract term, from the date the service is made
available to the customer. Contracts are generally 12 to 36 months, non-cancellable, and typically billed
annually in advance, with some customers electing to pay multiple years in advance. Access to the
platform and related support services is treated as a series of distinct services which comprise a single
performance obligation because they are substantially the same and have the same pattern of transfer.
: Professional services revenue and customer options
Professional services revenue primarily consists of fees for document set-up, XBRL tagging, and
consulting with customers on business processes and best practices. The Company has determined that an
agreement to purchase these professional services constitutes an option to purchase services rather than an
agreement that creates enforceable rights and obligations, because of the customer's contractual right to
cancel services that have not yet been used. In the limited case of agreements where the Company has
determined that the option provides the customer with a material right, a portion of the transaction price is
allocated to.the material right based upon the relative standalone selling price. Agreements that do not
contain a material right are accounted for when the customer exercises its option to purchase additional
services. Revenue is recognised for document set-ups when the service is complete and control has
transferred to the customer. Revenue from XBRL tagging and consulting services is recognised as the
services are performed.
Contracts with multiple performance obligations
Some contracts with customers contain multiple performance obligations. For these contracts, the
Company accounts for the individual performance obligations separately if they are distinct. The
transaction price is allocated to the separate performance obligations on a relative standalone selling price
basis. Standalone selling prices are determined based on the Company's overall pricing objectives, taking
into consideration market conditions and entity-specific factors, including the value of the arrangements,
length of term, customer demographics and the numbers and types of users. :

periods. The portion of deferred income that the Company expects to recognise after more than twelve
months is presented as deferred income due after more than one year, and the remainder is presented as
current deferred income.
