The directors of Siemens Energy Limited (the “Company”) present the annual report containing a strategic report, directors’ report and the financial

companies

1.1. Principal activities
Siemens Energy Limited offers a wide range of products, systems, solutions, and services in the fields of power transmission, central and distributed
power generation, as well as industrial applications for the oil and gas industry and for industrial process applications.
. .
1.2 General business review
As in the prior year, business performance benefited from the ongoing strength in the energy market. The development was driven by the
continuing transition of the energy market and, therefore, the increase in demand for electricity. This had a positive effect on the order intake and
revenue as well as the Profit development.
The directors work closely with management to anticipate risks from economic or global factors and plan accordingly. The Company remains
vigilant of warning signs exhibited in both the global economy and domestic economy. An evaluation of the potential impact of market factors is
undertaken regularly by the management so that the Company can respond appropriately. Risks around the macro-economic factors have not had
a material impact on the business and there is no impact on the figures presented as at September 30, 2025.
The directors have reviewed the ongoing geopolitical tensions and have not noted any risk or business impact to date but continue to monitor the
potential risks for inflationary cost increases and supply chain challenges. The Company continuously updates its business practices with regard to
local laws and regulations to ensure business continuity in all working environments. As a result, the Company has continued to operate and trade
successfully. This view is underpinned by business continuity planning, risk management and an integrated internal control framework.
The directors are also closely monitoring the evolving strategic and policy landscape in support of achieving net zero in the United Kingdom ("UK")
by 2050, and the outcomes from the 29th UN Climate Change Conference of the Parties (“COP29"). The Company is involved in a wide range of
activities to support its customers in their energy transition journey, and this is helping to shape the Company's response to the market.
.
1.3 Review of Statement of Income
The Company made a net income in the financial year, net of taxation of £63m (2024: £13m). The Company's profitability level is higher compared
to prior year, as the results of operations are affected by the portfolio mix sold in each segment. The business performance in 2025 is in line with
the directors’ expectations, and they believe the result for the year reflects a consistent trading performance of the Company, due to continuing
transition of the energy market.
In the current year, the volume and structure of sales were slightly lower compared to the prior year. Revenue decreased by (4)% year-on-year,
primarily due to the completion of larger projects in the prior year and the new projects being in their initial stages, where the revenue is recognised
under the percentage of completion method.

1.4 Review of Statement of Financial Position
The Company’s net assets position increased from £147m in the prior year to £214m in the current year, due to the improvement in business performance.
However, it remained in the net current liabilities position of £(128)m at the year-end (2024: £(147)m).
The Statement of financial position has total assets amounting to £949m (2024: £791m) and total liabilities amounting to £735m (2024: £644m). The balance
sheet movement reflects the operational movements of the year.
Total assets increased by £158m in the current year, largely due to increase in receivables from Group companies (£150m, mainly cash pooling balance),
property, investment in a subsidiary (£38m), plant and equipment (net increase of £6m) and contract assets (£2m); offset by decrease in trade receivables
(£41m), inventories (£2m) and tax receivable (£5m).
Total liabilities increased by £91m in the current year, primarily due to increase in contract liabilities (£48m), trade payables (£42m, net of external and
intercompany payables) and corporation tax payable balance {£11m); offset by decrease in other liabilities (E7m) and provisions (£3m, net of current and non-
current).
Equity increased by £67m in the current year, due to the profit generated by the business and gains on derivative financial instruments in the year.

1.6 —_— Principal risks and uncertainties

The directors undertake regular assessments of the key risks impacting the Company. As part of the Group's Enterprise Risk Management process ("ERM"), the

Company has imp!emented a co-ordinated set of risk management and control systems, including strategic planning and management reporting, to help

anticipate, measure, monitor and manage its exposure to risk. The process aims for early identification, evaluation, and response to risks and opportunities

that could materially affect the achievement of our strategic, operational, financial, compliance and climate-related objectives across the entire organisation.

It is not the ultimate purpose of ERM to avoid or eliminate business risks, but to support entrepreneurial decision-making by finding the right balance of

managing risks and pursuing opportunities. The key areas identified, and the related controls are as follows:

«Risks related to project execution: The Company engages in large and complex projects, where execution may take several years. These contracts are
exposed to risks, which are identified and evaluated for every project. Nevertheless, the business may see deviations from its execution plan and related
calculated cost. This is particularly true in projects with untested or new technology. In certain cases, there are risks of unforeseeable project-related
changes. To address those risks, the Group have established a global project excellence organisation to monitor a transparent risk assessment and approval
process for bid submission, a standard model for project execution, a curriculum to systematically improve the capabilities of our project management
personnel as well as a continuous improvement programme focused on lessons learned, in order to prevent failure repetition.

¢ Credit risk: The business activities could be negatively affected if the customers or suppliers do not have access to financing on economically viable terms,
Furthermore, the Company may incur unexpected losses on financial instruments if the credit quality of the contractual partners deteriorates or if they
default on, or fall behind schedule with, their payment obligations, arising from trade receivables. The level of receivables and amounts overdue is moni-
tored closely. In some areas, specific measures are in place to reduce the credit risk.

« Liquidity risk: In order to maintain liquidity to ensure that sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations and future developments, the Company
uses an intercompany loan account (Group cash pooling facility). Further details regarding liquidity risk can be found in the accounting policies in the
directors’ report and note 2 in the financial statements.

@ = Economic risk: The directors work closely with management to anticipate risks from economic or global factors and plan accordingly. The Company has
remained vigilant over the warning signs exhibited in the global economy and uncertainty in the UK economy. An evaluation of the potential impact of
market factors is undertaken regularly by the management so that the Company can respond appropriately. Equally, the directors have reviewed the recent
geopolitical tensions and have not noted any financial statement impact to date but continue to monitor the potential for inflationary cost increases and
supply chain challenges.

e Threats to Cybersecurity: The global increase in cybersecurity threats and higher levels of professionalism in cybercrime pose a risk to the security of
products, systems and networks and to the confidentiality, availability, and integrity of the Company’s data. The Group attempts to increase overall cyber-
security resilience by employing proven cybersecurity professionals and by utilising several measures, including awareness measures for its staff on a global
scale, target oriented employee training, comprehensive monitoring of the networks and systems through cybersecurity defence teams, and by using
security systems such as firewalls and virus scanners.

e — Climate related risks: This information can be found in the TCFD-aligned reporting on pages 11 to 15 in the Strategic Report.

1.7 Section 172 (1) Statement

The directors of the Company must act in accordance with a set of general duties. These include a duty under s.172 of the Companies Act 2006 to promote the

success of the Company for the benefit of its members, and in doing so have regard (amongst other matters) to:

1) the likely consequences of any decision in the long term;

2) the interests of the Company's employees;

3) the need to foster the Company's business relationships with suppliers, customers and others;

4) the impact of the Company's operations on the community and the environment;

5) the desirability of the Company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct; and

6) the need to act fairly as between members of the Company.

directors of the Company (“Board”). The Board uses forums such as the Extended Management Team (“EMT”) plus the Siemens Energy Management Meeting

(“SEMM”} cascades delivered via the EU Hub updates and other sessions to ensure they are well informed to make key decisions. All Board members participate

in the EMT and EU Hub cascades, which have representation from all Business Units and Support functions. Given the size and nature of Siemens Energy in the

UK, stakeholder engagement often takes place at both an operational and senior management level as well as by the Board, the mechanisms that are in place

provide a good basis to ensure stakeholder feedback is considered in the operation of the Company.

1.8 Task Force on Climate related financial disclosures (TCFD-
aligned climate disclosures)
Reporting requirements under sections 414C, 414CA and 414CB of the Companies Act 2006, and the Companies (Strategic
Report) (Climate-related Financial Disclosure) Regulations 2022
Introduction
Sustainability is an integral part of the Siemens Energy AG (the “Group’’) strategy, which is embedded in every Group subsidiary in the world, including Siemens
Energy UK companies (“SE UK” or the “Company”). To create transparency on the Company’s climate actions, the Company’s directors disclose how they
address risks and opportunities arising from climate change. To do so, the directors are committed to implement the recommendations and guidance provided
by the UK Government on Task Force Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD), and to meet the requirements of the EU's CSRD regulation published by the
Group.
Governance
a. Board Oversight
The Siemens Energy AG (the “Group”’) Supervisory Board oversees and advises the Executive Board in respect of the management of the Group, including the
management of sustainability matters. The Supervisory Board resolves, based on the recommendations of the Presiding Committee, on the diversity concepts
for the Supervisory Board and the Executive Board. It sets, based on the recommendations of the Remuneration Committee, the variable compensation targets
for the Executive Board and determines whether they have been achieved. It considers risks and opportunities when approving the annual budget of the Group.
The Executive Board is responsible for the Group's assessment of sustainability matters, such as climate-related impacts, risks and opportunities, and defines
the sustainability program and targets. It is likewise responsible for ensuring that the Group operates in compliance with applicable laws and upholds ethical
standards in its business activities. It has delegated certain tasks to the individuals, bodies, businesses, regions and functions described in more details below.
The Executive Board reports to the Supervisory Board at least quarterly. Where necessary, these reports include information on risks and opportunities, and
activities to assess, manage and monitor them. In addition, the Executive Board reports to the Supervisory Board on the Group’s strategy at least once a year.
The Executive Board reports to the Sustainability and Finance Committee at least twice a year specifically on sustainability matters such as progress against
sustainability targets, implementation of the sustainability program and key disclosures. It reports to the Audit Committee at least twice a year on the Group’s
risk management system and internal control system, which include climate-related risks and opportunities and activities to assess, manage and monitor them.
Within the Executive Board, the President and CEO, Dr. Christian Bruch, has been appointed Chief Sustainability Officer (CSO) of the Group and has held this
role during the reporting period. The CSO is responsible for coordinating and overseeing all activities with respect to sustainability, including for identifying and
managing IROs.
The Sustainability Council is chaired by the CSO and coordinates the Group’s sustainability activities. It holds three meetings per year and consists of decision-
makers representing Business Areas, Regional Hubs, and Functions. The Sustainability Council acts as a sounding board to the CSO and supports Executive

a. Climate-retated Risks and Opportunities
Siemens Energy AG (the “Group”) identifies and assesses climate-related impacts, risks, and opportunities (IROs) across the value chain, using a double mate-

+ Key risks: Transition risks (market, technology, reputation), physical risks (weather events, chronic climate change).
+ Key opportunities: Business growth in low-carbon technologies.
b. Impact on Business, Strategy, and Financial Planning
Climate-related risks and opportunities are integrated into strategic framework through asset management, strategic planning, and workforce development.
The Group conducts a physical climate risk assessment covering 75% of our major manufacturing locations. These locations are selected based on their physical
footprint (measured in square meters) to prioritise those with the highest potential exposure to climate-related hazards. The assessment is aligned with the
asset life cycles, focusing on the years 2030 (short-term), 2060 (medium/long-term), and 2100 (long-term outlook).
It is based on the IPCC’s Shared Socioeconomic Pathways. These pathways model a range of future climate conditions, all of which use pre-industrial tempera-
tures recorded in the period from 1850 to 1900 as a baseline for projected warmings:
+ §SP1-2.6: A low-emission scenario assuming ambitious climate action, with projected warming of 1.7°C by 2041-2060 and 1.8°C by 2081-2100. It reflects
low physical risks and low adaptation costs.
+ §SP2-4.5: A moderate scenario with delayed global action, projecting 2.0°C warming by 2041-2060 and 2.7°C by 2081-2100. It anticipates more frequent
and severe weather events.
+ SSP5-8.5: A high-emission scenario assuming continued reliance on fossil fuels, with projected warming of 2.4°C by 2041-2060 and 4.4°C by 2081-2100.
This scenario is associated with the highest physical risks and adaptation costs.
Transition risks are evaluated using a range of climate scenarios, including: .
+ S&P Global inflections: Reflects a 2.0-2.4°C trajectory, incorporating moderate policy action and growing market dynamics.
+ IEA Stated Energy Policies Scenario (STEPS): Based on the current policy framework, this scenario projects a 2.0-2.4°C outcome and considers planned
investments in clean energy
+ For market specific analysis, S&P Global Discord, a scenario focused on market fragmentation and geopolitical instability, is used. The range of scenarios
used represent the specific understanding and methodologies of these organisations. They cover plausible risks due to the diverse temperature increases
and associated socio-economic implications they represent.
The business model is considered resilient, with adaptive capacity and mitigation strategies in place.

Risk Management

a. Identification and Assessment

Climate-related risks are identified through the double materiality process and integrated into Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) system, using scenario-

based analysis and stakeholder input.

The Company follows the Group approach for identification of risk using climate scenarios.

b. Management and Integration

The Group risk management and internal control system is based on an ongoing process designed for identifying and prioritising risks to the achievement of

business objectives and at addressing these risks effectively and efficiently. The system considers the management of sustainability-related risks and opportu-

nities and those with non-sustainability relevance to be equally important.

Sustainability-related risks and opportunities are analysed through structured internal processes supported by a double materiality assessment, which facili-

tates their identification, assessment, and prioritisation.

Additionally, information on the key sustainability risks identified by the Group—along with the corresponding mitigation strategies and associated control

measures—is presented in the relevant topical standards.

Each risk as being reported through our ERM process is being assigned to a responsible risk owner, who is accountable for its regular monitoring and for

successfully executing on adequate risk mitigation measures. Company-wide risks are typically assigned to the respective corporate or global function head.

With that, any findings of the risk assessment process are constantly linked directly with relevant processes within internal functions (including any necessary

adjustments to these processes, if deemed appropriate). Similarly, within the internal controls process, each control objective is being accounted for by an

assigned company-wide process owner, ensuring that in case of any findings, these can be reflected in an adjusted controls setup.

The results of the risk management and internal controls process are reported regularly to the Executive Board on matters relating to the implementation,

operation, and oversight of the risk and internal control system and assist the Executive Board in reporting to the Audit Committee of the Supervisory Board.

Risks are managed through mitigation measures (e.g., infrastructure adaptation, portfolio transformation, supplier engagement).

Glossary of Terms
CFD Climate-related Financial Disclosure refers to UK regulations that require large companies and LLPs to report on their climate-related risks and
opportunities within their annual reports. The disclosures, which are based on the Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures
(TCFD) framework, must cover governance, risk management, strategy, and metrics and targets LO
CROs Climate-related risks are the potential negative impacts of climate change, including physical damage from events like floods and wildfires,
and transition risks from moving to a low-carbon economy (e.g., changing market demand). Opportunities involve cost savings through resource
efficiency, developing new low-carbon products and services, and gaining market access through green initiatives. Managing these risks and op-
portunities is increasingly crucial for businesses, driven by investor, customer, and regulatory demand for transparency
CSO Chief Sustainability Officer
CSRD The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) is an EU regulation that mandates expanded and standardised reporting on environ-
mental, social, and governance (ESG) issues for many companies, replacing the older Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD).
ERM Enterprise risk management (ERM) is a company-wide strategic approach to identifying, assessing, and managing risks that could prevent an
organisation from achieving its goals. It is a holistic process that integrates risk management across all departments, unlike traditional, siloed
methods, and focuses on aligning risk with strategy to help the business proactively manage threats and seize opportunities.
ESRS - European Sustainability Reporting Standards, a set of rules for companies in the EU to report on environmental, social, and governance
(ESG) issues. These standards, mandated by the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), require companies to assess and report on
their sustainability impact using a “double materiality” perspective—meaning they must report on both how their activities impact people and the
environment, and how those issues create financial risks and opportunities for the company.
GHG Protocol is a global accounting and reporting standard for greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, developed by a partnership of businesses, NGOs,
and governments convened by the World Resources Institute (WRI) and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD)
IEA The International Energy Agency (IEA) is an autonomous intergovernmental organisation, established in 1974 to respond to the 1973-74 oil
crisis. Headquartered in Paris, the IEA provides data, analysis, and policy recommendations on the global energy sector, with its work now expand-
ing beyond oil security to cover energy security, economic development, and clean energy across all fuels and technologies
IPCC Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change is the United Nations body for assessing the science related to climate change
PAS 2060 a British Standards Institution (BSI) specification for demonstrating carbon neutrality, which involves measuring, reducing, and offsetting
greenhouse gas emissions Mentioned in the

TCFD The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) was a group convened by the Financial Stability Board (FSB) in 2015 to de-
velop a common framework for companies to disclose how climate change affects their businesses
