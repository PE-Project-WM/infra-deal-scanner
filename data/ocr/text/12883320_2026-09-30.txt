GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025
Introduction
This Group Strategic Report has been prepared for Spectrum Fibre Limited (the company’) and-its subsidiary
undertakings (“the group” or “Ogi”) as a whole and therefore gives greater emphasis to those matters which are
significant to Spectrum Fibre Limited and its subsidiary undertakings when viewed as a whole.
Principal activity :

; The principal activity of the group and company is the building and operation of a fibre-to-the-premises network
across South Wales, the provision of managed IT services to business customers, and the provision of
broadband connectivity to residential and business customers.

Business review and future developments
The business review applies equally to the group and the company, as their activities are substantially aligned.
Details of the results for the year are set out in the Statement of Comprehensive Income on page 10. The loss
after tax for the year was £53,726k (2024: £32,924k) and the net liabilities at 31 December 2025 was £141,935k
(2024: £88,209k). The group's net liabilities increased to £141,935k (2024: £88,209k), reflecting the loss for the
year. Cash held at the year end reduced to £1,937k (2024: £2,999k), with reduced cash used in operations and
investing activity offset by continued funding from investor loan notes and drawdowns under the Cardiff Capital
Region facility.
The loss after tax increased in 2025. During the year, the directors reviewed the carrying value of the group's
fixed assets and goodwill for impairment. As a result, impairment charges totalling £16,454k were recognised,
comprising a £1,945k write-down of capital projects in progress and a £14,509k impairment charge arising from :
the assessment of the recoverable amount of the group's assets. Further details of the impairment assessment,
including the key judgements applied, are set out in Note 3. These impairment charges were recognised in the
Statement of Comprehensive Income and being non-cash in nature, had no direct impact on the group's cash
flows.
The increased loss also reflects higher finance costs and depreciation charges during the year. Interest payable
and similar expenses increased to £20,525k (2024: £16,722k), principally as a result of the group's borrowing
requirements, while depreciation increased to £8,973k (2024: £7,392k) reflecting the continued investment in
and expansion of the group's fixed asset base.
During the year, the group undertook a strategic transition from a build-led operating model, centred on fibre
rollout, to a customer-acquisition-led commercial strategy designed to drive market penetration, revenue growth,
and long-term sustainability. This transition occurred in a period in which the group surpassed 25,000 customers,
marking an important milestone in the maturation of the business and reinforcing the need to prioritise customer
centric growth.
To support this shift, the business implemented an organisational restructuring programme aimed at realigning
resources and capabilities with its new strategic priorities, focussed on customer-facing activity. In addition, the
Chief Executive Officer, Ben Allwright, left the business and was replaced by Sally-Anne Skinner (previously the
Chief Revenue Officer), whose appointment reflects the group’s commitment to executional discipline in
customer acquisition and commercial performance.
The group continues to be funded through a combination of investor loan notes and a term loan facility from the
Cardiff Capital Region, in line with the funding structure anticipated within the business plan. This approach
reflects the strategic intent to support growth until the operational base reaches sufficient scale. As capital
expenditure reduces as the business focusses on its customer-acquisition-led strategy, the group is entering a .
transitional period in which operating performance strengthens, revenue contribution from the growing customer
base increases, and the business moves progressively closer to achieving EBITDA positivity.

Principal risks and uncertainties

e — Liquidity risk - We are reliant on continued capital injections from investors and debt providers to deliver our
installations and strategic growth plans. We work closely with our investors to ensure they are fully aware of
all activities and key decisions made in the business, and we are confident that we can draw down sufficient
funds to support our operations.

e Market risk —- We regard competition from Openreach and its resellers as inevitable over time. In the short
term, however, we prioritised our build areas to focus on underserved communities in south Wales, to avoid
overbuilding existing fibre networks wherever possible. In the longer term, increased competition from other
full-fibre providers could impact upon the take-up of our services from customers. The group continually
monitors and updates its pricing strategy to remain competitive.

e Financial risk — Ongoing losses and access to funding, which is dependent on our operational and financial
performance.

The group uses the budget process and monthly board reporting to identify and then mitigate risks as early as

possible. :

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Key performance indicators for the group are its EBITDA and the cost of its fixed assets as a measure of the

build. EBITDA improved to a loss of £6,706k (2024: loss of £7,829k), an improvement of £1,123k, driven by

turnover growth of 26% to £9,856k (2024: £7,805k), partially offset by higher administrative costs incurred as
part of the organisational restructuring programme. These KPis are discussed as part of the business review.

Other measures used to assess the performance of the business include number of premises passed and

number of connected customers.
