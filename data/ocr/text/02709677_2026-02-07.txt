INTRODUCTION
The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 30 September 2025 and business review, which
includes the principal risks and uncertainties of the business, key performance indicators and future
developments.
BUSINESS REVIEW .
dB Broadcast Limited (the "Company’) is a leading independent technology partner in the global broadcast and
media industry and one of the world’s largest broadcast systems integrators. The Company's principal activities
include consultancy, project management, system design, equipment sourcing, installation, configuration, test,
commissioning, training, after-sales support of various broadcasting technologies; and the product design,
development and manufacture of broadcast technology as well as data hosting and managed services of
broadcast systems.
The Company continues to deliver strong and sustainable performance, driven by a stable and adaptable
operating model that enables swift strategic pivots in a fast-paced, technology-driven industry. Turnover
increased by 23%, supported by successful diversification into new markets and with new customers. This
growth, alongside improved profitability, demonstrates the effectiveness of the Company's strategy and the
strength of its operational focus.
Financial Performance
The financial results of the Company for the year ended 30 September 2025 are presented on page 11.
Turnover for the year amounted to £21,865,372 (2024 - £17,712,565), representing a significant increase on the
prior year. Profit before tax amounted to £1,183,645 (2024 - £1,042,039). The growth in turnover was driven by
an increase in both the number of projects and a change in the project mix. Whilst the project mix resulted in a
slightly lower gross margin of 11.1% from 12.6% in the prior year, profitability continued to improve year-on-year.
As at 30 September 2025, the Company reported net assets of £4,008,072 (2024 - £6,107,324). The reduction in
net assets reflects the payment of a dividend of £3m to the ultimate parent in the year, which exceeded the profit
generated during the year.
The Board of Directors are extremely pleased with the Company's performance. Growth in both turnover and
profitability exceeded expectations, and the successful delivery of new projects to new customers across new
markets has been a standout achievement. All key revenue streams — systems integration, consultancy, support,
satellite and product sales, hosting and managed services — recorded strong growth, supported by a record
number of new clients in the UK and overseas.
Despite the operational pressures associated with expansion, new client onboarding and increased investment in
research and development, the Company maintained robust profitability. These results reflect strong leadership,
strategic discipline and direction, and the continued effectiveness of the Company's diversification strategy.
Diversification Strategy
The Company's diversification strategy has been pivotal to the Company’s continued success. Continued
revenue growth from new sectors including corporate, outside broadcast vehicles, support and managed
services, highlights the effectiveness of the Company's expansion beyond its traditional project focus and its
ability to capitalise on its expertise and infrastructure.
Key to this diversification was the utilisation of the Company's cutting-edge rack room and lab facility and the on-
premise expertise housed within Aurora House, the Company's purpose-built headquarters in Cambridgeshire.
These investments have strengthened the Company's operational capabilities and enhanced its ability to deliver
value-added services to clients across a broader range of industries.

Innovation and R&D
The Company continued to invest in innovation within the broadcast and media sector, maintaining a strong
commitment to research and development. The Company made significant research and development
investments, particularly in configuration management, to remain at the forefront of technological advancements.
These investments underpin the development of new solutions and services, enabling the Company to address

Future Outlook
The Company is well-positioned to build on its growth and capitalise on recent and current investments in
innovation and infrastructure. With a broadened client base and an expanded range of services, the Company

Conclusion
The Company's ability to adapt and innovate has resulted in a strong financial performance relative to turnover

' investments in innovation position the Company for future opportunities. The Board thanks all employees for

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
As a technology company operating primarily in the broadcast and media industry, the Company faces a range
of risks and uncertainties inherent to its business activities. These risks are continually monitored and managed
to mitigate their potential impact on the Company's performance and strategic objectives. The three principal
business risks are:
1. Technological Advancement and Innovation
The broadcast and media industry is characterised by rapid technological advancements. Failure to keep pace
with emerging technologies, industry standards, or client expectations could result in reduced competitiveness
and loss of market share.
The Company prioritises investment in research and development to remain at the forefront of industry .
, innovation. By fostering a culture of continuous improvement and leveraging its state-of-the-art facilities, the
Company ensures it can deliver cutting-edge solutions that meet evolving client needs.
2. Market and Economic Volatility
Economic fluctuations and market-specific challenges, such as reduced capital expenditure by clients, can
directly impact the demand for the Company's services. Additionally, changes in global economic conditions or
geopolitical uncertainties could affect project timelines or budgets.
The Company's diversification strategy, which emphasises growth in consultancy, support, product development,
hosting and managed services, reduces reliance on any single revenue stream. A focus on long-term client
relationships further enhances revenue stability.

3. Talent Retention and Skills Shortage
The Company relies on the expertise of highly skilled engineering and consultancy professionals to deliver its
services. Difficulty in attracting or retaining key talent could impact the Company's ability to meet project
demands and innovate effectively. The Company invests in employee development through training programs,
competitive remuneration packages, and a supportive workplace culture. Collaboration with academic institutions
and participation in industry forums help maintain a pipeline of skilled professionals.
By actively managing these risks and uncertainties, the Company is well-positioned to adapt to challenges and
capitalise on opportunities in the dynamic broadcast and media industry.

FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS —
During the financial year, the Company entered into a four-year hire purchase contract for an outside broadcast
vehicle. In accordance with UK GAAP, the vehicle has been recognised as a tangible fixed asset, and the
associated customer arrangement has been classified as an operating lease.
The vehicle has been sub-leased to a customer for the same four-year term. The Directors have assessed this
hire purchase and sub-lease structure and considers it financially advantageous, providing predictable income
over the agreement period while retaining ownership of a specialist asset for potential future use beyond the
customer contract.
This arrangement introduces a new revenue stream for the Company and has contributed positively to financial
performance during the year. The Directors believe this model is consistent with the Company's strategy of
maximising returns from specialist equipment and may be expanded in the future where commercially
appropriate.
The Directors are satisfied that the obligations under the hire purchase contract can be met as they fall due and
that the arrangement does not adversely impact the Company's liquidity or ability to meet ongoing commitments.
The Company's exposure to price risk, credit risk, liquidity risk, and cash flow risk remains limited due to the
nature of its operations. The Company primarily engages with blue-chip customers, conducts credit checks on
new clients, and maintains a strong credit control function. Projects are structured to remain cash positive
: throughout their lifecycle. : :
The Company does not maintain formal financial risk management objectives or policies and does not undertake
hedging transactions. However, the Directors closely monitor the Company's cash flow position to ensure
resilience during periods of heightened risk.
