The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2025. :
Principal activities
The Company is a leading provider of designed and manufactured acoustic and communication products. Our core
offerings include electronic sounders, automotive hands-free microphones, telephony handsets, and commercial
headsets. We provide customised solutions to a diverse range of sectors, including the automotive,
telecommunications, medical, and leisure industries, continually seeking to expand our market presence through
innovation and quality.
Business review
The results for the year are set out in the profit and loss account. The position of the Company at the year-end is set
out in the balance sheet. The Company's key financial and other performance indicators during the year were as

The financial year ending 31 March 2025 was one of contrasting fortunes. The Company achieved a strong
operational performance, evidenced by a robust 18.6% increase in turnover to £6,032k (2024: £5,085k). This growth
was driven by securing new customer accounts and the successful launch of new product lines, reflecting the
company’s ongoing commitment to innovation and market expansion.
Most notably, the gross profit margin saw a significant improvement, increasing from 14.93% to 27.40%. This
reflects a more favourable product mix, improved production efficiencies, and effective cost management within the
cost of sales.
Despite this strong performance at the gross profit level, the Company reported a loss for the financial year of
£2,507k. This was primarily due to a substantial one-off administrative expense related to the recognition of a bad
debt following the unfortunate collapse of a key customer, Bioself (trading as Sensate). This single event,
accounting for over £1.4m, significantly inflated the administrative expenses for the period.
The company's stable financial base and strong underlying cash position have allowed it to absorb this exceptional .
loss without compromising its operational capabilities or strategic growth plans. The headcount increased from 64 to
. 69 to support the higher level of business activity.
Principal risks and uncertainties
+ Customer Credit Risk: The loss incurred from the Bioself collapse has highlighted the risk associated with
customer insolvency. In response, the Company has enhanced its credit control procedures and is actively
working to further diversify its customer base to mitigate the risk of over-concentration with any single
client.

Financial risk management policies and objectives
The Company's activities expose it to financial risks, including cash flow, credit, and liquidity risk. These risks are

« Cash flow risk: The Company minimises foreign currency risk by holding bank accounts in the major
currencies in which it trades. No forward exchange contracts were used in 2025 (2024: none).

¢ Credit risk: The Company's principal financial assets are bank balances, cash, and trade receivables. The

managed through rigorous credit control, which has been further strengthened following the bad debt event
this year.

pressures, including leveraging economies of scale in procurement and, where necessary, absorbing costs

Future prospects
Looking ahead, the company is positioned for a strong recovery and future growth. The underlying operational
performance in 2025, demonstrated by the significant improvement in gross margin and increased turnover,
provides a solid foundation.
The company is actively increasing its order book with the acquisition of new customers and is strengthening
relationships with its existing client base. This is supported by a strong pipeline of new product development which
is expected to open up further market opportunities.
Excluding the impact of the exceptional bad debt, the business demonstrated a move towards profitability. The
directors are confident that with the expanded customer base and new product streams, the Company is on track to
significantly increase revenue and return to sustained profitability in the forthcoming financial year.

1.3. Turnover

. Revenue comprises sales of goods or services provided to customers net of value added tax and other sales
taxes, less an appropriate deduction for actual and expected returns and discounts. Revenue is recognised
when performance obligations are satisfied and the control of goods or services is transferred to the buyer.
Where the performance obligation is satisfied over time, revenue is recognised in accordance with its
progress towards complete satisfaction of that performance obligation.
When cash inflows are deferred and represent a financing arrangement, the promised consideration is
adjusted for the effects of the time value of money, which is recognised as interest income.

Development expenditure is capitalised when the product's technical feasibility has been established and
ends when the product is available for general release to customers. Amortisation commences when the
product is available for general release to customers and is computed on an individual product basis at 10%
of current gross revenues for the product in the year. All other development costs are written off in the year of
expenditure.
Capitalised development expenditure is assessed for indicators of impairment at each balance sheet date. If
there is objective evidence of impairment, an impairment Joss is recognised in the profit and loss. :

1.6 Impairment of fixed assets
At each reporting period end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible
assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any
such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the
impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an individual asset,
the company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in use, :
the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for which the
estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted.
If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying
amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. An
impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued
amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.

Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of

Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that
occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected.
If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present

If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was
recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not
exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets

1.17 Rechargeable tooling costs
Tooling arrangements may exist with a customer where a tool is designed, developed and built that is
specifically and uniquely used in the production of customised products for that customer. In such an
arrangement the customer may provide specific consideration for the tool separate from the consideration
received for the products produced for the customer. There are generally three types of arrangements and
consideration.
Once a tool has been fully completed, the legal title of ownership passes to the customer, although the tool
will remain at the factory of subcontractor of the Company. The customer consideration is recognised as _
revenue and the associated costs prepaid to the balance sheet are charged to the profit and loss to match
against revenue.
The customer consideration for the tool will be specified at certain stages of completion of the design and
development. Once an agreed stage of completion is attained legal title for that stage will pass to the
customer. Revenue will be recognised and the associated prepaid costs charged to the profit and loss. At the
next agreed stage of completion, until an including the final stage, legai title will pass to the customer, revenue
will be recognised and the associated prepaid costs charged to the profit and loss account.
The arrangement with the customer is that the consideration for the tool will be included in the consideration
for the bespoke products being produced. The cost of the tool will therefore be amortised by being allocated
over the cost of sales of the bespoke products the tool is used to produce over its economic life.
