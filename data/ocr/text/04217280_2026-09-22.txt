. Introduction
The Directors present their strategic report and the financial statements for Commify UK Limited (“the Company”) for the
year ended 31 December 2025. :
In the year, the Company focused on growing the business whilst continuing to invest further resources in the ongoing
development of its brand-new messaging platform “Esendex Connect’, formerly “Titan”. The development of Esendex Con-
nect will strengthen the Company's position as the UK and Ireland's premier provider of mobile messaging solutions, with
the initial development work complete and the migration of customers from legacy platforms now underway.
Although 2025 was impacted by a flat macroeconomic environment, the Company has grown revenue and gross profit for
the full year by 20.5% (2024: 10.7%) and 3.3% (2024: 11.2%) respectively, demonstrating the strength of the commercial
proposition offered, the abilities of our commercial teams and the high regard which the Company's brands are held by
customers. Changes in customer mix and significant cost pressure from MNOs impacted the Company's gross profit margin,
which fell to 24.4% (2024: 28.5%) despite the cost increases being passed on to customers.
Principal Activities
The principal activity of the Company is that of a technology company providing business communication services in the UK
. and Ireland. — . ,
The primary services offered are the provision of SMS messaging products and software for customers, alongside a growing
selection of other innovative messaging solutions, utilising channels such as rich communication services (‘RCS' — an up-
dated communication protocol that is replacing SMS on some mobile devices), WhatsApp, two-way chat, email, voice, web-
and other mobile channels that the directors expect to make up an increasing proportion of the Company's gross profit in
future years.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The Company's operations expose it to a variety of risks, the principal of which are set out below:
« Economic risks
The Company is subject to the strength of the British and Irish economies. However, the number of individual customers and
their industry spread ensure no reliance on any one or group of customers. During 2025, the Company achieved significant
revenue growth of 20.5% despite a backdrop of minimal growth in the UK economy.
A combination of slow growth and cost rises experienced across the market continued to present a risk to financial perfor-
mance. For the third year in a row, UK Mobile Network Operators (MNOs) increased costs per unit by over 10%. Following
an Ofcom ruling in January 2025, which banned inflation linked percentage rises, MNOs transitioned to a fixed pounds and
pence model. In many instances, these fixed increases resulted in effective cost rises as high as 20% for certain services.
While the situation is being kept under review, the Company has focused on maintaining market share and supporting its
expanding customer base through this transition.
Cost price risk :
Global inflationary pressures resulted in significant increases in SMS cost prices during the current and prior years. While
revenue grew substantially in 2025, the Gross Profit margin fell from 28.5% to 24.4%. This margin compression was driven
by the cumulative impact of MNO cost increases, with price adjustments across our customer base not able to be imple-
mented as quickly as MNO costs increased, and the decrease was also impacted by a customer mix shift towards lower

. margin but higher volume customers.

: Beyond MNO costs, the inflation impact has been most notable in the increase in the cost of utilities and the energy intensive
services provided to the Company, such as data centre costs. To mitigate these pressures, the Company regularly reviews
it's SMS supplier base and contracts to optimise routing costs. The directors view the current margin level as a strategic
result of prioritising volume and market share during a period of significant wholesale price shifts.

Market resilience .

The increased cost prices noted above, along with a continuation of interest rates at higher levels than in recent history,
resulted in a period of slower activity in the UK economy. This slowdown reduced demand in some discretionary areas, yet
the 20.5% increase in turnover demonstrates the underlying strength of the Company's services. .

7 The broad range of industry segments the Company operates in, andthe wide variety of use cases for its services, provide
natural protection to economic slowdowns. Sectors such as financial services, logistics, and healthcare remain resistant to
economic cooling, and our services are an essential part of their operations. Evidence from past slowdowns gives the Direc-
tors confidence that the Company can continue to grow, even in a subdued economic environment.

Climate risk

In common with all global businesses, the ongoing climate emergency represents arisk to future operations and profitability.

The nature of the Company's operations means that this risk is not expected to have a direct impact on revenues, and our

services have a smaller environmental impact than most other forms of communication, but changes in the regulatory envi-

ronment may result in increased costs. .

e Competitive risks
The Company is a leading provider of mission-critical business messaging for customers and differentiates itself by aiming
to provide innovative products and an unrivalled quality of service.
The Directors believe that a significant ongoing risk to the business is the potentially disruptive effect of new messaging
technologies. Whilst the Directors see no immediate threat, the Company is constantly addressing this risk through a contin-
uous research and development programme. This means that whilst this has been noted as an area of threat, it also provides
future opportunities for the business, particularly in relation to the roll-out of Rich Communication Services and Facebook /
WhatsApp messaging that is currently underway across Mobile Network Operators.
e =©Financial risks
Foreign currency risk - A proportion of the Company's Revenue is earned in Euros, primarily in relation to sales in the
Republic of Ireland. To offset this risk, a proportion of the costs of the business are denominated in Euros.
Credit risk - The Company has policies that require appropriate credit checks on all potential customers before credit is
granted. Smaller customers are generally required to make payment in advance of being able to use the Company's services.
Many significant customers pay by direct debit, credit limits are reviewed on a regular basis, and the Company performs
regular, detailed reviews of all outstanding debts to ensure the Balance Sheet value is fairly stated. .
Liquidity risk - The Company seeks to manage financial risk by ensuring sufficient liquidity is available to meet foreseeable
cash needs. This approach involves regular reconciliation and review of the full Balance Sheet, comprehensive credit control
policies and a rolling cash forecasting procedure that gives early visibility of any potential liquidity issues that could result in
the Company being unable to pay its debts as they fall due.
People risk — The Company is reliant on key employees to maintain its competitive advantage. The Company manages this
risk through effective management, training and development, the alignment of pay and benefits to market rates, the use of
short-term contractors where required, and the sharing of resources with other Group companies where possible.
Regulatory risk -— The Company operates in a highly regulated environment and is subject to a changing and increasing
number of laws and regulations relating to communications, data protection and credit card payments, amongst other items.
This risk is managed via a dedicated Legal and Compliance team with responsibility for ensuring compliance with all opera-
tional regulations, including Global Data Protection Regulations, and through maintaining the 1S027001:2022 standard for
information security.

"3.2 Revenue recognition
IFRS 15 and its related amendments apply to all revenue arising from contracts with customers. IFRS 15 establishes a five- ‘
step model to account for revenue arising from contracts with customers. It requires revenue to be recognised when (or as)
control of a good or service transfers to a customer at an amount that reflects the consideration to which an entity expects to
be entitled in exchange for transferring goods or services to a customer.
IFRS 15 requires entities to exercise judgement, taking into consideration all of the relevant facts and circumstances when
applying each step of the model to contracts with their customers. The standard also specifies the accounting for the incre-
mental costs of obtaining a contract and the costs directly related to fulfilling a contract. In addition, the standard requires
enhanced and extensive disclosures about revenue to help investors better understand the nature, amount, timing and uncer-
tainty of revenue and cash flows from contracts with customers.
The Company provides business communication services. The majority of revenue derives from the sale of SMS credits and
is recognised based on the actual number of credits used in the reporting period. Revenue from set up and other support
services is recognised as performance obligations are satisfied. For most sales, the enforceable contract is the individual
purchase order, which is an individual, short-term contract. As the enforceable contract for most arrangements is the purchase
order, the transaction price is determined at the date of each sale and, therefore, there is no future variability that is within the
scope of IFRS 15 and no further remaining performance obligations under those contracts.
When the Company sells multiple services as a package, the components are separated and accounted for separately to the
extent that this is material.
Revenue received before services are delivered is recognised as deferred income and transferred to the profit and loss account
once the services have been performed. .
