Business Review
Quickline is one of the UK's fastest-growing broadband providers, dedicated to delivering lightning-fast, reliable
connectivity to rural communities across the North of England. We are passionate about closing the digital divide in
broadband provision between urban and deep rural areas, which leaves too many households and businesses, outside
towns and cities, suffering from sub-standard internet access.
Quickline is leading the way by building its own unique, hybrid gigabit-capable. network infrastructure to connect
undér-served communities. This network combines the tatest fibre and wireless technologies to provide ultrafast and
gigabit broadband speeds using both full fibre to the premise (FTTP) together with Fixed Wireless Access (FWA)
solutions.
Following the success of its Superfast Broadband programme, the UK Government launched Project Gigabit in March
2021 to deliver gigabit-capable connectivity to "hard to reach areas" by the end of 2025, supporting the Government's
target of at least 85% gigabit coverage by the same date. A further £1.8bn has been committed by the Government to
March 2030 out of a £5bn commitment to provide almost ubiquitous coverage by 2032.
During 2024, Quickline was successful in securing four Project Gigabit contracts, across Yorkshire and Lincolnshire.
These contracts cover approximately 160,000 funded FTTP premises with total subsidy of c£310m. Quickline is now
the third largest Project Gigabit delivery partner, after Openreach and CityFibre, with contiguous contracts across
Yorkshire and Lincolnshire. Delivery of all four contracts commenced in 2024 and is progressing to plan. On Ist
August 2024, Quickline secured a £250m debt financing package to support the network infrastructure build.
These follow Quickline’s work to deliver under the four multi-million-pound Superfast Broadband contracts secured
in 2020, to provide broadband to around 30,000 premises across North Lincolnshire, Lincolnshire, West and North
Yorkshire. Delivery of all four contracts has now been completed.
Quickline has continued to invest in its network, systems and people as part of its growth plans. Its hybrid
infrastructure can provide hundreds of thousands of premises with ultra-fast broadband speeds, many of which would
previously have been on the wrong side of the digital divide. Strong growth in customers was experienced during the
year.
Financial Review :
Quickline principally derives revenue from customer subscriptions from broadband and other related services.
Additionally, grant income is received from subsidy receipts from its BDUK contracts and vouchers issued under the
Gigabit Broadband. Voucher Scheme.
During the year Quickline had revenues of £6.7m (2024: £3.9m). The operating loss of £39.2m (2024: £33.1m) reflects
significant investment in scaling the business to deliver the growth targeted in the business plans, which will continue
into 2026 and beyond.
As at 3t December 2025, Quickline had £240.0m (2024: £130.8m) invested in property, plant and equipment, mainly
in the construction of its new network, and following additions in the year of £129.5m.
Quickline has continued to grow its employee base to deliver those plans, increasing its employee base to 458 at 31
December 2025, from just over 280 at the start of the year, with investment in employees in all areas: commercial,
operational, technical and support services.

Corporate Social Responsibility
Quickline’s biggest asset is its people, and the Company is committed to building a supportive corporate culture.

to a culture of community within Quickline, supported by monthly "Elevenses" sessions, reguiar "lunch & learn"

Emissions scopes :
The Company reports greenhouse gas emissions in accordance with the Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting
requirements, using the Greenhouse Gas Protocol categorisation of Scope 1, Scope 2 and, where applicable, Scope 3
emissions, .
Scope 1 emissions represent direct greenhouse gas emissions arising from sources owned or controlled by the
Company. These. include emissions from the combustion of fuels in company-controlled premises, plant, equipment :
and vehicles. Scope 1 therefore reflects emissions generated directly from the Company’s own operational activities.

Scope 2 emissions represent indirect greenhouse gas emissions associated with the generation of purchased electricity,
heat, steam or cooling consumed by the Company. Although these emissions occur at the point of generation rather
than at the Company’s premises, they arise as a consequence of the Company’s energy consumption and are therefore

Scope 3 emissions represent other indirect greenhouse gas emissions that occur as a consequence of the Company’s

associated with business travel, employee commuting, purchased goods and services, waste, distribution, and other

Scope | and Scope 2 reporting, but selected Scope 3 categories may be disclosed where they are material to the
