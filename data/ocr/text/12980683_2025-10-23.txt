The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Principal activities and review of the business
The group is a communication mediator providing SMS, Voice and all other general telecommunication
services worldwide. :

In 2024, the Group experienced a decrease in turnover due to strategic decisions to discontinue and exit
unprofitable deals. Additionally the Group transitioned from a commitment-based model to a consumption
model effective 2024, which led to the loss of deals with suppliers who were unwilling to comply with the newly
adopted approach. Despite these challenges, the Company successfully increased its gross profit margin
percentage from 20% in 2023 to 25% in 2024, reflecting improved operational efficiency and profitability.
The group benefitted from a Master Murabaha Loan obtained by the ultimate parent company Monty Mobile
Limited (Cyprus) in December 2022 for USD 25,000,000 from a financial institution, split among 2 tranches of
USD 15,000,000 and USD 10,000,000 respectively with a purpose of securing contract prepayments. The first
tranche was distributed to a group entity in January 2023 for the purposes of funding payments to vendors
with the second tranche not yet drawn down. The Group decided not to proceed with the second tranche of
the loan as it does not anticipate entering into new deals that require such funding. The remaining balance on
the loan and matters relating to repayment are discussed as part of the going concern section of the directors’
report. .
Principal risks and uncertainties
Relationships .
The companies of the group benefit from a close business relationship with several major suppliers (Mobile
Network operators and aggregators). Loss of these relationships could have a direct and detrimental effect on
the group’s existence.
Foreign exchange
The group has significant currency sales transactions in Euro and US Dollars and as such is exposed to
movement in exchange rates. To minimize this risk the company endeavors to purchase services in the same
currency where possible and lessen payment terms to maximize on receipt value date.

Going Concern
The group's business activities, the factors likely to affect its future development and position, and the principal
risks and uncertainties faced by the group, are set out in the Strategic Report. The directors have considered
the group’s forecasts and funding available to it for a period to 31 October:2026.
The directors modelled a downside scenario that considered the risk of a significant drop in demand of the
services offered for example due to adverse and disruptive changes in technology that shift demand in the
direction of alternatives, other than the core sales stream of the group.
The group concluded that this risk does not significantly affect the group’s operations and financial performance.
In fact, the group is meeting its day-to-day working capital requirements, and will be able to meet medium and
long term commitments based on the budgeted figures prepared to 31 October 2026. Additionally, and given
that the long term SMS market is expected to decline given the technological developments in the telecom
market, the group continues to explore and develop new solutions and business verticals in order to cater for
such potential SMS market declines.
The group is meeting its day-to-day cash commitments and payments as shown in its year-end financial
statements reflected by the profitability of the group, its cash position, its liabilities, and the absence of
commitments.
The current budget was developed by management to capture probable scenarios for a period to 31 October
2026. This includes downside scenario modelling and reverse stress testing. The projected figures along with
the actual figures to date, show a decrease in the group’s gross profit by around 17% in 2025 compared to 2024.
The positive cash position of USD 6,437,423 as at 31 December 2024 year end is sufficient to support the day
to day operations of the group. Cash has decreased from the prior year as a result of prepaid debt settlements,
and other working capital changes. Management have reviewed the recoverability of its receivables and
continually review the credit worthiness of its customer base. Management have concluded that receivables are
recoverable and forecasted cashflows support the ability to continue to meet its obligations as they fall due. The
group is expected to remain profitable and cash generative for the entire Going Concern period to 31 October
2026. This is true for downside scenarios modelled.

Auditor’s responsibilities for the audit of the financial statements
Our objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements as a whole are free
from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report that includes our
opinion. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance, but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in
accordance with ISAs (UK) will always detect a material misstatement when it exists. Misstatements can arise
from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually or in the aggregate, they could reasonably be
expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of these financial statements.

Explanation as to what extent the audit was considered capable of detecting irregularities, including

fraud

Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design

. procedures in line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect irregularities, including fraud. The risk of
not detecting a material misstatement due to fraud is higher than the risk of not detecting one resulting from
error, as fraud may involve deliberate concealment by, for example, forgery or intentional misrepresentations,
or through collusion. The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud
is detailed below. However, the primary responsibility for the prevention and detection of fraud rests with both
those charged with governance of the entity and management.

* We obtained an understanding of the legal and regulatory frameworks that are applicable to the company
and determined that the most significant are UK adopted International Accounting Standards, the
Companies Act 2006, the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, the Bribery Act 2010, relevant employment and
tax legislation including legislation applicable in other jurisdictions from the UK and UK General Data
Protection Regulation.

+ We understood how Monty Mobile Limited and its subsidiaries are compiying with those frameworks by
performing enquiries regarding the processes and procedures in place as well as reviewed documentation
relating to those processes and procedures as appropriate. We considered the oversight of those charged
with governance and the culture of honesty and ethical behaviour including the emphasis placed on fraud
prevention. We consider these factors to reduce opportunities for fraud to take place as they could
persuade individuals not to commit fraud because of the likelihood of detection and punishment.

- We assessed the susceptibility of the Company’s financial statements to material misstatement, including
how fraud might occur by identifying significant classes of transactions and significant accounts and

2.3 Going Concern
The group's business activities, the factors likely to affect its future development and position, and the
principal risks and uncertainties faced by the group, are set out in the Strategic Report. The directors
have considered the group’s forecasts and funding available to it for a period to 31 October 2026.
The directors modelled a downside scenario that considered the risk of a significant drop in demand of
the services offered for example due to adverse and disruptive changes in technology that shift demand
in the direction of alternatives, other than the core sales stream of the group.
The group concluded that this risk does not significantly affect the group's operations and financial
performance. In fact, the group is meeting its day-to-day working capital requirements, and will be able
to meet medium and long term commitments based on the budgeted figures prepared to 31 October
2026. Additionally, and given that the long term SMS market is expected to decline given the
technological developments in the telecom market, the group continues to explore and develop new
solutions and business verticals in order to cater for such potential SMS market declines.
The group is meeting its day-to-day cash commitments and payments as shown in its year-end financial
statements reflected by the profitability of the group, its cash position, its liabilities, and the absence of
commitments.
The current budget was developed by management to capture probable scenarios for a period to 31
October 2026. This includes downside scenario modelling and reverse stress testing. The projected
figures along with the actual figures to date, show a decrease in the group's gross profit by around 17%
in 2025 compared to 2024. The positive cash position of USD 6,437,423 as at 31 December 2024 year
end is sufficient to support the day to day operations of the group. Cash has decreased from the prior
year as a result of prepaid debt settlements, and other working capital changes. Management have
reviewed the recoverability of its receivables and continually review the credit worthiness of its customer
base. Management have concluded that receivables are recoverable and forecasted cashflows support
the ability to continue to meet its obligations as they fall due. The group is expected to remain profitable
and cash generative for the entire Going Concern period to 31 October 2026. This is true for downside
scenarios modelled.
In 2024, the Group experienced a decrease in turnover due to strategic decisions to discontinue and
: exit unprofitable deals. Additionally the Group transitioned from a commitment-based model to a
consumption model effective 2024, which led to the loss of deals with suppliers who were unwilling to
comply with the newly adopted approach. Despite these challenges, the Company successfully’
increased its gross profit margin percentage from 20% in 2023 to 25% in 2024, reflecting improved
operational efficiency and profitability.

parent undertaking is in discussion with the financing institution regarding early repayment. The loan
expires in December 2025. The Group is satisfied this does not represent a material uncertainty in
respect of going concern.

Estimating the incremental borrowing rate for lease
The Group cannot readily determine the interest rate implicit in certain leases, therefore, it uses its
incremental borrowing rate (IBR) to measure lease liabilities. The IBR is the rate of interest that the
Group would have to pay to borrow over a similar term, and with a similar security, the funds necessary
to obtain an asset of a similar value to the right-of-use asset in a similar economic environment. The
IBR therefore reflects what the Group ‘would have to pay’, which requires estimation when no
observable rates are available (such as for subsidiaries that do not enter into financing transactions) or
when they need to be adjusted to reflect the terms and conditions of the lease (for example, when leases
are not in the subsidiary’s functional currency).
The Group estimates the IBR using observable inputs (such as market interest rates etc.) when available
and is required to make certain entity-specific estimates (such as the Group's economic environment).
Please see Note 14 for a further description of the nature and carrying amount at the end of the reporting
period. Based on quantitative considerations and the lease liabilities not being highly sensitive to the
incremental borrowing rate, management have concluded that the risk of material adjustment to the
following years financial statements in respect of this judgement and estimate is low.

value and useful tives annually and future depreciation and amortization charge would be adjusted where
the management believes the useful lives differ from previous estimates. Please see Note 12 and 13 for
