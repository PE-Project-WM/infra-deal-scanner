Strategic Report for the Year Ended 31 December 2024
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 December 2024.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the company is to manufacture and sell equipment required for wireless
telecommunications infrastructure.
Review of the business and outlook
During 2024 we continued our journey to expand our market offering to include a level of field-based service
and support work for the main network operators, this has helped lay the foundation for an expansion of our
business in the marketplace in 2025 and beyond.
2024 saw the business activity remain reasonably static with a modest fall in turnover for the 12 month period to
31st December 2024, turnover fell by 2.06% to £19,558k (2023: £19,970k). This has been attributed to changes
in build programs of our main customers. We anticipate that 2025 will start to see an increase in tumover along
with an improvement in operational performance and gross margin over 2024 results. This will be driven by a
number of cost reduction projects, streamlining of operations and improvements in procurement.
For 2025 we are continuing to drive our core business to improve operational performance at the same time
working to lay foundations for 2026 and beyond through a level of diversification and expansion of service
activity in support of long term stability for the business.

Principal risks and uncertainties
The company is competing in a technologically intensive industry. It is important for the company to remain
competitive in this field and so invest appropriate resources in this area. In addition, the market is becoming
very cost centric with Networks under scrutiny from shareholders for their spend.
Russia-Ukraine and Israel-Gaza conflicts
The crisis in Russia and Ukraine began in February 2022 and in Israel and Gaza in October 2023 and continues
as of the date of this annual report. CommScope Group suspended all the services in Russia and Belarus, which
has not had and is not expected to have any impact on the financial statements or operating results of the
Company. The Company had no receivables from Russian customers. The conflict in Israel and Gaza is not
expected to have material impact on the financial statements or operating results of the Company. The attacks
on the ships on Red Sea have increased freight costs and transport times for products coming from Asia to
Europe although this impact has eased during the second half of 2024.

Going concern
The principal activity of the company is to manufacture and sell equipment required for wireless
telecommunications infrastructure.
Based on its forecasts to 31 January 2027, the Company expects to remain profitable on an annual basis. The
Company is a part of centralised treasury structure which includes Andrew Wireless UK Limited, Andrew
Design & Integration UK Limited and the UK Branch of Andrew Technologies UK (incorporated in
Switzerland ), together called as the ‘UK Group’. Therefore, the Company is dependent on the activities of the
UK group, given the centralised treasury structure whereby cash and cashflow is managed on a UK group-wide
basis.
Consequently, the Company has obtained a cross guarantee letter of support between the UK group that
confirms that they will provide financial support to each other to assist in meeting its liabilities to the extent that
funding is not otherwise available to the Company to meet such liabilities for the period from the date of
approval of the financial statements until 31 January 2027 , ‘the going concern period’.
The Directors have assessed the level of financial support available, taking into account the UK groups available
liquidity, consisting of $4.6m cash in hand at the end of November 2025 and its financial plans. The
intercompany loans in the company are contained within the UK group therefore is not expected to affect the
going concern position of the company due to the existence of cross guarantee letter of support. The Directors
have reviewed the UK Group’s budget and medium-term financial plan. The UK Group has modelled a range of
scenarios with the base forecast being on in which, over the 12 months ending 31 January 2027, the sales grow
in line with the budget. In addition, the Directors have considered reasonably possible downside scenarios,
including adjustments to the base forecast, a period of significantly lower sales, profitability and cash flows.
From this assessment, the Directors are satisfied that even in the reasonably possible downside scenarios, the
UK Group have sufficient liquidity throughout the going concern period, until 31 January 2027.

Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures

in line with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect irregularities, including fraud. The risk of not detecting

a material misstatement due to fraud is higher than the risk of not detecting one resulting from error, as fraud

may involve deliberate concealment by, for example, forgery or intentional misrepresentations, or through

collusion. The extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud is detailed
below. However, the primary responsibility for the prevention and detection of fraud rests with both those
charged with governance of the entity and management.

Our approach was as follows:

¢ We obtained an understanding of the legal and regulatory frameworks that are applicable to the company and
determined that the most significant are those that relate to the reporting framework (FRS 102 and the
Companies Act 2006) and the relevant tax laws and regulations in the UK. In addition, we concluded that
there are certain significant laws and regulations which may have an effect on the determination of the
amounts and disclosures in the financial statements being those laws and regulations relating to Health and
Safety legislation, General Data Protection Regulations, Employment Law and Corporate Crime Act.

+ We understood how Andrew Design & Integration UK Limited is complying with those frameworks by
making enquiries of those charged with governance and management, including those responsible for legal
and compliance procedures, to understand how the company maintains and communicates its policies and
procedures in these areas. We corroborated our enquiries through our review of board minutes and papers
provided to the Board and made inquiries of management to identify if there are matters where there is a risk
of breach of such frameworks that could have a material adverse impact on the company, as well as
consideration of the results of our audit procedures across the company.

+ We assessed the susceptibility of the company’s financial statements to material misstatement, including
how fraud might occur by meeting with management and those charged with governance to understand
where it considered there was a susceptibility to fraud. We considered the programmes and controls that the
company has established to address risks identified, or that otherwise prevent, deter and detect fraud; and
how senior management monitors those programmes and controls. Where the risk was considered to be
higher, we performed audit procedures to address each identified fraud risk. These procedures included

management override. We-have used data analytics to highlight potentially anomalous transactions in areas

: which we determined to have an elevated fraud risk. We tested revenue cut off, through selecting a sample of
items and validated these to invoice and other evidence to demonstrate the timing of revenue recognition was
appropriate.

Trade creditors are obligations to pay for goods or services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of

sufficient assets to pay all employees the benefits relating to employee service in the current and prior periods.

contribution payments exceed the contribution due for service, the excess is recognised as a prepayment.
