Principal risks and uncertainties
The activities of the company expose it to a variety of risks, both financial and operational. The company
operates in extremely competitive markets which are characterised by aggressive promotional activities
and rapidly improving product designs. The Board believes that the correct method of combating this
risk is through ongoing product research, development and Innovation.
The company's activities expose it to a number of financial riske including price risk, credit risk, foreign
exchange risk and liquidity risk,
Price risk
The company is exposed to commodity price risk. The company does not manage its exposure to
: commodity price risk due to cost benefit considerations.

Credit risk
The company's principal financial assets are trade and other receivables. The company’s credit risk
primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of :
aftowances for doubtful receivables. An allowance for impairment is made where there ts an Identified
loss event, which Is based on management's current and previous experiences. Specific provisions are
created where necessary to cover the recoverability risk associated with outstanding debts. Regular
teview of debtor positions on a month-on-month basis provides sufficient information to take necessary
judgements. Management monitors debtor position on a monthly basis and takes necessary steps such
as a) vendor and customer due diligence b) taking out necessary insurance where applicable; to .
mitigate company's credit risk.
The company has no significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread over a large number
of counterparties and customers.
Foreign exchange risk
The company’s trading activities exposes it to muitiple currencies. Exchange rates are subject to
fluctuations and fluctuations in rates can result in significant foreign exchange loss or gain which has a
direct impact on the cash flow of the company. The company does not manage its exposure to foreign
exchange risk due to cost benefit considerations. : :
The Company is exposed to liquidity risk insofar as it must ensure that it has sufficient funds available
to meet its liabilities as they fall due. The Company does not maintain any external borrowing facilities
and therefore manages liquidity risk through ongoing monitoring of forecast and actual cash flows,
maintaining adequate cash reserves, and careful working capital management. The Company prepares
regular cash flow forecasts which are reviewed by management to identify potential shortfalls in funding
and to ensure that sufficient liquid resources are available to meet operational and contractual
commitments
All risk exposures are monitored by the Board regularly, with the prime focus being on performance and

: strategy issues surrounding the mitigation and management of these above risks at an acceptable level.

2.3 Turnover
Tumover is recognised to the extent that it Is probable that the economic benefits will
flow to the company and the turnover can be reliably measured. Tumover is measured
as the fair value of the consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts,
: rebates, value added tax and other sales taxes. The following criterla must also be met
before turnover is recognised:
Sale of goods
Tumover from the sale of goods is recognised when all of the following conditions are
satisfied:
e the company has transferred the significant risks and rewards of ownership to
the buyer.
e the company retains neither continuing managerial invotvement to the degree
usually associated with ownership nor effective control over the goods sold.
e the amount of tumover can be measured reliably.
e it is probable that the company will receive the consideration due under the
transaction; and
e the costs Incurred or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be
. measured reliably.
Volume discounts/rebates and retums: When a customer has a right to retum a product
