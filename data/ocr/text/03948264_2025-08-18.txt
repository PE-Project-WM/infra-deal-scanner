Strategic Report
For The Year Ended 31 March 2025
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2025.
Principal Activity
The principal activities of Acorah Software Products Limited (the "Company") comprise of the design, build, quality control,
marketing, sales and support of software products under the TaxCalc brand.
The year ended 31 March 2025 has been an extremely successful year for the Company with revenues increasing by 16% to
£15,164k (2024: £13,031k). Growth was driven by customer acquisition across all demographics and incremental revenue
generated from additional product sales to existing customers all underpinned by continued investment into new products
whilst increasing features, benefits, functionality and integration within our existing product suite.

Review of the Business
. Following significant investment from new owners ‘STG Partners‘, the Company intends to continue to invest in people,
product and systems to ensure it continues to deliver exceptional products and service to it’s customers.
This continued investment in has been well received by customers with retention at a ‘World Class’ 97% (2024: 97%).
The Company is focused on building on its continued success through delivering on its growth strategies, supporting, and
managing employees, minimising workplace costs, maximising returns and managing risk.
The hybrid working model continues to facilitate recruitment across the UK and has proved to be successful. Increased
investment in employees has ensured employee retention remains stable across all departments.
It is the opinion of the Board that this approach to flexible working has strengthened the business and will contribute to the
continued growth and commercial stability of the Company.
Principal Risks and Uncertainties
Financial risk management
The Company operates a dynamic financial business model, and obligations and liabilities are visible well in advance. The
Company’s cash balances are held at financial institutions that have credit ratings that meet the Board's criteria.
Operational risk management
The Company is confident that it can sustain the level of growth as determined by the board. The Company enjoys a strong
relationship with HMRC ensuring that its products meet all legislative and compliance requirements, including the upcoming
transition to Making Tax Digital.
Cyber and technological advancement risk management
The Company has invested significantly in the protection of its systems and data. Additional security measures implemented
since the move towards hybrid working are subject to continued review to ensure client and corporate data is safeguarded.
Future Outlook
The Directors continually assess the trading environment for opportunity as well as risk and are confident that although
external influences may contribute to increased costs and disruption, there are substantial opportunities for further growth
across the trading demographic within which the Company operates.

2.3. Turnover
Turnover is measured at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable and represents amounts receivable
for goods and services provided in the normal course of business, net of discounts, VAT and other related taxes.
We define our turnover into 3 classes of business to be able to show a clear reflection of how our turnover is driven
and differentiating them between licenced recurring revenue, reucrring usage credits and one off consultancy fees.
Product sales are composed of a licence element and future product support element. The licence element is
recognised at the point of sale and the product support element is deferred over the length of the licence.
Turnover from all other product sales is recognised at the point of delivery and any prepaid amount is held on the
balance sheet.
2.4. Intangible Fixed Assets and Amortisation - Other Intangible
A judgement is made each year as how much of the development work on existing products is maintenance versus
enhancement and the elements that relate to enhancement are capitalised. Expenditure on research and development
is written off in the year it is incurred, unless it meets the criteria to allow it to be capitalised. Where development
costs are recognised as an asset, they are amortised over an estimated economic life of three years. Amortisation
begins once the developed product comes into use. Management believe that this approach is the most prudent even
though new products continue to generate a return for many years after the investment cost has been amortised.
2.5. Intangible Fixed Assets and Amortisation - Intellectual Property
Intellectual property assets are IT development costs. They are amortised to the income statement over their
estimated economic life of three years.
2.6. Tangible Fixed Assets and Depreciation
Tangible assets comprise of leasehold property, plant and equipment. Tangible fixed assets are measured at cost less
accumulated depreciation and any accumulated impairment losses. Depreciation is provided at rates calculated to write
off the cost of the fixed assets, less their estimated residual value, over their expected useful lives on the following
bases:

2.8. Financial Instruments

: Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised in the balance sheet when the company becomes a party to the

2.9. Foreign Currencies
Monetary assets and liabilities in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rates of exchange ruting at the
balance sheet date. Transactions in foreign currencies are translated into sterling at the rate ruling on the date of the
transaction. Exchange differences are taken into account in arriving at the operating profit.
2.10. Taxation
The charge for taxation takes into account taxation deferred as a result of timing differences between the treatment of
certain items for taxation and accounting purposes. In general, deferred taxation is recognised in respect of timing
differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance sheet date. However, deferred tax assets are
recognised only to the extent that the directors consider that it is more likely than not that there will be suitable
taxable profits from which the future reversal of the underlying timing differences can be deducted. Deferred taxation
is measured on a non-discounted basis at the tax rates that are expected to apply in periods in which the timing
differences reverse, based on tax rates and the law enacted or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date.
2.11. Provisions and Contingencies
Bad Debt Provisions
The trade debtors balance of £(6,199,674) recorded in the company’s balance sheet comprises of a large number of
relatively low value debt. A detailed review of trade debt is carried out at the end of each month and any amounts
considered to be at risk are addressed at that point. On this basis there is no provision for bad debt as at the year end.
Whilst every attempt is made to ensure that any debt at risk is addressed throughout the year, there is a risk that
some debt exists at year end that becomes uncollectable. It is the opinion of the management that this risk is low and
the value is immaterial.
Dilapidation provision
The building lease relating to the registered office is a fully repairing lease and subject to dilapidation costs at the end
of the lease. The value will remain unknown until the point of negotiation. Management are accruing a provision over
the life of the lease based on what they consider to be a reasonable amount to settle this obligation.
2.12. Pensions
The company operates a defined pension contribution scheme. Contributions are charged to the profit and loss account
as they become payable in accordance with the rules of the scheme. Differences between contributions payable in the
year and those actually paid are shown as either accruals or prepayments in the balance sheet.
2.13. Benefits
Short term employee benefits including holiday pay and annual bonuses are accrued as services are rendered.
