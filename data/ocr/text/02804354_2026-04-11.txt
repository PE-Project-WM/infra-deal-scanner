GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2025
Introduction
The directors have pleasure in presenting their strategic review of the group for the year ended 31
March 2025 (FY25). The comparative period is the 12 months ending 31 March 2024 (FY24).
Principal activity
The principal activity of the Company, throughout the period continued to be that of providing IT
services and products in the unified communication, networking, Microsoft Teams, and cyber security
areas.
Business review
Market conditions have been challenging as it is a highly competitive marketplace. This combined
with reduced Public Sector spending impacted top line financial performance during FY25. During the
year the Directors reduced costs with annualised savings of c.£2m of which approximately £1m were
seen in the FY25 results. .
Turnover decreased to £66.4m (2024: £73.3m) and gross profit falling to £28.1m (2024: £30.2m),
however due to the aforementioned cost management, the impact to adjusted EBITDA (Earnings
Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, Amortisation and Exceptional costs) was partially mitigated, with
adjusted EBITDA decreasing £1m to £4.9m (2024: £5.9m).

: During the year the Company continued to enhance data capabilities and continued its efforts on
streamlining processes and systems to drive efficiencies. This, combined with integration and
restructuring activity have largely led the Company to incur £1.1m of Exceptional costs during FY25
(FY24: £3.0m) as detailed further in note 11. Depreciation and Amortisation costs (non-cash) were
£7.8m which means an Operating loss of £0.3m, which represents a £0.7m improvement on FY24
(£1.1m).

The business continues to operate through several CCS (Crown Commercial Services) frameworks,
including current public frameworks RM6116 (Network Services 3), RM6100 (Technology Services 3),
RM3764 (Cyber Security Services 3), RM1043 (Digital Outcomes and Specialists), G-Cloud 13 and
14 in addition to continuing to deliver services contracted under previous frameworks including
RM3808 (Network Services 2) and G-Cloud 12. In addition, the business operates on several other
procurement frameworks including JISC. This means working with key Public Sector organisations
and good momentum is being seen in this area.

Business refinancing and change of control

On 19 January 2026 the Group, headed by Clayton Bidco Limited, an intermediate parent,
successfully completed a refinancing and change of control, which puts the Clayton Bidco Group in a
strong position for future success. The entire share capital of the Clayton Bidco Limited was acquired
by Clayton Holdco Limited, a company incorporated in England & Wales and the Group is now owned
and controlled by Muzinich Pan-European Private Debt |, SCSp, a debt fund in Luxembourg.

This change has resulted in full repayment of the revolving credit facility, reduction in overall debt
including bank loans and loan notes owed to parent undertakings (from £75,650k to £25,500k),

: extension of maturity on debt to 2029, amendment to the interest profile on debt to eliminate regular
cash interest payments, provision of additional funding and a facility for future additional funding
(£0.5m). Following the change in control August Equity LLP are no longer invoived with the Clayton
Bidco Group.

The Refinancing strengthens liquidity and simplifies banking covenants. Together, these steps
provide a robust basis for the Group to deliver high levels of service to our customers.

Principat risks and uncertainties
The management of the business and the execution of the Group's strategy are subject to several risks.
The key business risks affecting the Group are considered to relate to the UK economy in general,
including the potential impact of recession and inflation levels and the cost of borrowing, competition,
credit and liquidity risk.
The Group has in piace a risk management programme that seeks to limit the adverse effects on the
financial performance of the Company by monitoring levels of exposure to risk and the related finance
costs.
UK Economy
Inflationary pressures are managed through the alignment of customer pricing with supplier charges,
and our right to apply price increases as incurred. Overhead and Employee costs are managed by
identifying and executing efficiency schemes (e.g., graduate and apprenticeship schemes). Like
inflationary pressures, the impact of a UK recession would be managed by cost base rationalisation
where turnover is impacted.
Competitive risk
. The market for the Group's solutions and services remains highly competitive with pressure on
margins, and clients and projects are selected carefully.
Credit risk
The Group's principal financial asset subject to credit risk arises from its trade debtors. This is
managed through controlled credit allocation and cash collection processes. This is further mitigated
. through a diversified customer base meaning that no single customer represents a significant

proportion of the Group's trade.
Liquidity risk
The Group manages risk through regular short and medium-term cash forecasts which are reviewed
in detail by management on a weekly basis. .

Section 172 statement
Under section 172 of the Companies Act 2006, the Directors have a duty to promote the success of
the Company for the benefit of its members as a whole. This includes having due regard to the broad
range of stakeholders of the Company, such as its workforce, customers, suppliers, shareholders, as
well as the impact of the Company on the wider community and environment.
The Company engages with its key shareholders regularly through the presentation of regular
operational and financial information. Key financial metrics are also regularly presented to employees
at monthly town-hall events.
The Company maintains certification to ISO9001 and 1SO27001, which includes careful consideration
of the interests of various interested parties, including employees, customers, partners, and suppliers.
The Company has developed teams specifically to focus on customer experience and customer
success which regularly seek feedback from customers to support continual improvement. For
example, the Board considered and approved recommendations arising from customer feedback that
resulted in improvements in how customers could engage with the Company’s support teams through
an IT service management tool. The Company's technical experts continue to offer thought-leadership
to customers and the wider community through webinars and events on key technology and industry
developments.
The Company maintains a clear focus on strategic suppliers, has several best-in-class partner

_ accreditations, and maintains several roles with key focus on strategic partner relationship

. management. The Company maintains a supplier onboarding and review process to ensure

consistency and quality in supply chains. The Supplier Code of Conduct developed by the Company
includes key principles in relation to health and safety, data protection, modern slavery, and anti-
bribery.

- The Directors have sought employee feedback and engagement through a variety of means, including
in-person meetings, surveys, monthly virtual town-hall meetings, in-person events, and through the

. Company’s management structures. The Board sought employee feedback on current and preferred
ways of working which was crucial in Board consideration of continuous improvement ideas in the
Company's working practices.
The Company maintains a key focus on delivering its Environment, Social and Governance (ESG)
strategy and recognises the importance of setting meaningful and realistic long-term targets in this
area. The Company completes an annual ESG scoring exercise conducted by an external agency,
which continues to see year on year progress across key ESG metrics. During the Period, the
Company was successfully accredited by EcoVadis in relation to sustainability, demonstrating
additional recognition for the Company's ESG activities. The Company publishes annually a Carbon
Reduction Plan which shows progress to the Company's ambitious Net Zero target. Please refer to
