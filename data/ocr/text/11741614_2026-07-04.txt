Strategic Report
For the year ended 31 December 2025
The Directors of Clearstream London Limited (“CLL” or “the Company”) present the Strategic Report of
the Company for the year ended 31 December 2025 and have complied with section 414(c) of the
Companies Act 2006 in preparing the report.
REVIEW OF THE COMPANY’S BUSINESS
Principal activity and business model
The Board of Directors of the Company (“the Board”) is responsible for setting the Company's business
strategy and defining its principal activity. The Company’s principal activity remains as the arranging of
transactions between certain members of Deutsche Boerse Group of companies (“the Group”) domiciled
in the European Union (“EU”) and in Switzerland, and their customers and providing other unregulated
services to promote their businesses. The Company carries out business primarily with banks, investment
firms and other financial institutions; it does not conduct business with retail clients. CLL is authorised
and regulated by the United Kingdom’s (“UK”) Financial Conduct Authority (“FCA”).
The Company is remunerated on a cost plus margin basis by the Deutsche Boerse Group of companies it
provides services for. CLL also outsources certain business activities to a number of companies within the
Group. These outsourcing arrangements are performed under contracts. As a result, CLL operates as an
integral part of the Deutsche Boerse Group of companies.
Significant changes during the financial year
Subsequent to receiving the approval from the FCA for a Variation of Permission (“VOP”) in October 2024,
CLL has commenced the provision of arranging assets safeguarding and administration services for
Clearstream Fund Centre S.A. (“CFCL”) in 2025.
Events after the reporting period
On 20 February 2026, the Company signed a Licence to Occupy agreement with Deutsche Boerse AG,

, London Branch (“DBAG London Branch”) for the continued use of its office space and related fixtures and
fittings (including IT equipment). The new agreement takes effect from 25 April 2026 and expires on
23 October 2030. Note 21 provides further details.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The Board is responsible for identifying and evaluating the risks faced by the Company. The following risks
continue to be identified by the Board as material risks facing CLL:
i. Business risk
Business risk is the risk of unexpected residual loss which arises when a company is exposed to factors
which lead to lower than projected revenues or higher than projected costs. This may be due to the
competitive environment (example customer behaviour, industry trend) or erroneous strategic
management decisions.
ii. Operational risk
This is the risk of losses resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems or
from external events.
iii. Financial risk
Financial risks applicable to CLL are mainly credit and liquidity risks.
Credit risk
This is the risk of losses arising from failures of counterparties in fulfilling their financial obligations to the
Company in full or on time.

Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk arises when the Company faces the prospect of not being able to meet its payment
obligations when they become due or being able to meet these obligations at excessive costs.
The Board sets the overarching risk culture of CLL. It approves the current and future planned risk appetite
for the Company and confirms there are adequate mitigations for the material risks identified and that
these risks are managed within the Board’s risk appetite. The Board’s risk appetite for the above risks
continues to be generally low, therefore appropriate mitigating measures are put in place to manage
these risks. The risk profile of the Company is regularly monitored by the Board using a framework which
compares current risk levels to the risk appetite set by the Board.
The Board seeks to ensure that the risk profile of CLL does not expose it, or its group members to material
financial or non-financial losses. CLL has established and has access to appropriate systems, processes,
and controls to manage and mitigate the impacts of these risks.
Further information on CLU’s risk assessment and risk management processes is detailed in Note 19.
STATEMENT OF COMPLIANCE WITH S172(1) OF THE COMPANIES ACT 2006
Under section 172 (1) of the Companies Act 2006 (“s172”)}, the Directors of the Company must act in the
way that they consider, in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the Company for
the benefit of its members as a whole. In doing so, they should have regard to other factors, including but
not limited to:

a. the likely consequences of any decision in the long term;

b. the interests of the Company's employees;

c. the need to foster the Company's business relationships with suppliers, customers, and others;

d. the impact of the Company's operations on the community and the environment;

e. the desirability of the Company maintaining a reputation for high standards of business conduct

and;

f. the need to act fairly as between members of the Company.
Through the direction of the Board and the leadership of its management, CLL continues to strive to
maintain high standards of business conduct with its stakeholders, and it effectively discharges its
responsibilities under the UK Companies Act and the FCA regulations.

Revenue
Revenue is recognised when a performance obligation of providing services for the Deutsche Boerse
Group of companies under contracts of service is satisfied. The contracts of service are ongoing with
no fixed termination dates. The revenues are derived on a cost plus markup basis as per the terms of
the contracts and are recognised on a monthly basis as the performance obligations are satisfied.
Revenue is measured at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable and represents
amounts receivable for services provided in the normal course of business, net of Value Added Tax
(“VAT”), The revenues are currently derived from the EU and Switzerland where the relevant Deutsche
Boerse Group of companies are domiciled.
Foreign currency transactions and balances
Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency of the Company at the
exchange rate prevailing at the transaction date.
At the balance sheet date, monetary balance sheet items in foreign currency are remeasured at the
exchange rate at the balance sheet date. Non-monetary items in foreign currency initially measured
at historical cost are not retranslated at the period end but remain measured using the exchange rate
at the transaction date, except for non-monetary items measured at fair value which are translated
using the exchange rates at the date when fair value was determined. Exchange rate differences on
monetary balance sheet items are recognised as Finance Income or Expense in the period in which
they arise.
Operating expenses
Operating expenses are recognised in the Profit and Loss Account on an accrual basis.

Provisions
Provisions are recognised when the Company has a present obligation (legal or constructive} as a

A restructuring provision is recognised when the Company has developed a detailed formal plan for

restructuring by starting to implement the plan or announcing its main features to those affected by
it. The measurement of a restructuring provision includes only the direct expenditures arising from
the restructuring, which are those amounts that are necessarily entailed by the restructuring.

Stock Bonus Plan (“SBP”)
The SBP is open to senior executives of Deutsche Boerse AG and its participating subsidiaries including
CLL. In the reporting period, the Deutsche Boerse Group established an additional tranche of the SBP.
The SBP is intended to create long-term incentives for senior executives and offer them the
opportunity to participate in the company's value growth. As part of the programme, participants are
granted phantom shares whose entitlements vest after a one-year measurement period and a
subsequent three-year holding period. In order to participate in the SBP, beneficiaries must have
earned a bonus.
The number of phantom shares for the tranches up to and including 2023 is determined by the amount
of the individual and performance-based SBP bonus for the financial year, divided by the average share
price (Deutsche Boerse Xetra closing price) of Deutsche Boerse AG’s shares in the fourth quarter of
the financial year in question. The number of SBP shares for the 2024 tranches from 2024 onwards is
calculated by dividing the SBP bonus amount by the average stock exchange price (Deutsche Boerse
Xetra closing price) of Deutsche Boerse AG shares in December of the financial year in question. :
As the SBP shares are phantom shares, beneficiaries cannot assert shareholder rights (in particular,
the rights to receive dividends and attend the Annual General Meeting). After expiry of the holding

. period and an additional waiting period, the shares are settled in cash at the time of the bonus

: payment, which is usually made in the first quarter of the following year. Up to and including the 2023
tranches, the Deutsche Boerse Xetra closing auction price of Deutsche Boerse shares relevant for the
payout is determined on the first trading day following the last day of the waiting period. The payout
amount for the tranches from 2024 onwards is based on the average price of Deutsche Boerse AG
shares (Deutsche Boerse Xetra closing price) in the last calendar month before the end of the holding
period.
To determine the fair value of the phantom shares (provision amount), the intrinsic value of the
subscription rights allocated pro rata over the measurement and holding period is calculated on the
basis of the closing auction price of Deutsche Boerse shares, which also includes an expectation of
future dividend payments. The initial recognition and movements in the carrying amounts are
accounted for as part of employee expenses — share based payments in the Profit and Loss Account.
It is assumed that all beneficiaries will remain with the company until the end of the holding period
and that all subscription rights will therefore be earned.

Leases
IFRS 16 establishes principles for the recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure of
leases, A lease conveys the right to direct the use and obtain substantially all of the economic benefits
of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.
The Company recognises a right-of-use asset and a corresponding lease liability with respect to all
lease arrangements in which it is the Lessee, except for leases with a lease term of 12 months or less
and leases of low value assets as defined by IFRS 16.
Lease liability is initially measured at the present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the
commencement date less any lease incentives receivables, discounted by using its incremental
borrowing rate if the implicit rate of interest cannot be readily determined. The Company remeasures
the lease liability (and makes a corresponding adjustment to the related right-of-use asset) whenever
the lease contract is modified.
A right-of-use asset comprises the initial measurement of the corresponding lease liability, lease
payments made at or before the commencement day, less any lease incentives received and any initial
direct costs. It is subsequently measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses.
Depreciation is on a straight-line basis over the term of the lease. The Company applies IAS 36 to
determine if a right-of-use asset is impaired. The right-of-use asset is presented as a separate line item
in the statement of financial position.
As a practical expedient, IFRS 16 permits a lessee not to separate non-lease components and instead
account for any lease and associated non-lease components as a single arrangement. The Company
has not used this practical expedient for its right-of-use leases, but it accounts for each lease
component within a contract separately from the non-lease components of the contract.
Irrecoverable VAT on the lease payments are not included in the lease liability and the right of use
asset measurement.

Tax
Income tax expense includes current income tax and deferred tax.
Current tax
The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been enacted
or substantially enacted by the balance sheet date. Taxable profit differs from ‘profit before tax’ as
reported in the Profit and Loss Account because of items of income or expense that are taxable or
deductible in other years and items which are never taxable or deductible.
Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised on temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and
liabilities in the financial statements and the corresponding tax bases used in the computation of
taxable profit. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised for all taxable temporary differences.
Deferred tax assets are generally recognised for all deductible temporary differences to the extent
that it is probable that the taxable profits will be available in order to utilise the temporary differences.

in August 2025, CLL signed an electric vehicle leasing agreement with Octopus Electric Vehicles Limited
(“Octopus”). This agreement enables CLL to lease electric vehicles for lease term of 24, 36 or 48
months and make them available for employees’ use, subject to qualification criteria applied by the
Company and the employees agreeing to certain terms and conditions, including salary sacrifice. This
is offered to employees as part of the Electric Vehicle (“EV”) Scheme which was launched in September
2025 as Employee Benefit Scheme. As of 31 December 2025, CLL had two Electric Vehicles leased from
Octopus.
