Strategic Report for the 15-month period ended 31 March 2026
The directors present their strategic report on Mobile Broadband Network Limited (the "Company”") for the 15-
month period ended 31 March 2026.
(a) Background of the Business
The Company acts as the agent for its 50:50 ultimate beneficial shareholders, BT Group plc and Vodafone Group
plc, and directs a number of managed service partners in the operation and development of the consolidated radio
access network. The Company's management is based in Reading, operating as an infrastructure management
and asset provider.
During the period Vodaphone Group plc purchased the shares in Hutchinson 3G UK Ltd and became one of the
ultimate parent companies. As a result the Company changed the reporting period to align with both ultimate
parent companies.
(b) Review of the Financial Performance and Position of the Business and Key Performance
Indicators
The key financial performance indicators used for internal performance analysis are revenue and profit before
taxation.

The Company has two principal revenue streams, namely the Network Management Fee, being charges to the
shareholders to cover the Company's operating costs plus a margin, and the Network Asset Usage Fee, being
charges to the shareholders to cover the Company's depreciation and interest on borrowing costs plus a margin.
Revenue is higher due to the increased length of the accounting period.
The Company shows net current liabilities as at 31 March 2026 and 31 December 2024 due to capital loan
repayments planned for 2026 and made throughout prior years. However, these payments are not contractual,
and the overall balance sheet position remains strong with retained earnings growing year on year.
(c) Principal Risks and Uncertainties
The key risks and uncertainties affecting the Company relate to the successful implementation of infrastructure
maintenance programmes required to ensure the performance of the consolidated network at the levels necessary
to maintain its position in the United Kingdom in a cost-effective manner. The Company's key risks and the
corresponding mitigations are monitored and reviewed on a regular basis. These include the risk of infrastructure
failure, the risk of network performance degradation in terms of coverage and reliability, and the budgetary risk of
operating costs for the joint network. To mitigate these risks, there is a programme of maintenance and renewals
covering all sites. Network performance is continually monitored and reported by site to enable action to be taken
on any issues.

(d) Financial Risk Management ,
The Company is funded by the two shareholders in equal proportions. Interest-bearing loan drawdown agreements
with the shareholders, totalling £372 million (2024: £372 million), are in place to fund the Company's operations
and purchases of fixed assets. These loans are formally repayable on 18 December 2027 but can be extended
beyond that date, if necessary, with the agreement of both shareholders. As at 31 March 2026, £27 million (31
December 2024: £48 million) of the shareholders’ loan facilities had been drawn. See notes 12 and 13 to the
financial statements for further details. During the 15-month period ended 31 March 2026, the Company did not
enter into any derivative transactions (year to 31 December 2024: nil). It is also the Company's policy not to invest
liquidity in financial products, including hedge funds or similar vehicles, with significant underlying leverage or
derivative exposure.
{i) Currency Risk
The Company presently has no significant currency risk as its principal revenue and expenditure are denominated
in British pounds sterling.
(ii) Credit Risk
Financial instruments which potentially subject the Company to concentration of credit risk with a specific
counterparty consist principally of cash and debtors. Management believes the concentration of credit risk
associated with the Company's cash is mitigated by the fact that these amounts are placed with the Royal Bank
of Scotland, which management believes to be a reputable financial institution and which Moody's has given a
short-term credit rating of P-1 and a long-term credit rating of A1 (as of July 2026).
The Company's exposure to its customers defaulting on the payment of their debts is limited, as the sole customers
: of the Company are its immediate shareholders, and the shareholders are contractually committed to fund the
, operations of the Company as they are reliant on it to provide the infrastructure maintenance and the network
operation and support.
. (iii) Liquidity Risk ;
, The Company actively maintains a combination of equity and shareholders’ loans that is designed to ensure the
: Company has sufficient available funds for operations and investment in fixed assets. Cash from revenue is
received from both shareholders on a monthly basis enabling the Company to cover costs and maintain a positive
bank balance. :
(iv) Interest Rate Cash Flow Risk
As at 31 March 2026, the Company had drawn down £27 million (year ended 31 December 2024: £48 million) ,
against the interest-bearing facilities. The Company's interest rate risk arises from long-term borrowings, which
are obtained from shareholders and bear interest at a floating rate based on the Bank of England SONIA rate at
the beginning of each month. While the floating rate borrowings technically expose the Company to cash flow
interest rate risk, this is also included in the management recharge (revenue) and therefore does not pose an
actual cash flow risk.

Commentary on Energy Efficiency Action Undertaken During the 15-month period to 31 March 2026
MBNL has seen a sharp increase in Scope 1 and Scope 2 CO2e emissions between 2024 and 2025-26. Leaving aside
the fact that this submission is for 15 months, and the previous year comparisons are for a calendar year the increase
has been partly driven by increased floor space at our offices in Reading, but mainly by a change in the reporting
boundary.
In conjunction with our shareholders, we have sought to accurately implement an ‘operational control boundary’, meaning
MBNL is now disclosing emissions for non-RAN services (Foundation Services) in shared sites that we control. This
same amount of energy consumption and emissions is being discounted from the shareholders’ inventory to avoid
double-counting. Details of the rationale and calculations are available on. request.
There has also been an increase in mandatory Scope 3 transport emissions. This is due to a marked increase in the
number of miles claimed by employees.
MBNL continues to work with its shareholders to manage energy in the large radio access network (RAN) it manages
on their behalf. Work in 2025-26 included an outlier identification and investigation process, measurement and
verification of the impact of a number of network optimisation initiatives, and continued review of our energy usage and
projection model to guide decision-making.
