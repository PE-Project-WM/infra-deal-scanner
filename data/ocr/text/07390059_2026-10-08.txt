The directors present the Strategic Report of Axon Public Safety UK Limited (the ‘Company’), together with
the financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025.
Principal activities and business review
The Company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Axon Public Safety B.V., part of the Axon Enterprise, Inc. group,
aglobal leader in law enforcement and public safety technology solutions. The principal activity of the Company
continued to be the distribution and provision of goods and services related to the aforementioned technology
solutions, including conducted electrical devices (‘CEDs’), wearable on-officer cameras, digital evidence
management software, and related accessories, within the UK market. The Company serves as a critical
distribution and sales center, directly engaging with key UK customers. The Company's activities encompass
product demonstrations, participation in industry trade shows, maintaining localised websites, executing targeted
advertising campaigns, and coordinating training classes. The Company holds inventory at its Daventry facility,
managing the warehousing and drop shipment of products such as TASER devices, body cameras, and related
accessories. In addition to its distribution role, the Company is responsible for obtaining necessary import and
export licenses and handling routine warranty returns for UK customers, on behalf of Axon Enterprise, Inc. Since
year ended 31 December 2022, the Company's scope has expanded to include research and development
services (‘R&D’) for Axon Enterprise, Inc. These R&D services focus on advanced technologies like computer
vision, artificial intelligence, and the development of body cameras, in-car cameras, and digital evidence
management platforms. As of the date of this report, the directors do not anticipate any significant changes to the
Company's activities in the upcoming year.
As shown in the Company's income statement, the Company's turnover has increased by 38% to £90,219,807
(2024: £65,406,406) with profit before tax of £4,393,763 (2024: £2,971,695). The increase in revenue from
sales of goods was primarily attributable to increased Hardware shipments, particularly Axon Body 4 and TASER
10 shipments. In 2022, the Company began providing research and development services to Axon Enterprise,
Inc., charged at a mark-up on costs incurred. These services generated revenue of £28,708,905, an increase of
73% on the prior year, making it one of the key drivers of the year-over-year revenue growth.
The directors consider the results achieved during the financial year to be in line with expectations in the
current economic climate. The directors have not recommended a dividend for the year (2024: ENil).

2. ACCOUNTING POLICIES (continued)
Turnover
Revenue arising from sale of hardware is recognised upon delivery of the hardware to the customer, as that is
the point at which the control of the good passes to the customer.
Revenue arising from the sale of services is recognised over time. Services consist of subscriptions to our
digital evidence management software and include varying levels of support. Contracts for servicing are
typically 36-60 months. Control is transferred evenly over the service period as the Company provides a stand
ready service. Accordingly, revenue is recognised on a straight line basis.
Where contracts with customers include a bundle of both a hardware and service element, the Company
allocates the contract transaction price to the sale goods and services using an estimate of the standalone
selling price of each distinct good or service in the contract.
Deferred revenue consists of payments received in advance related to hardware and services for which the
criteria for revenue recognition have not yet been met. Deferred revenue that will be recognised during the
subsequent twelve month period from the statement of financial position date is recorded as current deferred
revenue and the remaining portion is recorded as non-current. Generally, customers are billed in annual
installments. Accrued income arises on sales which have been delivered to the customer whereby revenue has
been recognised but not yet been billed for.
Intercompany revenue relates to research and development services which are provided by the Company to
Axon Enterprise, Inc. The Company is reimbursed by Axon Enterprise, Inc for expenses incurred plus a mark-
up based on an intercompany agreement. intercompany revenue is recognised on an accruals basis in the
period that the service takes place.
Revenue is stated net of VAT and trade discounts.
Cost of sales '
Cost of sales includes materia! costs, direct labour costs, shipping costs and direct sales commissions. In addition,
an intercompany cost of sales is recorded as the Company operates primarily as a limited risk distributor
(*LRD”) under the transfer pricing structure of the wider Group. Under the LRD model, there is a target operating
profit for the Company within an acceptable range validated through transfer pricing studies. The Company
calculates an adjustment in order to arrive at a 3% margin excluding the impact of the aforementioned R&D
which is then recognised as a component of cost of sales. This is deemed to be an arms’ length rate and
has been determined through consideration of the various functions that the Group provides, along with
market comparison for similar companies.
Research and Development costs
In the research stage of any project, itis not possible to demonstrate that the project will generate future economic
benefits and hence all expenditure on research is expensed when it is incurred. The Company has elected to
expense all research and development costs under the option available under FRS 102 as it is not practicable to
determine when the costs represent development costs. These costs are recorded in administrative expenses
given they are payroll in nature.
Tangible fixed assets
Tangible fixed assets are stated at historical cost less accumulated depreciation and any accumulated
impairment losses. Historical cost includes expenditure that is directly attributable to bringing the asset to the
location and condition necessary for it to be capable of operating in the manner intended by management.

Tangible fixed assets (continued)
The Company adds to the carrying amount of an item of fixed assets the cost of replacing part of such an item
when that cost is incurred if the replacement part is expected to provide incremental future benefits to the
Company. The carrying amount of the replaced part is derecognised. Repairs and maintenance are charged to
the income statement during the period in which they are incurred.
Depreciation is provided at the following annual rates in order to write off each asset over its estimated useful life
or, if held under a finance lease, over the lease term, whichever is the shorter, on a straight line basis.
Plant and machinery - 25% on cost
Fixtures and fittings - 20% - 33% on cost
Motor vehicles - 25% on cost
Computer equipment - 33% on cost
Leasehold improvements - Over the length of the lease
Where parts of an item of property, plant and equipment have different useful lives, they are accounted for as
separate items of property, plant and equipment.
Depreciation methods, useful lives and residual values are reviewed at each statement of financial position date.
The assets’ residual vaiues, useful lives and depreciation methods are reviewed, and adjusted prospectively if
appropriate, if there is an indication of a significant change since the last reporting date. The selection of these
residual values and estimated lives requires the exercise of judgement. The directors are required to assess
whether there is an indication of impairment to the carrying value of assets. In making that assessment,
judgements are made in estimating value in use. The directors consider that the individual carrying values of
assets are supportable by their value in use
Fixed assets are assessed at each reporting date to determine whether there is any indication that the assets are
impaired. Where there is any indication that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset is tested
for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised for the amount by which the asset's carrying amount exceeds
its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the higher of an asset's fair value less costs to sell and value :
in use. For the purposes of assessing impairment, assets are grouped at the lowest tevels for which there are
separately identifiable cash flows. Fixed assets that have been previously impaired are reviewed at each reporting
date to assess whether there is any Indication that the impairment losses recognised in prior periods may no
longer exist and should be reversed.
