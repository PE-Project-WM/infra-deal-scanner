The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 29 April 2025.
Introduction .
North PB Limited (the ‘company’) is a member of the North Group (the ‘group’). North PB Limited is a subsidiary of
Perth Bidco Limited, with Perth Topco Limited as the ultimate parent company.
The company’s principal activity is to design, deliver, and support integrated technology solutions that make
networks stronger and places smarter. North Group provides customers with the ability to partner with a single
provider that has expertise relating to physical infrastructure, networking & connectivity, safety & security and
deployment of loT sensors and applications.
North Group reported strong trading performance for the financial year 2024-25 delivering year-on-year 5% organic
revenue growth and a 23% EBITDA growth, with EBITDA (excluding non-recurring expenses) increasing from
£4.4m to £5.4m, and EBITDA margins improving to 8%. The reported loss for the financial year of £12.1m includes
£9.8m of goodwill amortisation and £7.0m of interest charges (prior year interest included £14.5m of waived
interest).
North also further improved engagement with colleagues and customers over the financial year 2024-25. Using the
Net Promoter methodology, North's internal ‘eNPS’ stands at +56 and North’s customer NPS stands at +70.
This strong performance is the result of a number of strategic initiatives that have been run across the business.
These include
* Amarket sector-aligned organisational model. North's 3 Sectors are each headed by a Managing Director.
: This has delivered clearer accountabilities and greater proximity to the marketplaces that North competes
in and the customers that we serve.
¢ Focusing business and product & capability development around our trademarked mantra of “NORTH
Stronger Networks, Smarter Places”. This focuses North on proactively developing solutions to meet
customers’ needs, as opposed to reacting to more narrowly specified product installation tenders, which in
turn builds scale and profitability per customer.
* Developing ‘repeatable propositions’ results in a virtual cycle of improved expertise, deeper vendor
relationships, increased reference cases and improved design & deployment efficiency.
¢ Focus on specific market segments and customer profiles where North is able to develop long term &
growing partnerships with sustainable profitability. -
¢ Increasing focus on developing individual skills & development plans which enables colleagues to
undertake a greater variety of work and to develop their careers.
Over the course of the Financial Year 2024-25, North completed a significant consolidation of its legal entities to
simplify reporting and operations. Consequently, Perth Topco Limited remains the ultimate parent company of the
North Group, with North PB Limited as the sole day-to-day trading company.
The Group employs over 330 staff with multiple offices across the UK, providing nationwide services. The Group
also delivers international projects for existing customers.
North is focused on developing multi-year relationships with organisations across the UK and on further developing
capabilities to provide end-to-end networking and ‘smarter place’ solutions.
Business review
North has a long-standing strategy to become a leading force in the integrated networking and ‘smarter places’
market. This is captured by “NORTH Stronger Networks, Smarter Places” which was trademarked in March 2024.
North places great emphasis on understanding customers’ goals and requirements beyond specific tender
requirements, which results in an end-to-end design that aligns with a customer's wider technology and outcomes
roadmap. The success of this approach has been reflected within each Sector and includes wins with a major UK
data centre operator which has engaged North to provide a multi-site integrated safety & security and networking
solution; two Universities which have engaged North to provide a ‘smart campus’ solution; and a major defence
establishment which is engaging with North to provide a ‘smart base’. An Executive Briefing Centre has been :
developed to run hands-on solution workshops with customers to demonstrate what is possible via North's
integration of ‘best of breed’ technologies.

Business Review (continued)
North also places great importance on engagement with “excellent” (reference Bain & Co) NPS scores for both
colleagues and customers. Annual voluntary attrition has reduced and customer referrals have increased, with
tangible new business opportunities amongst Housing Associations, Local Authorities, Data Centres, and the
Defence industry.
North has continued to focus on customers and segments which offer profitable growth potential, where North has
a strong ‘right to win’ and where our colleagues are excited to work.
Over the course of financial year 2024-25, North increased size of its sales team, focusing on hiring industry &/or
technology domain experts. This has resulted in both a significant increase in the value of the sales pipeline and
strong win-rates.
North is well-positioned to succeed in this market due to its breadth of expertise and experience, which includes
expert design skills covering a wide range of wired & wireless networking and safety & security technologies,
dependable project management & installation, deployment of software & analytics tools, and nationwide resources
to provide ongoing maintenance support. It is this combination that enables North to make networks stronger and
places smarter.
Principal risks and uncertainties
Exposure to UK economic and/or political conditions

: Adverse economic conditions could lead customers to reduce spending. With circa 50% of North’s revenue being
related to the Public Sector, this may include any tightening or change of government spending plans.
North is, to a degree, protected from these risks since its business activities underpin critical infrastructure and
solutions for customers. Within the Private Sector, the majority of North’s revenue comes from a blue-chip customer
base and North has exited smaller customers and industry sectors such as retail & hospitality that are more
sensitive to macro-economic conditions. Within the Public Sector, North’s solutions enable our customers to
improve safety & security and realise cost efficiencies through the deployment of smart solutions, both central to
the current administration's priorities.
Technology developments
North has a long history of understanding and adopting new technologies and continues to closely monitor
technology developments within its market through its close customer and vendor engagements, as well as
monitoring industry press and attending industry shows and seminars.
Advances in technology are resulting in cloud-based and ‘software as a service’ solutions becoming more
widespread and economically attractive. Equally, there are also increasingly rapid advances in the adoption and
use of Artificial Intelligence, which plays a critical role when deploying ‘smart solutions’ which combine CCTV,
Access Control and a range of loT sensors.
North views this as more opportunity than risk and is working with all its major vendors to align technology and
commercial roadmaps; these are available for briefing and ‘live’ demonstration via North’s Executive Briefing
Centre.
Competition
North operates in a competitive marketplace. At the ‘smaller end’, there are many local and regionally focused
resell & installation competitors which are competitive for small and lower complexity work; and at the ‘top end’
there are a small number of telecoms operators and systems integrators which are competitive for large scale
customers and programmes.
North is clear on where it has a high ‘right to win’ and differentiates based on deep customer centricity combined
with the ability to design, implement, and maintain multi-technology solutions with in-house expertise. There are few
competitors that offer the breadth and depth of in-house solution expertise, combining consulting and solution
design capability, ‘smart place’ integration & secure networking expertise with high-quality installation and service.
In addition, in each Sector North has significant recurring business from long-term frameworks and contracts,
where competition is limited to a select few.

Principal risks and uncertainties (continued)
Management of growth
The ability of North to deliver its projected growth ambitions requires it to deliver against its strategy of selling &
delivering integrated networking and smarter places solutions, be able to efficiently scale whilst maintaining close
customer relationships, and to continually replenish, and add to, a pipeline of new projects.
To this effect, management control systems are in place together with effective demand & supply planning tools and
management information. In order to resource to grow, significant developments have been made with regards to
internal training, vendor certification levels, management development, pipelining of external talent and
engagement with selected 3rd party service partners.
In addition, North's focus on larger customers and provision of ongoing managed & maintenance services results in
an increased proportion of contracted and recurring revenue streams and in an increased level of direct award
project work.
Funding and working capital
The Directors deem the company to have sufficient cash reserves to fund working capital requirements as the
business continues to grow.
North pursues a capex light strategy and seeks to maintain a low fixed overhead to minimise the risk of high
operating leverage within what is inherently a projects based business. In addition, a ‘core flex’ resource model is .
used for cost of sales labour.
Financial risk management objectives and policies
Detailed financial and cash flow forecasts are prepared and reviewed by the Group, on a monthly basis covering
updates from the sales, operations and finance departments. Cash balances are reviewed weekly with a detailed
forecast produced every two weeks.
Financial contro! measures and financial risks are reviewed regularly and a programme of continuous improvement
against any risks identified is in place.
Health & Safety
Some of North’s operations inherently involve high-risk activities, including working at height, within asbestos
present buildings, in high voltage environments, and occasionally with hazardous materials. Each of these
conditions pose a health & safety risk to North colleagues, subcontractors and/or to the general public.
To successfully manage these risks, North deploys a robust operating controls framework. This framework includes
strict adherence to North's Life Saving Rules and Safety Directives for all high-risk activities, alongside all required
industry certifications and a programme of regular training and awareness. To help manage these risks, North has
deployed ‘SafetyCulture’, a digital platform for safety inspections, audits, and issue management to all project and
engineering teams to provide a centrally managed & coordinated application with ‘real-time’ functionality to
colleagues working in live operational settings.
Cyber Security
All organisations face cyber security risks. North minimises this risk by operating its systems almost exclusively
from the cloud and by deploying a full range of Microsoft cyber security tools. In addition, regular training &
awareness interventions are made across the business alongside a range of regular security audits and tests.
North records, monitors and manages all systems interfaces with suppliers and customers to minimise 3rd party
risks.

UK businesses are currently facing many uncertainties, such as environmental sustainability and geopolitical events

Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of
impairment at each reporting end date.
Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that
occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected.
If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present
value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss
is recognised in profit or loss.
If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was
recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not
exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The
impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.

ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of
the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
