FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 SEPTEMBER 2025
Introduction
The directors present their Strategic Report of I.P. Integration Limited (the 'Company') for the year ended 30 September 2025.
Principal activity
The principal activity of I.P. Integration is through delivering digital transformation to the market, enabled by a suite of Contact Centres, Customer Engagement and AI solutions to mid-market, enterprise, and public sector organisations. In conjunction with these technology solutions, the Company provides consulting services, managed services, and network connectivity services that enable organisations to optimise the customer journey and the customer experience. These solutions and services are now delivered as cloud services.
Business review
The directors are pleased to report that the Company had another successful year with 9.1% and 8.4% growth across revenue and gross profit respectively. This success has been achieved despite the recent economic challenges posed by the 2024 budget. The knock-on impact of National Insurance increases has culminated in a number of our clients fast tracking focus on technology transformation projects that will help with business efficiency and cost reduction initiatives. However, decision making is taking slightly longer for clients in terms of signing off on larger transformation project budgets. This transition to becoming a consulting and outcome led CX company delivering  digital transformation with the practical use of AI across the customer experience environment has helped us to take a strong foothold with new logo wins across a number of vertical markets such as insurance, utilities and the retail sector.
In the year, overall revenues grew to £39.7m, representing a 9.1% increase on 2024, with recurring revenue for the period increasing to £34.8m (2024: £33.5m) representing 4% in year growth and 88% of total turnover. The recurring growth is attributable to securing multi-year subscription fee contracts for CCaaS solutions together with attached revenues of connectivity and network services which are typically secured in conjunction with the wider suite of our Customer Experience (CX) solutions on multiyear contracts whilst remaining focused on industry leading levels of customer retention through our purpose of exceptional customer contact. The non-recurring element of revenue saw a significant increase of £1.9m representing 66% growth on prior year at £3.0m, which was due to a large retail contract for a UK wide branch rollout involving delivery of a complex 3,000+ stores project and represents a major achievement.  
The directors are disappointed by the £0.7m reduction on prior year EBITDA which is broken down into 4 areas. Firstly, the Company changed its policy on non-recurring revenue recognition resulting in deferral of ~£0.3M of profit into next financial year. Secondly, there is ~£0.1m related to accounting for EMI options under share based payments expense (note 6). The Company had an ~£0.1m increase in staff costs due to Employers National Insurance increases and the year also saw a large customer enter into administration and an ~£0.1m increase in provision for bad debts (see note 16). 
This year there was an operating profit of £0.9m (2024: operating profit of £1.8m).
The gross margin saw a decrease from 47.7% to 44.4%. This is attributable to our sales success and the winning of CCaaS new logos where the margins are lower than our historical averages but add growth to our contract base as can be seen in the Gross Profit increase. 11 new logos were onboarded within the financial year.

Directors' statement of compliance with duty to promote the success of the Company
Section 172(1) requires Directors to act in a way that they consider would be most likely to promote the success of the Company for the benefit of the Company’s stakeholders as a whole. The directors consider the likely consequences of its decisions in the long term and the need to act fairly between its stakeholders. We strive to achieve positive outcomes for all our stakeholders through innovation and operational brilliance whilst considering culture, inclusion, community and social responsibility. The Company’s key stakeholders and how they have been considered are set out below:
• 
Shareholders
The desire to deliver for the shareholders in the short, mid and long-term ensures the business focus on activities and objectives that gives the best chance of continued success and future growth, whilst incorporating the correct governance structure aligned with continued growth. The management team provides regular feedback to its shareholders through business review meetings and twice monthly calls to the Company.
• 
Employees
Our people are our biggest asset and we consistently strive for a culture of collaboration, respect and inclusion through a shared purpose and a set of common values. This culture cultivates innovation and creativity whilst encouraging challenge and diversity. Our common goals and objectives help to achieve alignment so our people feel empowered to deliver our goals whilst also maintaining an environment  for personal development and growth. We actively promote engagement and maintain an internal communications program involving twice monthly all hands calls where we update on business progress and recognise people for individual and team exceptional performance and success. Members of the management team also attend connect and collaborate sessions at each of our respective offices where, in smaller groups, employees are encouraged to raise challenges and to come up with new ideas and provide feedback on improvement areas.
The Company is also committed to a learning and development environment  for all employees with access to various online training materials as well in person training sessions to help develop new skills as the company grows. There is also specific management and leadership training for the managers across the company as well as for the early careers people. We also have a focus on delivering training for our early careers people that have been highlighted as future leaders (RISE Program). The RISE program is run by our CEO and Chief People Officer and aimed at highlighting and developing our best early careers talent. This  together with a focus on early careers recruitment is proving successful in bringing in new ideas and innovation and challenging the way we think.
• 
Customers
We continue to serve nearly 200 customers across the  UK but also in Europe and North America with our purpose being exceptional customer contact at the heart of all we do. The evolution of our channel strategy as part of our go to market plan has helped us to onboard new clients in international markets. We strive to achieve great outcomes for our customers through delivering best in class services and solutions that meet the needs of their company and customers in a fast changing technology environment. We consistently seek feedback through customer satisfaction surveys, customer events, forums and webinars in an effort to continuously improve our engagement and create relationships at all levels.

Investment activity
Investment in the fiscal year was principally focused on the continued development of our own IPR across 3 core areas: 1) In house applications; building on the success of launching ‘IPI Cloud PCI’’ into the global market place through partnerships with Amazon, Avaya Genesys and Nice providing channel access and global coverage. 2) The ongoing investment and feature enhancements to fully embed AI and analytics capability into our own Elastic CX CCaaS platform has resulted in key new logo wins. The IPI Innovation team are building a robust roadmap for the continual enhancement of our technology propositions; including a full suite of Artificial Intelligence (AI) applications that are being embedded into our key CX propositions together with adjacent security offerings 3) The continued investment in our consulting, customer success and managed services propositions aimed at helping our clients to achieve outcome led transformation to achieve efficiencies or growth opportunities.  
Principal risks and uncertainties
In relation to the key financial risks of competition, price, credit, liquidity, cash flow, foreign currency and interest rate risks, the directors have familiarised themselves with the concepts of these risks including the risks associated post UK budget and general macro-economic conditions. In the meantime, the directors are managing all risks by continued focus on ensuring that the organisation can carefully manage its continued growth. Our business improvement team together with the security and compliance team help the company to deliver  ‘Operational Brilliance’ used to maintain all compliance requirements across, legal, employment, health and safety and environmental matters. Whilst ensuring we have a robust security posture to protect the organisation and our customers. The directors have continued to maintain and invest in; robust systems, infrastructure and processes managed through the Quality Management System (QMS) and the Group being certified to:
• ISO 9001 Quality Management
• ISO 14001 Environmental Management
• ISO 27001 Information & Security management
• Cyber Essentials
The continued investment in the Company's platform and services will enhance our focus on being a digital transformation and AI first company.

Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include trade and other debtors, cash and bank balances, are initially measured at their transaction price (adjusted for transaction costs except in the initial measurement of financial assets that are subsequently measured at fair value through profit and loss) and are subsequently carried at their amortised cost using the effective interest method, less any provision for impairment, unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest.

Impairment of financial assets
At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cashflows discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.
Financial assets are impaired when events, subsequent to their initial recognition, indicate the estimated future cash flows derived from the financial asset(s) have been adversely impacted. The impairment loss will be the difference between the current carrying amount and the present value of the future cash flows at the asset(s) original effective interest rate.
If there is a favourable change in relation to the events surrounding the impairment loss then the impairment can be reviewed for possible reversal. The reversal will not cause the current carrying amount to exceed the original carrying amount had the impairment not been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Judgements in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In applying the Company’s accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions in determining the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities. The directors’ judgements, estimates and assumptions are based on the best and most reliable evidence available at the time when the decisions are made and are based on historical experience and other factors that are considered to be applicable. Due to the inherent subjectivity involved in making such judgements, estimates and assumptions, the actual results and outcomes may differ.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the year in which the estimate is revised, if the revision affects only that year, or in the year of the revision and future years, if the revision affects both current and future years.
3.1 Critical judgements in applying the Company’s accounting policies
(i) Assessing indicators of impairment 
In assessing whether there have been any indicators of impairment of assets, the directors have considered both external and internal sources of information such as market conditions, counterparty credit ratings and experience of recoverability. The key assumptions concerning the future, and other key sources of estimation uncertainty, that have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities within the next financial year are discussed below.
(i) Determining useful economic lives of goodwill and intangible assets
On acquisition, the group determines a reliable estimate of the useful life of goodwill and intangible assets based upon factors such as the expected use of the acquired business, forecasts of expected future results and cash flows, and any legal, regulatory or contractual provisions that can limit useful life. 
At each subsequent reporting date the directors consider whether there are any factors such as technological advancements or changes in market conditions that indicate a need to reconsider the useful life of goodwill and intangible assets.
(ii) Determining the percentage completion of service based revenue
Revenue arising from fixed price professional services contracts is estimated using a percentage of completion method. The percentage of completion method is based on the actual service performed as a percentage of the total services to be provided. Estimates of revenues, cost or extent of which progress towards completion are revised if circumstances change and are reflected in the Statement of Comprehensive Income in the period in which the circumstances have arisen.
