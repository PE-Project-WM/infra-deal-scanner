STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MAY 2026
Introduction
Nano Fibre UK Limited (“the Company”) presents its strategic report for the year ended 31 May 2026. The report provides a fair review of the business, an analysis of performance using financial and other key performance indicators, and outlines the principal risks and uncertainties facing the Company.
Business review
The Company continued to operate as a specialist provider of telecommunications infrastructure and engineering services across the United Kingdom. During the year, the business strengthened its market position through continued growth in installation and civil engineering activities, supported by long-term customer relationships and increased operational capacity.
Turnover increased to £24,124,352 (2025: £21,934,414), representing growth of approximately 10.0%. The increase in turnover was accompanied by an improvement in gross profit, which increased to £7,411,353 (2025: £5,589,448). The gross profit margin increased to 30.7% (2025: 25.5%).
Other operating income increased to £74,891 (2025: £46,763), including insurance claims receivable, royalty income and recoveries.
Operating profit increased to £1,984,766 (2025: £384,601). After interest receivable of £3,411 and interest payable of £348,895, profit before tax was £1,639,282 (2025: £144,758).
The Company recognised a deferred tax charge of £441,950 (2025: credit of £313,072), resulting in a profit for the year of £1,197,332 (2025: £457,830).
The directors consider the overall financial performance for the year to be positive, with improved turnover, gross margin and operating profitability compared with the prior year.
Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors continually assess the principal risks affecting the business and implement appropriate controls to mitigate those risks.
The principal risks facing the Company include:
• Market risk – changes in demand within the telecommunications infrastructure sector and broader economic conditions.
• Operational risk – maintaining service quality, operational efficiency and health and safety standards whilst supporting continued growth.
• Credit risk – exposure to customer default, managed through ongoing credit assessments and monitoring of receivable balances.
• Liquidity risk – maintaining adequate working capital and funding to support operations and meet financial obligations as they fall due.
• Regulatory and compliance risk – ensuring continued compliance with telecommunications, employment, health and safety, environmental and data protection legislation.
The directors regularly review financial performance, cash flow forecasts and operational metrics to ensure these risks remain appropriately managed.
