Business review 
Our priority continues to be working with our customers and partners to transition their business with modern secure technology workplace solutions. 
Our acquisitions in FY25 have proved successful and are making strong contributions to gross profit in both cases. We continue to maintain a strong acquisition pipeline with further acquisitions expected to complete in 2026.
We maintain a strong relationship with our associated Cybersecurity business, Chess T2 Limited trading as ‘CyberLab’, to maximise cross-sales opportunities. 
We continue to position the business and product offerings to focus on more profitable cash generative activities resulting in a further deleveraging of the Balance Sheet post year end. The company’s relatively low gearing compared to many of our peers enables us to drive growth by acquisition as well as organically. Our product set is centred around the transition of legacy voice customers to cloud-hosted solutions. Chess is, by turnover, 83% direct business underpinned by 86% contracted recurring revenues highlighting the stability and strength of our business model.
Gross Margin remains our key performance indicator, and we saw improvement in Gross Margin percentage improved from 46.8% to 47.8%. Adjusted EBITDA decreased from £8.2m to £6.2m as, despite the improvement in Gross Profit percentage, we saw a decline in revenues due to churn, and we have continued to invest in additional human and financial resource to achieve market leading solutions for our customers.
Investing in industry leading customer service is illustrated by the overwhelmingly positive feedback from customers, with a 4.6 Trustpilot score.
Our corporate, enterprise and public sector customers are demonstrating 5% organic growth in cloud connectivity as they transition to modern secure communications and solutions. 
During the year we have driven growth in NGN cloud numbers and mobile, but in line with our peers, legacy small business connectivity customers churn at 16% p.a. of which one third is driven by business closures and bad debt. 
Our IT Support and Professional Services division streamlined to focus on growing profitable customers. 
Our Digital transformation division performs consistently, pleasingly growing its portfolio of services with the acquisition of Silversands Limited in November 2024.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Like all businesses, Chess faces a range of risks and uncertainties that could impact the delivery of our long-term strategy, spanning operational, finance, and regulatory areas. The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Group are monitored with a Risk Register. The board meets once a month to identify, evaluate and manage our exposure to known risks to all divisions of the business. For the risks within our control, the board puts appropriate processes and controls in place to mitigate them. The principal risks and uncertainties facing the business are set out below.
Acquisition integration risk
Acquisitions have driven the significant growth of the Group over the past few years and remains a key part of our future strategy. Acquisitions by their nature have the potential to cause operational, cultural and system issues during the integration phase, all of which can impact the ability of the business to deliver on the expected financial performance. To mitigate this risk, there is a detailed planning and due diligence process run by a dedicated acquisitions team prior acquisition and board level approval is required on acquisition agreements and payments.
During the integration phase, there are regular peer-to-peer senior management meetings to respond to day-to-day complications with the assimilation process, as well as consultation meetings with new People and a strategy focused away day to ensure we deliver on performance. Where possible, an element of the acquisition consideration is contingent upon performance of the acquisition, which encourages all parties to work together to overcome any issues that arise as soon as possible.
Market risk
The ICT and Telecommunications markets are constantly evolving, and failure to keep abreast of emerging product trends could impact financial performance and growth. There is a risk that the business is unable to deliver on customers' requirements or that we invest in areas that do not generate the expected demand. Chess monitors market developments closely by having active dialogue with suppliers to keep abreast of market changes and training our People on the latest technological developments (including, for example, new AI technologies during the current year). We also undertake strategic reviews of the broader market periodically, as part of our budgetary process.
Chess puts significant internal resource into new product development with a team of People focused in this area and regularly launches new products based on customer feedback and needs.
Liquidity and interest rate cash flow risk
The Group's policy is to finance its operations and expansion through working capital. The interest rate on long-term loans used to finance acquisitions is linked to SOFR and therefore the Group is exposed to interest rate risk on the loan. The Group's banking facilities also includes a financial leverage covenant, to manage this risk the Group regularly reviews forecasts to ensure continued compliance with the financial covenants and continued availability of the banking facilities.
Credit risk
The Group's credit risk is primarily attributed to its trade debtors. The amounts presented in the balance sheet are net of any allowance for doubtful debtors, estimated by the directors. The Group has  no significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread across our large and diverse customer base. The trade receivable balances are monitored on an ongoing basis to ensure that the Group's exposure to bad debt is not significant and appropriate provision for bad debt risk is made. The Group also requires appropriate credit checks on potential customers before sales are made.

Data protection and cyber security
The Group holds a significant volume of personal information, both confidential and sensitive in nature. Failure to comply with data privacy regulations, such as GDPR, and adopting best practices across information network security may result in potential data breach(es) across our business, which would cause detrimental brand damage to us and loss of confidence and privacy to our customers. The Group has a dedicated Information Security Officer to oversee operational compliance towards all data privacy regulations and to instil the security best practices across our IT Network infrastructure, most notably information security and Cyber Essentials accreditations. Our Group holds the ISO 27001 standard and subsequently carries out regular penetration testing, and system upgrades to meet the audit requirements.
Cyber Essentials has been implemented across our key operational sites and is subject to regular and frequent audits as to its effectiveness. The Group also holds the ISO 9001 accreditation which drives continual improvement, regulatory compliance and customer satisfaction across the business, our internal audit team are responsible for the ongoing maintenance of the quality standards and raise opportunities for improvement. should any nonconformities arise.
General economic risk
The Company, like other businesses, recognises that the current economic uncertainty is likely to continue. Therefore, the Group continually reviews its forecasts including any impact on consumer confidence to monitor the general economic climate.

Going concern
The directors have undertaken a rigorous risk assessment of the profitability and liquidity of the Group over the next 12 months. Considerable internal effort has been focussed on our cash position, credit management and cost control and Chess have implemented a series of actions to generate cash and maintain liquidity through FY25 and beyond. At the date of signing the financial statements, all of the Group's activities are continuing to operate as normal.
In addition, the directors have reviewed the Group's trading forecasts for the 12 months from the date of this report, and stress-tested the key inputs and assumptions. This analysis supports the conclusion of the directors that it is appropriate to prepare the financial statements on a going concern basis for the Chess Group.
The directors have considered the principal risks and uncertainties facing the Group and have established policies for managing these. 2.2
Basis of consolidation
The consolidated financial statements present the results of the Company and its own subsidiaries ("the Group") as if they form a single entity. Intercompany transactions and balances between group companies are therefore eliminated in full.
The consolidated financial statements incorporate the results of business combinations using the purchase method. In the Balance Sheet, the acquiree's identifiable assets, liabilities and contingent liabilities are initially recognised at their fair values at the acquisition date. The results of acquired operations are included in the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income from the date on which control is obtained. They are deconsolidated from the date control ceases.

2.3
Going concern
The directors have undertaken a rigorous risk assessment of the profitability and liquidity of the Group over the next 12 months. Considerable internal effort has been focused on our cash position, credit management and cost control and Chess have implemented a series of actions to generate cash and maintain liquidity through FY25 and beyond. At the date of signing the financial statements, all of the Group's activities are continuing to operate as normal.
In addition, the directors have reviewed the Group's trading forecasts for a minimum period of 12 months from the date of this report, and stress-tested the key inputs and assumptions. This analysis supports the conclusion of the directors that it is appropriate to prepare the financial statements on a going concern basis for the Chess Group.
The directors have considered the principal risks and uncertainties facing the Group and have established policies for managing these. The directors have prepared a cash flow forecast covering future periods, which shows, when considering all reasonably possible changes in trading, that the Company should be able to comfortably meet its liabilities as they fall due.
As at 30 April 2025, the Group had net assets of £2.2m (2024 - £0.7m net assets). The Group also generated an Adjusted EBITDA of £6.2m (2024 - £8.2m) in the last financial year.
On 27 January 2026, the directors extended the £15m facility with Barclays, drawn in relation to the JSOP, to a revised termination date of 31 January 2027. The renewed facility includes a further option for both parties to extend the termination date by one further year to 31 January 2028, subject to ongoing compliance with relevant adjusted leverage covenants.
In making their going concern assessment, the directors have considered the Group's ability to complete loan refinancing as outlined above, and consider this to be feasible. As such, the going concern assessment includes the assumption that such refinancing will be achievable by the Group. Based on their assessment, the directors consider that the Group has sufficient resources to meet its obligations as they fall due for a period of at least 12 months from the date of this report, and accordingly these financial statements are prepared on the going concern basis.

2.5
Turnover
Turnover is recognised to the extent that the Group obtains the right to consideration in exchange for its performance. Turnover is measured at the fair value of the consideration received, excluding discounts, rebates, VAT and other sales taxes or duty.
Call turnover
Call turnover is recognised in the month in which calls are made. Calls made in the year, but not billed by the year-end are accrued within debtors as accrued income.
Line rental
Line rental income is recognised during the month of rental.
Handsets
Turnover and related costs from the sale of handsets are recognised on the date of connection.
Connection commissions
Connection commissions are receivable from mobile network operators when the Group connects or retains end users to their network. The commissions are recognised as income by the Group over the period of service required by the commission agreement. When the Group assesses that there is an ongoing obligation beyond connection, commission is recognised evenly over the period of the obligation. When the Group assesses that there is no ongoing obligation beyond connection, the full commission expected to be recovered over the life of the contract is recognised upon connection and an associated accrued income balance is recognised within "Amounts due from network operators" as part of the overall debtors balance. This balance due from network operators is typically recoverable over the term of the contract as commission payments are received from the mobile network operators.
The full commission to be received over the life of the contract is dependent on usage, and so related spend, by the consumer. As such, the consideration receivable by the Group is variable and involves estimation (see note 3 where this has been disclosed as a key source of estimation uncertainty and how this estimate is derived). Variable consideration is recognised as revenue to the extent that it is highly probable that a significant reversal shall not occur in the future.
In some instances commission income is subject to adjustment by the mobile network operator. Where this is the case, the Group assesses the likelihood of adjustment by reference to historical experience and adjusts commissions income accordingly.
Long-term contracts
These contracts are accounted for in accordance with the percentage of completion guidance in Section 23 of FRS 102. The only contracts that are accounted for in this manner are support service contracts, which typically have a term of between 12 months and 60 months.
The percentage of completion is measured by reference to the number of days completed on a contract at the balance sheet date, compared to the total expected number of days required on that contract. When it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue the expected loss shall be recognised as an expense immediately with a corresponding provision for an onerous contract.

Subscriber acquisition costs
Subscriber acquisition costs (including within customer lists and relationships) comprise the direct third-party costs of recruiting and retaining customers, net of incentives from network operators and provisions for in-contract churn. These costs are expensed as incurred, unless they meet the criteria for capitalisation as an intangible asset, to the extent that they are supported by expected future cash inflows, in which case they are amortised on a straight-line basis over the shorter of the customer life and the contractual period.

Amounts due from network operators
As detailed in the turnover recognition policy, connection commissions are receivable from mobile network operators when the Group connects or retains end users to their network. This commission is received over the life of the connection and therefore the future amount due on a connection is recognised within trade debtors. Similarly, the Group has an obligation at the point of the connection to pay the Partner who sold the connection. This commission payable is recognised within creditors.

2.9
Operating leases: the Group as lessee
Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

2.11
Research and development
In the research phase of an internal project it is not possible to demonstrate that the project will generate future economic benefits and hence all expenditure on research shall be recognised as an expense when it is incurred. Intangible assets are recognised from the development phase of a project if and only if certain specific criteria are met in order to demonstrate the asset will generate probable future economic benefits and that its cost can be reliably measured. The capitalised development costs are subsequently amortised on a straight-line basis over their useful economic lives, which range from 2 to 5 years.
If it is not possible to distinguish between the research phase and the development phase of an internal project, the expenditure is treated as if it were all incurred in the research phase only.

2.17
Intangible assets
Goodwill
Positive goodwill arising on acquisitions is capitalised as an asset on the Balance Sheet and amortised on a straight-line basis over its useful economic life up to a maximum of 5 years. It is reviewed for impairment at the end of the first full financial year following the acquisition and in other periods if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable.
Where goodwill arises through a share acquisition it is classified as Goodwill on Consolidation. Where it arises through a trade and asset acquisition it is classified as Purchased Goodwill.
Where the fair value of the assets and liabilities acquired exceed the cost of the acquisition, negative goodwill arises. The Group after consideration of the assets, liabilities and contingent consideration acquired and the cost of the combination, recognises negative goodwill on the Balance Sheet. This is released to the profit and loss over the period in which the benefit is expected to arise up to a maximum of 5 years.
Other intangible assets
Intangible assets acquired separately from a business are capitalised at cost. Intangible assets acquired as part of an acquisition of a business are capitalised separately from goodwill if the fair value can be measured reliably on initial recognition, subject to the constraint that, unless the asset had a readily ascertainable market value, the fair value is limited to an amount that does not create or increase any negative goodwill arising on the acquisition. Intangible assets, excluding development costs, created within the business are not capitalised and expenditure is charged against profits in the year in which it was incurred.
Intangible assets are amortised on a straight-line basis over their estimated useful lives. The carrying value of intangible assets is reviewed for impairment at the end of the first full year following acquisition and in other periods if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable.

Provisions for liabilities
Provisions are recognised when an event has taken place that gives rise to a legal or constructive obligation, a transfer of economic benefits is probable and a reliable estimate can be made.

2.24
Financial instruments
Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised in the Balance Sheet when the Group becomes a party to the contractual provisions of the instrument. 
Trade and other debtors and creditors are classified as basic financial instruments and measured at initial recognition at transaction price. Debtors and creditors are subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method. A provision is established when there is objective evidence that the Group will not be able to collect all amounts due. 
Cash and cash equivalents are classified as basic financial instruments and comprise cash in hand and at bank, short-term bank deposits with an original maturity of three months or less and bank overdrafts which are an integral part of the Group's cash management. 
Financial liabilities and equity instruments issued by the Group are classified in accordance with the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into and the definitions of a financial liability and an equity instrument. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the Group after deducting all of its liabilities. Equity instruments issued by the Group are recorded at the proceeds received, net of direct issue costs. 
Interest bearing bank loans, overdrafts and other loans which meet the criteria to be classified as basic financial instruments are initially recorded at the present value of cash payable to the bank, which is ordinarily equal to the proceeds received net of direct issue costs. These liabilities are subsequently measured at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method.

2.25
Impairment of non-financial assets
At each balance sheet date non-financial assets not carried at fair value are assessed to determine whether there is an indication that the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) may be impaired. If there is such an indication the recoverable amount of the asset (or asset's cash generating unit) is compared to the carrying amount of the asset (or asset's cash generating unit).
The recoverable amount of the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) is the higher of the fair value less costs to sell and value in use. Value in use is defined as the present value  of the future  cash flows before interest and tax obtainable as a result of the asset's (or asset's cash generating unit) continued use. These cash flows are discounted using a pre-tax discount rate that represents the current market risk free and the risks inherent in the assets.
If the recoverable amount of the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) is estimated to be lower than the carrying amount, the carrying amount is reduced to its recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised in the profit and loss account.
If an impairment loss is subsequently reversed, the carrying amount of the assets (or asset's cash generating unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but only to the extent that the revised carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of depreciation or amortisation) had no impairment loss been recognised in prior periods. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised in the profit and loss account.
Goodwill is allocated on acquisition to the cash generating unit expected to benefit from the synergies of the combination. Goodwill is included in the carrying value of cash generating units for impairment testing.

3.
Judgements in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty
In the application of the Group's accounting policies, the directors are required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions about the carrying amount of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are based on historical experience and other factors that are considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.
The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised if the revision affects only that period, or in the period of the revision and future periods if the revision affects both current and future periods.
The directors consider that the following judgements including key sources of estimation uncertainty have had the most significant effect on amounts recognised in the financial statements:
Commissions 
Judgement is applied to determine whether commission contracts contain performance obligations beyond connection. Management forms this judgement based on the terms of the contract. The revenue recognition policy note details the treatment applied in the cases where there are ongoing performance obligations and also where all obligations are complete upon connection.
The commission recognised as receivable from network operators and the commission payable to partners, are based on expected spend on the mobile contract by the end user over the life of the contract.
When the Group assesses that there is not an ongoing obligation beyond connection, commission is recognised upon connection. This is therefore initially based on an expectation of what the future spend will be by end user. Where available, such as on a customer renewing a contract, this estimate is based on historic information specific to that customer. Where it is a new customer, the estimate is based on the average revenue per user over the entire mobile base. As such there is inherent estimation in the amounts recognised and this reconsidered by management throughout the life of the contract.
Intangible assets and goodwill carrying values 
Intangible assets and goodwill are assessed at each reporting date to determine whether there is any indication that the assets are impaired. Where there is an indication that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset is tested for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised for the amount by Which the assets carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the higher of an assets fair value less costs to and its value in use. The value in use calculation requires estimation of future discounted cash flows.
Fair value of acquired identifiable intangible assets 
The Group can acquire identifiable assets both individually and as part of business combinations. Management estimates the fair value of identifiable intangible assets by considering the present value of the expected future economic benefit to be generated by the asset.
Trade debtors and amounts due from network operators
At each reporting date, trade debtors are assessed for recoverability. If there is any evidence of impairment, the carrying amount is reduced to its recoverable amount. The impairment loss is recognised immediately in the profit and loss account. Estimation is required regarding expected future cash receipts.

The Chess Limited Joint Share Ownership Plan (JSOP)
In January 2016 the Company established an employees' joint share ownership plan ('the JSOP') under which selected employees of the Company were invited to acquire interests, as joint beneficial owners upon the terms of a joint ownership agreement ('JOA'), in a total of 600,962 shares in the Company held by the Trust. In 2022, the JSOP was revised with additional invites to selected senior employees to acquire interests on revised terms. Such shares ('Jointly Owned Shares') are beneficially jointly owned by the Trust and the employee to whom such an interest in the shares has been awarded. The JOA provides for:
∙
the initial acquisition of the employee's interest, as joint Owner, in the Jointly Owned Shares: and
∙
the division of the proceeds of sale, as between the employee and the Trust, when vested Jointly Owned Shares are sold or otherwise disposed of.
In particular, the JOA provides that the proceeds of sale of vested Jointly Owned Shares will be divided unequally between the employee and the Trust so that the employee will receive all of the growth in value of the Jointly Owned Shares above a threshold amount of £21.59 per share (plus a fixed rate of interest on their initial value), and the Trust will be entitled to the balance, i.e. the threshold amount of the Jointly Owned Shares plus the interest calculated on that amount ("the Hurdle Amount'). If the disposal proceeds are Less than the Hurdle Amount, the Trust will be entitled to the whole of the proceeds (less a nominal amount).
Vesting of the employees' interests in the Jointly Owned Shares is subject to performance targets relating to the Level of EBITDA achieved in the financial years up to and including 30 April 2024. The EBITDA target was not achieved and therefore the value attributed to the shares at 30 April 2024 was Nil. If an employee's interest in Jointly Owned Shares does not become 'vested', the Trust will have the right to buy back the interest for a nominal consideration.
Vested Jointly Owned Shares may only be sold with the agreement of both joint owners and subject to the JOA and the Articles of Association of the Company.
